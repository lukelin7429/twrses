Unit 11: Who Sits Where in an Orchestra?

An orchestra is a large instrumental ensemble that combines instruments from different families. There are typically four main sections: strings, woodwinds, brass, and percussion. A full-size orchestra may be called a symphony orchestra or a philharmonic orchestra, and Taiwan’s dictionary says there is no real difference between the two names. Its size may vary from seventy to over one hundred players, depending on the music and the hall.

管弦樂團是一個大型的樂器合奏團體，把不同家族的樂器結合在一起。通常有四個主要的組：弦樂、木管、銅管和打擊。全編制的樂團可以叫交響樂團或愛樂樂團，台灣的辭典說這兩個名稱之間並沒有實質的差別。人數可以從七十到一百多位不等，要看曲子和場地。

The strings are the first and second violins, violas, cellos, and double basses, and they are usually the most numerous group. The woodwind section usually includes flutes, oboes, clarinets, and bassoons. The brass are French horns, trumpets, trombones, and a tuba. The percussion include the timpani, snare drum, bass drum, cymbals, and triangle. The timpani can be tuned to particular notes. A harp is often added, and keyboard instruments may form a fifth section.

弦樂是第一和第二小提琴、中提琴、大提琴和低音提琴，通常是人數最多的一組。木管通常包括長笛、雙簧管、單簧管和低音管。銅管是法國號、小號、長號和一支低音號。打擊包括定音鼓、小鼓、大鼓、鈸和三角鐵。定音鼓可以調到特定的音。常常還會加上豎琴，鍵盤樂器有時構成第五組。

These four sections are not the same as the four families in lesson 9. The woodwinds and the brass are both aerophones, because air vibrates in both. The percussion section includes membranophones, such as the timpani, and idiophones, such as the cymbals. A piano is a chordophone. And the names do not describe materials: a saxophone is usually made of brass but belongs to the woodwinds, and a brass instrument is defined by the way its sound is made.

這四個組和第九課的四個家族不一樣。木管和銅管都是氣鳴樂器，因為兩者振動的都是空氣。打擊這一組同時包含膜鳴樂器（例如定音鼓）和體鳴樂器（例如鈸）。鋼琴是弦鳴樂器。而且名稱不是在說材料：薩克斯風通常是銅做的，卻屬於木管；銅管樂器是由發聲的方式來定義的。

In the usual plan the strings sit at the front, in a semicircle around the conductor’s podium. The Berlin Philharmonic describes one general principle: the sections are arranged by volume. The softer strings sit at the front, then the more powerful woodwinds, then the louder brass, and finally the percussion at the back. But it is a tradition and not a rule. The conductors decide, thinking about the music, the hall, how well the sections blend, and whether they can see the players.

通常的座位，是弦樂坐在最前面，圍著指揮台排成半圓形。柏林愛樂官網描述了一個通則：各組依音量來排。比較輕的弦樂坐在前面，接著是比較有力的木管，然後是比較響的銅管，最後面是打擊。但這是傳統，不是規定。由指揮決定，他們考慮曲子、場地、各組合不合得起來，還有看不看得到演奏者。

Even the strings can be arranged in different ways. In the so-called American setup, the first violins, second violins, violas, and cellos sit in that order from the audience’s left. In the German setup, the first and second violins sit opposite each other, with the cellos and violas between them. The double basses sit behind the strings, on the left or the right. Even the horns may switch sides. So the picture in this lesson is one possible plan.

連弦樂也有不同的排法。所謂的美式排法，從觀眾的左邊起，依序是第一小提琴、第二小提琴、中提琴、大提琴。德式排法，是第一與第二小提琴面對面，大提琴和中提琴在它們中間。低音提琴坐在弦樂後面，可以在左邊，也可以在右邊。連法國號也可能換邊。所以這一課的圖，只是一種可能的排法。

The conductor stands on a raised podium. The conductor sets the tempo, makes sure each group comes in at the right time, and shapes the phrasing, often with a baton. Most conductors do not play an instrument when they conduct, so all the sound is made by the musicians. The concertmaster, who leads the first violins, is the second-in-command and leads the tuning. By a convention hundreds of years old, the first oboe plays the note A for the orchestra to tune to.

指揮站在一個升高的台上。指揮定速度、確保每一組在對的時間進來、塑造樂句的形狀，常常用指揮棒。大多數指揮在指揮時不演奏樂器，所以所有的聲音都由樂手發出。樂團首席帶領第一小提琴，是僅次於指揮的第二號人物，也帶領調音。依一個幾百年的慣例，由第一雙簧管吹出 A 音，讓全團對準。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Why do the strings usually sit at the front?

One general principle is to seat the sections by volume, with the softer strings at the front and the louder brass and percussion behind. It is a tradition, and conductors adjust it for the music and the hall.

What does a conductor do, if the conductor does not make the sound?

The conductor sets the tempo, makes sure each group comes in at the right time, and shapes the phrasing, so the musicians play together. The musicians make all the sound.

Why are the orchestra’s four sections not the same as the four families of lesson 9?

The orchestra’s sections follow a custom of grouping players. Woodwinds and brass are both aerophones, the percussion split into membranophones and idiophones, and a piano is a chordophone, so the two ways of sorting cut across each other.

Stories Behind the Notes · 音符背後的故事

A Greek word, two layouts, an experimenter, and three Taiwanese orchestras 一個希臘字、兩種排法、一位實驗者，和三個台灣的樂團

Four short stories about how orchestras came to sit the way they do.
關於樂團怎麼坐成今天這樣的四個小故事。

A Greek Word · 希臘字

The place in front of the stage舞台前面的那塊地方

The word orchestra comes from the Greek orchestra, the name of the area in front of a stage in ancient Greek theaters. Taiwan’s dictionary says the same: it was the place where the chorus stood, and the meaning later grew to a group of instruments playing together, usually in music after the eighteenth century.
orchestra 這個字來自希臘文的 orchestra，是古希臘劇場裡舞台前面那塊地方的名稱。台灣的辭典也這麼說：那是合唱團站的位置，後來延伸成一群樂器合奏的團體，通常用在十八世紀之後的樂團。

Two Layouts · 兩種排法

American and German美式與德式

In the 19th century it was standard to put the first and second violins on opposite sides. Today the most common layout puts the first violins, second violins, violas, and cellos in a line, with the basses behind. Some conductors prefer the German layout, with the violins facing each other. A composer may even specify the seating: Bartók wrote for strings placed in a conversational, antiphonal way.
十九世紀的標準是把第一和第二小提琴放在相對的兩邊。今天最常見的排法，是第一小提琴、第二小提琴、中提琴、大提琴排成一線，低音提琴在後面。有些指揮喜歡德式排法，讓小提琴面對面。作曲家甚至可以指定座位：巴爾托克的曲子，就要求弦樂用像對話一樣的「交替」方式擺放。

An Experimenter · 實驗者

Stokowski moved everyone史托科夫斯基讓大家換位子

The conductor Leopold Stokowski is credited as the first to adopt the seating plan that most orchestras use today, with the first and second violins together on the conductor’s left and the violas and cellos to the right. He kept changing the seating of his sections. At one time he even put the winds and brass in front of the strings, which, as one article tells it, horrified the people of Philadelphia.
指揮史托科夫斯基（Leopold Stokowski）被認為是第一位採用今天大多數樂團所用座位的人：第一和第二小提琴一起在指揮的左邊，中提琴和大提琴在右邊。他不斷改變各組的座位。有一次他甚至把木管和銅管放在弦樂前面；有一篇文章說，這讓費城的人大吃一驚。

In Taiwan · 在台灣

Three orchestras三個樂團

The National Symphony Orchestra has been in residence at the National Theater and Concert Hall in Taipei since 1987. The National Taiwan Symphony Orchestra, in Wufeng, Taichung, was founded in 1945 and is the oldest symphony orchestra in Taiwan. The Taipei Symphony Orchestra was founded in 1969. They are three different organizations.
國家交響樂團從 1987 年起，常駐台北的國家戲劇院與音樂廳（兩廳院）。位在台中霧峰的國立臺灣交響樂團，成立於 1945 年，是台灣歷史最悠久的交響樂團。臺北市立交響樂團成立於 1969 年。它們是三個不同的單位。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思Quiet instruments always sit in front.輕的樂器一定坐在前面。

✓ Fact · 事實Arranging by volume is one general principle, not a law. Conductors adjust the seating for the music and the hall, and Stokowski once put the winds and brass in front.依音量排只是一個通則，不是法律。指揮依曲子和場地調整座位；史托科夫斯基有一次把木管和銅管放在前面。

✗ Myth · 迷思All orchestras use the same seating plan.所有樂團的座位都一樣。

✓ Fact · 事實American and German layouts differ, the basses can be on the left or the right, and the horns may switch sides. The exact layout varies.美式與德式排法不同，低音提琴可以在左也可以在右，法國號可能換邊。確切的排法各有不同。

✗ Myth · 迷思The conductor makes the sound.指揮發出聲音。

✓ Fact · 事實Most conductors do not play an instrument while conducting. The conductor sets the tempo, gives cues, and shapes the phrasing, and the musicians make all the sound.大多數指揮在指揮時不演奏樂器。指揮定速度、給提示、塑造樂句，所有的聲音都由樂手發出。

✗ Myth · 迷思The concertmaster is the conductor.樂團首席就是指揮。

✓ Fact · 事實The concertmaster leads the first violins and is the second-in-command, behind only the conductor. Early orchestras, with no conductor, were led by the concertmaster.樂團首席帶領第一小提琴，是僅次於指揮的第二號人物。沒有指揮的早期樂團，由首席帶領。

Protect Your Ears · 保護耳朵

Before you listen 聽之前

  • Start with the volume low and turn it up a little at a time. When several sections play together, the total is louder than one, so be careful.先把音量調小，再一點一點調大。好幾組一起奏時，總音量比一組大，要特別小心。
  • With headphones, keep the volume at no more than 60% of maximum, and use personal audio devices for less than one hour a day, as the World Health Organization suggests.戴耳機時，音量不要超過最大音量的 60%，個人音響裝置每天使用少於一小時，這是世界衛生組織的建議。
  • In a real concert hall, the brass and percussion are very loud for the players sitting in front of them. If you ever play in a band or orchestra, ask about ear protection.在真正的音樂廳裡，坐在銅管和打擊前面的演奏者會覺得它們非常響。如果你有一天在管樂團或樂團裡演奏，請問問有沒有護耳的辦法。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

The Classroom Orchestra 教室裡的樂團

You need · 準備材料

  • Chairs for about twenty students二十位左右學生的椅子
  • Four colored cards: strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion四張彩色卡片：弦樂、木管、銅管、打擊
  • A ruler for a conductor’s baton一把尺當指揮棒

Steps · 步驟

  1. Set the chairs in a semicircle, with a space for the conductor in the middle. Give each student a section card.把椅子排成半圓形，中間留位置給指揮。每位同學發一張組別卡。
  2. Seat the sections: strings at the front, then woodwinds, then brass, then percussion at the back.讓各組坐好：弦樂在最前面，然後木管、銅管，打擊在最後。
  3. The conductor shows a steady beat, and each section takes a turn to clap its own pattern, softly. Then all four together.指揮打出穩定的拍子，每一組輪流輕輕拍自己的節奏型。然後四組一起。
  4. Now change the seating: put the brass in front. Is it easier or harder to hear the strings? Talk about why the traditional plan has stayed.再換座位：把銅管放在前面。聽弦樂變容易還是變難？討論傳統的座位為什麼留下來。

Clap softly with hands or on knees, so it does not get too loud. Let each student try being the conductor.
用手或在膝蓋上輕輕拍，不要太大聲。讓每位同學都試試當指揮。

  • Paper, pencils, and colored pens紙、鉛筆和彩色筆

Steps · 步驟

  1. Look at the stage and find the conductor. Mark the podium in the middle of your paper.看舞台，找到指揮。在紙的中間畫出指揮台。
  2. Find the four sections. Mark each one in its own color.找到四個組。用不同顏色標出每一組。
  3. Where are the first violins? The cellos and basses? Compare with the American and German layouts on this page.第一小提琴在哪裡？大提琴和低音提琴呢？和本頁的美式與德式排法比較。
  4. Compare your drawing with a classmate’s. Did the two orchestras sit in the same way?和同學的圖比一比。這兩個樂團的坐法一樣嗎？

Different concerts have different layouts, so there is no single right answer. Notice the differences instead of marking things wrong.
不同的音樂會有不同的排法，所以沒有唯一的答案。注意差別，不要把不一樣的當成錯。
