Reading · 英文閱讀

Unit 19: How Did Beethoven Compose After He Lost His Hearing?

Musical notation is any system used to visually represent music, and the process of interpreting it is often called reading music. Sheet music is a written or printed form of musical notation that uses symbols to indicate pitches, rhythms, or chords. The word score is a common alternative term. On a staff, a set of horizontal lines, usually five, each line and space represents a different pitch. Lesson 5 shows how to read it.

樂譜（musical notation）是用視覺表現音樂的任何系統，解讀它的過程常被稱為讀譜。譜（sheet music）是手寫或印刷的記譜形式，用符號標出音高、節奏或和弦。「score」是常用的另一個詞。在五線譜上，一組水平的線（通常五條），每一條線和每一個間，代表不同的音高。第五課教你怎麼讀。

Sight-reading is the practice of reading and performing a piece in music notation. Highly skilled musicians can sight-read silently: they look at the printed music and hear it in their heads without playing or singing. Audiation is a term coined by Edwin Gordon in 1975 for comprehension and internal realization of music, the sensation of hearing sound when it is not physically present. Skilled composers and conductors have this ability, with Beethoven being a noted historical example.

視奏（sight-reading）是讀著譜演奏一首曲子的做法。非常熟練的音樂家可以無聲地視奏：看著印好的譜，在腦子裡聽到，不用演奏也不用唱。「內在聽覺」（audiation）是戈登（Edwin Gordon）在 1975 年提出的詞，指對音樂的理解與內在實現，也就是聲音不在眼前時，卻有聽到聲音的感覺。熟練的作曲家和指揮有這種能力，貝多芬是歷史上著名的例子。

Ludwig van Beethoven was a German composer and pianist. He was baptized on December 17, 1770, in Bonn, and he died on March 26, 1827. There is no authentic record of the date of his birth. He was a musical prodigy, and at age 21 he moved to Vienna, which became his base. Our Lesson 13 shows where he fits between the Classical and the Romantic.

路德維希・范・貝多芬是德國的作曲家與鋼琴家。他在 1770 年 12 月 17 日於波昂受洗，1827 年 3 月 26 日去世。他出生的日期沒有可靠的紀錄。他是音樂神童，21 歲搬到維也納，那裡成了他的根據地。第十三課介紹過他在古典與浪漫之間的位置。

Around 1798, Beethoven began to lose his hearing, as he told a friend in 1815. The decline was gradual, and it came with a severe form of tinnitus, a ringing in the ear. Historians are not completely sure why it happened. One of his students reported that he could still hear speech and music normally until 1812. Contrary to common belief, Beethoven never became totally deaf. In his final years he could still distinguish low tones and sudden loud sounds.

大約 1798 年，貝多芬開始失去聽力，這是他 1815 年告訴一位朋友的。聽力是慢慢下降的，還伴隨嚴重的耳鳴（耳朵裡的鳴叫聲）。歷史學者並不完全確定原因。他的一位學生說，一直到 1812 年，他還能正常聽見談話與音樂。和一般人的想法相反，貝多芬從來沒有完全失聰。在最後的幾年，他仍能分辨低音和突然的巨響。

Beethoven’s hearing loss did not prevent him from composing music. From 1814 he used ear trumpets for conversation. Later his hearing seriously deteriorated, and he and the people talking to him wrote in notebooks, called conversation books. His late period, 1812 to 1827, included some of his most innovative works. His Ninth Symphony was first performed on May 7, 1824, in Vienna. He stood by the conductor, beating time, though the conductor had told the performers to follow him instead. A singer turned him around to see the applause he could not hear.

貝多芬的聽力損失並沒有妨礙他作曲。1814 年起，他用助聽喇叭來交談。後來聽力嚴重惡化，他和說話的人就在筆記本上寫字，這些筆記本叫「談話簿」。他的晚期（1812 到 1827 年）有一些他最創新的作品。第九號交響曲 1824 年 5 月 7 日在維也納首演。他站在指揮旁邊打拍子，但指揮已告訴演奏者要跟著指揮，不要跟他。一位歌手把他轉過來，讓他看到他聽不到的掌聲。

Hearing loss is either a partial inability to hear, or a total inability called deafness. Evelyn Glennie, a Scottish percussionist, has been profoundly deaf since childhood, and she performs as a soloist with many orchestras. She plays barefoot to feel the music, and she says deafness is largely misunderstood by the public. For us, the lesson is about reading music and imagining it. It is also a reminder to protect our ears: keep headphones at no more than 60% of maximum, and limit personal audio players to an hour a day.

聽力損失，可以是部分聽不到，也可以是完全聽不到，叫做失聰。愛芙琳・葛蘭妮（Evelyn Glennie）是蘇格蘭的打擊樂家，從小就深度失聰，她以獨奏家身分和許多樂團演出。她赤腳演奏，為了「感覺音樂」，她也說大眾對失聰有很大的誤解。對我們來說，這一課是關於讀譜與想像音樂，也提醒我們保護耳朵：耳機音量不超過最大音量的 60%，個人音響裝置每天用不超過一小時。

Questions · 閱讀理解

What is a score?

A score is the written form of music, with symbols that show pitches and rhythms. It is another word for sheet music.

Was Beethoven totally deaf?

No. Wikipedia says that, contrary to common belief, he never became totally deaf. In his final years he could still hear low tones and sudden loud sounds.

What does it mean to “hear music in your head”?

It means looking at written music and imagining how it sounds without playing or singing. Skilled musicians can do this, which is called sight-reading silently.

A Fact · 事實

Not totally deaf不是完全失聰

Many people believe Beethoven was totally deaf. Wikipedia says, contrary to common belief, that he never became totally deaf, and that in his final years he could still distinguish low tones and sudden loud sounds. One of his students reported that he could hear speech and music normally until 1812. The sources do not agree on every word, so we say only that he could hear very little late in life.
許多人以為貝多芬完全失聰。維基百科說，和一般人的想法相反，他從來沒有完全失聰，最後幾年他仍能分辨低音與突然的巨響。他的一位學生說，一直到 1812 年他還能正常聽見談話與音樂。各來源並非每個字都一致，所以我們只說他晚年能聽到的很少。

Tools · 工具

Ear trumpets and conversation books助聽喇叭與談話簿

From 1814 Beethoven used ear trumpets designed by Johann Nepomuk Maelzel for conversation, which strengthen the sound reaching the eardrum. Later he and the people he talked to wrote in notebooks. These conversation books are a rich written resource for his life from that period on, and they contain discussions about music, business, and personal life. A piano maker also tried a special resonator for him, but it failed in this task.
從 1814 年起，貝多芬用梅爾策爾（Maelzel）設計的助聽喇叭來交談，它能增強傳到耳膜的聲音。後來他和說話的人在筆記本上寫字。這些談話簿是那段時期之後他生活的豐富文字資料，內容有音樂、事務與私人生活的討論。一位鋼琴製造者也為他試過特別的共鳴裝置，但沒有成功。

Today · 今天

Evelyn Glennie愛芙琳・葛蘭妮

Evelyn Glennie is a Scottish percussionist who has been profoundly deaf since childhood, having started to lose her hearing at the age of eight. This does not inhibit her ability to perform. She plays barefoot to feel the music, and her teacher taught her to hear with parts of her body other than her ears. She says deafness is largely misunderstood by the public.
愛芙琳・葛蘭妮是蘇格蘭的打擊樂家，從小就深度失聰，八歲開始失去聽力。這並不妨礙她演出。她赤腳演奏來感覺音樂，她的老師教她用耳朵以外的身體部位去聽。她說大眾對失聰有很大的誤解。

Words · 用詞

Words that respect people尊重人的用詞

Taiwan’s dictionary notes that the older word for a person who cannot hear is now replaced by 聽覺障礙者, “a person with a hearing impairment”. The same dictionary defines 失聰 as losing one’s hearing. In this lesson we say hearing loss, or lost much of his hearing, and we do not use labels. We also do not guess at causes the sources are not sure about.
台灣的辭典說，稱呼聽不到的人的舊詞，現在改稱「聽覺障礙者」。同一本辭典把「失聰」解釋為失去聽力。這一課我們說「聽力損失」或「失去很多聽力」，不用標籤。來源不確定的原因，我們也不猜。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思Beethoven was totally deaf.貝多芬完全失聰。

✓ Fact · 事實Wikipedia says that, contrary to common belief, he never became totally deaf. In his final years he could still hear low tones and sudden loud sounds.維基百科說，和一般人的想法相反，他從來沒有完全失聰。最後幾年他仍能聽到低音和突然的巨響。

✗ Myth · 迷思He composed everything after he lost his hearing.他所有的作品都是失聰後寫的。

✓ Fact · 事實His hearing began to decline around 1798, and his First Symphony premiered in 1800. One student said he could hear normally until 1812.他的聽力約在 1798 年開始下降，第一號交響曲 1800 年首演。有學生說他到 1812 年還能正常聽。

✗ Myth · 迷思He conducted the premiere of the Ninth Symphony.他指揮了第九號交響曲的首演。

✓ Fact · 事實He stood by the conductor, Michael Umlauf, beating time, although Umlauf had warned the singers and orchestra to ignore him.他站在指揮烏姆勞夫旁邊打拍子，但烏姆勞夫已警告歌手與樂團不要理他。

✗ Myth · 迷思Only Beethoven could hear music in his head.只有貝多芬能在腦子裡聽音樂。

✓ Fact · 事實Highly skilled musicians can sight-read silently, hearing the music in their heads. Beethoven is a noted historical example, not the only one.非常熟練的音樂家可以無聲地視奏，在腦子裡聽音樂。貝多芬是歷史上著名的例子，不是唯一的。

Protect Your Ears · 保護耳朵

Before you listen 聽之前

  • Start with the volume low and turn it up a little at a time.先把音量調小，再一點一點調大。
  • With headphones, keep the volume at no more than 60% of maximum, and use personal audio devices for less than one hour a day, as the World Health Organization suggests.戴耳機時，音量不要超過最大音量的 60%，個人音響裝置每天使用少於一小時，這是世界衛生組織的建議。
  • Wikipedia says about half of hearing loss around the world is preventable through public health measures. Taking care of your ears now is a good start.維基百科說，全球約一半的聽力損失可以靠公共衛生措施預防。現在就照顧耳朵，是個好的開始。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

Read First, Then Hear 先讀，再聽

You need · 準備材料

  • Four notes written on a staff, on the board or on paper寫在黑板或紙上五線譜的四個音
  • A keyboard or a recorder, if you have one鍵盤或直笛，如果有的話

Steps · 步驟

  1. Show four notes on a staff. Do not play them.展示五線譜上的四個音。先不要彈。
  2. Ask: does the tune climb, fall, make a hill, a valley, or a zigzag? Vote with your hands.問：旋律是往上、往下、山峰、山谷，還是鋸齒？用手勢投票。
  3. Play the notes and check. Who read it correctly?彈出來對答案。誰讀對了？
  4. Write a new four-note tune, and let a partner read it before they hear it.寫一段新的四個音的旋律，讓同伴在聽到之前先讀。

Stay on the shape only. Names of notes can come later.
先只看形狀。音名之後再學。

Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

A Silent Bar 一個無聲的小節

You need · 準備材料

  • A short tune your class already knows well全班已經很熟的短旋律
  • A steady beat counted aloud大聲數出來的穩定拍子

Steps · 步驟

  1. Sing the tune together with the beat.全班跟著拍子一起唱。
  2. Choose one phrase to be silent. Everyone keeps the beat and imagines singing it.選一個樂句變成無聲。大家保持拍子，並在心裡想像唱它。
  3. Join in again at the next phrase, together. Did you come in at the same time?到下一個樂句，一起再加入。你們同時進來了嗎？
  4. Talk about it: how did you know when to come in?討論：你怎麼知道什麼時候要進來？

Count the beats silently in your head. That is the same skill as hearing the notes in your head.
在腦子裡默數拍子。這和在腦子裡聽音符是同一種能力。
