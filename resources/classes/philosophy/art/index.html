A29 · Beauty and Art 美與藝術

What Is Art? 什麼是藝術？

In 1917 a urinal was sent to an art exhibition in New York. Four definitions of art, from Plato’s mirror to the artworld, each tested against ten objects that you sort yourself, with Xie He, Su Shi, and Dewey for company.
1917 年，有人把一個小便斗送到紐約的一場藝術展覽。四種藝術的定義，從柏拉圖的鏡子到「藝術界」；每一種都用十件由你自己分類的東西來檢驗。同行的還有謝赫、蘇軾與杜威。

A29 Big Questions · 大哉問Beauty and Art · 美與藝術Advanced · 高級1,133 words · about 25 min
The big idea · 大觀念

People have made paintings, songs, and poems in every society we know of, and for most of history nobody needed to say what they had in common. The question became urgent when artists began to make things that looked like nothing art had been before. This lesson opens the unit on beauty and art by asking what the word covers.

在我們所知的每一個社會裡，人們都創作繪畫、歌曲與詩；而在歷史上大部分的時間裡，沒有人需要說出它們的共同點是什麼。當藝術家開始做出一些「跟從前的藝術完全不像」的東西時，這個問題才變得迫切。這一課是「美與藝術」單元的開頭；它問的是：這個詞到底涵蓋了什麼？

The essay · 課文

What Is Art? 什麼是藝術？

01A urinal in New York 紐約的一個小便斗

In April 1917 the Society of Independent Artists held its first exhibition in New York. Its rule was simple: any artist who paid the fee could show a work, and there would be no jury. One entry was a porcelain urinal, laid on its back, signed “R. Mutt 1917,” and titled Fountain. The directors argued, and the object was kept out of sight behind a partition for the length of the show. Marcel Duchamp, a French artist who sat on the board, resigned in protest. A small magazine edited by his friends then printed a defense: “Whether Mr Mutt with his own hands made the fountain or not has no importance. He CHOSE it.” He took an ordinary article, the writer said, and “created a new thought for that object.”

1917 年 4 月，獨立藝術家協會在紐約舉辦了它的第一次展覽。它的規則很簡單：任何付了費用的藝術家都可以展出一件作品，而且不設評審。有一件參展品是一個瓷製的小便斗：平放著，署名「R. Mutt 1917」，題為〈噴泉〉。理事們爭論了起來；整個展期，這件東西都被放在一道隔板後面，不讓人看見。法國藝術家馬塞爾・杜象是理事之一，他辭職以示抗議。接著，一份由他的朋友們編輯的小雜誌刊出了一篇辯護：「至於馬特先生是不是親手做了這個噴泉，並不重要。他『選擇』了它。」作者說，他拿了一件普通的物品，並且「為那件物品創造了一個新的想法」。

The original was lost soon afterward. The examples now in museums are replicas made decades later. In 2004 five hundred British art professionals voted Fountain the most influential work of the twentieth century. Whatever one thinks of it, it did what a good philosophical example does. It was a case that every existing definition of art got wrong, or else got right for an embarrassing reason.

原件不久之後就遺失了。現在博物館裡的那些，是幾十年後製作的複製品。2004 年，五百位英國的藝術界專業人士把〈噴泉〉票選為二十世紀最有影響力的作品。不管你怎麼看它，它做到了一個好的哲學例子該做的事。它是這樣一個案例：每一個既有的藝術定義，都把它判錯了；不然就是判對了，而理由很難堪。

02A mirror held up to the world 對著世界舉起的一面鏡子

The oldest definition says that art is imitation. Plato states it in order to attack it. There is the idea of a bed, he says, and there is the bed that a carpenter makes by looking to the idea, and then there is the bed in a painting, which copies only how the carpenter’s bed looks from one side. The imitator, he concludes, is “thrice removed from the king and from the truth.” Anyone can do what he does by “turning a mirror round and round.” Aristotle kept the definition and dropped the contempt: people learn by imitating and take pleasure in recognizing a likeness. For two thousand years most European theory began here. The trouble is that music imitates nothing in particular, and neither does a temple, a carpet, or a line of calligraphy.

最古老的定義說：藝術是模仿。柏拉圖把它說出來，是為了攻擊它。他說：有床的理型；有木匠看著那個理型而做出來的床；然後有畫裡的床，它只摹仿了木匠的床從某一側看起來的樣子。他的結論是：模仿者「與王、與真理隔了三層」。他做的事情，任何人只要「拿一面鏡子轉來轉去」就做得到。亞里斯多德保留了這個定義，拿掉了輕蔑：人是藉著模仿來學習的，並且在認出相似之處時感到愉快。兩千年間，歐洲大部分的理論都從這裡出發。麻煩在於：音樂並不特別模仿什麼；一座神廟、一張地毯、一行書法也是。

03A feeling handed on 被傳遞出去的一份感受

A second definition looks to the maker. In 1897 Leo Tolstoy, by then more preacher than novelist, wrote that “art is a human activity, consisting in this, that one man consciously, by means of certain external signs, hands on to others feelings he has lived through, and that other people are infected by these feelings, and also experience them.” A boy who meets a wolf and then tells the story so that his listeners feel his fear has made art. By this test Tolstoy rejected most of Shakespeare and Beethoven as insincere or unintelligible to plain people, and praised peasant songs.

第二個定義看的是創作者。1897 年，那時候與其說是小說家、不如說是傳道者的列夫・托爾斯泰寫道：「藝術是人的一種活動，它在於：一個人有意識地藉著某些外在的符號，把自己體驗過的感受傳遞給別人；而別人被這些感受所感染，也體驗到它們。」一個男孩遇到了一隻狼，然後把這件事講出來，讓聽的人感受到他的恐懼；他就創作了藝術。用這個標準，托爾斯泰把莎士比亞與貝多芬的大部分作品都否定了，說它們不真誠，或者一般人聽不懂；而他稱讚農民的歌。

China arrived at this view first and never held the other. The Book of Documents says that “poetry speaks what the heart is set on,” and the preface to the Book of Songs explains: “In the heart it is intent. Sent forth in words it is poetry.” Chinese critics took it for granted that a poem or a piece of calligraphy shows the person who made it. The weakness of the expression theory lies elsewhere. A cry of pain expresses a feeling and is not a poem, and much great art was made to order by people whose feelings we know nothing about.

中國先得出了這個看法，而且從來沒有主張過另一個。《尚書》說：「詩言志。」〈毛詩序〉解釋：「在心為志，發言為詩。」中國的批評家理所當然地認為，一首詩或一件書法，顯露了創作它的那個人。表現論的弱點在別的地方。一聲痛苦的叫喊表現了一份感受，而它不是一首詩；而許多偉大的藝術，是由我們對其感受一無所知的人，受委託而製作的。

04Lines and colors 線條與顏色

A third definition looks at the work alone. In 1914 the English critic Clive Bell asked what a Persian bowl, a Chinese carpet, a Giotto fresco, and a Cézanne have in common, and answered: “lines and colours combined in a particular way, certain forms and relations of forms, stir our aesthetic emotions.” He called this significant form. What a picture shows is irrelevant, and Bell dismissed a famous painting of a crowded railway station, full of lifelike detail, as interesting and not art at all.

第三個定義只看作品本身。1914 年，英國批評家克萊夫・貝爾問：一只波斯碗、一張中國地毯、一幅喬托的濕壁畫、一幅塞尚，有什麼共同之處？他回答：「以某種特定方式組合起來的線條與顏色、某些形式與形式之間的關係，激起了我們的審美情感。」他稱之為「有意味的形式」。一幅畫畫了什麼，是無關緊要的；有一幅畫著擁擠的火車站、充滿逼真細節的名畫，貝爾把它打發掉了：很有趣，但根本不是藝術。

Chinese painters had long agreed that likeness is not the point. Around 500 CE Xie He listed six principles of painting and put first “spirit resonance, which means vitality.” Fidelity to the object came third. Su Shi wrote in the eleventh century: “To judge a painting by its likeness is to see as a child sees.” What these writers valued, though, was the life in the brushwork and the character of the painter that it revealed, which is nearer to expression than to Bell’s pure pattern. Bell’s definition has a further problem. When asked what makes a form significant, he could only say that it is the kind that moves us aesthetically, and the definition goes round in a circle.

中國的畫家早就同意：形似不是重點。大約公元 500 年，謝赫列出了繪畫的六法，把「氣韻生動」放在第一。忠於物象的「應物象形」排在第三。蘇軾在十一世紀寫道：「論畫以形似，見與兒童鄰。」不過，這些作者所看重的，是筆墨裡的生命，以及它所顯露的畫家的品格；這更接近表現，而不是貝爾那種純粹的圖案。貝爾的定義還有一個問題。有人問：是什麼使一個形式「有意味」？他只能說：就是那種在審美上打動我們的形式；於是這個定義繞了一個圈。

05Whatever the artworld says 藝術界說了算

None of the three could explain Fountain. It imitates nothing, expresses no feeling that anyone can name, and has the same form as every other urinal from the same factory. In 1964 the philosopher Arthur Danto saw Andy Warhol’s Brillo Boxes, plywood copies of supermarket cartons, and concluded that what makes something art cannot be anything the eye can see. It is “an atmosphere of artistic theory, a knowledge of the history of art: an artworld.” George Dickie turned this into a definition: a work of art is an artifact that someone acting on behalf of the artworld has offered as a candidate for appreciation. On this account Fountain is art for the plain reason that it was put forward and taken up as art.

這三個定義，沒有一個解釋得了〈噴泉〉。它不模仿任何東西；它不表現任何一種說得出名字的感受；它的形式，跟同一家工廠出產的每一個小便斗一模一樣。1964 年，哲學家亞瑟・丹托看到了安迪・沃荷的〈布瑞洛箱〉——超級市場紙箱的夾板複製品——並得出結論：使某樣東西成為藝術的，不可能是眼睛看得見的任何東西。它是「一種藝術理論的氛圍，一種對藝術史的知識：一個藝術界」。喬治・迪基把這變成了一個定義：藝術作品是一件人造物，由某個代表藝術界行事的人提出來，作為供人欣賞的候選者。照這個說法，〈噴泉〉是藝術，理由很平常：它被當作藝術提出來，也被當作藝術接受了。

The institutional theory fits every case, and critics say that this is its fault. It tells us that something is art when the right people call it so, and is silent about why they should. Morris Weitz had argued in 1956 that the search was a mistake from the start. Art, like a game, has no single feature shared by all its instances. They are linked by overlapping resemblances, and each new movement extends the family. On this view the question “What is art?” has no final answer, and the changes in the answer from one age to the next are a record of what each age cared about.

制度論符合每一個案例；而批評者說，這正是它的毛病。它告訴我們：當對的人把某樣東西稱為藝術的時候，它就是藝術；至於他們為什麼該這樣稱呼，它不說。莫里斯・韋茲在 1956 年就主張過：這場尋找從一開始就是個錯誤。藝術就像遊戲一樣，並沒有一個所有實例都共有的特徵。它們是由彼此交疊的相似之處連在一起的；而每一個新的流派，都把這個家族擴大了一點。照這個看法，「什麼是藝術？」這個問題沒有最終的答案；而答案在一個又一個時代之間的變化，記錄了每個時代在乎的是什麼。

06The object and the experience 物品與經驗

John Dewey thought all four definitions looked in the wrong place. They ask what kind of object a work of art is. In Art as Experience (1934) he argued that art is something that happens between a person and the world, and that the museum piece is only its most concentrated form. A mechanic absorbed in a job well done and a cook who cares about the meal have experiences of the same kind as the painter’s. A society that locks art in special buildings, he thought, has usually let it drain out of ordinary work.

約翰・杜威認為，這四個定義都看錯了地方。它們問的是：藝術作品是哪一種「物品」？在《藝術即經驗》（1934）裡，他主張藝術是發生在一個人與世界之間的事，而博物館裡的作品只是它最濃縮的形式。一位全神貫注把工作做好的技工、一位在乎那頓飯的廚師，他們的經驗與畫家的經驗是同一類的。他認為，一個把藝術鎖在特別的建築物裡的社會，通常已經讓藝術從日常的工作裡流失了。

The question has a new form today. A computer program can now produce an image that imitates well, has striking form, and wins a prize at an art fair, while no one involved has had any feeling to express. Whether it is art depends on which of these theories is right, and the answer will affect who is paid and who is credited.

這個問題今天有了新的形式。現在，一個電腦程式就能產生一幅圖像：模仿得很好，形式醒目，還在藝術展覽上得了獎；而牽涉其中的人，沒有一個有任何感受要表現。它是不是藝術，取決於這些理論當中哪一個是對的；而答案會影響誰拿到報酬、誰得到名分。

Thought experiment · 思想實驗

Try it: you are the curator 親手做一次：你來策展

Ten things. For each, answer by your own lights: is it art? There are no right answers here. When you finish, you will see which of the four definitions agrees with you most, and which of your own answers it cannot explain.

十樣東西。每一樣，請照你自己的看法回答：它是藝術嗎？這裡沒有正確答案。做完之後，你會看到四個定義當中哪一個跟你最一致，以及你自己的哪幾個答案是它解釋不了的。

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

“Duchamp made Fountain.”「〈噴泉〉是杜象做的。」

Probably, and there is a letter that says otherwise · 大概是；而有一封信說的不一樣

Duchamp said in later life that he bought the urinal, signed it, and sent it in, and nearly every museum label repeats this. But on April 11, 1917, within days of the object’s being refused, he wrote to his sister: “One of my female friends under a masculine pseudonym, Richard Mutt, sent in a porcelain urinal as a sculpture.” He never said who the friend was. Some researchers believe that she was the poet and artist Elsa von Freytag-Loringhoven and that the work was hers. Others think that the “friend” was a cover story or an early form of Duchamp’s own female alter ego. The original is lost and the question cannot be settled. The dispute is itself a lesson in the institutional theory: for a century the artworld attributed the work to the person who was in a position to speak for it.

杜象晚年說，是他買了那個小便斗、署了名、送去參展；幾乎每一張博物館的說明牌都重複這個說法。但是在 1917 年 4 月 11 日，也就是那件東西被拒絕之後沒幾天，他寫信給他的妹妹：「我的一位女性朋友，用一個男性的化名理查・馬特，送了一個瓷製的小便斗去當雕塑。」他從來沒有說那位朋友是誰。有些研究者認為，她就是詩人兼藝術家艾爾莎・馮・弗萊塔格—洛琳霍芬，而這件作品是她的。另一些人認為，那位「朋友」是一個掩護的說法，或者是杜象自己那個女性分身的早期形式。原件已經遺失，這個問題無法定案。這場爭議本身，就是制度論的一課：一百年來，藝術界把這件作品歸給了那個有地位為它發言的人。

Both sides · 正反兩面

Objections and replies 反駁與回應

Objection 1 · 反駁

This is a quarrel about a word. Call Fountain art or do not. Nothing changes.

這只是關於一個詞的爭吵。把〈噴泉〉叫做藝術也好，不叫也好。什麼都不會改變。

Reply · 回應

A good deal changes. The word decides what museums buy with public money, what schools teach, what copyright protects, and whose work is called craft and paid accordingly. For centuries embroidery and pottery were excluded and oil painting included, and the people on each side of that line were not a random selection.

會改變的可多了。這個詞決定了博物館用公家的錢買什麼、學校教什麼、著作權保護什麼，以及誰的作品被叫做「工藝」、並依此計酬。好幾個世紀裡，刺繡與陶器被排除在外，油畫被包括在內；而站在那條線兩邊的人，並不是隨機分配的。

Objection 2 · 反駁

If anything can be art, then the word means nothing, and skill, which is the one thing people can actually judge, has been thrown away.

如果任何東西都可以是藝術，那這個詞就沒有意義了；而技巧——人們唯一真正能評判的東西——就被丟掉了。

Reply · 回應

The institutional theory separates two questions that the objection runs together: whether something is art, and whether it is any good. A thing can be art and bad art. Skill remains a standard of quality. The reply has limits. If the classification carries no judgment at all, it is hard to see why anyone fought so hard to have Fountain admitted.

制度論把這個反駁混在一起的兩個問題分開了：某樣東西是不是藝術？它好不好？一樣東西可以是藝術，而且是壞的藝術。技巧仍然是品質的一個標準。這個回應有它的限度。如果這個分類完全不帶任何評價，那就很難看出，為什麼有人那麼拚命地要讓〈噴泉〉被接納。

Objection 3 · 反駁

“Art” as a single category is a modern European idea. Other cultures made beautiful and meaningful things without it.

把「藝術」當作一個單一的類別，是近代歐洲的觀念。其他的文化不用這個類別，也照樣製作出了美而有意義的東西。

Reply · 回應

This is true and important. The grouping of painting, sculpture, music, poetry, and architecture as the fine arts dates from eighteenth-century Europe. Classical China ranked calligraphy, poetry, and painting together as the pursuits of the educated and left sculpture and building to artisans. The modern Chinese word for art, 藝術, took its present sense from Japanese in the late nineteenth century. Recognizing this supports Weitz: the category has a history, and its edges have always been drawn by particular people for particular reasons.

這是真的，而且很重要。把繪畫、雕塑、音樂、詩與建築合稱為「美術」，始於十八世紀的歐洲。古典中國把書、詩、畫並列為讀書人的事業，而把雕塑與營造留給工匠。現代中文的「藝術」一詞，是在十九世紀末從日文取得現在這個意思的。認清這一點，支持了韋茲：這個類別有它的歷史；而它的邊界，從來都是由特定的人、為了特定的理由劃出來的。

Terms of art · 哲學術語

The vocabulary of the subject 這門學問的用語

aesthetics美學

Gk. aisthēsis ‘perception’

The branch of philosophy concerned with art, beauty, and taste.

哲學的一個分支；關注藝術、美與品味。

mimesis模仿

Gk. mimēsis

Imitation or representation, the oldest account of what art does.

模仿或再現；對「藝術在做什麼」最古老的說明。

readymade現成物

Duchamp’s term for an ordinary manufactured object chosen and presented as a work of art.

杜象的用語：一件普通的工業製品，被選出來、當作藝術作品來呈現。

significant form有意味的形式

Bell’s term for the arrangement of lines and colors that produces aesthetic emotion.

貝爾的用語：能產生審美情感的線條與顏色的安排。

artworld藝術界

The network of artists, critics, museums, theories, and history within which things count as art.

由藝術家、批評家、博物館、理論與歷史構成的網絡；東西是在它之內才算作藝術的。

family resemblance家族相似

G. Familienähnlichkeit

Wittgenstein’s term for a set of overlapping likenesses with no single feature common to all.

維根斯坦的用語：一組彼此交疊的相似之處，其中沒有任何一個特徵是全體共有的。

spirit resonance氣韻

Ch. 氣韻 qìyùn

The life or vitality in a painting, the first of Xie He’s six principles.

一幅畫裡的生命或生氣；謝赫六法的第一條。

likeness of form形似

Ch. 形似 xíngsì

Resemblance to the outward shape of the thing depicted.

與所畫之物外在形狀的相似。

Write · 你怎麼想？

  1. Choose something you have made, a meal, a lesson, a garden, a photograph, that you would call well made. Was it art? Answer with one of the four definitions, and then say what Dewey would add. About 150 words.

    選一樣你自己做過、而且會說「做得好」的東西：一頓飯、一堂課、一座花園、一張照片。它是藝術嗎？用四個定義當中的一個來回答；然後說說杜威會補充什麼。約 150 個字。

  2. A computer program produces a picture that moves everyone who sees it. No person intended anything by it. Is it art? Defend your answer against the definition that disagrees with you most.

    一個電腦程式產生了一幅圖，看到的人都被打動了。沒有任何人藉著它想要表達什麼。它是藝術嗎？為你的答案辯護，回應跟你最不一致的那個定義。

  3. Su Shi says that to judge a painting by its likeness is to see as a child sees. Explain what he thinks an adult should look for, and say whether the same holds for photographs.

    蘇軾說：「論畫以形似，見與兒童鄰。」解釋他認為成年人應該看的是什麼；並且說說看，同樣的道理對照片成不成立。

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

A view from the Dharma 佛法怎麼看

☸

The early texts are wary of the arts as entertainment. Those who keep the eight precepts on observance days give up dancing, singing, music, and shows, and so do monks and nuns at all times. One discourse explains why. Tālapuṭa, the head of a company of performers, asks the Buddha whether it is true, as his teachers said, that a dancer who makes people laugh is reborn among the laughing gods. Twice the Buddha answers, “Enough, chief, let it be. Don’t ask me that.” Asked a third time, he says that people are already bound by greed, hate, and delusion, and that a performer who presents them with “even more arousing things” makes them more heedless. Tālapuṭa weeps, and says that he is not weeping at the answer. He weeps because he has been deceived for so long. The test applied is Tolstoy’s, with a different standard: art is judged by the states of mind it passes on.

早期的經典對於「作為娛樂的藝術」是戒慎的。在齋日持守八關齋戒的人，要放下歌舞、音樂與觀看表演；比丘與比丘尼則是任何時候都如此。有一部經解釋了為什麼。一個表演團體的首領塔拉普塔問佛陀：他的老師們說，逗人發笑的舞者，死後會生到「歡笑天」，這是真的嗎？佛陀兩次回答：「夠了，首領，別管它。不要問我這個。」第三次被問的時候，他說：人們本來就被貪、瞋、癡所束縛；而一位表演者把「更加令人動情的東西」呈現給他們，使他們更加放逸。塔拉普塔哭了；他說，他不是為這個答案而哭。他哭，是因為自己被欺騙了這麼久。這裡用的檢驗，是托爾斯泰式的，只是標準不同：藝術是以它所傳遞的心的狀態來評判的。

That standard cuts both ways, and the tradition drew the other conclusion as well. The Buddha taught in verse and in images that people remembered for a lifetime. Tālapuṭa became a monk, and a long poem under his name is preserved in the canon. Later Buddhists filled Asia with sculpture, painting, chant, and calligraphy, and gave an account of what these are for: they are a finger pointing at the moon, to be valued for what they direct the mind toward and not held on to for themselves. In Chan and Zen, brush painting and calligraphy became practices in their own right, in which the quality of the line was taken to show the state of the mind that made it, which is close to what Xie He meant by spirit resonance (Dharma and Western Philosophy D18).

那個標準是兩面刃；而這個傳統也得出了另一個結論。佛陀用偈頌、用人們一輩子都記得的意象來教導。塔拉普塔後來出家了；經藏裡保存著一首歸在他名下的長詩。後來的佛教徒，讓整個亞洲充滿了雕塑、繪畫、梵唄與書法，並且說明了這些東西是做什麼用的：它們是指著月亮的手指；它們的價值在於把心引向哪裡，而不該為了它們本身而被執取。在禪宗，水墨與書法本身就成了修行；人們認為，線條的品質顯露了寫下它的那顆心的狀態。這與謝赫所說的「氣韻」很接近（佛法與西方哲學 D18）。
