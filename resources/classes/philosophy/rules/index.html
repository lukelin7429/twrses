Why Do We Need Rules? 為什麼需要規則？

Imagine people with no government, no law, and no one to enforce a promise. Hobbes, Locke, Xunzi, and Mozi on what would go wrong, a game in which you can watch it go wrong, and the villages that manage without a sovereign.
想像一群人：沒有政府、沒有法律，也沒有人強制執行任何承諾。霍布斯、洛克、荀子與墨子，談會出什麼差錯；一個讓你親眼看著它出差錯的遊戲；以及那些沒有主權者也管得好的村莊。

A25 Big Questions · 大哉問Society and Justice · 社會與正義Advanced · 高級1,295 words · about 25 min
The big idea · 大觀念

Every rule limits someone, and most of us obey thousands of them without being asked. Political philosophy begins by asking what we would lose if they were gone, since the answer tells us what rules are for and when one has ceased to deserve obedience.

每一條規則都限制了某個人；而我們大多數人，沒有人要求就遵守著成千上萬條規則。政治哲學是從這個問題開始的：如果它們不見了，我們會失去什麼？因為答案告訴了我們，規則是做什麼用的，以及一條規則什麼時候已經不再值得服從。

The essay · 課文

Why Do We Need Rules? 為什麼需要規則？

01Take the rules away 把規則拿掉

Philosophers have a method for finding out what a thing is for. They imagine the world without it. Applied to government, the method gives the thought experiment known as the state of nature: human beings as they are, with their needs, fears, and abilities, and no authority over them. There are no police, courts, or laws, and no one is in a position to make anyone keep his word. The question is what life would be like, and the answers have differed so widely that they define the main positions in political thought. The purpose is to understand the present. No one who uses the method supposes that people could return to such a state.

哲學家有一個方法，用來找出一樣東西是做什麼用的。他們想像沒有它的世界。把這個方法用在政府上，就得出了那個稱為「自然狀態」的思想實驗：人就是現在這個樣子，有他們的需要、恐懼與能力，而沒有任何權威在他們之上。沒有警察、法庭或法律；也沒有任何人有辦法讓任何人守信。問題是：生活會是什麼樣子？而答案的分歧之大，界定了政治思想裡的幾個主要立場。這樣做的目的是理解當下。用這個方法的人，沒有一個認為人們可以回到那樣的狀態。

02Hobbes: war of all against all 霍布斯：一切人對一切人的戰爭

Thomas Hobbes published Leviathan in 1651, two years after the English had beheaded their king, and his account is the darkest. He starts from three facts. People are roughly equal, in that the weakest can kill the strongest by stealth or in company. They want many of the same scarce things. And none can be sure of the others’ intentions. It follows that each has reason to strike first, even if he wishes only to be safe, and each knows that the others have the same reason. “During the time men live without a common Power to keep them all in awe, they are in that condition which is called Warre; and such a warre, as is of every man, against every man.” War in his sense need not be fighting. It is the known readiness to fight, as bad weather is a tendency to rain. In that condition there is no farming, trade, building, or learning, since no one can count on keeping what he makes.

霍布斯在 1651 年出版了《利維坦》，那是英國人把自己的國王斬首之後兩年；而他的說明是最陰暗的。他從三個事實出發。人大致上是平等的，因為最弱的人靠著偷襲或結夥，也殺得了最強的人。他們想要許多同樣稀少的東西。而誰也無法確定別人的意圖。由此推出：每個人都有理由先下手，即使他只是想求安全；而每個人也都知道，別人有同樣的理由。「在人們沒有一個共同的權力使大家都有所畏懼的時候，他們就處在那種稱為戰爭的狀況裡；而這種戰爭，是每一個人對每一個人的戰爭。」他所說的戰爭，不一定是在打仗。它是「眾所周知的隨時準備打」，就像壞天氣是一種「會下雨的傾向」。在那種狀況下，沒有農耕、貿易、建築或學問，因為誰也無法指望保得住自己做出來的東西。

The way out is an agreement. Each person gives up his right to do whatever he judges necessary, on condition that the others do the same. But an agreement of that kind is worth nothing if anyone can break it when it suits him. “Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words, and of no strength to secure a man at all.” So the parties must also set up a power strong enough to punish any of them, a sovereign whom Hobbes calls the Leviathan and “that Mortall God.” His conclusion is that almost any government is better than none, and that to resist the sovereign is to risk the return of the war.

出路是一個約定。每個人都放棄「凡是自己判斷為必要的事都可以去做」的權利，條件是別人也這樣做。然而，如果任何人只要覺得划算就可以背約，那樣的約定就一文不值。「沒有劍的信約，不過是空話，根本沒有力量保障一個人。」所以，立約的各方還必須設立一個強大到足以懲罰他們當中任何一個人的權力：一位主權者；霍布斯稱之為利維坦，稱之為「那位會死的神」。他的結論是：幾乎任何政府都比沒有政府好；而反抗主權者，就是冒著讓那場戰爭回來的風險。

03Locke: no common judge 洛克：沒有共同的裁判

John Locke, writing forty years later, thought Hobbes had described the state of nature at its worst and called it the whole. Even without government, he argued, people are bound by a moral law that reason can discover. The state of nature is “a state of liberty, yet it is not a state of licence.” No one may destroy another’s life, health, liberty, or possessions, and most people know it. What is missing is a way of settling disputes. Each person must be judge in his own case, and people are partial to themselves and carried too far by anger when they punish. “Want of a common judge with authority, puts all men in a state of nature.” Government is set up to supply the judge. Its purpose is to protect rights that people already had, and Locke draws the consequence that Hobbes refused. A government that attacks the rights it was made to protect has broken the agreement, and the people may replace it.

四十年後寫作的洛克認為，霍布斯描述的是自然狀態最糟的樣子，卻把它說成了全部。他論證：即使沒有政府，人也受到一條理性可以發現的道德法則約束。自然狀態「是一種自由的狀態，卻不是一種放縱的狀態」。誰也不可以毀壞別人的生命、健康、自由或財產；而多數人知道這一點。缺少的，是一種解決爭端的辦法。每個人都得在自己的案子裡當裁判；而人對自己是偏私的，懲罰別人時又會被怒氣帶得太遠。「沒有一位具有權威的共同裁判，就使所有人處於自然狀態。」設立政府，是為了提供那位裁判。它的目的，是保護人們本來就有的權利；而洛克引出了霍布斯所拒絕的那個後果。一個攻擊「它被設立來保護的那些權利」的政府，已經破壞了約定；人民可以把它換掉。

04Xunzi and Mozi: desire and disagreement 荀子與墨子：欲望與分歧

Chinese philosophers reasoned about the origin of order in the same way nearly two thousand years earlier. Xunzi asks where ritual and rules come from, and answers in a sentence that could be Hobbes’s: “People are born with desires. When they desire and do not get, they cannot but seek. When they seek without measure or limit, they cannot but contend. Contention brings disorder, and disorder brings exhaustion. The former kings hated this disorder, and so they established ritual and rightness to make divisions.” His account of what rules achieve is more generous than Hobbes’s. Rules exist, he says, to nourish desires and to see that goods and wants keep pace with each other. He also explains what rules make possible. A human being is weaker than an ox and slower than a horse, and yet oxen and horses work for us. “Why? Because people can form groups and they cannot.” Groups are possible because of divisions, and divisions because of a shared sense of what is right.

中國的哲學家，早了將近兩千年就用同樣的方式推論秩序的起源。荀子問：禮與規則是從哪裡來的？他用一句可以出自霍布斯之口的話回答：「人生而有欲，欲而不得，則不能無求。求而無度量分界，則不能不爭；爭則亂，亂則窮。先王惡其亂也，故制禮義以分之。」他對「規則成就了什麼」的說明，比霍布斯的更寬厚。他說，規則的存在，是為了「養人之欲」，並且使物與欲「兩者相持而長」。他還說明了規則使什麼成為可能。人的力氣不如牛，跑得不如馬，而牛馬卻為我們所用。「何也？曰：人能群，彼不能群也。」人之所以能群，靠的是「分」；而「分」之所以行得通，靠的是共有的「義」。

Mozi locates the trouble somewhere else. In the beginning, before there were rulers, he says, “each person had his own view of what is right. One person, one view. Two people, two views. Ten people, ten views.” Each approved his own and condemned the others’, families broke apart, and the world was in chaos like that of birds and beasts. The cause of disorder, on his account, is that people sincerely disagree about what ought to be done and have no way of settling it. His remedy is a ruler chosen for his worth, whose judgment unifies the standards of all. Locke’s missing judge is here already, and so is the danger that Mozi did not see, that the one who unifies the standards may be wrong.

墨子把毛病放在另一個地方。他說，起初還沒有統治者的時候，「人異義。是以一人則一義，二人則二義，十人則十義」。每個人都肯定自己的、否定別人的；家人離散，天下大亂，「若禽獸然」。照他的說法，禍亂的原因是：人們真心地對「該怎麼做」意見分歧，卻沒有辦法解決它。他的對治，是一位因賢能而被選出的統治者，由他的判斷來統一所有人的標準（尚同）。洛克那位缺席的裁判，在這裡已經出現了；墨子沒有看到的那個危險也已經在了：那位統一標準的人，可能是錯的。

05The trap 陷阱

In the twentieth century the argument was given a precise form. Two suspects are questioned separately. If both stay silent, each serves one year. If one informs and the other does not, the informer goes free and the other serves ten. If both inform, each serves five. Whatever the other does, each does better by informing, and so both inform and serve five years when they could have served one. This is the prisoner’s dilemma. Its lesson is that people who each choose sensibly can arrive together at a result that none of them wants. Herdsmen who each add one more animal to a shared pasture destroy it. Nations that each catch a few more fish empty the sea. The ecologist Garrett Hardin called this the tragedy of the commons and wrote, “Freedom in a commons brings ruin to all.”

到了二十世紀，這個論證得到了一個精確的形式。兩名嫌犯被分開審訊。如果兩人都保持沉默，各關一年。如果一人告密、另一人不告，告密的獲釋，另一個關十年。如果兩人都告密，各關五年。不管對方怎麼做，每個人自己告密都比較划算；於是兩人都告了密，各關五年，而他們本來可以各關一年。這就是「囚徒困境」。它的教訓是：每個人各自作出合理的選擇，卻可能一起走到一個誰也不想要的結果。在共用的牧場上，每一位牧人各自多放一頭牲口，牧場就毀了。每一個國家各自多捕幾條魚，海就空了。生態學家哈丁把這稱為「公地悲劇」，並且寫道：「公地上的自由，給所有人帶來毀滅。」

This is why Hobbes insisted on the sword. A rule that everyone would benefit from will still be broken if each person gains by breaking it while the others comply. Rules need enforcement, and the game below lets you watch that happen.

這就是為什麼霍布斯堅持要有那把劍。一條人人都會從中受益的規則，如果每個人在別人遵守的時候自己違反能得到好處，它仍然會被打破。規則需要執行；下面的遊戲讓你親眼看著這件事發生。

06Rules without a Leviathan 沒有利維坦的規則

Hobbes concluded that only a single overwhelming power can end the trap, and here the evidence has gone against him. The political scientist Elinor Ostrom spent her career studying communities that share a resource: Swiss villages with common alpine meadows, Japanese mountain forests, Spanish irrigation canals, lobster fisheries in Maine. Many of them have managed without ruin for centuries, and they have done it with rules that the users made themselves, watched over by one another, with small penalties that grow for repeated offenses. She received the Nobel Prize in economics in 2009. Experiments tell the same story. In laboratory games, cooperation among strangers decays when free riding goes unpunished and recovers as soon as players are allowed to fine one another, which many will do even at a cost to themselves.

霍布斯的結論是：只有單一而壓倒性的權力，才能終結那個陷阱；而在這一點上，證據對他不利。政治學家歐斯壯窮畢生之力，研究共用某項資源的社群：擁有共同高山草場的瑞士村莊、日本的山林、西班牙的灌溉水渠、緬因州的龍蝦漁場。其中許多已經管理了好幾個世紀而沒有毀掉；它們靠的是使用者自己訂的規則，彼此監督，罰則輕微，而屢犯者加重。她在 2009 年獲得了諾貝爾經濟學獎。實驗說的是同一個故事。在實驗室的遊戲裡，當搭便車的人不受懲罰時，陌生人之間的合作就衰退；而一旦允許玩家彼此罰款，合作就恢復了。許多人即使自己要付出代價，也願意去罰。

So the old question has a more exact answer than it had. We need rules because sincere and reasonable people, left to themselves, fall into contests and disagreements that leave all of them worse off. We need the rules to be enforced, because a good rule can be undone by a few who ignore it. And we have some choice about who does the enforcing, which is where the next three lessons begin. A philosopher of the Legalist school, Han Fei, drew the harshest conclusion from the same facts. “The people submit to power, and few can be moved by rightness,” he wrote, and he advised rulers to rely on rewards and punishments alone. The state that followed his advice unified China and lasted fifteen years.

所以，那個老問題有了一個比從前更精確的答案。我們需要規則，是因為真誠而講理的人，放任不管，會落入使所有人都更糟的爭奪與分歧。我們需要規則被「執行」，因為一條好規則可以被少數無視它的人毀掉。而由誰來執行，我們是有某些選擇的；接下來的三課就從這裡開始。法家的哲學家韓非，從同樣的事實引出了最嚴酷的結論。他寫道：「民者固服於勢，寡能懷於義」；他建議統治者只倚靠賞與罰。聽從了他的建議的那個國家統一了中國，維持了十五年。

Thought experiment · 思想實驗

Try it: the common pot 親手做一次：公共的鍋

You and three computer players each get ten tokens a round. Whatever is put into the common pot is multiplied by 1.6 and shared equally among all four, whether they contributed or not. Play five rounds with no rules, and then five in which players may fine one another.

你與三位電腦玩家，每一輪各拿到十枚代幣。投進公共鍋裡的代幣會乘以 1.6，再平分給四個人，不管他有沒有投。先玩五輪沒有規則的，再玩五輪玩家可以彼此罰款的。

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

“The life of man, solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short.” (Hobbes, on human nature)「人的生活孤獨、貧困、污穢、野蠻而短暫。」（霍布斯論人性）

His words, about a condition · 是他的話，但談的是一種處境

The phrase is authentic, from chapter 13 of Leviathan, and it is regularly quoted as Hobbes’s verdict on human life or human nature. It is neither. The sentence describes life in one situation, where there is no power to keep the peace, and its point is how much we gain by leaving that situation. Hobbes did not think people wicked. He says in the same chapter that the desires and passions of man are in themselves no sin, and that in the state of nature nothing can be unjust, since there is no law to break. The trouble, on his account, lies in the situation. He was by all reports a cheerful man who played tennis at seventy-five and lived to ninety-one.

這句話是真的，出自《利維坦》第十三章；而它經常被引用，當作霍布斯對人生或人性的判決。兩者都不是。這個句子描述的是「某一種處境」下的生活：沒有權力維持和平的時候；而它的重點是，離開那種處境讓我們得到了多少。霍布斯並不認為人是邪惡的。他在同一章裡說：人的欲望與激情本身並不是罪；而在自然狀態裡，沒有什麼事可以算不正義，因為沒有法律可以違反。照他的說法，毛病出在處境上。據各方記載，他是個開朗的人，七十五歲還在打網球，活到了九十一歲。

Both sides · 正反兩面

Objections and replies 反駁與回應

Objection 1 · 反駁

There never was a state of nature and no one ever signed a contract. The whole argument is a fiction.

從來就沒有什麼自然狀態，也沒有人簽過什麼契約。整個論證是虛構的。

Reply · 回應

David Hume made this objection in 1748. Nearly all governments, he observed, were founded on conquest or usurpation, and to tell a poor peasant that he has consented by staying is like telling a man carried aboard a ship asleep that he consents to the captain by not jumping into the sea. The better defenders of the method agree that it is a device. It asks what rational people would agree to, and uses the answer to test the arrangements we have. A rule that no one could reasonably have accepted is suspect whether or not anyone was asked.

休謨在 1748 年提出了這個反駁。他觀察到，幾乎所有的政府都是建立在征服或篡奪之上的；而對一個貧窮的農民說「你留下來就表示同意了」，就像對一個在睡夢中被抬上船的人說：你沒有跳海，就表示你同意聽船長的。為這個方法辯護得比較好的人，同意它是一個「裝置」。它問的是：理性的人會同意什麼？然後用這個答案來檢驗我們現有的安排。一條沒有任何人可能合理地接受的規則，不管有沒有人被問過，都是可疑的。

Objection 2 · 反駁

People are naturally cooperative. Rules and rulers are what make them selfish and violent.

人天生就是合作的。使他們變得自私而暴力的，正是規則與統治者。

Reply · 回應

This is the anarchist’s reply, and it has support in the studies of infants and of small communities. But the same evidence shows cooperation breaking down as groups grow and as free riders appear. The communities Ostrom studied did not lack rules. They had made their own and enforced them. The finding that people can govern themselves is a finding about how rules may be made. It gives no reason to think that a large society could do without them.

這是無政府主義者的回答；而它在嬰兒研究與小型社群的研究裡，是有支持的。然而，同樣的證據也顯示：隨著群體變大、隨著搭便車的人出現，合作就瓦解了。歐斯壯研究的那些社群並不是沒有規則。它們訂了自己的規則，並且加以執行。「人能夠自治」這個發現，是一個關於「規則可以怎麼訂」的發現。它並沒有給出理由讓人認為，一個大型社會可以不要規則。

Objection 3 · 反駁

If rules exist for our benefit, then I may break any rule whenever breaking it benefits me and harms no one.

如果規則是為了我們的利益而存在的，那麼，只要違反某條規則對我有利、又不傷害任何人，我就可以違反它。

Reply · 回應

The reasoning is the free rider’s, and the game shows where it leads when everyone uses it. There is a narrower point in it that is sound. A rule that has lost its purpose, or that serves only those who made it, has lost its claim, and Locke built a theory of resistance on that. The test is whether you could accept everyone’s breaking the rule in cases like yours. The person who runs a red light on an empty road at night can perhaps pass it. The person who evades a tax cannot.

這是搭便車者的推理；而那個遊戲顯示了，人人都這樣推理的時候它通向哪裡。其中有一個比較窄的論點是站得住的。一條已經失去目的、或只為訂立它的人服務的規則，已經失去了它的資格；洛克就是在這一點上建立了一套反抗的理論。檢驗的方法是：在像你這樣的情況裡，你能不能接受每個人都違反這條規則？深夜在空無一人的路上闖紅燈的人，也許過得了這一關。逃稅的人過不了。

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

A view from the Dharma 佛法怎麼看

☸

The Buddhist canon contains a state-of-nature story of its own, told as a myth and with some humor. In the beginning, it says, beings lived on food that grew by itself. Then some began to hoard. So they said, “We’d better divide up the rice and lay down boundaries.” With boundaries came theft, then accusation, lying, and blows. The beings gathered and said: “Why don’t we elect one being who would rightly accuse those who deserve it, blame those who deserve it, and expel those who deserve it?” In return they would give him a share of rice. He was called Mahāsammata, which the discourse explains as “elected by the populace.” The story has greed leading to property, property to conflict, and conflict to a ruler chosen by agreement and paid for a service. It places the origin of kingship in a contract and gives it no divine sanction, which was a radical thing to say in ancient India.

佛教的經藏裡，有它自己的一個自然狀態的故事；它是以神話的方式講的，還帶著幾分幽默。故事說，起初眾生靠著自己生長出來的食物過活。後來有些眾生開始囤積。於是他們說：「我們最好把稻米分一分，劃定界線。」有了界線，就有了偷盜；接著是指責、說謊與毆打。眾生聚集起來說：「我們何不推選一位眾生，由他對該指責的加以指責、該訶責的加以訶責、該驅逐的加以驅逐？」作為回報，他們會給他一份稻米。他被稱為 Mahāsammata；這部經解釋說，意思就是「大眾所推選的」。這個故事說：貪導致私產，私產導致衝突，衝突導致一位由約定選出、因提供服務而受供養的統治者。它把王權的起源放在一個契約裡，沒有給它任何神聖的授權；在古代印度，這是很激進的說法。

The Buddha’s own community was governed in a way that Ostrom would have recognized. The monastic rules were laid down one at a time, each in response to a particular problem, and the texts record the occasion for every one. The community met regularly, decided matters by agreement of all present, and had graded penalties ending in expulsion. There was no head with power over the rest. The Buddha declined to appoint a successor and told the monks that the teaching and the discipline would be their teacher (Dharma and the West, D18).

佛陀自己的僧團，治理的方式是歐斯壯認得出來的。戒律是一條一條制定的，每一條都是回應一個具體的問題；而律典記錄了每一條的緣起。僧團定期集會，以全體在場者的同意來決定事務，並且有分等級的處分，最重的是擯出。沒有一位握有支配其他人的權力的首領。佛陀拒絕指定繼承人；他告訴比丘們，法與律就是他們的老師（佛法與西方哲學 D18）。
