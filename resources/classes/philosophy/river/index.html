A10 · Reality 真實

Can You Step into the Same River Twice? 人能踏進同一條河兩次嗎？

Heraclitus held that everything flows. Parmenides proved that nothing can change. Nearly all later metaphysics is an attempt to keep what each of them saw.
赫拉克利特主張萬物流轉。巴門尼德證明沒有東西能改變。後來幾乎全部的形上學，都是想把他們各自看到的東西同時保住。

A10 Big Questions · 大哉問Reality · 真實Advanced · 高級1,157 words · about 25 min
The big idea · 大觀念

Change needs something that persists through it, and persistence shows itself only through change. A river is the plainest case: it is the same river because its water is always different.

變化需要有個東西在變化之中持續下去，而持續只有透過變化才顯現出來。河是最明白的例子：它之所以是同一條河，正因為它的水總是不同的。

The essay · 課文

Can You Step into the Same River Twice? 人能踏進同一條河兩次嗎？

01Two men who never met 兩個從未見面的人

Around 500 BCE, at opposite ends of the Greek world, two thinkers reached conclusions that could hardly be further apart. Heraclitus of Ephesus, on the coast of what is now Turkey, taught that the world is a process, like a fire or a river, in which nothing stays as it is. Parmenides of Elea, in southern Italy, argued that change is impossible and that what truly exists is one, whole, and motionless. Neither could be dismissed. Heraclitus had the evidence of the senses on his side, and Parmenides had an argument that nobody could answer. Philosophy after them had to explain how both could be so persuasive.

大約西元前 500 年，在希臘世界的兩端，有兩位思想家得出了相距不能更遠的結論。以弗所（在今天土耳其的海岸）的赫拉克利特教導：世界是一個過程，像火或河，其中沒有任何東西保持原樣。義大利南部埃利亞的巴門尼德則論證：變化是不可能的，真正存在的東西是唯一、完整、不動的。兩個人都沒辦法被打發掉。赫拉克利特有感官的證據站在他這邊；巴門尼德有一個沒有人答得了的論證。在他們之後的哲學，必須解釋這兩者怎麼都能這麼有說服力。

02The river 河

Heraclitus wrote in short, dense sayings, of which about a hundred and thirty survive as quotations in later authors. The most famous concern a river. “You cannot step twice into the same rivers; for fresh waters are ever flowing in upon you.” And, more strangely: “We step and do not step into the same rivers; we are and are not.” Plato reported the doctrine in the form that became proverbial, that “all things are in motion and nothing at rest,” and that “you cannot go into the same water twice.”

赫拉克利特用簡短而濃縮的格言寫作，其中大約一百三十條，以被後世作者引用的形式保存了下來。最有名的幾條談的是河。「你不能兩次踏進同一條河；因為新的水不斷地流到你身上。」還有更奇怪的一條：「我們踏進又沒有踏進同一條河；我們存在又不存在。」柏拉圖轉述這個學說，用的是後來成為諺語的形式：「萬物都在運動，沒有東西靜止」，以及「你不能兩次走進同樣的水」。

It is easy to hear this as a denial that anything is ever the same, and one of his followers took it that way. Cratylus, according to Aristotle, criticized his master for saying that one cannot step twice into the same river, “for he thought one could not do it even once,” and in the end he gave up speaking and only moved his finger. But look at the first saying again. It speaks of the same rivers. Heraclitus does not deny that there is one river, the Cayster, into which the Ephesians stepped every day. His point is about what makes it one. Dam it, so that the water stops being replaced, and you no longer have a river. The sameness of the river consists in the exchange of its waters.

人很容易把這聽成是在否認任何東西曾經保持同一，而他的一位追隨者就是這樣理解的。照亞里斯多德的說法，克拉底魯批評老師說「人不能兩次踏進同一條河」，「因為他認為連一次都不行」；到最後他不再說話，只動動手指。但是再看一次第一句格言。它說的是「同一條河」。赫拉克利特並不否認有一條河，凱斯特河，以弗所人天天踏進去的那一條。他的重點在於：是什麼使它成為一條河？把它攔起來，讓水不再更替，你就不再有一條河了。河的同一，就在於它的水的更替。

03The law in the flow 流變之中的法則

Heraclitus therefore taught more than flux. He taught that the flux is ordered. The world is “an ever-living Fire, with measures of it kindling, and measures going out,” and the word to notice is “measures.” Fire is his image for a thing that exists only by consuming and being fed, and a steady flame is a balance between the two. He called the principle of this balance the logos, a word that means both “account” and “proportion,” and he complained that people live as though they each had a private understanding when the logos is common to all. He saw the same balance in opposites. “The way up and the way down is one and the same.” Day and night, waking and sleeping, the bow’s wood pulling one way and its string the other: in each case two contrary things turn out to be one process seen from two sides. “The hidden attunement,” he said, “is better than the open.”

所以赫拉克利特教的不只是流變。他教的是：流變是有秩序的。世界是「一團永恆的活火，按一定的分寸燃燒，按一定的分寸熄滅」，要注意的詞是「分寸」。火是他用來比喻這樣一種東西的意象：它只有靠消耗與被餵養才存在；而穩定的火焰，是這兩者之間的平衡。他把這個平衡的原理稱為 logos，這個詞兼有「說明」與「比例」的意思；他抱怨說，人們活得好像各自有一套私人的理解，而 logos 其實是眾人共有的。他在對立的事物裡看到同樣的平衡。「上坡的路與下坡的路，是同一條路。」日與夜、醒與睡、弓的木頭往一邊拉而弦往另一邊拉：在每一種情形裡，兩個相反的東西，結果是同一個過程從兩面看過去的樣子。他說：「隱藏的和諧，勝過顯明的和諧。」

04The argument that nothing changes 「沒有東西改變」的論證

Parmenides set out his view in a poem, in which a goddess reveals to him “the only two ways of search that can be thought of.” The first is “that It is, and that it is impossible for it not to be.” The second is “that It is not.” The second way, she says, cannot be followed at all: “For thou canst not know what is not—that is impossible—nor utter it; for it is the same thing that can be thought and that can be.” To think is to think of something. What is not, is nothing, and nothing is not there to be thought of.

巴門尼德用一首詩陳述他的看法；詩中有一位女神向他揭示「僅有的兩條可以設想的探究之路」。第一條是：「它存在，而且它不可能不存在。」第二條是：「它不存在。」女神說，第二條路根本走不通：「因為你無法認識不存在的東西——那是不可能的——也無法說出它；因為能被思想的與能存在的，是同一回事。」思想，就是思想到某個東西。不存在的就是無，而無並不在那裡供人思想。

From this the rest follows with terrible ease. For a thing to come into being, it would have to come from what is not, and there is no such thing. For a thing to change, it would have to become what it is not. For there to be two things, there would have to be something between them that is not either of them. So what is “is uncreated and indestructible,” single, continuous, and unmoving. The changing world of the senses is an appearance that mortals have agreed to name. Parmenides’ pupil Zeno wrote the paradoxes of lesson A4 in order to show that those who laughed at this conclusion were worse off than his master.

由此，其餘的一切輕易得可怕地推了出來。一樣東西要生成，就得從不存在的東西而來，而沒有這樣的東西。一樣東西要改變，就得變成它所不是的。要有兩樣東西，它們之間就得有某個既不是這個、也不是那個的東西。所以存在者是「不生不滅的」，唯一、連續、不動。感官所見的那個變動的世界，是凡人約定命名出來的表象。巴門尼德的學生芝諾，寫下了 A4 那一課的悖論，為的是證明：嘲笑這個結論的人，處境比他的老師更糟。

05Keeping both 兩者都保住

The next two centuries of Greek philosophy can be read as a series of attempts to grant Parmenides his premise and still keep the river. The atomists accepted that what truly exists never comes into being or perishes, and said that there are countless such things, atoms, whose rearrangement in empty space is what we call change. Plato divided the world in two. The Forms are as Parmenides described, eternal and unchanging, and they are what knowledge is about. The world we perceive is as Heraclitus described, and of it we have only opinion.

此後兩個世紀的希臘哲學，可以讀成一連串的嘗試：承認巴門尼德的前提，同時還把那條河保住。原子論者接受「真正存在的東西既不生成也不毀滅」，然後說：這樣的東西有無數個，就是原子；它們在虛空裡的重新排列，就是我們所說的變化。柏拉圖把世界一分為二。理型正如巴門尼德所描述的，永恆而不變，是知識的對象。我們感知的世界正如赫拉克利特所描述的，對它我們只有意見。

Aristotle’s answer has lasted longest. He pointed out that “is not” is ambiguous. An acorn is not an oak, but it is not simply nothing either. It is potentially an oak. Change is the passage from being something potentially to being it actually, and it always has three terms: something that persists, a condition it lacked, and a condition it acquires. When a green leaf turns red, the leaf remains. Change, on this analysis, requires sameness. Parmenides was right that nothing comes from nothing, and wrong to think that change needs it to.

亞里斯多德的答案流傳得最久。他指出，「不是」是有歧義的。一顆橡實不是橡樹，但它也不是單純的「無」；它「潛在地」是一棵橡樹。變化，就是從「潛在地是某物」過渡到「現實地是某物」；它永遠有三項：一個持續存在的東西、一個它原本缺少的狀態、一個它後來獲得的狀態。綠葉變紅，葉子還在。照這個分析，變化需要同一。巴門尼德說「無中不能生有」是對的；他以為變化需要「無中生有」，那就錯了。

06The Master on the riverbank 川上的夫子

At about the time Heraclitus was writing, Confucius stood beside a river and said: “What passes away is like this, is it not? It does not cease, day or night” (逝者如斯夫，不舍晝夜). The Analects gives no explanation, and readers have supplied two. One hears a sigh over time and mortality, the feeling that everything is carried off. The other, which became standard among the Neo-Confucians, hears admiration. The stream never rests, and the person who is learning should be like it. Both readings find in the river what Heraclitus found there. Heraclitus made a theory of the world from it, and the Chinese tradition took it as a lesson in how to live.

大約在赫拉克利特寫作的同時，孔子站在一條河邊說：「逝者如斯夫！不舍晝夜。」《論語》沒有給任何解釋，讀者補上了兩種。一種聽到的是對時間與生命有限的嘆息，覺得一切都被帶走了。另一種，後來成為宋明理學家的標準讀法，聽到的是讚嘆：這條水流從不停歇，求學的人應當像它。兩種讀法在河裡找到的，都是赫拉克利特在那裡找到的東西。赫拉克利特從它造出一套關於世界的理論，中國的傳統則把它當作一堂怎麼活的課。

07Still with us 仍然與我們同在

Neither side has gone away. Modern physics has a Parmenidean face. Its deepest laws are conservation laws, statements that some quantity stays the same through every change, and the theory of relativity is often read as describing a four-dimensional “block” in which past, present, and future are equally real and nothing flows at all. It also has a Heraclitean face. A flame, a whirlpool, a living cell, and a human body are stable patterns in a stream of matter and energy, and they exist only as long as the stream continues. If you take the first picture as basic you have to explain why we seem to experience change. If you take the second as basic you have to explain what it is that stays the same. The river is where the two questions meet, and that is why the saying is still quoted after twenty-five centuries.

兩邊都沒有消失。現代物理學有一張巴門尼德的臉。它最深的定律是守恆定律，也就是「某個量在一切變化中保持不變」的陳述；而相對論常被解讀為在描述一個四維的「塊體」，其中過去、現在、未來同樣真實，根本沒有任何東西在流動。它也有一張赫拉克利特的臉。火焰、漩渦、活細胞、人的身體，都是物質與能量之流裡的穩定樣式，只有在那道流持續的時候才存在。如果你把第一幅圖像當作根本，你就得解釋我們為什麼似乎經驗到變化。如果你把第二幅當作根本，你就得解釋保持同一的到底是什麼。河，就是這兩個問題相遇的地方；這就是為什麼這句話在兩千五百年後還在被引用。

Thought experiment · 思想實驗

Try it: step into the river 親手做一次：踏進這條河

A river, seen from above. Step in, and the water that touches you is marked. Watch where it goes. Then step in again, and after that stop the river and see what is left of it.

一條河，從上方看。踏進去，碰到你的水會被標記起來。看它去了哪裡。然後再踏進去一次；之後把河停下來，看它還剩下什麼。

Was it the same river? 那是同一條河嗎？

You stepped in twice. Was it the same river?

你踏進去了兩次。那是同一條河嗎？

Heraclitus, read carefully · 仔細讀的赫拉克利特

This is probably what Heraclitus meant, and Aristotle would add that the river is the form and the water is its matter. You now owe an account of what the form of a river is, when its course, its depth, and even its name can change over the centuries.

這大概就是赫拉克利特的意思；亞里斯多德還會補一句：河是形式，水是它的質料。現在你欠一個說明：河的「形式」是什麼？它的河道、深度，甚至它的名字，幾百年下來都可以改變。

Cratylus · 克拉底魯

Then you did not step in even once, since the river changed while your foot was going down, and you are not the person who began reading this sentence. Cratylus drew the conclusion and stopped talking, because words assume that things stay put long enough to be named. If you are still willing to speak, you do not hold this view.

那麼你連一次都沒有踏進去，因為你的腳往下踩的時候，河就已經變了；而你也不是開始讀這個句子的那個人。克拉底魯得出了這個結論，然後不再說話，因為語言預設了事物會停留得夠久、久到可以被命名。如果你還願意開口說話，你就不持這個看法。

Parmenides · 巴門尼德

You hold that the flowing is an appearance and that reality is one and unchanging. You have a strong argument and a hard task: to explain how a changeless reality gives rise to the appearance of change, when the appearing is itself something that happens.

你主張流動是表象，而實在是唯一而不變的。你有一個很強的論證，也有一項艱難的任務：解釋一個不變的實在，怎麼會產生「變化」的表象；因為「顯現」本身，就是一件發生的事。

Convention · 約定

This is the answer you may have given for the ship. It works well for rivers, whose boundaries we plainly draw ourselves. The Buddhist tradition accepts it and adds that the usefulness of the way of speaking is real even though no single thing answers to the name.

這也許就是你對那艘船給的答案。它用在河上很合適，因為河的邊界明明是我們自己畫的。佛教傳統接受這個答案，並且補充說：雖然沒有一個單獨的東西與這個名字對應，這種說法的用處卻是真實的。

Primary text · 原典

In their own words 原典怎麼說

Neither man’s book survives. We have only the lines that later writers happened to quote, and scholars still dispute their order and sometimes their wording.

這兩個人的書都沒有流傳下來。我們有的只是後世作者碰巧引用的那些句子；它們的次序，有時連措辭，學者至今仍有爭論。

Heraclitus and Parmenides, fragments 赫拉克利特與巴門尼德的殘篇 · translated by John Burnet, Early Greek Philosophy (public domain)

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

“You cannot step into the same river twice.” (Heraclitus)「人不能兩次踏進同一條河。」（赫拉克利特）

Probably Plato’s version · 多半是柏拉圖的版本

The sentence in this form comes from Plato’s Cratylus, where it is introduced with the words “Heracleitus is supposed to say.” Many scholars think that the nearest thing to Heraclitus’s own words is a different fragment: on those who step into the same rivers, different and again different waters flow. That sentence asserts that the rivers are the same. If the scholars are right, the most famous line in early Greek philosophy reverses its author’s meaning.

這個形式的句子出自柏拉圖的《克拉底魯篇》，而且是用「據說赫拉克利特說過」這幾個字引出來的。許多學者認為，最接近赫拉克利特本人用語的是另一條殘篇：對那些踏進同一條河的人，流過來的是不同的、又不同的水。那個句子主張的是：河是同一條。如果這些學者是對的，那麼早期希臘哲學裡最有名的一句話，把作者的意思顛倒了。

Both sides · 正反兩面

Objections and replies 反駁與回應

Objection 1 · 反駁

If everything is always changing, nothing can be known or even named. By the time you have said “river,” it is something else.

如果一切都一直在變，就沒有東西能被認識，甚至沒有東西能被命名。等你說出「河」這個字，它已經是別的東西了。

Reply · 回應

This is Plato’s objection, and it is fatal to the extreme flux of Cratylus. It does not touch Heraclitus as read above, for whom the pattern of change is constant. What we know about a river is how it flows, and what a physicist knows about a flame is the law of its burning. Science is largely the search for what stays fixed in what changes.

這是柏拉圖的反駁；對克拉底魯那種極端的流變說，它是致命的。但它碰不到上面那樣解讀的赫拉克利特，因為對他來說，變化的樣式是恆定的。我們對一條河的了解，是它怎麼流；物理學家對一團火焰的了解，是它燃燒的法則。科學大體上就是在變動的東西裡，尋找固定不變的東西。

Objection 2 · 反駁

Parmenides is refuted every time someone crosses a room. An argument against a plain fact must contain a mistake, and there is no need to find it.

每當有人走過一個房間，巴門尼德就被駁倒了一次。一個反對明顯事實的論證，裡面一定有錯，而我們不需要把它找出來。

Reply · 回應

The first sentence is true, and the second is a refusal to do philosophy. Parmenides knew that people cross rooms. His claim was that our description of what happens when they do is incoherent. The replies of Plato and Aristotle took the argument seriously enough to find the fault in it, and logic and the theory of change are what they got for their trouble.

第一句話是真的；第二句話是拒絕做哲學。巴門尼德知道人會走過房間。他的主張是：我們對「人走過房間時發生了什麼」的描述，是前後不一致的。柏拉圖與亞里斯多德的回應，把這個論證看得夠認真，認真到去找出它的毛病；邏輯學與變化的理論，就是他們費這番工夫的收穫。

Objection 3 · 反駁

Heraclitus says that opposites are the same. That is a plain contradiction, and from a contradiction anything follows.

赫拉克利特說對立的東西是同一的。那是明明白白的矛盾；而從矛盾可以推出任何東西。

Reply · 回應

He says that the way up and the way down are one, which is true of any road. His examples are of a single thing that has contrary descriptions from different standpoints or at different times: sea water is healthy for fish and deadly for men. That is no contradiction. The serious question is whether he meant more than this, and some of his sayings suggest that he did.

他說的是上坡的路與下坡的路是同一條，而這對任何一條路都是真的。他舉的例子，都是同一個東西從不同的立場、或在不同的時間，有相反的描述：海水對魚有益，對人致命。這並不矛盾。認真的問題是：他的意思是不是不止於此？他的某些格言暗示，是的。

Terms of art · 哲學術語

The vocabulary of the subject 這門學問的用語

flux流變

L. fluxus ‘a flowing’

Continuous change; the doctrine that all things are in process.

持續不斷的變化；主張萬物都在過程之中的學說。

logos邏各斯

Gk. ‘word, account, proportion’

In Heraclitus, the rational principle or measure that governs change.

在赫拉克利特，指支配變化的理性原則或分寸。

unity of opposites對立的統一

The doctrine that contrary things are aspects of one process.

主張相反的事物是同一個過程的不同面向。

being and becoming存有與生成

What simply is, as against what comes to be and passes away.

單純「是」的東西，相對於生成與消逝的東西。

monism一元論

Gk. monos ‘single’

The view that reality is one thing, or of one kind.

主張實在是一個東西，或只有一種。

potentiality and actuality潛能與現實

Gk. dynamis and energeia

Aristotle’s pair of concepts for analyzing change: what a thing can become, and what it is.

亞里斯多德用來分析變化的一對概念：一樣東西能夠成為什麼，以及它現在是什麼。

substance and accident實體與偶性

L. substantia; accidens ‘what happens to’

The thing that persists, and the features it gains and loses.

持續存在的那個東西，以及它獲得與失去的特徵。

process philosophy歷程哲學

The modern view, associated with A. N. Whitehead, that events and processes are more basic than things.

現代的一種看法，與懷海德有關：事件與歷程比事物更基本。

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

A view from the Dharma 佛法怎麼看

☸

On impermanence the Buddha and Heraclitus say nearly the same thing, and they use the same image. “All conditioned things are impermanent” (sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā), says the Dhammapada. A discourse in the Aṅguttara Nikāya compares human life to a mountain river, flowing far and fast, of which there is no moment, no instant, no second when it turns back (7.74). Later Buddhist philosophers took the thought to its limit and held that each thing lasts only a moment, so that what we call one object is a rapid series of similar ones. That is very close to the position of Cratylus.

在無常這件事上，佛陀與赫拉克利特說的幾乎一樣，而且用了同一個意象。《法句經》說：「一切行無常」（sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā）。《增支部》有一部經把人的生命比作山間的河流，流得遠、流得急，沒有一剎那、一瞬間、一須臾是會回頭的（7.74）。後來的佛教哲學家把這個想法推到極限，主張每一樣東西只存續一剎那，所以我們稱為「一個物體」的，是一連串快速相續的相似者。這跟克拉底魯的立場非常接近。

The differences are instructive. Heraclitus’s flux has something permanent in it. The logos does not change, and the fire is ever-living. Early Buddhism names no such underlying stuff, and its regularity, dependent origination, is a pattern among events and is not treated as a thing in its own right. The two also disagree about what the insight is for. To Heraclitus the fire of the world is something to be understood and admired. In the Fire Sermon the Buddha tells his monks that “all is burning,” the eye, the ear, the mind, burning with greed, hatred, and delusion, and the point of seeing this is to let the fire go out (Saṃyutta Nikāya 35.28). The word nirvāṇa means exactly that. And where the Greek debate had two sides, the Buddhist tradition counts three. Against Parmenides it denies that anything is permanent. Against the extreme of Cratylus it affirms that conditions reliably give rise to their effects. Its own position is the middle one, that there is continuity and no identity (Dharma and the West, D5).

差別之處很有啟發性。赫拉克利特的流變裡，有某種恆常的東西：logos 不變，那團火是「永恆的活火」。早期佛教沒有指認任何這樣的底層材料；它的規律性——緣起——是事件之間的一種樣式，沒有被當成一個獨立自存的東西。兩者對於「這個洞見是拿來做什麼的」也看法不同。對赫拉克利特來說，世界之火是要被理解、被讚嘆的。在〈燃燒經〉裡，佛陀告訴比丘們「一切都在燃燒」：眼、耳、意，被貪、瞋、癡之火燃燒；而看見這一點，是為了讓火熄滅（《相應部》35.28）。「涅槃」這個詞，意思正是如此。還有，希臘的辯論有兩邊，佛教傳統則算出三邊。針對巴門尼德，它否認有任何東西是恆常的。針對克拉底魯那種極端，它肯定條件會可靠地產生它們的結果。它自己的立場是中間那一個：有連續，而沒有同一（佛法與西方哲學 D5）。
