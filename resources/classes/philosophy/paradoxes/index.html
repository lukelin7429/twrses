Can You Ever Catch the Tortoise? 你追得上烏龜嗎？

Paradoxes: arguments that start from what everyone accepts, proceed by steps no one can fault, and arrive at what no one can believe.
悖論：從人人接受的前提出發，每一步都挑不出毛病，卻走到沒有人能相信的結論。

The big idea · 大觀念

A paradox shows that some belief we hold must be wrong without telling us which. That is why paradoxes, more than any other kind of argument, have forced people to rebuild their basic concepts.

悖論告訴我們，我們的某個信念一定錯了，卻不告訴我們是哪一個。所以悖論比任何別種論證，都更能逼人重建自己的基本概念。

The essay · 課文

Can You Ever Catch the Tortoise? 你追得上烏龜嗎？

01A proof of the impossible 對不可能之事的證明

The last two lessons were about telling good arguments from bad ones. A paradox is the case where that skill gives out. The premises look true. Each step looks valid. And the conclusion is something no sane person accepts: that the fastest runner cannot catch the slowest, that a sentence is true and false at once, that there is no such thing as a heap of sand. Since a sound argument cannot have a false conclusion, something must give way, and there are only three choices. One of the premises is false after all. Or one of the steps is invalid. Or the conclusion, incredible as it seems, is true. Philosophers call the third option biting the bullet, and the history of the subject is full of people who did.

前兩課談的是分辨好論證與壞論證。悖論，是這項本事失靈的地方。前提看起來是真的；每一步看起來都有效；而結論是任何神智正常的人都不接受的：跑得最快的人追不上最慢的；一個句子同時又真又假；世界上沒有「一堆沙」這種東西。既然健全的論證不可能有假的結論，總得有什麼東西讓步，而選擇只有三個：原來有一個前提是假的；或者有一步是無效的；或者那個結論，儘管看來難以置信，卻是真的。哲學家把第三種選擇叫作「咬子彈」（硬著頭皮接受），而這門學問的歷史上，這樣做的人多得是。

02The race 那場賽跑

The oldest paradoxes we have come from Zeno of Elea, a Greek of the fifth century BCE. His teacher Parmenides had argued that reality is one and unchanging and that motion is an appearance. Zeno defended him by showing that the commonsense view, on which things move, leads to absurdities of its own. Aristotle preserves the most famous of his arguments: “in a race the quickest runner can never overtake the slowest, since the pursuer must first reach the point whence the pursued started, so that the slower must always hold a lead.”

我們手上最古老的悖論出自埃利亞的芝諾，西元前五世紀的希臘人。他的老師巴門尼德主張，實在是唯一而不變的，運動只是表象。芝諾替老師辯護的方式，是證明常識的看法——東西會動——自己會導出荒謬。亞里斯多德保存了他最有名的一個論證：「在賽跑中，跑得最快的永遠追不上最慢的，因為追的人必須先到達被追的人出發的那一點，所以慢的那個必定一直保持領先。」

Give the slow runner a start of a hundred meters. By the time Achilles has covered the hundred, the other has crept forward ten. By the time Achilles has covered those ten, it is one meter further on, and so without end. At every stage a gap remains. The stages never run out. So it seems that Achilles is always on the point of drawing level and never does.

讓慢的那個先跑一百公尺。等阿基里斯跑完這一百公尺，對方已經往前爬了十公尺。等阿基里斯跑完這十公尺，牠又在前面一公尺了；如此下去，沒有盡頭。每一個階段都還剩一段差距，而階段永遠用不完。於是看起來，阿基里斯永遠就要追平，卻永遠沒有追平。

03Half of a stick 半根棍子

Chinese thinkers found the same puzzle independently. The last chapter of the Zhuangzi records a saying of the debaters around Hui Shi, in the fourth century BCE: “A stick one foot long: take away half of it every day, and in ten thousand generations it will not be used up.” This is Zeno’s other great argument, the Dichotomy, turned from motion to matter. Before you reach the door you must reach the halfway point, and before that the halfway point of the first half. Run in one direction the argument says you never arrive, and run in the other it says you can never set out.

中國的思想家各自獨立地發現了同一個難題。《莊子》最後一篇記下了西元前四世紀惠施周圍那群辯者的一句話：「一尺之捶，日取其半，萬世不竭。」這是芝諾另一個重要論證「二分法」，只是從運動換成了物質。你走到門口之前，必須先走到一半；在那之前，又必須先走到前一半的一半。這個論證往一個方向推，說你永遠到不了；往另一個方向推，說你根本出發不了。

04The sum of infinitely many things 無窮多個東西的總和

The standard answer took two thousand years to state properly. Zeno assumes that infinitely many distances, or infinitely many intervals of time, must add up to an infinite amount. They need not. The distances in the race are 100, 10, 1, 0.1 and so on, and the more of them you add, the closer the total comes to 111.11… meters without ever exceeding it. Mathematicians of the nineteenth century, Cauchy foremost among them, made this exact with the concept of a limit. The times behave the same way. If Achilles runs ten meters a second, the stages take 10 seconds, then 1, then 0.1, and all of them together take 11.11… seconds. After that he is in front.

標準答案花了兩千年才被說清楚。芝諾假定，無窮多段距離，或無窮多段時間，加起來一定是無窮大。其實不必。賽跑裡的各段距離是 100、10、1、0.1……，你加進去的項愈多，總和就愈接近 111.11… 公尺，卻永遠不會超過它。十九世紀的數學家，以柯西為首，用「極限」這個概念把這件事說得精確。時間也是同樣的情形。如果阿基里斯每秒跑十公尺，各階段分別花 10 秒、1 秒、0.1 秒……，全部加起來是 11.11… 秒。在那之後，他就在前面了。

05Is the arithmetic enough? 光有算術夠嗎？

Many philosophers think this dissolves the paradox, and many do not. The arithmetic tells us where and when Achilles draws level. It does not obviously tell us how anyone finishes a sequence of tasks that has no last member. In 1954 James Thomson sharpened the worry with a lamp. Switch it on for a minute, off for half a minute, on for a quarter, and so on; after two minutes infinitely many switchings are done. Is the lamp then on or off? It cannot be on, since every time it was turned on it was turned off again, and it cannot be off for the same reason. Thomson concluded that completing infinitely many tasks, a “supertask,” is impossible. His critics replied that the story merely fails to say what happens at two minutes. The argument continues, and what is at stake in it is whether space, time, and motion are really as the mathematics of the continuum describes them.

許多哲學家認為這樣就化解了悖論，也有許多人不這麼想。算術告訴我們阿基里斯在哪裡、在什麼時候追平。可是，一個沒有「最後一項」的任務序列，到底是怎麼被做完的，算術並沒有明白地告訴我們。1954 年，湯姆森用一盞燈把這個疑慮磨得更尖。把燈打開一分鐘，關掉半分鐘，再開四分之一分鐘，如此下去；兩分鐘後，無窮多次開關都做完了。這時燈是亮的還是暗的？它不可能是亮的，因為每一次打開之後都又被關掉了；基於同樣的理由，它也不可能是暗的。湯姆森的結論是：完成無窮多個任務，也就是所謂的「超任務」，是不可能的。批評他的人回答說，這個故事只是沒有交代兩分鐘那一刻發生了什麼。這場爭論還在繼續；而它真正牽涉到的是：空間、時間與運動，是不是真的像連續統的數學所描述的那樣。

06A sentence that turns on itself 一個回頭咬自己的句子

A second family of paradoxes needs no racetrack, only a sentence. Consider: “This sentence is false.” If it is true, then what it says is the case, so it is false. If it is false, then what it says is not the case, so it is true. The ancient version is credited to Eubulides, a contemporary of Aristotle, and it is known as the Liar. It is tempting to dismiss the Liar as a trick, and for two thousand years most people did. Then, in the early twentieth century, the same structure of self-reference turned up in the foundations of mathematics. Bertrand Russell found it in the notion of a set, and Kurt Gödel used a relative of the Liar, a sentence that says of itself that it cannot be proved, to show that arithmetic contains truths no consistent system of rules can derive. Alfred Tarski’s response to the Liar was that no language can contain its own truth predicate: to say of a sentence that it is true, one has to step up into a richer language. Ordinary English falls short of that standard, which may mean that ordinary English is, strictly speaking, inconsistent.

第二個家族的悖論不需要跑道，只需要一個句子。請看：「這個句子是假的。」如果它是真的，那麼它說的就是事實，所以它是假的。如果它是假的，那麼它說的就不是事實，所以它是真的。它的古代版本相傳出自與亞里斯多德同時代的歐布利德斯，稱為「說謊者悖論」。把說謊者當成文字把戲打發掉，是很誘人的做法；兩千年來，多數人正是這樣做的。然後在二十世紀初，同一種自我指涉的結構，出現在數學的基礎裡。羅素在「集合」這個概念裡發現了它；哥德爾則用了說謊者的一個親戚——一個說自己「不可被證明」的句子——證明算術裡有些真理，是任何一致的規則系統都推導不出來的。塔斯基對說謊者的回應是：沒有任何語言能包含它自己的「真」這個謂詞；要說一個句子是真的，就得往上跨進一個更豐富的語言。日常的英語達不到這個標準；這也許表示，嚴格說來，日常的英語是不一致的。

07One grain at a time 一次一粒

Eubulides is also credited with the paradox of the heap, the sorites. One grain of sand is not a heap. Adding a single grain to something that is not a heap never turns it into one. So no number of grains makes a heap. Each premise seems undeniable, the logic is modus ponens repeated, and the conclusion is false. What makes the sorites so hard is that the trouble is everywhere. “Heap” is vague, and so are “tall,” “adult,” “alive,” and “person,” and the same argument can be run on each of them. Law and ethics depend on such words, and on lines that have to be drawn somewhere although no particular place for them can be defended.

相傳「堆垛悖論」（sorites）也出自歐布利德斯。一粒沙不是一堆。在不成堆的東西上加一粒沙，絕不會讓它變成一堆。所以不管多少粒沙，都不成一堆。每一個前提看來都無法否認，邏輯只是反覆使用肯定前件，而結論是假的。堆垛悖論之所以這麼難，是因為問題無所不在。「堆」是模糊的，「高」「成年」「活著」「人」也都是；同一個論證可以套在每一個詞上。法律與倫理都仰賴這類詞語，也仰賴那些總得畫在某處的界線；儘管沒有哪一個特定的位置能被辯護。

08What paradoxes are for 悖論是做什麼用的

It would be a mistake to file all this under puzzles. A paradox is an argument that our own concepts make against us. It shows that what we believe about motion, or truth, or the meaning of ordinary words cannot all be right together, and it does so using nothing except those beliefs. The great ones are therefore never simply solved. They are answered by changing something: by inventing the limit, or separating a language from its metalanguage, or by rethinking what a set is. With this the first unit ends, on a caution. Logic can show us that our premises conflict. It does not tell us which of them to give up.

把這一切歸到「益智謎題」那一類，會是個錯誤。悖論是我們自己的概念對我們提出的論證。它證明我們對運動、對真、對日常詞語意義的種種信念，不可能同時全部正確；而且它除了這些信念之外，什麼都沒用到。所以偉大的悖論從來不是單純被「解開」的。回應它們的方式，是改變某樣東西：發明極限，或把一個語言跟它的後設語言分開，或重新思考集合是什麼。第一個單元就在這裡結束，結束在一句提醒上：邏輯能告訴我們，我們的前提互相衝突；它不告訴我們該放棄哪一個。

Thought experiment · 思想實驗

Try it: run the race two ways 親手做一次：用兩種方式跑這場比賽

First run the race Zeno’s way, one stage at a time, and watch the gap shrink without ever closing. Then let the clock run and watch Achilles go past. The same race is being described both times, and the paradox is the question of how both descriptions can be right.

先照芝諾的方式跑，一次一個階段，看差距不斷縮小卻始終沒有合上。然後讓時鐘自己走，看阿基里斯超過去。兩次描述的是同一場比賽；悖論就是在問：這兩種描述怎麼可能都對？

Primary text · 原典

In their own words 原典怎麼說

Zeno’s book is lost. Everything we know of his arguments comes from later writers, and chiefly from Aristotle, who reports them in order to refute them.

芝諾的書失傳了。我們對他論證的了解全部來自後來的作者，主要是亞里斯多德；而亞里斯多德記下它們，是為了反駁。

Aristotle, Physics VI.9; Zhuangzi, chapter 33 亞里斯多德《物理學》卷六第九章；《莊子・天下》 · Aristotle translated by R. P. Hardie and R. K. Gaye (public domain); the Zhuangzi line in our own translation

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

Zeno set Achilles to race against a tortoise.芝諾讓阿基里斯跟一隻烏龜賽跑。

The tortoise came later · 烏龜是後來才出現的

Our earliest source, Aristotle, mentions no tortoise. He says only that the quickest runner cannot overtake the slowest, and the name “Achilles” tells us who the quick one was. The tortoise appears in later commentators and has been in every retelling since. Zeno’s own words are lost, so we cannot say what example he used.

我們最早的史料亞里斯多德並沒有提到烏龜。他只說跑得最快的追不上最慢的，而「阿基里斯」這個名字告訴我們快的那個是誰。烏龜出現在後來的註釋者筆下，從此每一次轉述都有牠。芝諾自己的話已經失傳，所以我們沒辦法說他用的是什麼例子。

Both sides · 正反兩面

Objections and replies 反駁與回應

Objection 1 · 反駁

Calculus solved Zeno’s paradoxes long ago. Only philosophers still pretend there is a problem.

微積分早就解決了芝諾的悖論。只有哲學家還在假裝這裡有問題。

Reply · 回應

Calculus shows that the infinite series has a finite sum, and so refutes the premise that infinitely many runs need infinite time. It does not by itself show that physical space and time are infinitely divisible, as the mathematics assumes, and it does not say what it is to complete a sequence with no last step. One may think those further questions have good answers. They are still questions, and they are not answered by doing the sum.

微積分證明了這個無窮級數有有限的總和，因此駁倒了「無窮多段的跑需要無窮長的時間」這個前提。但它本身並沒有證明物理的空間與時間像數學所假定的那樣可以無限分割，也沒有說明「做完一個沒有最後一步的序列」是怎麼一回事。你可以認為這些進一步的問題都有好答案。它們仍然是問題，而且不是算出總和就回答了的。

Objection 2 · 反駁

The Liar is not a real sentence. A sentence that talks about itself says nothing, so it is neither true nor false and there is no paradox.

說謊者根本不是真正的句子。一個談論自己的句子什麼也沒說，所以它既不真也不假，沒有悖論可言。

Reply · 回應

Self-reference as such is harmless: “This sentence has five words” is plainly true. And the proposed way out leads straight back in. Take the sentence “This sentence is not true.” If it is neither true nor false, then it is not true, which is just what it says, so it is true after all. This “strengthened Liar” has defeated a long line of solutions.

自我指涉本身是無害的：「這個句子有八個字」明明白白是真的。而且這條出路會直接繞回原地。看這個句子：「這個句子不是真的。」如果它既不真也不假，那麼它就不是真的，而這正是它說的，所以它終究是真的。這個「強化版說謊者」已經打敗了一長串的解法。

Objection 3 · 反駁

Paradoxes are games with words. Nobody doubts that runners overtake one another or that heaps exist, so nothing is at stake.

悖論是文字遊戲。沒有人懷疑跑者會互相超越，也沒有人懷疑沙堆存在，所以這裡沒有什麼要緊的東西。

Reply · 回應

Nobody doubts the conclusions are false. What is in doubt is which of our own premises is to blame, and finding out has repeatedly been productive: the theory of limits, modern set theory, and Gödel’s theorems all grew out of taking a paradox seriously. The sorites is also a practical matter. Any law that fixes an age of majority or a speed limit has drawn a sharp line through a vague concept.

沒有人懷疑那些結論是假的。有疑問的是：該怪我們自己的哪一個前提？而把這件事弄清楚，一再帶來成果：極限理論、現代集合論、哥德爾定理，都是認真對待某個悖論之後長出來的。堆垛悖論也是很實際的事。任何規定成年年齡或速限的法律，都是在一個模糊的概念上畫了一條清楚的線。

Terms of art · 哲學術語

The vocabulary of the subject 這門學問的用語

paradox悖論

Gk. para ‘beyond, contrary to’ + doxa ‘opinion’

An apparently sound argument for an unacceptable conclusion.

一個看來健全的論證，卻導出無法接受的結論。

reductio ad absurdum歸謬法

L. ‘reduction to the absurd’

Refuting a claim by deriving an absurdity from it. Zeno used it against the belief in motion.

從一個主張推出荒謬，藉此反駁它。芝諾用它來反對「運動存在」這個信念。

limit極限

L. limes ‘boundary’

The value that the partial sums of a series approach as closely as one likes.

一個級數的部分和可以任意接近的那個值。

convergent series收斂級數

L. convergere ‘to incline together’

An infinite series whose partial sums have a finite limit.

部分和有有限極限的無窮級數。

supertask超任務

An infinite sequence of tasks completed in a finite time.

在有限時間內完成的無窮多個任務。

self-reference自我指涉

A sentence’s referring to itself, as in the Liar.

句子指涉它自己，例如說謊者悖論。

metalanguage後設語言

Gk. meta ‘beyond’

A language used to talk about another language, including about which of its sentences are true.

用來談論另一個語言的語言，包括談論後者哪些句子為真。

sorites堆垛悖論

Gk. sōros ‘heap’

A paradox that proceeds by many small, individually harmless steps.

靠許多個別看來無害的小步驟推進的悖論。

vagueness模糊性

L. vagus ‘wandering’

The feature of a word that has borderline cases and no sharp boundary.

一個詞有邊界個案、卻沒有明確界線的特性。

Advanced English · 高級英文

Words and phrases worth keeping 值得帶走的字與片語

give way(v. phr.)讓步、撐不住

Since a sound argument cannot have a false conclusion, something must give way.

既然健全的論證不可能有假的結論，總得有什麼東西讓步。

bite the bullet(v. phr.)硬著頭皮接受（不討喜的結論）

Philosophers call accepting the incredible conclusion biting the bullet.

哲學家把接受那個難以置信的結論叫作「咬子彈」。

overtake(v.)追過、超越

The quickest runner can never overtake the slowest.

跑得最快的永遠追不上最慢的。

by the time(conj. phr.)等到……的時候

By the time Achilles has covered the hundred, the other has crept forward ten.

等阿基里斯跑完這一百公尺，對方已經往前爬了十公尺。

draw level(v. phr.)追平

Achilles is always on the point of drawing level and never does.

阿基里斯永遠就要追平，卻永遠沒有追平。

add up to(phr. v.)總計為

Infinitely many distances need not add up to an infinite amount.

無窮多段距離，加起來不必是無窮大。

dissolve(v.)化解（使問題不成問題）

Many philosophers think this dissolves the paradox.

許多哲學家認為這樣就化解了悖論。

at stake(adj. phr.)攸關、成敗所繫

What is at stake is whether space and time are as the mathematics describes them.

真正牽涉到的是：空間與時間是不是像數學所描述的那樣。

turn up(phr. v.)（意外）出現

The same structure turned up in the foundations of mathematics.

同一種結構出現在數學的基礎裡。

fall short of(v. phr.)達不到

Ordinary English falls short of that standard.

日常的英語達不到那個標準。

Write · 你怎麼想？

  1. Which premise of the Achilles would you reject, and why? Answer in about 150 words, and say what your answer commits you to believing about space and time.

    阿基里斯論證的哪一個前提你會否定？為什麼？用大約 150 個英文字回答，並說明你的答案讓你對空間與時間必須相信什麼。

  2. Run a sorites argument on a word that matters to you, such as “adult,” “fluent,” or “rich.” Where would you draw the line, and can you defend drawing it there and not one step earlier?

    拿一個對你重要的詞（例如「成年」「流利」「有錢」）跑一次堆垛論證。你會把線畫在哪裡？你能為「畫在這裡、而不是早一步」辯護嗎？

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

A view from the Dharma 佛法怎麼看

☸

Around 200 CE the Indian philosopher Nāgārjuna opened the second chapter of his Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way with an argument that could be mistaken for Zeno’s. What has already been traversed is not being traversed. What has not yet been traversed is not being traversed. And apart from these two there is no third stretch, “being traversed now,” to be found. In Kumārajīva’s Chinese: 已去無有去，未去亦無去，離已去未去，去時亦無去. Two centuries later his Chinese follower Sengzhao drew the conclusion in a famous line: the whirlwind that topples mountains is forever still, and the rivers that race to the sea do not flow.

大約西元 200 年，印度哲學家龍樹在《中論》第二品開頭提出了一個論證，很容易被誤認成芝諾的。已經走過的路段，不是「正在走」的。還沒走到的路段，也不是「正在走」的。而在這兩者之外，找不到第三段「此刻正在走」的路。鳩摩羅什的漢譯是：「已去無有去，未去亦無去，離已去未去，去時亦無去。」兩個世紀後，他的中國後學僧肇在一句名言裡引出結論：「旋嵐偃嶽而常靜，江河兢注而不流。」

The resemblance is real and the purposes are opposite. Zeno argued against motion in order to defend Parmenides’ single unchanging reality. Nāgārjuna has no such reality to defend. His target is the assumption that a mover, a motion, and a path each exist with a nature of their own (svabhāva); grant that assumption, he argues, and motion becomes impossible, so the assumption has to go. People walk, conventionally speaking, and nothing in the argument denies it. Where Zeno accepts the conclusion and gives up change, Nāgārjuna uses the same kind of reductio to give up fixed essences, and that leaves him with a world in which there is nothing except change. Of the three responses to a paradox, he makes the first, and the premise he rejects is one that most of his readers had never noticed they held (Dharma and the West, D8 and D9).

相似是真的，目的卻相反。芝諾反對運動，是為了捍衛巴門尼德那個唯一而不變的實在。龍樹沒有這樣的實在要捍衛。他瞄準的是一個預設：行走的人、行走這件事、所走的路，各自有它自己的本性（自性，svabhāva）。他論證說，一旦承認這個預設，運動就變得不可能，所以該放棄的是這個預設。就世俗的說法而言，人是會走路的，這個論證絲毫沒有否認這一點。芝諾接受結論而放棄了變化；龍樹用同一種歸謬法放棄的是固定的本質，結果留給他的是一個除了變化別無他物的世界。在回應悖論的三種方式裡，他採取的是第一種；而他否定的那個前提，是大多數讀者從沒察覺自己抱持著的（佛法與西方哲學 D8、D9）。
