B1 · The Story of Philosophy 哲學史 · Miletus, c. 600–500 BCE

From Myth to Reason 從神話到理性

Around 600 BCE, in a busy Greek port on the coast of what is now Turkey, a man named Thales said that everything comes from water. The answer was wrong, and the question was new. This lesson tells how the first Greek philosophers began to explain the world by nature instead of by the will of the gods, and why the old story of a clean break “from myth to reason” needs some care.
大約西元前 600 年，在今天土耳其海岸一座繁忙的希臘港口，一個叫泰利斯的人說：萬物都來自水。答案是錯的，問題卻是新的。這一課講最早的希臘哲學家，怎麼開始用自然、而不是用神的意志來解釋世界；以及為什麼「從神話到理性」這個乾淨俐落的老故事，需要小心地講。

The big idea · 大觀念

The first philosophers did not get the answers right. What they started was a habit: explaining nature by nature, giving reasons that others could test, and expecting their own students to disagree with them.

最早的哲學家並沒有答對。他們開始的是一種習慣：用自然解釋自然；提出別人可以檢驗的理由；並且預期自己的學生會不同意自己。

The story · 課文

From Myth to Reason 從神話到理性

01Before Thales: the world as a family story 泰利斯之前：世界是一個家族故事

Every culture has an account of how the world began. The Greek poet Hesiod, writing around 700 BCE, gave one in his Theogony, the “birth of the gods.” In H. G. Evelyn-White’s translation, it begins: “Verily at the first Chaos came to be, but next wide-bosomed Earth, the ever-sure foundations of all.” Earth then gives birth to Heaven, and Heaven and Earth produce the next generation of gods, who quarrel, plot, and overthrow their parents. In this kind of account, the world is explained the way a family is explained, by who was born from whom and who fought whom. Thunder happens because Zeus is angry. The sea is rough because Poseidon wills it. The explanation is a story about persons, and it cannot be checked except by trusting the poet who tells it.

每一個文化都有一套關於世界怎麼開始的說法。希臘詩人赫西俄德大約在西元前 700 年，在他的《神譜》（「眾神的誕生」）裡給了一套。H. G. Evelyn-White 的英譯是這樣開始的：「真的，最先產生的是混沌，其次是胸懷寬廣的大地，萬物永遠穩固的根基。」大地接著生下天空；天空與大地又生出下一代的神；他們爭吵、密謀，推翻自己的父母。在這種說法裡，世界被解釋的方式，就像解釋一個家族：誰生了誰、誰跟誰打仗。打雷是因為宙斯生氣了。海浪洶湧是因為波賽頓要它如此。解釋是一個關於「人格」的故事；除了相信說故事的詩人之外，沒有辦法查核。

Philosophy, as the Greeks later told it, began about a century after Hesiod in Miletus, a wealthy trading city on the coast of Ionia, in what is now western Turkey. Miletus had ships in Egypt and colonies around the Black Sea, and it lived next door to the Lydian kingdom and within reach of Babylon. Its merchants met people with other gods and other stories, and saw that the stories did not agree. They also had access to Egyptian methods of measuring land and to centuries of Babylonian records of the stars. A city like this had leisure, money, and a reason to wonder which account of the world, if any, was true.

照希臘人後來的說法，哲學大約在赫西俄德之後一個世紀，開始於米利都：愛奧尼亞海岸（今天土耳其西部）一座富裕的貿易城市。米利都的船隊到過埃及，它在黑海周圍有殖民地；它緊鄰呂底亞王國，也能接觸到巴比倫。它的商人遇見信奉別的神、說著別的故事的人，並且看到這些故事彼此不一致。他們也接觸得到埃及丈量土地的方法，以及巴比倫幾百年來的星象紀錄。這樣一座城市有閒暇、有財富，也有理由去好奇：關於世界的哪一套說法是真的（如果有的話）。

02Thales: a wrong answer to a new question 泰利斯：對一個新問題的錯誤答案

Thales of Miletus left no writings, and everything we know about him comes from authors who lived long after him. The most important is Aristotle, writing about two and a half centuries later. In W. D. Ross’s translation of the Metaphysics: “Thales, the founder of this type of philosophy, says the principle is water (for which reason he declared that the earth rests on water).” Aristotle then guesses at his reasons, “getting the notion perhaps from seeing that the nutriment of all things is moist,” and that “the seeds of all things have a moist nature.” Notice the word “perhaps.” Even Aristotle was reconstructing. But the shape of the claim is clear. Behind the variety of things there is one basic stuff, and the world is explained by what that stuff does, not by what a god wants.

米利都的泰利斯沒有留下任何著作；我們所知道關於他的一切，都來自比他晚很久的作者。最重要的是亞里斯多德，他寫作的時間大約晚了兩個半世紀。W. D. Ross 所譯的《形上學》說：「泰利斯，這一類哲學的創始者，說本原是水（因此他宣稱大地浮在水上）。」亞里斯多德接著猜測他的理由：「或許是因為看到萬物的養分都是濕的」，以及「萬物的種子都有濕的性質」。注意那個「或許」。連亞里斯多德也是在重建。但這個主張的形狀是清楚的：在事物的多樣性背後，有一種基本的質料；世界是由這種質料做了什麼來解釋的，而不是由某位神想要什麼來解釋的。

Three stories about Thales show how the Greeks remembered him. Herodotus reports that during a battle between the Lydians and the Medes “suddenly the day became night,” and that Thales “had foretold” this change. Modern astronomers date a solar eclipse visible there to 585 BCE, though historians doubt he could have predicted it so precisely. Aristotle tells that when people mocked Thales’ poverty as proof that philosophy is useless, he foresaw a large olive harvest and rented all the olive presses in advance, to show “that philosophers can easily be rich if they like, but that their ambition is of another sort.” And Plato tells the opposite story, of a “clever witty Thracian handmaid” who laughed when Thales “fell into a well as he was looking up at the stars.” The absent-minded professor and the shrewd investor are both born here.

三個關於泰利斯的故事，顯示希臘人怎麼記得他。希羅多德記載，在呂底亞人與米底人的一場戰役中，「白晝突然變成了黑夜」，而泰利斯「曾經預言過」這個變化。現代天文學家把一次在那裡看得到的日蝕，定在西元前 585 年；不過歷史學家懷疑他能預測得那麼精確。亞里斯多德說，當人們嘲笑泰利斯貧窮、說這證明哲學沒有用時，他預見了一次橄欖大豐收，事先租下了所有的榨油坊，以顯示「哲學家只要願意，很容易致富，但他們的志向是另一種」。柏拉圖則講了相反的故事：一位「聰明而機智的色雷斯女僕」，在泰利斯「仰望星星時掉進井裡」的時候笑了他。心不在焉的教授與精明的投資人，兩種形象都是從這裡誕生的。

03The students who disagreed 不同意老師的學生

What made Miletus a beginning was not Thales alone but the people after him. Anaximander, said to be his student, rejected water. The basic stuff, he argued, could not be any one of the familiar elements, since each is the opposite of another and would have overwhelmed the rest. He called it the apeiron, the boundless. One sentence of his survives, in John Burnet’s translation: things pass away into that from which they arose, “as is ordained; for they make reparation and satisfaction to one another for their injustice according to the appointed time.” Anaximenes, the next generation, proposed air, which becomes fire when thinned and wind, cloud, water, and stone when condensed. Here, for the first time, a teacher’s view was openly corrected by his students, with reasons, and the correction was treated as progress.

使米利都成為一個開端的，不只是泰利斯一個人，而是他之後的那些人。據說是他學生的阿那克西曼德，否定了水。他論證說：基本的質料不可能是任何一種熟悉的元素，因為每一種元素都是另一種的對立面，會壓倒其餘的。他稱它為 apeiron，「無限定者」。他有一句話留存了下來，John Burnet 的英譯是：事物消逝，回到它們所由生起的那裡，「如同所命定的；因為它們依照指定的時間，為彼此的不義作出賠償與補償」。再下一代的阿那克西米尼提出了空氣：稀薄時變成火，凝聚時變成風、雲、水與石頭。在這裡，老師的觀點第一次被他的學生帶著理由公開地修正；而這種修正被當作進步。

The poet-philosopher Xenophanes, from another Ionian city, turned the new habit of criticism on the gods themselves. In Burnet’s translation: “if oxen and horses or lions had hands, and could paint with their hands, and produce works of art as men do, horses would paint the forms of the gods like horses, and oxen like oxen.” “The Ethiopians make their gods black and snub-nosed,” he added, and the Thracians give theirs blue eyes and red hair. The old phrase for all this is “from myth to reason,” and it needs care. Thales himself is reported to have said that “all things are full of gods,” the Milesians borrowed from Babylonian and Egyptian learning, and myth went on living beside philosophy for a thousand years. What changed was not that people stopped believing in gods. It was that a new kind of explanation appeared, one that could be argued with.

另一座愛奧尼亞城市的詩人哲學家色諾芬尼，把這種新的批評習慣轉向了神本身。Burnet 的英譯是：「如果牛、馬或獅子有手，能用手作畫，能像人一樣創作藝術品，馬會把神畫成馬的樣子，牛會把神畫成牛的樣子。」他又說：「衣索比亞人把他們的神畫成黑皮膚、塌鼻子」，色雷斯人則給他們的神藍眼睛、紅頭髮。對這一切的老說法是「從神話到理性」；而這個說法需要小心。據說泰利斯自己就說過「萬物充滿了神」；米利都人向巴比倫與埃及的學問借了很多；神話也在哲學旁邊繼續活了一千年。改變的不是人們不再相信神，而是出現了一種新的解釋：一種可以跟它爭論的解釋。

What this age changed · 這個時代改變了什麼

Three things that were different afterward 從此不一樣的三件事

  1. 1

    Nature explained by nature 用自然解釋自然

    Thunder and floods were no longer explained by a god’s mood but by what water, air, or the boundless does. The Greeks called this subject physis, nature, and the word survives in “physics.”

    打雷與洪水不再用神的心情來解釋，而是用水、空氣或無限定者做了什麼來解釋。希臘人稱這個主題為 physis（自然）；這個字留存在「物理學」（physics）裡。

  2. 2

    Criticism as a tradition 把批評變成傳統

    Anaximander corrected Thales and Anaximenes corrected Anaximander. A school in which students are expected to improve on their teacher, by giving reasons, is the seed of every later science and philosophy.

    阿那克西曼德修正了泰利斯，阿那克西米尼修正了阿那克西曼德。一個預期學生會帶著理由超越老師的學派，是後來一切科學與哲學的種子。

  3. 3

    The search for one principle 尋找一個本原

    The question of what single thing underlies the many, the archē, set the agenda for centuries. The atomists, Plato, and Aristotle all answered it in different ways, and the next lessons follow them.

    「是什麼單一的東西構成了眾多事物的基礎」這個關於 archē（本原）的問題，為往後幾個世紀定下了議題。原子論者、柏拉圖與亞里斯多德都用不同的方式回答它；接下來的幾課會跟著他們走。

Key people · 關鍵人物

The first Greek philosophers 最早的希臘哲學家

Miletus · 米利都

Thales 泰利斯c. 624–546 BCE

Counted by Aristotle as the founder of natural philosophy. Said that the principle of all things is water, and remembered for predicting an eclipse, cornering the olive presses, and falling into a well.

亞里斯多德把他算作自然哲學的創始者。他說萬物的本原是水；人們記得他預言了日蝕、壟斷了榨油坊，還掉進了井裡。

Miletus · 米利都

Anaximander 阿那克西曼德c. 610–546 BCE

Proposed the apeiron, the boundless, as the source of everything, and left the oldest surviving sentence of Greek philosophy. Also credited with an early map of the world.

提出 apeiron（無限定者）作為一切的來源，並留下了希臘哲學現存最古老的一句話。也被認為畫了早期的世界地圖。

Miletus · 米利都

Anaximenes 阿那克西米尼c. 586–526 BCE

Chose air, and explained the other substances by thinning and condensing. His idea that change in kind comes from change in density is one of the first physical mechanisms.

選擇了空氣，並用稀薄化與凝聚來解釋其他物質。他認為「種類的改變來自密度的改變」，這是最早的物理機制之一。

Colophon, later Sicily · 科洛封，後來到西西里

Xenophanes 色諾芬尼c. 570–478 BCE

A traveling poet who criticized the human-shaped gods of Homer and Hesiod and noticed that every people makes its gods in its own image.

一位雲遊的詩人；他批評荷馬與赫西俄德那些人形的神，並注意到每一個民族都照自己的形象造神。

Three fragments from the beginning 開端的三段殘篇

Almost nothing the Milesians wrote survives. What we have are reports by later writers and a handful of quotations. Read them as the Greeks themselves did: as clues.

米利都人寫的東西幾乎都沒有留下來。我們有的，是後來作者的轉述與少數幾句引文。像希臘人自己那樣讀它們：當作線索。

Advanced English · 高級英文

Eight words worth keeping 值得帶走的八個字

cosmology(n.)宇宙論

Hesiod’s cosmology was a family story of gods.

赫西俄德的宇宙論是一個眾神的家族故事。

principle(n.)本原；原理

Thales said that the principle of all things is water.

泰利斯說萬物的本原是水。

to attribute (to)(v.)歸於；認為是某人所說

Many sayings are attributed to Thales, but none survive in his own words.

許多話被歸於泰利斯，但沒有一句以他自己的文字留存。

to reconstruct(v.)重建

Historians reconstruct early Greek thought from later reports.

歷史學家從後來的轉述重建早期希臘思想。

speculative(adj.)思辨的；推測性的

The Milesian theories were speculative, but they invited argument.

米利都學派的理論是推測性的，但它們引來了爭論。

anecdote(n.)軼事

The anecdote of the olive presses shows Thales as a shrewd investor.

榨油坊的軼事，把泰利斯描繪成一位精明的投資人。

to condense(v.)凝聚；濃縮

Anaximenes said that air turns to water when it condenses.

阿那克西米尼說，空氣凝聚時會變成水。

anthropomorphic(adj.)擬人化的；人形的

Xenophanes mocked the anthropomorphic gods of Homer.

色諾芬尼嘲笑荷馬那些人形的神。

Write · 你怎麼想？

  1. Choose a natural event, such as an earthquake or a rainbow. Write one explanation in the style of Hesiod and one in the style of Anaximenes. What can you do with the second that you cannot do with the first? About 150 words.

    選一個自然現象，例如地震或彩虹。用赫西俄德的風格寫一個解釋，再用阿那克西米尼的風格寫一個。第二個解釋能做到、第一個做不到的是什麼？約 150 個字。

  2. Xenophanes said that every people makes its gods in its own image. Is this an argument against religion, or against a certain picture of the divine? Explain.

    色諾芬尼說，每一個民族都照自己的形象造神。這是一個反對宗教的論證，還是反對某一種神的圖像的論證？請說明。

  3. Think of a teacher you disagreed with, for good reasons. How did it feel, and what did you learn? Connect your answer to Anaximander and Thales.

    想一位你曾經帶著好理由不同意的老師。那是什麼感覺？你學到了什麼？把你的回答連到阿那克西曼德與泰利斯。
