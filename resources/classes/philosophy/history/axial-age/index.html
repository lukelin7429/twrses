B2 · The Story of Philosophy 哲學史 · Greece, India, China, c. 800–200 BCE

Three Beginnings at Once 三個地方同時開始

Confucius, the Buddha, and Heraclitus were alive at roughly the same time, and none of them knew the others existed. Within a few centuries, Greece, India, and China each produced teachers who questioned the old rituals and asked how a person should live. The philosopher Karl Jaspers called this period the Axial Age. This lesson asks what happened in the three places, what they had in common, and whether the pattern is real.
孔子、佛陀與赫拉克利特大約生活在同一個時代，而且他們誰也不知道別人的存在。在短短幾個世紀之內，希臘、印度與中國各自出現了一些老師，質疑舊有的儀式，並且追問一個人應該怎麼活。哲學家雅斯培稱這個時期為「軸心時代」。這一課問：三個地方各發生了什麼？它們有什麼共同點？這個模式是真的嗎？

The big idea · 大觀念

Between about 800 and 200 BCE, three civilizations that barely knew of one another each turned from inherited ritual toward reflection, and each produced teachers whose questions are still asked. Whether this was one event or three is still debated.

大約在西元前 800 年到 200 年之間，三個幾乎不知道彼此存在的文明，各自從承襲的儀式轉向反思，各自出現了一些老師；他們的問題至今仍有人在問。這是一件事還是三件事，至今仍有爭論。

The story · 課文

Three Beginnings at Once 三個地方同時開始

01A remarkable coincidence 一個驚人的巧合

In 1949 the German philosopher Karl Jaspers pointed out something that every student of world history eventually notices. In a few centuries around 500 BCE, and in three regions that had little or no contact with one another, people began to think about the world and themselves in a new way. In China there were Confucius and Laozi and, a little later, Mozi, Mencius, and Zhuangzi. In India there were the sages of the Upanishads, the Buddha, and Mahāvīra, the founder of Jainism. In Greece there were the Milesians of the last lesson, then Heraclitus, Parmenides, and finally Socrates and Plato. Jaspers called this period the Axial Age, the time on which later history turned as on an axis.

1949 年，德國哲學家雅斯培指出了一件每個學世界史的人遲早都會注意到的事。在西元前 500 年前後的幾個世紀裡，在三個彼此幾乎沒有接觸的地區，人們開始用一種新的方式思考世界與自己。在中國，有孔子與老子，稍晚有墨子、孟子與莊子。在印度，有奧義書的聖者、佛陀，以及耆那教的創立者大雄。在希臘，有上一課的米利都學派，然後是赫拉克利特、巴門尼德，最後是蘇格拉底與柏拉圖。雅斯培稱這個時期為「軸心時代」：後來的歷史，就像繞著一根軸那樣，繞著這段時期轉動。

What these people shared was not a set of doctrines. Their answers differed enormously. What they shared was a turn of attention. Older religion in all three places centered on ritual, the correct performance of sacrifices that kept the gods or ancestors favorable. The new teachers asked what lay behind the ritual and whether it mattered at all. They asked what a good person is, what the self is, why there is suffering, and what is real beneath appearances. They taught students, not only kings and priests, and their students argued with one another. In each place, an inherited way of life became something that could be examined.

這些人共享的不是一套學說；他們的答案差異極大。他們共享的是注意力的一次轉向。在三個地方，較古老的宗教都以儀式為中心：正確地舉行祭祀，好讓神或祖先保持眷顧。新的老師們追問儀式背後是什麼，以及儀式究竟重不重要。他們問：什麼是好人？什麼是自我？為什麼有苦？在表象底下什麼是真實的？他們教的是學生，不只是國王與祭司；而他們的學生彼此爭論。在每一個地方，一種承襲的生活方式，變成了可以被檢查的東西。

02Three places 三個地方

In India, the Vedic religion of fire sacrifices was already ancient. The Upanishads, composed by forest-dwelling teachers over several centuries, turned inward. The Katha Upanishad tells of a boy, Nachiketas, who is granted three wishes by Death himself and uses the third to ask, in Swami Paramananda’s translation, “There is this doubt regarding what becomes of a man after death. Some say he exists, others that he does not exist.” Death begs him to choose something else, and the boy refuses. The Buddha and Mahāvīra went further and rejected the authority of the Vedas altogether. In his first sermon, in Bhikkhu Sujato’s translation, the Buddha described a path between two extremes: “Indulgence in sensual pleasures, which is low,” and “indulgence in self-mortification, which is painful.”

在印度，以火祭為中心的吠陀宗教那時已經很古老了。奧義書由住在森林裡的老師們，在幾個世紀中陸續寫成；它們把目光轉向內在。《卡塔奧義書》講一個男孩納奇凱塔斯的故事：死神親自給了他三個願望，他用第三個願望問（依 Swami Paramananda 的英譯）：「關於人死後會怎樣，有這樣一個疑惑：有人說他存在，有人說他不存在。」死神求他選別的，男孩拒絕了。佛陀與大雄走得更遠，完全拒絕了吠陀的權威。佛陀在他的初轉法輪裡（依 Sujato 比丘的英譯），描述了一條介於兩個極端之間的道路：「沉溺於感官的享樂，那是低下的」，以及「沉溺於自我折磨，那是痛苦的」。

In China, the Zhou dynasty’s rituals had kept order for centuries, but by Confucius’ time the Zhou kings were powerless and the states fought one another. Confucius did not reject ritual. He asked what made it worth keeping, and found the answer in human virtue. When his student Zigong asked for one word to guide a whole life, Confucius replied, in James Legge’s translation, “Is not RECIPROCITY such a word? What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others.” In Greece, the old poets’ gods were doubted by Xenophanes, and Heraclitus taught that “this world, which is the same for all, no one of gods or men has made; but it was ever, is now, and ever shall be an ever-living Fire.” Three different idioms, ritual reformed, ritual transcended, ritual replaced by nature, all expressed one change. People were now looking for an order they could understand.

在中國，周朝的禮已經維持秩序好幾個世紀；但到了孔子的時代，周天子已無實權，諸侯互相攻伐。孔子並沒有拒絕禮。他追問是什麼使禮值得保存，並在人的德行裡找到答案。當學生子貢問：有沒有一個字可以終身奉行？孔子回答：「其恕乎！己所不欲，勿施於人。」在希臘，詩人們的神被色諾芬尼懷疑；赫拉克利特則教導：「這個對一切都相同的世界，不是任何神或人所造的；它過去一直是、現在是、將來也永遠是一團永恆的活火。」三種不同的語言：改革禮、超越禮、用自然取代禮；都表達了同一個改變：人們現在要尋找一種他們能夠理解的秩序。

03Coincidence or pattern? 巧合，還是模式？

Why then, and why there? Historians have offered several explanations. All three regions were becoming richer and more urban. The Lydians, Herodotus reports, “were the first of men, so far as we know, who struck and used coin of gold or silver,” and coinage appeared in India and China in the same centuries. Trade created merchants who traveled, compared customs, and had money for teachers. All three regions were also politically broken into rival states, the Greek cities, the Indian kingdoms of the Ganges plain, and the warring states of China. Rival courts competed for advisers, and a teacher who was unwelcome in one state could move to another. Wealth, writing, travel, and political competition together may have made room for people whose job was to think.

那為什麼是那個時候、那些地方？歷史學家提出了幾種解釋。三個地區都在變得更富裕、更城市化。希羅多德記載，呂底亞人「據我們所知，是最早鑄造並使用金幣或銀幣的人」；而錢幣在同樣的幾個世紀裡，也出現在印度與中國。貿易造就了到處旅行、比較風俗、並且有錢供養老師的商人。三個地區在政治上也都分裂成彼此競爭的國家：希臘的城邦、恆河平原上的印度諸國、中國的戰國。互相競爭的朝廷爭相延攬謀士；一位在某國不受歡迎的老師，可以到另一國去。財富、文字、旅行與政治競爭，或許合起來，為那些以思考為職業的人騰出了空間。

Some scholars doubt the whole idea. The “age” stretches over six hundred years, which is long for a coincidence. Important thinkers, such as Zarathustra in Iran and the Hebrew prophets, fit it only awkwardly, and earlier civilizations, Egypt and Mesopotamia, had wisdom literature of their own. The concept may say more about how modern Europeans wanted to see world history than about the ancient world. These are fair objections, and this course treats the Axial Age as a useful lens, not a law of history. Even so, the lens shows something true. The questions that still define philosophy, how to live, what is real, how we can know, what we owe one another, were first asked in recognizably modern form in these three places, at roughly the same time, by people who had never heard of one another.

有些學者懷疑整個觀念。這個「時代」橫跨六百年，對一個巧合來說太長了。一些重要的思想家，例如伊朗的瑣羅亞斯德與希伯來的先知，只能勉強放進這個框架；而更早的文明，埃及與美索不達米亞，也有它們自己的智慧文學。這個概念可能更多地反映了現代歐洲人想怎麼看世界史，而不是古代世界本身。這些反駁都是公道的；這門課把軸心時代當作一個有用的鏡頭，而不是歷史的定律。即便如此，這個鏡頭照出了一些真實的東西。至今仍界定著哲學的那些問題（怎麼活、什麼是真實的、我們怎麼知道、我們虧欠彼此什麼），最早是在這三個地方、大約同一個時候，由一些從來沒聽說過彼此的人，以今天還認得出來的形式提出的。

What this age changed · 這個時代改變了什麼

Three things that were different afterward 從此不一樣的三件事

  1. 1

    Ritual became a question 儀式變成了一個問題

    In all three places, inherited ritual was no longer simply performed. It was reformed by Confucius, transcended by the Buddha and the Upanishads, and replaced by nature in Greece. Each time someone asked what it was for.

    在三個地方，承襲的儀式都不再只是照做：孔子改革它，佛陀與奧義書超越它，希臘用自然取代它。每一次，都有人問：它是為了什麼？

  2. 2

    Ethics became universal 倫理變成普遍的

    Confucius’ reciprocity, the Buddha’s compassion for all beings, and later the Stoic idea of a world citizen all reach beyond one’s own clan or city. Rules began to be justified by reasons that apply to everyone.

    孔子的「恕」、佛陀對一切眾生的慈悲，以及後來斯多噶的「世界公民」觀念，都超出了自己的宗族或城邦。規則開始以適用於每一個人的理由來證成。

  3. 3

    The independent teacher appeared 獨立的老師出現了

    Teachers who were neither priests nor officials gathered students, traveled between states, and founded schools that outlived them. The Academy, the Confucian school, and the Buddhist sangha are all products of this age.

    既不是祭司、也不是官員的老師，聚集學生、在各國之間遊走，並創立了比他們自己更長命的學派。柏拉圖的學院、儒家的學派、佛教的僧團，都是這個時代的產物。

Key people · 關鍵人物

Four teachers who never met 從未見過面的四位老師

State of Lu, China · 中國魯國

Confucius 孔子551–479 BCE (traditional)

A minor official and teacher who wanted to restore the virtue behind Zhou ritual. His sayings, collected by students as the Analects, shaped East Asian ethics and education for two thousand years.

一位小官與老師，想要恢復周禮背後的德行。他的話由學生編成《論語》，塑造了東亞兩千年的倫理與教育。

The Ganges plain, India · 印度恆河平原

The Buddha 佛陀c. 480–400 BCE (one scholarly dating)

Siddhattha Gotama left a comfortable life, practiced severe austerities, rejected them, and taught a middle way. His four noble truths treat suffering as a problem with a cause and a cure.

悉達多・喬達摩離開了舒適的生活，修了極嚴苛的苦行，又放棄了它，然後教導一條中道。他的四聖諦，把苦當作一個有原因、也有解藥的問題。

Videha, India · 印度毘提訶

Yājñavalkya 耶若婆佉c. 700s BCE

The great debater of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upanishad, who taught that the true self cannot be described by any positive term and can only be indicated by saying “not this, not this.”

《廣林奧義書》裡偉大的辯論者；他教導：真正的自我無法用任何正面的語詞描述，只能以「不是這個，不是這個」來指出。

Ephesus, Ionia · 愛奧尼亞的以弗所

Heraclitus 赫拉克利特c. 535–475 BCE

A proud and obscure writer who taught that the world is an ever-living fire, always changing according to measure, and that most people fail to grasp the order, the logos, that runs through everything.

一位高傲而晦澀的作者；他教導：世界是一團永恆的活火，按照尺度不斷變化；而大多數人沒能掌握貫穿一切的那個秩序，logos。

Advanced English · 高級英文

Eight words worth keeping 值得帶走的八個字

axial(adj.)軸心的

Jaspers called it the Axial Age because later history turned on it.

雅斯培稱它為軸心時代，因為後來的歷史繞著它轉。

ritual(n.)儀式；禮

Confucius reformed ritual instead of rejecting it.

孔子改革禮，而不是拒絕禮。

reciprocity(n.)互惠；恕

Legge translated shu as “reciprocity.”

Legge 把「恕」譯成 reciprocity。

austerity(n.)苦行；嚴苛的自律

The Buddha practiced severe austerities before rejecting them.

佛陀在放棄苦行之前，曾修過極嚴苛的苦行。

to transcend(v.)超越

The Upanishads sought a self that transcends ritual.

奧義書尋求一個超越儀式的自我。

urbanization(n.)城市化

Urbanization brought merchants who could pay teachers.

城市化帶來了付得起老師的商人。

a lens(n.)鏡頭；觀察的角度

This course treats the Axial Age as a lens, not a law.

這門課把軸心時代當作一個鏡頭，而不是一條定律。

to fit awkwardly(v. phr.)勉強放進（框架）

Zarathustra fits the Axial Age only awkwardly.

瑣羅亞斯德只能勉強放進軸心時代的框架。

Write · 你怎麼想？

  1. Pick two of the three regions. Write a short imaginary conversation between a teacher from each, in which they discover what they agree on and where they differ. About 200 words.

    從三個地區中挑兩個。寫一段想像的對話：兩地各一位老師，發現他們同意什麼、在哪裡不同。約 200 個字。

  2. Is “What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others” a good rule for a whole life? Give one case where it works well and one where it may not be enough.

    「己所不欲，勿施於人」是一條好的終身規則嗎？舉一個它很管用的例子，再舉一個它可能不夠的例子。

  3. Scholars disagree about whether the Axial Age was one event or three. Which view do you find more convincing, and why?

    學者們對軸心時代是一件事還是三件事意見不一。你覺得哪一種看法比較有說服力？為什麼？
