B3 · The Story of Philosophy 哲學史 · Greece and southern Italy, c. 540–370 BCE

What Is Everything Made Of? 萬物是什麼做的？

After the Milesians, Greek thinkers offered one bold answer after another. Everything is number. Everything flows. Nothing changes at all. Everything is four roots moved by Love and Strife. Everything is atoms and the void. This lesson follows the argument from Pythagoras to Democritus, and shows how a single challenge from Parmenides forced everyone after him to think harder.
在米利都學派之後，希臘的思想家提出了一個又一個大膽的答案：萬物是數。萬物流變。根本沒有任何東西改變。萬物是四種根源，被愛與爭推動。萬物是原子與虛空。這一課從畢達哥拉斯走到德謨克利特，並顯示巴門尼德的一個挑戰，怎麼迫使他之後的每一個人想得更深。

The big idea · 大觀念

The early Greek debate about what the world is made of was driven less by observation than by argument. Parmenides showed that change seems impossible, and every later theory, including atomism, was an attempt to rescue change from his logic.

早期希臘關於「世界是什麼做的」的辯論，推動它的與其說是觀察，不如說是論證。巴門尼德顯示：變化似乎是不可能的；而後來的每一套理論，包括原子論，都是想把變化從他的邏輯裡救出來。

The story · 課文

What Is Everything Made Of? 萬物是什麼做的？

01Number and flux 數與流變

Pythagoras moved from Samos to southern Italy around 530 BCE and founded a community that was part school and part religious order. Its members discovered that musical harmony depends on simple ratios of string lengths, and they generalized boldly. In W. D. Ross’s translation of Aristotle’s Metaphysics, the Pythagoreans “thought its principles were the principles of all things,” meaning the principles of mathematics, because “in numbers they seemed to see many resemblances to the things that exist.” The Milesians had looked for a stuff. The Pythagoreans looked for a structure. It was the first appearance of an idea that modern physics still takes seriously, that the world is written in mathematics.

畢達哥拉斯大約在西元前 530 年從薩摩斯島遷到義大利南部，創立了一個半是學校、半是宗教團體的社群。它的成員發現：音樂的和諧取決於弦長的簡單比例；而他們大膽地把這一點推廣。在 W. D. Ross 翻譯的亞里斯多德《形上學》裡，畢達哥拉斯學派「認為它的原理就是萬物的原理」（指數學的原理），因為「在數裡，他們似乎看到了許多與存在之物相似之處」。米利都人尋找的是一種質料；畢達哥拉斯學派尋找的是一種結構。這是一個至今現代物理學仍然認真看待的觀念第一次出現：世界是用數學寫成的。

Heraclitus of Ephesus, writing around 500 BCE, made change itself the key. In John Burnet’s translation: “You cannot step twice into the same rivers; for fresh waters are ever flowing in upon you.” For Heraclitus the world is an ever-living fire, and its stability is like the stability of a flame or a river, a pattern kept up by constant change. He also thought opposites depend on each other. “Cold things become warm, and what is warm cools; what is wet dries, and the parched is moisted.” When Homer prayed that strife might perish, Heraclitus said, he did not see that he was praying for the destruction of the universe. The lesson on the river in the Big Questions shelf takes up his puzzle in detail.

以弗所的赫拉克利特，大約在西元前 500 年寫作，把變化本身當作關鍵。John Burnet 的英譯是：「你不能兩次踏進同樣的河流；因為新的水不斷流向你。」對赫拉克利特來說，世界是一團永恆的活火；它的穩定，就像火焰或河流的穩定：一個由不斷變化所維持的模式。他也認為對立面彼此依存：「冷的變暖，暖的變冷；濕的變乾，乾的變濕。」他說，荷馬祈求紛爭從世上消失的時候，並沒有看到他是在祈求宇宙的毀滅。大哉問書架裡講河流的那一課，會詳細處理他的難題。

02Parmenides’ challenge 巴門尼德的挑戰

Parmenides of Elea, in southern Italy, wrote a poem in which a goddess teaches him “the only two ways of search that can be thought of.” In Burnet’s translation, the first is “that It is, and that it is impossible for it not to be.” The second, that “It is not,” is “a path that none can learn of at all. For thou canst not know what is not—that is impossible—nor utter it; for it is the same thing that can be thought and that can be.” From this he drew startling conclusions. If what is not cannot be, then nothing can come into being, since it would have to come from nothing. Nothing can perish, since it would have to become nothing. There can be no empty space, no motion, and no real plurality. The world of change that our senses show us is an illusion.

義大利南部埃利亞的巴門尼德，寫了一首詩；詩中一位女神教導他「唯一可以設想的兩條探究之路」。Burnet 的英譯是：第一條是「它存在，而且它不可能不存在」。第二條，「它不存在」，是「一條任何人都無法學會的路。因為你無法認識不存在的東西（那是不可能的），也無法說出它；因為能被思想的與能存在的，是同一個東西」。他從這裡推出驚人的結論。如果不存在的東西不能存在，那麼沒有任何東西能夠生成，因為它必須從無中來。沒有任何東西能夠消滅，因為它必須變成無。不可能有空的空間、不可能有運動、不可能有真正的多。我們的感官所顯示的這個變化的世界，是一個幻覺。

This was the first sustained argument in Western philosophy, and it was purely logical. Parmenides did not appeal to observation. He followed a premise to its conclusion and accepted the conclusion even though it contradicted everything we see. His student Zeno defended him with paradoxes, such as Achilles who can never catch the tortoise, designed to show that motion leads to absurdity, and the lesson on Zeno’s paradoxes examines them. Most philosophers since have rejected Parmenides’ conclusion. But they could not simply ignore his argument. If you want change, you have to show where the reasoning goes wrong, or find a way for things to change without anything coming from nothing.

這是西方哲學裡第一個持續展開的論證，而且是純粹邏輯的。巴門尼德沒有訴諸觀察。他把一個前提推到它的結論，並且接受了這個結論，即使它與我們所見的一切相矛盾。他的學生芝諾用悖論來為他辯護，例如阿基里斯永遠追不上烏龜；這些悖論是設計來顯示運動會導致荒謬的；講芝諾悖論的那一課檢查過它們。此後大多數哲學家都拒絕巴門尼德的結論。但他們不能就這樣忽略他的論證。如果你想要變化，你就必須指出這個推理錯在哪裡，或者找到一種方式，讓事物能夠改變，而又沒有任何東西從無中生出。

03Roots, seeds, and atoms 根源、種子與原子

The next generation took the second route. Empedocles of Acragas, in Sicily, accepted that nothing comes from nothing, but said there are four eternal “roots,” which we call earth, water, air, and fire. In Burnet’s translation: “Hear first the four roots of all things.” They never come into being or perish. They only mix and separate, moved by two forces, Love and Strife. “At one time all the limbs that are the body’s portion are brought together by Love,” and at another they are “severed by cruel Strife.” Anaxagoras of Clazomenae, who brought philosophy to Athens, went further. Every kind of stuff is in everything, in tiny portions: “All things were together, infinite both in number and in smallness.” What sets the mixture moving is Nous, mind, which “is infinite and self-ruled, and is mixed with nothing.”

下一代走了第二條路。西西里阿克拉加斯的恩培多克勒接受「沒有任何東西從無中生出」，卻說有四種永恆的「根源」，也就是我們說的土、水、氣、火。Burnet 的英譯是：「先聽萬物的四種根源。」它們永遠不生成、也不消滅；它們只是混合與分離，由兩種力量推動：愛與爭。「有時，所有屬於身體的肢體被愛聚集在一起」；有時，它們「被殘酷的爭撕裂」。把哲學帶到雅典的克拉佐美尼人阿那克薩哥拉，走得更遠：每一種質料都在每一樣東西裡，以極微小的分量存在：「萬物曾經在一起，在數量上與微小上都是無限的。」使這個混合開始運動的，是 Nous，心靈；它「是無限的、自主的，不與任何東西混合」。

The most lasting answer came from Leucippus and his student Democritus of Abdera. In Ross’s translation, Aristotle reports that they “say that the full and the empty are the elements, calling the one being and the other non-being.” The full is made of atoms, from the Greek for “uncuttable,” tiny solid bodies that differ only in shape, arrangement, and position. The empty is the void in which they move. Atoms never come into being or perish, which satisfies Parmenides, but they move and combine, which rescues change. Everything else, color, taste, warmth, exists only by convention. The atomists were not doing experiments, and their atoms are not the atoms of chemistry. But the idea that the visible world is made of invisible particles in empty space, governed by necessity and not by purpose, would return two thousand years later as the foundation of modern science.

最持久的答案，來自留基伯與他的學生、阿布德拉的德謨克利特。在 Ross 的英譯裡，亞里斯多德記載他們「說充實與虛空是元素，稱前者為存在，後者為非存在」。充實是由原子構成的；「原子」來自希臘文的「不可切割的」：極微小的固體，只在形狀、排列與位置上不同。虛空是它們在其中運動的空間。原子永遠不生成、也不消滅，這滿足了巴門尼德；但它們會運動、會結合，這救回了變化。其他一切，顏色、味道、溫暖，都只是依約定而存在。原子論者並沒有做實驗，他們的原子也不是化學的原子。但「看得見的世界，是由空間中看不見的粒子所構成，受必然性而非目的所支配」這個觀念，在兩千年後會回來，成為現代科學的基礎。

What this age changed · 這個時代改變了什麼

Three things that were different afterward 從此不一樣的三件事

  1. 1

    Argument could overrule the senses 論證可以推翻感官

    Parmenides showed that a chain of reasoning could reach a conclusion that contradicts everything we see, and that the conclusion must be answered with better reasoning, not with a shrug. Philosophy has argued this way ever since.

    巴門尼德顯示：一連串的推理，可以得出一個與我們所見的一切相矛盾的結論；而這個結論必須用更好的推理來回應，而不是聳聳肩帶過。從此以後，哲學一直是這樣論證的。

  2. 2

    Nothing comes from nothing 無中不能生有

    After Parmenides, every serious theory kept some basic stuff that is never created or destroyed. The same principle reappears in the modern laws of conservation of matter and energy.

    在巴門尼德之後，每一套認真的理論，都保留了某種永遠不被創造、也不被毀滅的基本質料。同樣的原則，重新出現在現代的物質與能量守恆定律裡。

  3. 3

    Matter and mind were separated 物質與心靈被分開了

    Anaxagoras’ Nous, a mind that orders matter but is mixed with nothing, was the first clear distinction between mind and matter. The atomists went the other way, explaining everything by bodies in motion. The two options still divide philosophers of mind.

    阿那克薩哥拉的 Nous，一個安排物質卻不與任何東西混合的心靈，是心與物之間第一次清楚的區分。原子論者走了相反的方向，用運動中的物體解釋一切。這兩個選項，至今仍把心靈哲學家分成兩派。

Key people · 關鍵人物

Five answers to one question 一個問題的五個答案

Samos, then Croton in Italy · 薩摩斯，後到義大利的克羅頓

Pythagoras 畢達哥拉斯c. 570–495 BCE

Founder of a community that studied number, music, and the soul. His followers held that the principles of mathematics are the principles of all things.

一個研究數、音樂與靈魂的社群的創立者。他的追隨者主張：數學的原理就是萬物的原理。

Elea, southern Italy · 義大利南部的埃利亞

Parmenides 巴門尼德c. 515–450 BCE

Argued in a poem that what is not cannot be, and concluded that change, motion, and plurality are illusions. His argument set the problem for everyone after him.

在一首詩裡論證：不存在的東西不能存在；並得出結論：變化、運動與多都是幻覺。他的論證為之後的每一個人設定了問題。

Acragas, Sicily · 西西里的阿克拉加斯

Empedocles 恩培多克勒c. 494–434 BCE

Proposed four eternal roots, earth, water, air, and fire, mixed and separated by Love and Strife. The four elements lasted in European science until the seventeenth century.

提出四種永恆的根源：土、水、氣、火，由愛與爭使它們混合與分離。四元素說在歐洲科學裡一直延續到十七世紀。

Clazomenae, then Athens · 克拉佐美尼，後到雅典

Anaxagoras 阿那克薩哥拉c. 500–428 BCE

Brought philosophy to Athens and taught that a portion of everything is in everything, set in motion by Nous, mind. He was prosecuted for saying the sun is a hot stone.

把哲學帶到雅典；他教導：萬物之中都有萬物的一部分，由 Nous（心靈）使之運動。他因為說太陽是一塊熾熱的石頭而被起訴。

Abdera, Thrace · 色雷斯的阿布德拉

Democritus 德謨克利特c. 460–370 BCE

With his teacher Leucippus, explained everything as atoms moving in the void. Colors and tastes, he said, exist by convention, and only atoms and the void exist in truth.

與他的老師留基伯一起，把一切解釋為在虛空中運動的原子。他說，顏色與味道是依約定而存在的；在真實中只有原子與虛空。

Advanced English · 高級英文

Eight words worth keeping 值得帶走的八個字

flux(n.)流變；不斷的變化

Heraclitus saw the world as a state of constant flux.

赫拉克利特把世界看成不斷流變的狀態。

plurality(n.)多；多樣性

Parmenides denied that real plurality exists.

巴門尼德否認真正的多存在。

illusion(n.)幻覺

For Parmenides, change was an illusion of the senses.

對巴門尼德來說，變化是感官的幻覺。

to rescue(v.)救回；挽救

The atomists rescued change without breaking Parmenides’ rule.

原子論者在不違反巴門尼德規則的情況下，救回了變化。

void(n.)虛空

Atoms move through the void.

原子在虛空中運動。

indivisible(adj.)不可分割的

The word “atom” means indivisible, or uncuttable.

「原子」這個字的意思是不可分割的。

conservation(n.)守恆

The conservation of matter echoes the rule that nothing comes from nothing.

物質守恆呼應了「無中不能生有」的規則。

by convention(phr.)依約定；按習慣

Democritus said that sweet and bitter exist by convention.

德謨克利特說，甜與苦是依約定而存在的。

Write · 你怎麼想？

  1. Parmenides followed an argument to a conclusion that contradicts experience. When, if ever, should we trust an argument over what we see? About 150 words.

    巴門尼德把一個論證推到一個與經驗相矛盾的結論。什麼時候（如果有的話），我們應該相信論證勝過相信眼睛所見？約 150 個字。

  2. Choose one ancient answer, number, flux, four roots, seeds and mind, or atoms, and explain what modern science kept from it and what it dropped.

    選一個古代的答案（數、流變、四根源、種子與心靈，或原子），說明現代科學保留了它的什麼、丟掉了什麼。

  3. Anaxagoras separated mind from matter; the atomists explained everything by matter. Which approach seems more promising to you, and why?

    阿那克薩哥拉把心靈從物質中分開；原子論者用物質解釋一切。你覺得哪一種方法比較有希望？為什麼？
