A6 · Knowledge 知識

Is Seeing Believing? 眼見為憑嗎？

Plato’s cave: prisoners who take shadows for reality, one who is dragged into the sun, and the question of what it would mean to see things as they are.
柏拉圖的洞穴：把影子當成實在的囚徒，一個被拖到陽光下的人，以及這個問題——「如實地看見事物」到底是什麼意思。

A6 Big Questions · 大哉問Knowledge · 知識Advanced · 高級1,111 words · about 30 min
The big idea · 大觀念

What the senses show us may stand to reality as a shadow stands to the thing that casts it. If so, education is a turning of the whole person toward what is real, and it will be resisted.

感官呈現給我們的東西，跟實在的關係，也許就像影子跟投下影子的東西。如果是這樣，教育就是把整個人轉向真實的那個過程，而這個過程會遭到抗拒。

The essay · 課文

Is Seeing Believing? 眼見為憑嗎？

01The trouble with seeing 「看見」的麻煩

“Seeing is believing” is a proverb about evidence, and as a rule of thumb it is a good one. Yet the things we see are a strange guide to how the world is. The sun looks about the size of a coin and appears to cross the sky. A straight stick looks bent in water. The night sky shows stars that may have burned out long ago. In each case we have learned to correct what we see by what we understand. Plato asked how far that correction might go. Suppose that everything the senses show us is related to reality in the way that a picture of a thing is related to the thing. He put the thought in the form of a story, told by Socrates in Book VII of the Republic, and it has become the best-known image in philosophy.

「眼見為憑」是一句關於證據的俗話；當作經驗法則，它是不錯的。可是我們看見的東西，對世界實際的樣子來說，是個古怪的嚮導。太陽看起來跟硬幣差不多大，而且好像橫越天空。筆直的棍子插進水裡看起來是彎的。夜空裡顯現的星星，也許早就燃盡了。在每一種情形裡，我們都學會了用「理解到的」去修正「看見的」。柏拉圖問的是：這種修正可以走到多遠？假設感官呈現給我們的一切，跟實在的關係，就像一樣東西的圖像跟那樣東西本身的關係。他把這個想法寫成一個故事，由蘇格拉底在《理想國》第七卷說出來；這個故事成了哲學裡最廣為人知的意象。

02The prisoners 囚徒

Picture people living in an underground cave whose long entrance is open to the daylight. “Here they have been from their childhood, and have their legs and necks chained so that they cannot move, and can only see before them.” Behind and above them a fire is burning. Between the fire and the prisoners runs a raised path with a low wall along it, like the screen at a puppet show, and along the path people carry statues and figures of animals, which stick up above the wall. The firelight throws the shadows of these figures on the wall of the cave in front of the prisoners.

想像有一群人住在地下的洞穴裡，洞穴有一條長長的通道通向日光。「他們從小就在這裡，腿和脖子都被鍊住，所以不能移動，只能看見前面。」在他們背後的高處，有一堆火在燒。火與囚徒之間有一條墊高的路，路邊有一道矮牆，就像木偶戲的屏幕；有人沿著這條路，搬運人像與動物的造像，這些東西高出矮牆。火光把這些造像的影子，投在囚徒面前的洞壁上。

The prisoners have never seen anything else. They give the shadows names, and they honor whoever is quickest at predicting which shadow will come next. If the carriers speak, the echo seems to come from the shadows. “To them,” Socrates says, “the truth would be literally nothing but the shadows of the images.” His companion remarks that these are strange prisoners. Socrates answers: “Like ourselves.”

囚徒從來沒有見過別的東西。他們替影子取名字，並且推崇最會預測下一個影子的人。如果搬運的人說話，回聲聽起來就像是從影子發出來的。蘇格拉底說：「對他們來說，真實簡直就只是那些造像的影子。」他的同伴說，這些囚徒真奇怪。蘇格拉底回答：「跟我們一樣。」

03The ascent 上升

Now suppose one prisoner is released and forced to stand up and turn around. The movement hurts, and the fire dazzles him. Told that what he sees now is more real than the shadows, he does not believe it, for the shadows were clear and these shapes are a blur. If he is then dragged up the steep, rough passage into the sunlight, he is at first unable to see anything at all. His eyes adjust by stages. He can look first at shadows, then at reflections in water, then at things themselves, then at the moon and stars by night. “Last of all he will be able to see the sun, and not mere reflections of him in the water.” He comes to understand that the sun produces the seasons and governs everything in the visible world, and that it is in some way the cause of all that he and his companions used to see.

現在假設有一個囚徒被鬆開，被強迫站起來、轉過身去。這個動作讓他疼痛，火光讓他目眩。有人告訴他，他現在看到的比影子更真實；他不相信，因為影子是清楚的，而這些形體一片模糊。如果接著他被人拖上那條陡峭崎嶇的通道，拉到陽光下，他起初什麼也看不見。他的眼睛是一階一階適應的。他先能看影子，然後是水中的倒影，然後是事物本身，然後是夜裡的月亮與星星。「最後他將能夠看見太陽，而不只是太陽在水中的倒影。」他終於明白，是太陽造成四季、主宰可見世界裡的一切，而且在某種意義上，它是他與同伴們過去所見一切的原因。

04What the picture means 這幅圖像的意思

Socrates explains the image himself. “The prison-house is the world of sight, the light of the fire is the sun,” and the journey upward is “the ascent of the soul into the intellectual world.” So the cave is the whole world that our senses reveal, and the world outside stands for what can be grasped only by thinking. The things out there are what Plato calls Forms: justice itself, beauty itself, the triangle that geometry studies, as opposed to this or that just act, beautiful face, or chalk drawing. The sun stands for the highest of them, the Form of the Good, which, he says, “appears last of all, and is seen only with an effort.”

蘇格拉底自己解釋了這個意象。「牢房就是視覺的世界，火的光就是太陽」，而向上的旅程是「靈魂上升到理智的世界」。所以洞穴就是感官向我們揭示的整個世界，洞外的世界則代表只有靠思考才能掌握的東西。外面的那些事物，就是柏拉圖所說的「理型」：正義本身、美本身、幾何學研究的那個三角形，而不是這個或那個正義的行為、美麗的臉孔、粉筆畫的圖。太陽代表其中最高的一個，「善的理型」；他說，它「最後才出現，而且要費力才看得見」。

Why believe in such things? Plato’s main reason concerns knowledge. Whatever we perceive is changing, and it has its qualities only more or less: two sticks are equal from here and unequal from there, and a law is just in one city and unjust in another. Yet we do seem to know what equality and justice are, well enough to see that the sticks and the laws fall short. Plato concludes that knowledge must have objects of its own, stable and perfect, which particular things only resemble.

為什麼要相信有這種東西？柏拉圖的主要理由跟知識有關。我們感知到的一切都在變，而且它們具有某種性質，總是只到某個程度：兩根棍子從這裡看相等，從那裡看不等；一條法律在這個城邦是正義的，在另一個城邦是不義的。然而我們似乎確實知道相等與正義是什麼，而且知道得夠清楚，才看得出那些棍子與法律有所不足。柏拉圖的結論是：知識必定有它自己的對象，穩定而完美，個別事物只是與它們相似。

05The return 回來

The story does not end in the sunlight. The freed prisoner remembers the others and pities them, and he goes back down. His eyes, used to the sun, are now useless in the dark. He does badly at the shadow contests. “Men would say of him that up he went and down he came without his eyes,” and that the journey is not worth making. If anyone tried to free them and lead them up, Socrates adds, they would kill him if they could. Plato’s first readers knew whom he meant. Athens had put Socrates to death some twenty years before.

故事並沒有在陽光下結束。被釋放的囚徒想起其他人，憐憫他們，於是走回洞裡。他的眼睛習慣了太陽，現在在黑暗裡毫無用處。他在猜影子的比賽裡表現很差。「人們會說，他上去一趟，回來眼睛就壞了」，並且說這趟旅程根本不值得走。蘇格拉底又說，如果有人想解開他們、帶他們上去，他們要是辦得到，會把那個人殺掉。柏拉圖最早的讀者知道他指的是誰。雅典在大約二十年前處死了蘇格拉底。

The return is also a duty. In Plato’s ideal city, those who have seen the Good are not allowed to stay above. “They must be made to descend again among the prisoners in the den, and partake of their labours and honours.” They would prefer to stay in the light, and Plato argues that this is exactly what fits them to govern.

回去同時也是一項責任。在柏拉圖的理想城邦裡，見過「善」的人不准留在上面。「必須讓他們再下到洞穴的囚徒當中，分擔他們的勞苦與榮譽。」他們寧可留在光裡；柏拉圖論證說，正是這一點，使他們適合治理城邦。

06A theory of education 一套教育理論

Before he leaves the image, Socrates draws a conclusion about teaching that every teacher should know. Education is not what some people say it is, putting knowledge into a soul that lacks it, “like sight into blind eyes.” The power to learn “exists in the soul already.” What is needed is a turning around: “just as the eye was unable to turn from darkness to light without the whole body, so too the instrument of knowledge can only by the movement of the whole soul be turned from the world of becoming into that of being.” On this view a teacher transmits nothing. The teacher’s work is to arrange things so that the student turns and looks. The turning is uncomfortable, and the student may well resent the person who causes it.

在離開這個意象之前，蘇格拉底引出了一個關於教學的結論，每一位老師都該知道。教育並不是某些人說的那樣，把知識放進缺少知識的靈魂裡，「像把視力放進瞎了的眼睛」。學習的能力「本來就在靈魂之中」。需要的是一次轉向：「正如眼睛不連著整個身體就無法從黑暗轉向光明，認識的工具也只有藉著整個靈魂的運動，才能從生成變化的世界轉向存有的世界。」照這個看法，老師什麼也沒有傳遞。老師的工作是把事情安排好，讓學生轉過身去看。這個轉身並不舒服，而學生很可能怨恨促成它的那個人。

07Is seeing believing? 那麼，眼見為憑嗎？

Few philosophers now accept Plato’s Forms. His own pupil Aristotle argued that the form of a thing exists in the thing and cannot be found in a separate realm. But the cave has outlived the theory it was built to illustrate, because the situation it describes keeps recurring. People do mistake the images of things for things. Much of what anyone believes about the wider world was seen on a screen, arranged by people who remain out of sight behind a wall. And the story makes a point about method that does not depend on any theory of Forms. From inside the cave, the shadows are clear and the truth is a painful blur. So the vividness of an experience is no measure of its reality. Sight is good evidence, in other words, and it still has to answer to understanding.

現在很少有哲學家接受柏拉圖的理型。他自己的學生亞里斯多德就論證過，事物的形式存在於事物之中，在另一個分離的領域裡是找不到的。可是洞穴比它原本要說明的理論活得更久，因為它描述的處境不斷重現。人確實會把事物的影像誤認為事物。任何人對更大的世界所相信的事，有許多是在螢幕上看到的，而安排這些影像的人，始終躲在牆後、不在視線之內。而且這個故事提出了一個關於方法的論點，這個論點並不依賴任何理型論。從洞穴裡面看，影子是清楚的，真相是一片刺痛的模糊。所以一個經驗有多鮮明，並不能衡量它有多真實。換句話說，看見是好的證據，但它仍然得向理解負責。

Republic 514a–515a

The whole cave 整個洞穴

A cutaway view. Follow the light from right to left: the fire, the figures carried above the low wall, the prisoners facing away, and the back wall where the shadows fall. The way out is the long slope on the right.

剖面全景。順著光從右往左看：火、矮牆上方被搬運的造像、背對著的囚徒，以及影子落下的洞底牆面。出路是右邊那條長長的斜坡。

Plato matches the journey to four states of mind, the “divided line” of Book VI: imagination, belief, reasoning, and understanding.

柏拉圖把這趟旅程對應到四種心靈狀態，也就是第六卷的「線喻」：想像、信念、推理、理解。

515a–c

The shadows 影子

This is all a prisoner ever sees. The shapes cross the wall in a regular order, and a clever prisoner learns to predict them. Nothing here suggests that they are shadows of anything.

囚徒一輩子看到的就只有這些。形影以固定的次序掠過牆面，聰明的囚徒學會預測它們。這裡沒有任何東西暗示：它們是別的東西的影子。

Imagination (eikasia): taking images for the things themselves.

想像（eikasia）：把影像當成事物本身。

515c–e

The fire 火

Released and made to turn, he sees the wall, the figures, and the fire. The glare hurts, and the real figures look less clear to him than their shadows did. He wants to turn back.

被鬆開、被迫轉身之後，他看見了矮牆、造像與火。強光刺痛他；在他看來，真正的造像還不如它們的影子清楚。他想轉回去。

Belief (pistis): seeing the objects, with no understanding yet of what they are copies of.

信念（pistis）：看見了物體，但還不明白它們是什麼的仿製品。

515e–516a

The ascent 上坡

He is dragged up “a steep and rugged ascent.” Plato’s verbs are verbs of force. Nobody leaves the cave by choice, and at this point the light ahead is only painful.

他被人拖上「陡峭崎嶇的上坡」。柏拉圖用的全是強制性的動詞。沒有人是自願離開洞穴的；在這個階段，前方的光只是令人疼痛。

Reasoning (dianoia): thinking that still leans on images, as geometry leans on diagrams.

推理（dianoia）：仍然倚靠影像的思考，就像幾何學倚靠圖形。

516a–c

The sun 太陽

Outside, he looks first at shadows, then at reflections in the water, then at the things themselves, and at last at the sun. He understands that it is the source of everything he has seen, including the fire below.

到了外面，他先看影子，再看水中的倒影，再看事物本身，最後才看太陽。他明白了：它是他所見一切的來源，連下面那堆火也包括在內。

Understanding (noēsis): grasping the Forms, and finally the Good.

理解（noēsis）：掌握理型，最後掌握「善」。

516e–517a

The return 回來

He goes back down. Now he is the one who cannot see, and the prisoners conclude that the journey has ruined him. If he tries to free them, they will kill him if they can.

他回到下面。現在看不見的人換成他了；囚徒們斷定，這趟旅程把他毀了。如果他試著解開他們，他們只要辦得到，就會殺了他。

Plato’s readers would think of Socrates. In the Republic the return is also a duty: those who have seen the Good must go back and govern.

柏拉圖的讀者會想到蘇格拉底。在《理想國》裡，回來同時是責任：見過「善」的人必須回去治理城邦。

Primary text · 原典

In his own words 原典怎麼說

The allegory takes up about four pages at the start of Book VII. It follows directly on two other images, the Sun and the Divided Line, and is meant to be read together with them.

這個寓言在第七卷開頭，大約佔四頁。它緊接在另外兩個意象「日喻」與「線喻」之後，本來就是要跟它們一起讀的。

Plato, Republic, Book VII 柏拉圖《理想國》卷七 · translated by Benjamin Jowett (public domain)

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

“We can easily forgive a child who is afraid of the dark; the real tragedy of life is when men are afraid of the light.”「孩子怕黑，我們很容易原諒；人生真正的悲劇，是成人怕光。」

Not in Plato · 柏拉圖沒有寫過

This line is attributed to Plato all over the internet and appears in none of his works. It reads like a modern summary of the cave. Plato’s point is in fact harsher than the quotation: the prisoners are not afraid of the light. They have no idea that there is any.

這句話在網路上到處被歸給柏拉圖，卻不見於他的任何著作。它讀起來像是現代人對洞穴寓言的摘要。柏拉圖的重點其實比這句話更嚴厲：囚徒並不是怕光，他們根本不知道有光。

Both sides · 正反兩面

Objections and replies 反駁與回應

Objection 1 · 反駁

The allegory slanders the only world we have. By placing reality somewhere else, it teaches contempt for the body, the senses, and ordinary life.

這個寓言誣衊了我們僅有的這個世界。它把實在放到別處，等於教人輕視身體、感官與平常的生活。

Reply · 回應

Nietzsche made this charge with great force and traced a long history of world-denial back to Plato. A defender can answer that the prisoner who has seen the sun understands the cave better than before, and goes back to it. Still, the charge has weight. Plato’s image does rank the visible world below another, and much later thought, religious and secular, inherited that ranking.

尼采極有力地提出了這項指控，並且把一段漫長的「否定世界」的歷史追溯到柏拉圖。辯護的人可以回答：見過太陽的囚徒對洞穴的了解比從前更深，而且他回去了。不過這項指控是有分量的。柏拉圖的意象確實把可見的世界排在另一個世界之下，而後來許多思想，宗教的與世俗的，都繼承了這個排序。

Objection 2 · 反駁

Anyone can claim to have seen the sun. The story gives the prisoners no way of telling a philosopher from a fraud, and it is used to justify rule by those who say they know best.

誰都可以自稱見過太陽。這個故事沒有給囚徒任何辦法去分辨哲學家與騙子，而它被用來替「自稱最懂的人來統治」辯護。

Reply · 回應

Karl Popper pressed this objection in 1945, writing in exile from a Europe ruled by people who were certain they knew. Plato’s answer is the fifteen-year program of mathematics and argument that follows in Book VII: the claim to have seen is to be tested by one’s ability to give an account and to defend it under questioning. That is a real safeguard inside philosophy. As a rule for deciding who should hold power, most readers today think it is not nearly enough.

波普在 1945 年力陳這個反駁；他寫作時流亡在外，而當時統治歐洲的，正是一群確信自己什麼都知道的人。柏拉圖的回答，是第七卷接下來那套十五年的數學與論辯訓練：一個人自稱見過，要用他能不能給出說明、並且在詰問之下守得住來檢驗。在哲學內部，這是一道真正的防線。但當作決定誰該掌權的規則，今天多數讀者認為它遠遠不夠。

Objection 3 · 反駁

A picture is not an argument. The cave assumes what it needs to show, that there is an “outside” at all.

圖像不是論證。洞穴預設了它需要證明的事：到底有沒有一個「外面」。

Reply · 回應

This is correct, and Plato knew it: Socrates introduces the story with the words “let me show in a figure.” The argument for an outside is the one set out above, and it must be judged on its merits. What the image adds is a warning about that judgment. If Plato is right, the truth will look less convincing than the illusion to anyone who is still chained, so a feeling of obviousness cannot settle the matter either way.

這是對的，而且柏拉圖自己知道：蘇格拉底是用「讓我用一個比喻來說明」這句話引出故事的。支持「有一個外面」的論證是上面攤開的那一個，它必須就它本身的優劣來評判。這個意象增添的，是對這個評判的一項警告。如果柏拉圖是對的，那麼對任何仍被鍊著的人來說，真相看起來會比幻象更沒有說服力；所以「顯然如此」的感覺，不管往哪一邊，都無法定奪這件事。

Terms of art · 哲學術語

The vocabulary of the subject 這門學問的用語

allegory寓言

Gk. allos ‘other’ + agoreuein ‘to speak’

A story in which the persons and events stand for something else.

故事裡的人物與事件另有所指的敘事。

Form理型

Gk. eidos, idea ‘look, shape’

In Plato, the unchanging reality (justice itself, the triangle itself) of which particular things are imperfect copies.

在柏拉圖，指不變的實在（正義本身、三角形本身），個別事物是它不完美的仿製品。

appearance and reality表象與實在

The distinction between how things seem and how they are.

事物看起來的樣子，與事物實際的樣子，兩者的區分。

doxa意見

Gk. ‘opinion, seeming’

Belief about changing, perceptible things, contrasted with knowledge.

關於變動的、可感事物的信念，與知識相對。

epistēmē知識

Gk. ‘knowledge’

Secure understanding of what cannot be otherwise; the root of “epistemology.”

對「不可能是別樣」的事物的確切理解；epistemology（知識論）的字根。

the intelligible可理知的

L. intellegere ‘to understand’

What can be grasped by thought and not by the senses.

能由思想掌握、而不能由感官掌握的東西。

periagōgē轉向

Gk. ‘a turning around’

Plato’s word for education: the turning of the whole soul toward what is real.

柏拉圖對教育的稱呼：整個靈魂轉向真實。

the Third Man第三人論證

指理型論會導致理型無窮後退的那個反駁。

    描述一次你發現：某個你一直當成「事物本身」的東西，其實只是它的影像。那次「轉身」是什麼感覺？你歡迎它嗎？

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

A view from the Dharma 佛法怎麼看

☸

A Buddhist reader recognizes a great deal in the cave. There is a condition of bondage that the bound do not notice, which the tradition calls avidyā, ignorance. There is a path of gradual seeing. And there is the return. The one who has seen and goes back among the prisoners out of pity, expecting to be mocked, is very close to the Mahāyāna ideal of the bodhisattva, who declines to rest in his own liberation while others remain bound. The Lotus Sūtra tells a comparable story of a father who coaxes his children out of a burning house in which they are happily playing.

佛教的讀者在洞穴裡認得出許多東西。有一種被束縛而不自知的狀態，傳統稱之為無明（avidyā）。有一條逐步看見的道路。還有「回來」。那個見過之後、出於憐憫回到囚徒當中、而且預料會被嘲笑的人，非常接近大乘的菩薩理想：別人還被綁著，他就不肯安住在自己的解脫裡。《法華經》講了一個可以相比的故事：父親把正玩得開心的孩子們，從著火的房子裡哄出來。

On what is seen outside, the two part completely. Plato’s freed prisoner finds permanent, self-standing essences, and they are more real than anything in the cave. The Madhyamaka philosophers taught two truths, conventional and ultimate, which sounds like the same division. But their ultimate truth is not a second world of better objects. It is the emptiness of the things of this world: the fact that nothing, anywhere, has the kind of independent, unchanging nature that a Platonic Form is supposed to have. For Nāgārjuna, the belief in fixed essences is the chain. On his view the prisoner turns around and finds no sun. What he comes to see is that even the fire and the wall arise in dependence on conditions, and this is the insight that frees him (Dharma and the West, D10).

至於在外面看見了什麼，兩者徹底分道揚鑣。柏拉圖那位被釋放的囚徒找到的是恆常、自立的本質，而且它們比洞裡的任何東西都更真實。中觀學派的哲學家講二諦，世俗諦與勝義諦，聽起來像是同一種劃分。但他們的勝義諦不是「另一個擺著更好的東西的世界」。它是這個世界的事物之「空」：不管在哪裡，沒有任何東西具有柏拉圖的理型被認為具有的那種獨立、不變的本性。對龍樹來說，相信有固定的本質，那才是鎖鍊。照他的看法，囚徒轉過身去，並沒有找到太陽。他看見的是：連那堆火、那道牆，也都是依條件而生起的；讓他自由的，正是這個洞見（佛法與西方哲學 D10）。
