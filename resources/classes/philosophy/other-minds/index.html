A14 · Mind and Self 心靈與自我

Can You Know What Another Mind Feels? 你能知道別人的感受嗎？

You have never felt any pain but your own. Mill’s analogy, Nagel’s bat, a scientist raised without color, and two friends on a bridge arguing about whether the fish are happy.
你從來沒有感受過自己以外任何人的痛。彌爾的類比、內格爾的蝙蝠、一位在沒有顏色的房間裡長大的科學家，還有橋上兩個朋友，為了魚快不快樂爭論起來。

A14 Big Questions · 大哉問Mind and Self · 心靈與自我Advanced · 高級1,399 words · about 25 min
The big idea · 大觀念

Everything we know about other minds comes through bodies, faces, and words. The question is whether that evidence can ever reach what it is evidence for, and the answer now decides how we treat animals, patients, and machines.

我們對別人的心所知道的一切，都是透過身體、臉孔與語言得來的。問題是：這些證據到底搆不搆得到它所要證明的東西？而這個答案，如今決定了我們怎麼對待動物、病人與機器。

The essay · 課文

Can You Know What Another Mind Feels? 你能知道別人的感受嗎？

01The pain you have never felt 你從沒感受過的痛

A friend catches her hand in a door. You see her face tighten, you hear the cry, and you wince. You are entirely sure that she is in pain. Yet you did not feel it. You have never felt anyone’s pain but your own, and you never will. What reached you was a sound and a movement, and from these you arrived at once, without any sense of reasoning, at a fact about an experience that is closed to you. Philosophers call the puzzle this raises the problem of other minds. It has two parts. The first asks how you know that other people have an inner life at all. The second assumes that they do and asks whether you can know what it is like.

一位朋友的手被門夾到了。你看見她的臉一緊，聽見那聲叫喊，自己也跟著縮了一下。你完全確定她在痛。可是你並沒有感受到那個痛。除了自己的痛，你從來沒有感受過任何人的痛，以後也不會。傳到你這裡的只是一個聲音、一個動作；而你從這些東西，沒有任何「在推理」的感覺，一下子就到達了一個事實，那個事實關乎一個對你關閉的經驗。哲學家把由此而來的難題稱為「他心問題」。它有兩個部分。第一個問：你怎麼知道別人真的有內在生活？第二個假定他們有，然後問：你能不能知道那是什麼感覺？

02Reasoning from my own case 從我自己推出去

The classic answer to the first question was given by John Stuart Mill in 1865. I know in my own case, he said, that a burn is followed by pain and that the pain is followed by a cry. I observe that other people have bodies like mine, and that when they are burned they cry out as I do. I conclude that the middle link is present in them as well. This is the argument from analogy, and it describes fairly well what we would say if asked to defend our belief.

對第一個問題的經典回答，是彌爾在 1865 年提出的。他說：就我自己而言，我知道燙傷之後會痛，痛之後會叫。我觀察到別人有跟我一樣的身體，被燙到的時候也像我一樣叫出聲來。我於是推斷：中間那一環，在他們身上也有。這就是「類比論證」；如果有人要我們為自己的信念辯護，我們會說的話，它描述得相當準確。

Its weakness is that it is an induction from a single case. The lesson on induction showed how little we may infer from one observation. If I had opened one box and found a beetle in it, I would have poor grounds for claiming that every box in the world contains a beetle. And here the conclusion can never be checked, since I cannot open a second box. There is a further oddity. Nobody acquires the belief in this way. An infant responds to its mother’s smile long before it could notice any correlation between its own feelings and its own face, which it has never seen.

它的弱點在於：這是從單一個案做的歸納。「歸納」那一課顯示過，從一次觀察能推出的東西有多麼少。假如我只打開過一個盒子，發現裡面有一隻甲蟲，我就沒有什麼根據宣稱世界上每一個盒子裡都有甲蟲。而且在這裡，結論永遠無法檢驗，因為我打不開第二個盒子。還有一點更奇怪：沒有人是這樣得到這個信念的。嬰兒對母親的微笑有反應的時候，還遠遠不可能注意到自己的感受與自己的臉之間有什麼關聯；它從來沒看過自己的臉。

03Hats, coats, and machines 帽子、外套與機器

Descartes saw the gap clearly. Looking down from a window at people passing in the street, he remarks in the Meditations, what he strictly sees is hats and coats, which could conceal automata. He judges that they are men. In the Discourse on Method he asked how one could tell a perfect mechanical imitation of a human being from the real thing, and proposed “two most certain tests.” A machine could never arrange words “so as appositely to reply to what is said in its presence,” and it could never act sensibly across “all the occurrences of life,” because each of its skills would need its own special mechanism. Animals, he concluded, fail both tests and are machines without thought.

笛卡兒把這道缺口看得很清楚。他在《沉思錄》裡說，從窗口往下看街上走過的人，嚴格說來他看見的是帽子與外套，底下藏的可能是自動機。是他「判斷」那些是人。在《方法論》裡，他問：一個模仿人類模仿得十全十美的機械，要怎麼跟真人分辨？他提出了「兩個最確定的檢驗」。機器永遠無法把字詞排列得「恰當地回應在它面前說的話」；它也永遠無法在「生活中的一切境遇」裡都做出合理的行動，因為它的每一項本事都需要一個專屬的機構。他的結論是：動物兩項檢驗都通不過，是沒有思想的機器。

The passage is worth rereading today. Descartes was confident that no machine would ever pass his first test, and for more than three centuries he was right.

這段話今天值得重讀。笛卡兒很有把握，沒有任何機器通得過他的第一項檢驗；而在三個多世紀裡，他是對的。

04What is it like to be a bat? 當一隻蝙蝠是什麼感覺？

Suppose the first question is settled and other creatures do have experience. In 1974 the American philosopher Thomas Nagel asked how far our knowledge of it can go. He chose bats because they are mammals, close enough to us that few doubt they feel something, and because they perceive the world chiefly by echolocation, a sense we lack. A bat sends out high-pitched cries and builds its picture of the space around it from the echoes. We can describe this process in complete physical detail. What we cannot do, Nagel argued, is know what it is like for the bat. If I imagine hanging upside down and hearing my way through the dark, I learn what it would be like for me to behave as a bat does. The bat’s own experience has a point of view built into it, and an objective description, which is written from no point of view in particular, leaves exactly that out.

假設第一個問題解決了，別的生物確實有經驗。1974 年，美國哲學家內格爾問：我們對這種經驗的認識，能走多遠？他選了蝙蝠，因為牠們是哺乳類，跟我們夠近，很少有人懷疑牠們有某種感受；也因為牠們主要靠回聲定位來知覺世界，那是我們沒有的一種感官。蝙蝠發出高頻的叫聲，再從回聲建構出周圍空間的圖像。這個過程，我們可以用完整的物理細節來描述。內格爾論證，我們做不到的，是知道那對蝙蝠來說是什麼感覺。如果我想像自己倒掛著、在黑暗中靠聽覺找路，我得知的是：「我」表現得像蝙蝠會是什麼感覺。蝙蝠自己的經驗內建著一個觀點；而客觀的描述，是不從任何特定觀點寫成的，它漏掉的恰恰就是這個。

05The scientist who had never seen red 從沒看過紅色的科學家

Eight years later the Australian philosopher Frank Jackson made the point with a human being. Mary is a brilliant scientist who has lived all her life in a black-and-white room and learned about the world from black-and-white screens. Her field is color vision, and she has come to know every physical fact about it: the wavelengths, the cells of the retina, the pathways in the brain, the way people use the word “red.” One day the door is opened and she sees a ripe tomato. Does she learn anything? Most people answer that she does. She learns what it is like to see red. But she already knew all the physical facts. Jackson concluded that there are facts about experience that physics cannot state, and the argument has been disputed at every step ever since. You can try it below.

八年後，澳洲哲學家傑克森用一個人說了同樣的道理。瑪麗是一位傑出的科學家，一輩子住在一個只有黑白的房間裡，透過黑白的螢幕認識世界。她的專長是色彩視覺，而她已經知道了關於它的每一個物理事實：波長、視網膜上的細胞、腦裡的路徑、人們怎麼使用「紅」這個字。有一天門打開了，她看見一顆熟透的番茄。她學到了什麼嗎？多數人回答：學到了。她學到看見紅色是什麼感覺。可是她原本就知道全部的物理事實了。傑克森的結論是：有一些關於經驗的事實，是物理學說不出來的；而這個論證，此後每一步都有人爭論。你可以在下面親自試一次。

06The beetle in the box 盒子裡的甲蟲

Wittgenstein thought the whole problem rested on a wrong picture. Imagine, he says, that everyone has a box with something in it that we call a “beetle.” No one can look into anyone else’s box, and each says that he knows what a beetle is only from his own. In that situation the thing in the box would play no part in the meaning of the word. The boxes could hold different things, or nothing. His point is that words such as “pain” cannot get their meaning from a private inner object that each speaker points to alone. We learn them in public, from people who comfort us when we fall. And in ordinary life we do not infer that someone is in pain. We see it in the face, as directly as we see that the face is pale. On this view the skeptic’s picture of minds sealed inside bodies is itself the mistake.

維根斯坦認為，整個問題建立在一幅錯誤的圖像上。他說，想像每個人都有一個盒子，裡面裝著我們叫作「甲蟲」的東西。誰也看不到別人的盒子，每個人都說他只是從自己的那一隻知道甲蟲是什麼。在那種處境裡，盒子裡的東西對這個詞的意義毫無作用。各個盒子裝的可以是不同的東西，也可以什麼都沒有。他的重點是：「痛」這類的詞，不可能從每個說話者獨自指著的一個私有的內在對象得到意義。我們是在公共場合學會這些詞的，是從我們跌倒時安慰我們的人那裡學來的。而在日常生活裡，我們並不是「推論」出某人在痛。我們在臉上看見它，就像看見那張臉發白一樣直接。照這個看法，懷疑論者那幅「心靈密封在身體裡」的圖像，本身就是錯誤所在。

07The happiness of fish 魚之樂

The oldest statement of the problem is a conversation of about sixty words. Zhuangzi and his friend Huizi, a logician, were strolling on the bridge over the Hao River. Zhuangzi remarked that the minnows were swimming about freely, and that this was the happiness of fish. Huizi said: “You are not a fish. How do you know the happiness of fish?” Zhuangzi replied: “You are not I. How do you know that I do not know it?” Huizi pressed his advantage. “I am not you, so I do not know what you know. You are not a fish, so you do not know the happiness of fish. The case is complete.” Zhuangzi’s answer has been argued over for two thousand years. “Let us go back to the beginning. When you asked how I know, you already knew that I knew. I know it from up here on the Hao.”

這個問題最古老的陳述，是一段大約六十個字的對話。莊子與他的朋友、名家的惠子，在濠水的橋上散步。莊子說，鯈魚游得從容自在，這就是魚的快樂。惠子說：「子非魚，安知魚之樂？」莊子答：「子非我，安知我不知魚之樂？」惠子乘勝追擊：「我不是你，所以不知道你知道什麼；你不是魚，所以你不知道魚的快樂。這就說完了。」莊子的回答，被爭論了兩千年：「請回到開頭。你問我『怎麼知道』的時候，就已經知道我知道了。我是在濠水上面知道的。」

Huizi argues as Nagel’s reader might: knowledge of an experience requires being the one who has it. Zhuangzi first shows that the principle defeats itself, since Huizi relies on knowing something about Zhuangzi’s mind in order to state it. His last line turns on the Chinese word ān, which means both “how” and “from where.” He answers the second sense. He knows it from where he stands, on a bridge, on a fine day, watching them. The suggestion is that knowledge of another creature is had from within a shared situation, by a being that is itself alive and at ease, and that no proof conducted from outside is needed or possible. Mencius, his contemporary, made a similar observation about human beings. Anyone who suddenly sees a child about to fall into a well feels alarm and compassion, and feels it before any thought of reward. For Mencius that response is the root of all morality.

惠子的論證方式，像是內格爾的一位讀者會用的：要知道一個經驗，就必須是擁有那個經驗的那一個。莊子先指出這條原則自己打敗自己，因為惠子要說出這條原則，就得靠著知道莊子心裡的某件事。他的最後一句，關鍵在「安」這個字，它既是「怎麼」，也是「在哪裡」。他回答的是第二個意思。他是從他站的地方知道的：在一座橋上，在一個好天氣裡，看著牠們。這裡的暗示是：對另一個生命的認識，是在一個共同的處境之內得到的，是由一個自己也活著、也自在的存在者得到的；從外面進行的證明，既不需要，也不可能。跟他同時代的孟子，對人類做過類似的觀察。任何人突然看見一個小孩快要掉進井裡，都會感到驚懼與不忍，而且是在想到任何好處之前就感到了。對孟子來說，那個反應是一切道德的根苗。

08Three places where the answer is needed 需要答案的三個地方

The problem has left the seminar room. In 2012 a group of neuroscientists meeting at Cambridge signed a declaration that mammals, birds, and other creatures, octopuses among them, have the neural equipment for conscious states, and laws on animal welfare have begun to follow. In hospitals, some patients who show no response at all have been found, when placed in a scanner and asked to imagine playing tennis, to produce the same brain activity as healthy volunteers. And there are now machines that pass the first of Descartes’s tests with ease. They reply appositely to whatever is said to them, and when asked they will describe their feelings. Whether there is anything it is like to be such a system is a question on which serious researchers disagree, and no agreed method exists for settling it. In each of the three cases behavior has come apart from the usual signs, and we find that we have been relying on resemblance to ourselves without ever deciding which resemblances count.

這個問題已經離開了研討室。2012 年，一群在劍橋開會的神經科學家簽署了一份宣言：哺乳類、鳥類與其他生物（章魚也在其中）具備產生意識狀態的神經構造；動物福利的法律也已經開始跟進。在醫院裡，有些完全沒有反應的病人，被放進掃描儀、並被要求想像自己在打網球的時候，產生了跟健康受試者相同的腦部活動。而現在也有了輕易就通過笛卡兒第一項檢驗的機器。不管對它們說什麼，它們都能恰當地回應；被問到的時候，還會描述自己的感受。「當這樣一個系統」有沒有任何感覺可言？認真的研究者在這個問題上意見分歧，也還沒有一個公認的方法可以解決它。在這三種情況裡，行為都跟通常的徵象脫了鉤；而我們發現，自己一直依賴的是「跟我們相似」，卻從來沒有決定過，哪些相似才算數。

Thought experiment · 思想實驗

Try it: Mary’s room, then the ladder of minds 親手做一次：瑪麗的房間，然後是心靈的階梯

Two experiments. In the first you learn everything science can say about red, and then see it. In the second you say how sure you are that each of nine things has an inner life, and find out what rule you were using.

兩個實驗。第一個：你先學會科學對紅色能說的一切，然後看見它。第二個：對九樣東西，你各自說出你有多確定它有內在生活，然後看看你用的是哪一條規則。

Part 1 · Mary’s room 第一部分：瑪麗的房間

You are Mary. You have never seen any color. Read the four files on your screen. Together they stand for everything physical there is to know about red.

你是瑪麗。你從來沒看過任何顏色。讀完螢幕上的四份檔案。它們合起來代表關於紅色所有可知的物理事實。

This is red.這就是紅色。

Did Mary learn anything new just now?瑪麗剛才學到了任何新東西嗎？

Frank Jackson, 1982

This was Jackson’s conclusion. If she learned a fact, and she already knew every physical fact, then some facts are not physical, and the view that everything is physical is false. The price is high: these extra facts seem to do no causal work, since physics already explains every movement of Mary’s body, including her saying “So that is red!” Jackson himself later gave the argument up for this reason.

這是傑克森的結論。如果她學到了一個事實，而她原本就知道每一個物理事實，那麼有些事實就不是物理的；「一切都是物理的」這個看法就是錯的。代價很高：這些額外的事實似乎不起任何因果作用，因為物理學已經解釋了瑪麗身體的每一個動作，包括她說出「原來這就是紅！」。傑克森自己後來就為了這個理由放棄了這個論證。

David Lewis, Laurence Nemirow

On this reply Mary gains know-how: she can now recognize red, imagine it, and remember it. Learning to ride a bicycle also gives you something no textbook could, and nobody concludes that there are non-physical facts about cycling. Critics answer that the abilities seem to rest on something she now knows, and that she could lose the abilities and still know it.

照這個回應，瑪麗得到的是「知道怎麼做」：她現在能認出紅色、想像它、記住它。學會騎腳踏車也給了你教科書給不了的東西，卻沒有人因此推斷有關於騎車的非物理事實。批評者回答：這些能力似乎是建立在她現在「知道」的某件事上；而且她可以失去這些能力，卻仍然知道那件事。

Brian Loar and others

This is the most popular reply among physicalists today. Someone who knows that water fills the lake may not know that H₂O does, though it is the same fact. Mary knew the fact about red under a scientific description. She now knows it under a concept that can only be acquired by having the experience. The difficulty is to explain why a mere new way of thinking should feel like such a discovery.

這是今天物理主義者最常用的回應。一個知道「湖裡都是水」的人，可能不知道「湖裡都是 H₂O」，儘管那是同一個事實。瑪麗原本是在科學的描述之下知道關於紅色的那個事實。她現在是在一個只有擁有過那個經驗才能取得的概念之下知道它。困難在於說明：僅僅是一種新的思考方式，為什麼感覺起來會像這麼大的一個發現。

Daniel Dennett

Dennett held that we misjudge the case because we cannot imagine knowing all the physical facts. We picture someone who has read a great deal. A Mary who truly knew everything would know exactly how her brain was about to react, and if you tried to fool her with a blue banana she would catch the trick at once. Most people find this hard to believe, and Dennett’s answer is that the difficulty of believing it is not evidence.

丹尼特主張，我們誤判了這個情況，因為我們想像不出「知道全部的物理事實」是什麼樣子。我們想到的是一個讀了很多書的人。一位真正什麼都知道的瑪麗，會確切知道自己的腦即將怎麼反應；如果你拿一根藍色的香蕉想騙她，她會立刻識破。多數人覺得這很難相信；丹尼特的回答是：難以相信，不算證據。

Part 2 · The ladder of minds 第二部分：心靈的階梯

Now the other question. For each of these, how sure are you that there is something it is like to be it? Move every slider, then press the button.

現在是另一個問題。對下面每一樣，你有多確定「當它」是有某種感覺的？把每一條滑桿都拉過，再按按鈕。

What four philosophers would say 四位哲學家會怎麼說

Huizi · 惠子

You are none of these things. On what grounds do you claim to know about any of them? Put every slider in the middle.

這些東西你一樣也不是。你憑什麼宣稱知道其中任何一個？把每一條滑桿都放在中間吧。

Zhuangzi · 莊子

You moved the sliders without difficulty, so you knew something. You knew it from where you stand, as a living thing among living things.

你毫不費力就拉動了滑桿，可見你知道些什麼。你是從你站的地方知道的：一個活著的東西，在活著的東西當中。

Descartes · 笛卡兒

Ask two things. Can it reply appositely to whatever is said? Can it act sensibly in every circumstance? What fails is a machine. (He put the dog, the bat, and the octopus at zero.)

問兩件事。不管對它說什麼，它能恰當地回應嗎？它能在每一種境遇裡合理地行動嗎？通不過的就是機器。（他把狗、蝙蝠與章魚都放在零。）

Nagel · 內格爾

The bat almost certainly has experience. What you cannot know is what that experience is like, and no amount of further fact about its brain will tell you.

蝙蝠幾乎可以確定有經驗。你無法知道的是：那個經驗是什麼感覺；再多關於牠的腦的事實，也不會告訴你。

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

“Be kind, for everyone you meet is fighting a hard battle.” (Plato)「要仁慈，因為你遇見的每個人都在打一場艱苦的仗。」（柏拉圖）

A Scottish minister, 1890s · 一位蘇格蘭牧師，1890 年代

The sentence is found nowhere in Plato. Researchers have traced it to the Scottish minister and novelist John Watson, who wrote under the name Ian Maclaren, in the form “Be pitiful, for every man is fighting a hard battle.” It was credited to Plato and to Philo of Alexandria only in the later twentieth century. It belongs in this lesson all the same, since it states the practical conclusion many people draw from the problem of other minds: you cannot see what the person in front of you is carrying, so assume that it is heavy.

柏拉圖的著作裡找不到這句話。研究者把它追溯到蘇格蘭牧師兼小說家約翰・華森（筆名伊恩・麥克拉倫），原來的形式是：「要有憐憫，因為每個人都在打一場艱苦的仗。」直到二十世紀後期，它才被歸到柏拉圖與亞歷山卓的斐洛名下。儘管如此，它還是屬於這一課，因為它說出了許多人從他心問題得出的實際結論：你看不見眼前這個人背負著什麼，所以就當它很重吧。

Both sides · 正反兩面

Objections and replies 反駁與回應

Objection 1 · 反駁

This is not a real problem. Nobody doubts that other people have minds, and a doubt that no one can feel is not worth discussing.

這不是真的問題。沒有人懷疑別人有心；一個誰也感受不到的懷疑，不值得討論。

Reply · 回應

The question was never whether we believe it. It is what entitles us to, and the answer is needed wherever the belief runs out. We are certain about our friends and quite uncertain about fish, insects, the unresponsive patient, and the machine. A person who cannot say what grounds the easy cases has nothing to go on in the hard ones.

問題從來不在於我們相不相信。問題在於：是什麼讓我們有資格相信？而凡是這個信念用盡的地方，都需要這個答案。我們對朋友很確定，對魚、昆蟲、沒有反應的病人、機器卻相當不確定。一個說不出簡單情況的根據是什麼的人，在困難的情況裡就無所依憑。

Objection 2 · 反駁

Neuroscience will settle it. Once we know which brain processes go with consciousness, we can simply check whether a creature has them.

神經科學會解決這個問題。一旦我們知道哪些腦部過程與意識相伴，我們只要檢查某個生物有沒有那些過程就行了。

Reply · 回應

The method depends on the thing in question. We find out which brain processes go with experience by asking human subjects what they experience and trusting their reports. That gives us the signs of consciousness in creatures like us. Applying them to an octopus, whose nervous system is arranged on a different plan, or to a machine with no neurons, is once more an argument from analogy, and the question of how far the analogy holds is the original problem.

這個方法依賴的正是有待解決的東西。我們是靠著詢問人類受試者經驗到什麼、並且信任他們的報告，才找出哪些腦部過程與經驗相伴的。那給了我們「像我們這樣的生物」身上的意識徵象。把這些徵象用到章魚（牠的神經系統是照另一套藍圖安排的），或用到一台沒有神經元的機器上，就又是一個類比論證了；而類比能延伸多遠，正是原來的那個問題。

Objection 3 · 反駁

If something behaves in every respect as though it had feelings, there is nothing further to ask. To have a mind is to behave in those ways.

如果某個東西在每一方面都表現得好像有感受，就沒有什麼可以再問的了。有心，就是那樣表現。

Reply · 回應

This is behaviorism, and its attraction is that it makes other minds knowable. The standard reply is that it gets one’s own case wrong. When I am in pain, I am aware of more than a tendency to groan, and a trained actor can produce the behavior without the pain while a paralyzed patient has the pain without the behavior. The view survives in a subtler form, functionalism, which identifies a mental state with its whole causal role inside a system. Whether the right organization is enough, whatever it is made of, is the question now being asked about machines.

這是行為主義；它吸引人的地方，是讓他心變得可知。標準的回應是：它把一個人自己的情況弄錯了。我在痛的時候，我覺察到的不只是一種想呻吟的傾向；而一位受過訓練的演員可以做出那些行為卻沒有痛，一位癱瘓的病人有痛卻沒有那些行為。這個看法以一種更細緻的形式存活下來，就是功能論：它把一個心理狀態，等同於它在一個系統之內的整個因果角色。只要組織方式對了，不管是什麼材料做的，是否就夠了？這正是現在針對機器在問的問題。

Terms of art · 哲學術語

The vocabulary of the subject 這門學問的用語

the problem of other minds他心問題

The problem of how one can know that others have experiences, and what those experiences are like.

一個人如何能知道別人有經驗，以及那些經驗是什麼樣子的問題。

argument from analogy類比論證

Gk. analogia ‘proportion’

The inference that others have minds because their bodies and behavior resemble mine.

由於別人的身體與行為跟我的相似，而推論他們有心。

qualia感質

L. qualis ‘of what kind’; singular quale

The felt qualities of experience, such as the way red looks or pain feels.

經驗被感受到的性質，例如紅色看起來的樣子、痛感覺起來的樣子。

physicalism物理主義

The view that everything that exists is physical, so that a complete physical description would leave nothing out.

主張一切存在的東西都是物理的，因此一份完整的物理描述不會漏掉任何東西。

the knowledge argument知識論證

Jackson’s argument that Mary learns a non-physical fact on first seeing color.

傑克森的論證：瑪麗第一次看見顏色時，學到了一個非物理的事實。

solipsism唯我論

L. solus ‘alone’ + ipse ‘self’

The view that only one’s own mind exists, or can be known to exist.

主張只有自己的心存在，或只有自己的心能被知道存在。

behaviorism行為主義

The view that to be in a mental state is to behave, or be disposed to behave, in certain ways.

主張處於某種心理狀態，就是以某些方式行動，或傾向於那樣行動。

functionalism功能論

The view that a mental state is defined by its causal role, whatever material carries it out.

主張心理狀態是由它的因果角色界定的，不管由什麼材料來實現。

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

A view from the Dharma 佛法怎麼看

☸

The Buddha uses the argument from analogy, and he uses it for a different purpose. “All tremble at the rod, all fear death,” says the Dhammapada. “Treating others like oneself, neither kill nor incite to kill.” The Pali is attānaṃ upamaṃ katvā, literally “having made oneself the comparison.” In another discourse he teaches householders to reason in this way: I want to live and do not want to die, and others are the same; “the thing that is disliked by me is also disliked by others.” Mill offered the analogy as a proof that other minds exist, and as a proof it is weak. The Buddha offers it as a practice. He takes the existence of other beings’ suffering for granted and asks his hearers to keep turning their attention to it until it weighs with them as their own does. The later tradition calls the result the equality of self and other.

佛陀也用類比論證，只是用在不同的目的上。《法句經》說：「一切皆懼刀杖，一切皆畏死。以己為喻，不殺、不教他殺。」巴利文是 attānaṃ upamaṃ katvā，字面是「以自己作為比喻」。在另一部經裡，他教在家人這樣推想：我想活、不想死，別人也一樣；「我所不喜歡的事，別人也不喜歡。」彌爾提出類比，是當作「他心存在」的證明；作為證明，它是弱的。佛陀提出它，是當作一種修習。他把其他眾生的苦當作既定的事實，然後要聽的人一再把注意力轉向它，直到它在他們心裡的分量，跟自己的苦一樣重。後來的傳統把這個結果稱為「自他平等」。

Buddhist philosophers also met the skeptical problem head-on. The Yogācāra school held that what we experience is only mind, and its opponents replied that it must then deny that other minds exist. In the seventh century Dharmakīrti answered in a short treatise, the Proof of Other Mindstreams, and his proof is Mill’s: I observe that my own speech and action are preceded by intention, and I infer the same of speech and action that are not mine. Four centuries later Ratnakīrti argued that, on Yogācāra principles, the inference fails. The tradition also claims that the gap can be crossed directly. The early texts list knowledge of others’ minds among the powers that come with deep concentration, and compare it to seeing one’s face in a clean mirror. Whatever one makes of that claim, it shows that Buddhism never treated another mind as sealed in principle (Dharma and the West, D11 and D19).

佛教哲學家也正面遇上了懷疑論的問題。瑜伽行派主張我們所經驗的唯是心識，反對者就回應：那麼它必須否認他心存在。七世紀，法稱在一篇短論《成他相續論》裡回答了；而他的證明就是彌爾的那一個：我觀察到自己的言語與行動之前有意圖，於是推論那些不屬於我的言語與行動也是如此。四個世紀之後，寶稱論證：照瑜伽行派的原則，這個推論不成立。這個傳統還主張，這道缺口可以直接跨過去。早期經典把「知他人心」列在深定所帶來的能力當中，並且把它比作在乾淨的鏡子裡看見自己的臉。不管我們怎麼看待這個主張，它顯示佛教從來沒有把另一個心當成原則上密封的（佛法與西方哲學 D11、D19）。
