A33 · Language and Science 語言與科學

How Do Words Get Their Meaning? 字的意思從哪裡來？

A child points and says “dog,” and somehow the noise comes to mean the animal. Augustine’s picture of how that happens, Locke’s ideas in the head, Frege’s two stars, Wittgenstein’s beetle in a box, and Xunzi on names fixed by agreement.
一個孩子指著說「狗」，然後不知怎麼地，那個聲音就有了「那種動物」的意思。奧古斯丁對這件事的描繪、洛克說的腦袋裡的觀念、弗雷格的兩顆星、維根斯坦的盒中甲蟲，以及荀子的「約定俗成」。

A33 Big Questions · 大哉問Language and Science · 語言與科學Advanced · 高級1,061 words · about 25 min
The big idea · 大觀念

Every earlier lesson in this series was conducted in words, and assumed that we understood them. This lesson, the first of a unit on language and science, asks what that understanding consists in. A sound or a mark is a physical thing like any other. What has to be added for it to mean something?

這個系列前面的每一課，都是用字詞進行的，並且假定了我們懂得它們。這一課是「語言與科學」單元的第一課；它問的是：那個「懂」是由什麼構成的？一個聲音或一個記號，是跟別的東西一樣的物理事物。要加上什麼，它才有意思？

The essay · 課文

How Do Words Get Their Meaning? 字的意思從哪裡來？

01Pointing and naming 指著，叫出名字

The most natural answer was given by Augustine, remembering how he learned to speak. “When they named any thing, and as they spoke turned towards it, I saw and remembered that they called what they would point out by the name they uttered.” Words are names, and each name stands for an object. To learn a language is to learn which name goes with which thing, and a sentence is a string of such names.

最自然的答案是奧古斯丁給的；他回憶自己是怎麼學會說話的。「當他們叫出某樣東西的名字，並且一邊說一邊轉向它的時候，我看見了，也記住了：他們用發出的那個名字，來稱呼他們要指出的東西。」字詞是名字；每一個名字代表一樣東西。學一種語言，就是學會哪個名字配哪樣東西；而一個句子，是這樣的名字串成的一串。

The picture fits “dog” and “table.” It fits less well when we try other words. Nothing can be pointed at for “five,” “because,” “perhaps,” or “hello.” And pointing itself has to be understood. If I point at a rabbit running past and say “gavagai,” you cannot tell from the gesture whether I am naming the animal, its color, its running, the number one, or the act of pointing. A child who learns from pointing must already know a good deal about what kind of thing is likely to be meant.

這個圖像很適合「狗」與「桌子」。換別的字試試看，它就沒那麼適合了。「五」、「因為」、「也許」、「哈囉」，都沒有東西可以指。而且「指」本身也需要被理解。如果我指著一隻跑過去的兔子說「gavagai」，你無法從這個手勢看出：我是在叫那隻動物、牠的顏色、牠的奔跑、數字「一」，還是「指」這個動作的名字。一個從別人的指點裡學習的孩子，必定已經相當知道，對方多半是指哪一類的東西。

02Ideas in the head 腦袋裡的觀念

John Locke moved meaning indoors. “Words, in their primary or immediate signification, stand for nothing but the ideas in the mind of him that uses them.” I have an idea of gold, I attach the sound “gold” to it, and when I speak, the sound calls up the matching idea in you. The link between sound and idea is “perfectly arbitrary,” which is why there are many languages. This explains how words for absent or imaginary things can have meaning. It leaves a difficulty that Locke saw. I can never look into your mind to check that your idea matches mine. If meaning is a private item, no one can know that anyone else means what he means.

約翰・洛克把意義搬到了室內。「字詞，就其原初或直接的意指而言，代表的不是別的，就是使用它的人心裡的觀念。」我有一個「金子」的觀念；我把「金子」這個聲音繫在它上面；當我說話的時候，那個聲音在你心裡喚起相配的觀念。聲音與觀念之間的連結是「完全任意的」；這就是為什麼有許多種語言。這解釋了：指稱不在場或想像中的事物的字詞，怎麼能有意義。它也留下了一個洛克自己看到的困難。我永遠無法看進你的心裡，去核對你的觀念跟我的相不相配。如果意義是一件私人的東西，就沒有人能知道別人的意思跟自己的一樣。

In 1892 the logician Gottlob Frege showed that meaning cannot simply be the thing named either. The morning star and the evening star are one object, the planet Venus. Yet “the morning star is the evening star” was a discovery of astronomy, while “the morning star is the morning star” tells us nothing. Two names for one thing can differ in meaning. Frege concluded that a name has a sense, a way in which it presents its object, as well as a reference. Sense, he insisted, is public. It is what a whole community grasps, and no one keeps it in his head as a private image.

1892 年，邏輯學家戈特洛布・弗雷格指出：意義也不可能單單就是被叫到名字的那樣東西。晨星與暮星是同一個對象，也就是金星。然而「晨星就是暮星」是天文學的一項發現；而「晨星就是晨星」什麼也沒告訴我們。同一樣東西的兩個名字，意義可以不同。弗雷格的結論是：一個名字除了有「指稱」，還有「意義」，也就是它呈現其對象的方式。他堅持，意義是公共的。它是一整個社群所掌握的東西；並沒有哪一個人把它當作私人的意象，收在自己的腦袋裡。

03The beetle in the box 盒子裡的甲蟲

Ludwig Wittgenstein began his last book by quoting Augustine’s account of learning to speak, and spent the book taking it apart. He asked his readers to stop looking for the thing a word stands for and to look at what people do with it. “For a large class of cases,” he wrote, “the meaning of a word is its use in the language.” Words are like the tools in a toolbox, a hammer, a ruler, a pot of glue, and are as various in their work. To know what “five” means is to be able to count, and to know what “hello” means is to know when to say it.

路德維希・維根斯坦在他最後一本書的開頭，引了奧古斯丁對學說話的那段描述；然後用整本書把它拆開。他請讀者不要再去找一個字「代表」的東西，而去看人們拿它做什麼。他寫道：「在很大一類的情況裡，一個字的意義就是它在語言中的用法。」字詞就像工具箱裡的工具：一把鎚子、一把尺、一罐膠水；它們的工作也同樣五花八門。知道「五」是什麼意思，就是會數數；知道「哈囉」是什麼意思，就是知道什麼時候說它。

Against Locke he offered a small story. Suppose everyone has a box with something in it, which we call a beetle. No one can look into anyone else’s box, and each says that he knows what a beetle is only from his own. It would be quite possible for everyone to have something different in his box, or for the thing to keep changing, or for a box to be empty. The word “beetle” would go on being used all the same. The thing in the box, Wittgenstein says, “has no place in the language-game at all.” It drops out as irrelevant. If the meaning of “pain” were a private object that only I can inspect, the same would follow for pain. Since we plainly do talk about pain and understand one another, the word must get its meaning elsewhere, from the cries, the winces, the comforting, and the shared life in which it is used.

針對洛克，他講了一個小故事。假設每個人都有一個盒子，裡面有某樣東西，我們叫它「甲蟲」。誰也不能看進別人的盒子；而每個人都說，他只從自己的那一隻知道甲蟲是什麼。很有可能每個人的盒子裡是不同的東西；或者那樣東西不停地在變；或者有一個盒子是空的。「甲蟲」這個詞還是會照樣被使用。維根斯坦說，盒子裡的那樣東西「在這個語言遊戲裡根本沒有位置」。它作為不相干的東西，被消掉了。如果「痛」的意義是一個只有我能檢視的私人對象，那麼對痛也會得出同樣的結論。既然我們顯然確實在談論痛、也彼此聽得懂，這個詞的意義必定來自別處：來自叫喊、皺眉、安慰，以及使用它的那個共同的生活。

04Fixed by agreement 約定俗成

Chinese philosophers had argued about names for two centuries before the first empire. The logician Gongsun Long defended the claim that “a white horse is not a horse,” on the ground that “horse” names a shape and “white” a color, so that the two terms do not mean the same. Thinkers who wanted language to serve government found such puzzles dangerous. Xunzi replied with the clearest statement of conventionalism in the ancient world: “Names have no fixed rightness. They are fixed by agreement. When the agreement is settled and the custom formed, they are called right, and what departs from the agreement is called wrong.” A name is correct because a community uses it so. For Xunzi it follows that keeping names stable is a public duty, and that those who play with them unsettle everything built on them.

在第一個帝國之前，中國的哲學家已經為「名」爭論了兩個世紀。名家的公孫龍為「白馬非馬」這個主張辯護；理由是「馬」命名的是形，「白」命名的是色，所以這兩個詞的意思並不相同。希望語言為治理服務的思想家，覺得這樣的難題很危險。荀子的回應，是古代世界對約定論最清楚的陳述：「名無固宜，約之以命，約定俗成謂之宜，異於約則謂之不宜。」一個名之所以正確，是因為一個社群這樣用它。對荀子來說，由此可以推出：保持名的穩定是一項公共的責任；而玩弄名的人，動搖了建立在它們之上的一切。

The Daoists drew the opposite lesson from the same fact. If names are our arrangement and nothing more, they cannot reach what was there before any arrangement. The Daodejing opens: “The name that can be named is not the constant name.” Zhuangzi put it as an image. “The fish trap exists for the sake of the fish. Once you have the fish, you can forget the trap. Words exist for the sake of meaning. Once you have the meaning, you can forget the words. Where can I find a man who has forgotten words, so that I can have a word with him?”

道家從同一個事實裡，引出了相反的教訓。如果名只是我們的安排、別無其他，它們就搆不到任何安排之前就已經在那裡的東西。《道德經》開頭就說：「名可名，非常名。」莊子把它說成一個意象：「荃者所以在魚，得魚而忘荃……言者所以在意，得意而忘言。吾安得夫忘言之人而與之言哉？」

05What use leaves open 「用法」沒有回答的

“Meaning is use” is closer to a direction for looking than to a theory, and it has costs. If a word means whatever a community does with it, it becomes hard to say how a whole community could use a word wrongly, and yet people once applied “fish” to whales. In the 1970s Saul Kripke and Hilary Putnam argued that some words are tied to the world in a way that use does not capture. “Water” named a certain stuff long before anyone knew its chemistry, and what the word meant all along depended on what that stuff really is. Meanings, as Putnam put it, are not entirely in the head, and on his account they are not entirely in our practices either. The present view is a division of labor. Use and agreement explain most words, and the world itself helps to fix the meaning of some. Almost no one now defends the private image in the mind that Locke began with.

「意義即用法」與其說是一個理論，不如說是一個「往哪裡看」的指示；而它是有代價的。如果一個字的意思，就是一個社群拿它做的任何事；那就很難說明，一整個社群怎麼可能把一個字用錯。然而人們確實曾經把鯨魚叫做「魚」。1970 年代，索爾・克里普克與希拉蕊・普特南主張：有些字與世界連結的方式，是「用法」捕捉不到的。早在任何人懂得它的化學成分之前，「水」就已經是某一種東西的名字了；而這個字一直以來的意思，取決於那種東西實際上是什麼。照普特南的說法，意義並不完全在腦袋裡；而照他的講法，它們也不完全在我們的實踐裡。目前的看法是一種分工。用法與約定解釋了大多數的字；而世界本身，幫忙固定了其中一些字的意義。洛克當初拿來當起點的那個「心裡的私人意象」，現在幾乎沒有人為它辯護了。

Thought experiment · 思想實驗

Try it: the village of boxes 親手做一次：盒子村

Five villagers each carry a small box. No one may look into another’s. Everyone calls what is in his own box a “beetle.” Listen to them talk, answer three questions, and then open the boxes.

五位村民，各自帶著一個小盒子。誰也不可以看別人的盒子。每個人都把自己盒子裡的東西叫做「甲蟲」。聽聽他們說話，回答三個問題，然後把盒子打開。

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

“The limits of my language mean the limits of my world.” (Wittgenstein, on why you should learn more words)「我的語言的界限，意味著我的世界的界限。」（維根斯坦：所以你該多學一些字）

His sentence, and not about vocabulary · 是他的句子；但談的不是字彙量

The line is proposition 5.6 of the Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (1921), and it appears on posters in language schools as advice to enlarge one’s vocabulary. In its place it means something stranger. The Tractatus holds that language pictures facts, and that whatever can be the case can be said. The “limits of language” are the limits of what can be said in any language whatever, which he takes to be the limits of logic. The remark belongs to a discussion of solipsism, the view that only I and my experience exist, and the “my” is doing philosophical work. Learning Spanish does not move the limit he had in mind. He later rejected the theory of language behind the remark. The poster’s sentiment is harmless, and it is closer to the later Wittgenstein, for whom to learn a language is to learn a form of life.

這句話是《邏輯哲學論》（1921）的命題 5.6；它出現在語言學校的海報上，當作一句勸人擴充字彙的話。在它原來的位置上，它的意思奇怪得多。《邏輯哲學論》主張：語言描繪事實；凡是可能成立的情況，都可以被說出來。所謂「語言的界限」，是任何一種語言裡「可以被說出來的東西」的界限；他認為那就是邏輯的界限。這句話屬於一段關於唯我論的討論（唯我論主張只有我與我的經驗存在）；而那個「我的」，是有哲學上的作用的。學會西班牙文，並不會移動他心裡想的那條界限。他後來放棄了這句話背後的那個語言理論。海報上的那個想法無傷大雅；而它其實更接近後期的維根斯坦：對後期的他來說，學一種語言，就是學一種生活形式。

Both sides · 正反兩面

Objections and replies 反駁與回應

Objection 1 · 反駁

If meaning is use, then to know the meaning of a word I would have to know every way it is ever used. No one does.

如果意義就是用法，那麼要知道一個字的意義，我就得知道它被使用的每一種方式。沒有人知道。

Reply · 回應

Knowing how to use a word is a skill, like knowing how to ride a bicycle, and skills come in degrees. A child who can ask for milk knows something of what “milk” means, and a dairy chemist knows more. Putnam added that we lean on one another. I can use “elm” correctly without being able to tell an elm from a beech, because others in my community can. He called this the division of linguistic labor.

知道怎麼用一個字是一種技能，就像知道怎麼騎腳踏車；而技能是有程度之分的。一個會要牛奶喝的孩子，知道一些「牛奶」的意思；一位乳品化學家知道得更多。普特南補充說：我們彼此倚靠。我分不出榆樹與山毛櫸，卻能正確地使用「榆樹」這個詞；因為我的社群裡有別人分得出來。他把這叫做「語言的分工」。

Objection 2 · 反駁

Xunzi’s view makes truth a matter of agreement. If we all agreed to call the deer a horse, it would be one.

荀子的看法使真理成了約定的事。如果我們全都同意把鹿叫做馬，牠就是馬了。

Reply · 回應

Convention fixes which sound goes with which kind of thing. It does not fix the facts about the thing. If we swapped the words, the sentence “that is a horse,” said of a deer, would become true, and the animal would still have antlers. Xunzi is clear on this. He says in the same chapter that names are made in order to point at realities and mark their differences. The story of the minister who called a deer a horse to see who would dare to contradict him is a story about power corrupting usage, and Xunzi’s demand that names be kept correct is aimed at exactly that.

約定決定的是：哪一個聲音配哪一種東西。它並不決定關於那樣東西的事實。如果我們把這兩個詞對調，那麼指著一頭鹿說「那是一匹馬」這個句子，就會變成真的；而那隻動物照樣長著鹿角。荀子在這一點上很清楚。他在同一篇裡說，制名是為了指稱實、辨別同異。那位大臣指鹿為馬、看看誰敢反駁他的故事，講的是權力敗壞了用法；而荀子要求「正名」，針對的正是這種事。

Objection 3 · 反駁

Some things cannot be put into words at all. A theory that ties meaning to public use has no room for them.

有些東西根本無法用言語表達。一個把意義綁在公共用法上的理論，沒有位置給它們。

Reply · 回應

Wittgenstein agreed that there are such things and spent his early book marking the limit: “Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.” The later view does not deny what Laozi and Zhuangzi say. It gives it a different description. Words are tools for shared activities, and where an experience is tied to no shared activity, the tools have nothing to grip. Much of the tradition of pointing, silence, paradox, and the fish trap can be read as a set of techniques for that situation.

維根斯坦同意有這樣的東西；而他早年的那本書，就是在標出那條界線：「凡是不可說的，就必須保持沉默。」後期的看法並不否認老子與莊子所說的。它給了另一種描述。字詞是用於共同活動的工具；當一種經驗不與任何共同的活動相連時，這些工具就沒有東西可以抓。指點、沉默、弔詭與「筌」的那個傳統，有很大一部分可以讀成一套針對這種處境的技巧。

Terms of art · 哲學術語

The vocabulary of the subject 這門學問的用語

reference指稱

G. Bedeutung (Frege)

The thing a word or phrase stands for.

一個字或詞組所代表的那樣東西。

sense意義（涵義）

G. Sinn (Frege)

The way a word presents what it stands for. Two names for one thing can differ in sense.

一個字呈現其所指的方式。同一樣東西的兩個名字，涵義可以不同。

ostensive definition實指定義

L. ostendere ‘to show’

Explaining a word by pointing at an example.

藉著指出一個例子來解釋一個字。

language-game語言遊戲

G. Sprachspiel

Wittgenstein’s term for an activity in which words are used, such as giving orders, telling jokes, or praying.

維根斯坦的用語：使用字詞的一種活動，例如下命令、說笑話或祈禱。

private language私人語言

A language whose words refer to what only the speaker can know. Wittgenstein argued that there could be no such thing.

一種語言，其中的字詞指的是只有說話者能知道的東西。維根斯坦論證說，不可能有這樣的東西。

convention約定、慣例

L. convenire ‘to come together’

A practice that holds because people follow it and expect others to.

一種做法；它之所以成立，是因為人們遵循它，並且預期別人也會。

rectifying names正名

Ch. 正名 zhèngmíng

The Confucian demand that names be used so as to fit the realities they are meant to mark.

儒家的要求：名的使用，要與它們所要標示的實相符。

School of Names名家

Ch. 名家 míngjiā

The early Chinese thinkers, such as Gongsun Long and Hui Shi, who studied paradoxes of language.

中國早期研究語言弔詭的思想家，例如公孫龍與惠施。

Write · 你怎麼想？

  1. Choose a word in Chinese or Taiwanese that has no good English translation. Explain its meaning to an English speaker by describing how and when it is used, without giving a definition. About 150 words.

    選一個中文或台語裡沒有好的英文翻譯的詞。向一位說英文的人解釋它的意思；方法是描述它怎麼用、什麼時候用，而不要下定義。約 150 個字。

  2. You cannot feel anyone else’s pain. In 150 words, say how you know what your friend means when she says that her tooth hurts.

    你感覺不到任何別人的痛。用 150 個字說明：當你的朋友說她牙痛的時候，你是怎麼知道她的意思的？

  3. Xunzi wants names kept stable. Zhuangzi wants to forget them. Which attitude would you want in a judge, and which in a poet? Give reasons.

    荀子要名保持穩定。莊子要把它們忘掉。你希望一位法官有哪一種態度？一位詩人呢？說明理由。

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

A view from the Dharma 佛法怎麼看

☸

The Buddha treated words as tools and warned against two ways of misusing them. One discourse puts the rule briefly: “Don’t insist on popular definitions and don’t overstep normal labels.” The same thing, it explains, is called a cup in one district and a bowl, a jar, or a dish in others. A person who insists on his own word and says, “This is the only truth, anything else is futile,” has made the first mistake. The right course is to speak as the people there speak, thinking, “It seems that the venerables are referring to this.” The second mistake is to throw away ordinary usage because one has seen through it. The Buddha held that words like “I” and “person” name nothing lasting, and he went on using them. A verse says of the awakened that “skillful, understanding the world’s labels, they’d use these terms as mere expressions.”

佛陀把字詞當作工具，並且警告人不要以兩種方式誤用它們。有一部經把這條規則說得很簡短：「不要執著於地方的說法；也不要逾越通行的名稱。」經裡解釋：同一樣東西，在一個地方叫杯，在別的地方叫碗、叫罐、叫盤。一個人堅持自己的那個字，說「只有這個才是真的，其他都是虛妄」，就犯了第一種錯。正確的做法，是照著當地人的說法來說，心裡想：「這幾位尊者指的似乎是這個。」第二種錯，是因為自己看穿了日常的用法，就把它丟掉。佛陀主張「我」與「人」這樣的字，並不指稱任何持久的東西；而他照樣使用它們。有一首偈這樣說覺悟的人：「善巧的人，了解世間的名稱；他們只把這些詞當作說法來用。」

This is close to Wittgenstein and goes a step beyond him. Both hold that a word has meaning through its use in a shared practice and that trouble starts when we take the word to name a hidden thing. For Wittgenstein the trouble is philosophical confusion. For the Buddha it is suffering. To hear “I” and suppose a fixed owner of experience behind it is, in his analysis, the root of craving and fear. The later tradition made this into a doctrine. Things exist as dependent designations: “chariot” is a name applied to wheels, axle, and frame, and “person” a name applied to body and mind, and neither names an extra thing (Dharma and Western Philosophy D3). Zhuangzi’s fish trap has its Buddhist twins in the raft that is left on the shore and the finger that points at the moon.

這很接近維根斯坦，而且比他多走了一步。兩人都主張：一個字是經由它在共同實踐裡的使用而有意義的；而當我們以為那個字是某樣隱藏的東西的名字時，麻煩就開始了。對維根斯坦來說，那個麻煩是哲學上的混亂。對佛陀來說，它是苦。照他的分析，聽到「我」就認定它背後有一個固定的經驗擁有者，是渴愛與恐懼的根源。後來的傳統把這變成了一個教義。事物是作為「依緣的假名」而存在的：「車」是加在輪、軸與車架上的一個名稱；「人」是加在身與心上的一個名稱；而兩者都不是另外一樣東西的名字（佛法與西方哲學 D3）。莊子的筌，在佛教裡有它的孿生兄弟：留在岸邊的筏，以及指著月亮的手指。
