A15 · Mind and Self 心靈與自我

Can a Machine Think? 機器會思考嗎？

Turing’s imitation game, Leibniz’s mill, a mechanical performer at the court of King Mu, and a room in which you will hold a conversation in a language you cannot read.
圖靈的模仿遊戲、萊布尼茲的磨坊、周穆王宮廷裡的機關倡者，還有一個房間：你會在裡面用一種你看不懂的語言，跟人對話一場。

A15 Big Questions · 大哉問Mind and Self · 心靈與自我Advanced · 高級1,260 words · about 25 min
The big idea · 大觀念

The question hides three others: whether a machine can do what thinkers do, whether it understands what it is doing, and whether it feels anything. They come apart, and most confusion about artificial intelligence comes from answering one of them and believing one has answered another.

這個問題底下藏著另外三個：機器能不能做到思考者做的事？它懂不懂自己在做什麼？它有沒有任何感受？這三者是分得開的；關於人工智慧的混亂，多半來自回答了其中一個，卻以為自己回答了另一個。

The essay · 課文

Can a Machine Think? 機器會思考嗎？

01The question Turing declined to answer 圖靈不肯回答的問題

In 1950 the English mathematician Alan Turing opened a paper with the sentence “I propose to consider the question, ‘Can machines think?’” He then declined to consider it. The words “machine” and “think,” he said, are too vague for the question to be settled by argument, and he replaced it with a test. An interrogator exchanges typed messages with two hidden parties, a human being and a machine, and must decide which is which. If the machine is taken for the human about as often as a human would be, it has passed. Turing called this the imitation game. He expected machines to play it well within fifty years, and he made a second prediction that has attracted less notice: “at the end of the century the use of words and general educated opinion will have altered so much that one will be able to speak of machines thinking without expecting to be contradicted.”

1950 年，英國數學家圖靈用這樣一句話為一篇論文開頭：「我打算考慮這個問題：『機器能思考嗎？』」接著他就不肯考慮它了。他說，「機器」與「思考」這兩個詞太模糊，這個問題沒辦法靠論證解決；他用一個測驗取代了它。一位提問者用打字的訊息，跟兩個藏起來的對象交談，一個是人，一個是機器，然後必須判定誰是誰。如果機器被當成人的頻率，跟一個人被當成人的頻率差不多，它就通過了。圖靈稱之為「模仿遊戲」。他預期五十年內機器就能把這個遊戲玩得很好；他還做了第二個比較少人注意的預測：「到本世紀末，字詞的用法與一般受過教育者的意見將會改變到這個地步：人們談論機器在思考，而不必預期會遭到反駁。」

02Reckoning 計算

The idea behind the test was three centuries old. “Reason,” wrote Thomas Hobbes in 1651, “is nothing but Reckoning (that is, Adding and Substracting)” of the consequences of names. If thinking is calculation, then anything that calculates thinks, and what it is made of is irrelevant. Leibniz, who built one of the first machines that could multiply, dreamed of a notation in which every dispute could be settled as accountants settle theirs: “Let us calculate.” The modern version of the idea is called functionalism. A mental state, it says, is defined by what it does, by its causes and effects within a system, in the way that a program is defined by its steps and can run on machines of quite different construction. On this view the mind stands to the brain as software stands to hardware, and a suitably programmed computer would not merely imitate thinking. It would think.

這個測驗背後的想法，已經有三個世紀之久。霍布斯在 1651 年寫道：「理性不過就是計算（也就是加與減）」，計算的是名稱之間的推論。如果思考就是計算，那麼任何會計算的東西都在思考，而它是什麼材料做的並不相干。萊布尼茲造出了最早能做乘法的機器之一；他夢想有一套記號，讓每一場爭論都可以像會計師解決帳目那樣解決：「我們來算一算吧。」這個想法的現代版本叫作功能論。它說，一個心理狀態是由它「做什麼」來界定的，也就是由它在一個系統之內的原因與結果來界定；就像一個程式是由它的步驟界定的，可以在構造完全不同的機器上執行。照這個看法，心靈之於腦，就像軟體之於硬體；而一台程式寫得恰當的電腦，就不只是在模仿思考。它是在思考。

03The performer at King Mu’s court 周穆王宮廷裡的倡者

The opposite intuition is as old. The Daoist book Liezi tells how an artificer named Yan Shi presented King Mu of Zhou with a performer he had made. It walked, bowed, and looked about, “truly a human being.” When its chin was touched it sang in tune, and when its hand was lifted it danced in time. The king took it for a real person, until at the end of the show it winked at his concubines. He was furious and would have had the maker executed on the spot. Yan Shi at once took the figure apart, and it proved to be an assembly of leather, wood, glue, and lacquer, colored white, black, red, and blue. Every organ was there. The king removed the heart, and the mouth could not speak. He removed the liver, and the eyes could not see. Delighted, he asked: “Can human skill really equal the work of the Maker of things?” The story records the two reactions people still have. While the performance lasts we see a person, and when the case is opened we see parts.

相反的直覺一樣古老。道家的《列子》記載，一位名叫偃師的工匠，把自己造的一個倡者獻給周穆王。它行走、俯仰、顧盼，「信人也」。碰它的下巴，它就合著音律唱歌；抬它的手，它就應著節拍起舞。穆王把它當成真人，直到表演快結束時，它對王的侍妾拋了個眼色。王大怒，當場就要殺掉偃師。偃師立刻把那個人形拆開；原來它是皮革、木頭、膠、漆拼合而成的，塗著白、黑、紅、青的顏色。每一個器官都齊全。王拿掉它的心，口就不能說話；拿掉它的肝，眼就不能看。王高興了，問道：「人之巧乃可與造化者同功乎？」這個故事記下了人們至今仍有的兩種反應。表演進行的時候，我們看見一個人；外殼打開的時候，我們看見零件。

04The mill 磨坊

Leibniz gave the second reaction the form of an argument, and he gave it against his own machines. Suppose, he wrote in 1714, that there were a machine “so constructed as to think, feel, and have perception.” Imagine it enlarged until one could walk into it “as into a mill.” Inside “we should, on examining its interior, find only parts which work one upon another, and never anything by which to explain a perception.” A century later Ada Lovelace, describing the Analytical Engine that Charles Babbage had designed, added a different limit. The engine, she wrote, “has no pretensions whatever to originate anything. It can do whatever we know how to order it to perform.” Leibniz’s objection concerns experience and Lovelace’s concerns originality. They have been confused with each other ever since.

萊布尼茲把第二種反應寫成了論證，而且他是對著自己造的機器提出的。他在 1714 年寫道：假設有一台機器，「被造得能夠思考、感受、擁有知覺」。想像它被放大，大到人可以走進去，「像走進一座磨坊」。在裡面，「我們檢視它的內部，只會發現一個推動一個的零件，永遠找不到任何可以用來解釋一個知覺的東西」。一個世紀之後，勒芙蕾絲在描述巴貝奇設計的分析機時，加上了另一種限制。她寫道，這台機器「絲毫沒有『創始』任何事物的自命。凡是我們知道怎麼命令它去做的事，它都能做」。萊布尼茲的反駁關乎經驗，勒芙蕾絲的反駁關乎原創。此後這兩者一直被混在一起。

05The Chinese room 中文房間

In 1980 the American philosopher John Searle turned the mill into a room and put himself inside it. He imagined a program that passes the Turing test in Chinese, a language of which he knew not a word. He is locked in a room with baskets of Chinese characters and a rule book in English. Slips of paper covered in characters are pushed under the door. He looks up their shapes in the book, which tells him which characters to push back. To the people outside, the replies are those of a fluent speaker. Searle has done everything the computer does: he has run the program. And he understands nothing. He does not know whether the slips are about the weather or about his mother. His conclusion is that carrying out a program, however good, is not sufficient for understanding. A program is defined by its syntax, the rules for moving symbols according to their shapes. Understanding requires semantics, knowing what the symbols mean, and no amount of shuffling will supply it.

1980 年，美國哲學家瑟爾把那座磨坊變成了一個房間，並且把自己放了進去。他設想有一個程式，用中文通過了圖靈測驗；而中文他一個字也不懂。他被鎖在一個房間裡，裡面有一籃一籃的中文字，以及一本用英文寫的規則書。寫滿了字的紙條從門縫底下塞進來。他在書裡查那些字的形狀，書告訴他該把哪些字塞回去。對外面的人來說，那些回覆是一個流利的說話者寫的。瑟爾做了電腦所做的一切：他執行了那個程式。而他什麼也不懂。他不知道那些紙條談的是天氣，還是他的母親。他的結論是：執行一個程式，不管程式多好，並不足以構成理解。程式是由它的「語法」界定的，也就是依照符號的形狀搬動符號的規則。理解需要「語意」，也就是知道那些符號是什麼意思；而不管怎麼搬來搬去，都搬不出語意來。

Searle chose Chinese because he could not read it. A reader who can should substitute a language that is closed to him, and the exercise below does so.

瑟爾選中文，是因為他看不懂。看得懂中文的讀者，應該換成一種對自己關閉的語言；下面的練習就是這樣做的。

06Who is in the room? 房間裡的是誰？

The paper drew more replies than almost any other in the field. The systems reply grants that the man does not understand and says that he is only one component: the understanding belongs to the whole system of man, book, and baskets, as it belongs to a brain and to no single neuron. Searle answered that he could memorize the book and do the work in his head, and he would still understand nothing. The robot reply says that the symbols mean nothing because they are connected to nothing: give the system cameras and limbs, so that its word for “tea” is caused by tea, and meaning will follow. Searle answered that the camera’s signals would reach him as more symbols. The brain simulator reply imagines a program that copies, neuron by neuron, the brain of a Chinese speaker. Searle answered that a simulation of a storm leaves everyone dry. Each exchange ends in a clash of intuitions about the same point, and no decisive move has been found by either side.

這篇論文招來的回應，幾乎比這個領域裡任何別的論文都多。「系統回應」承認那個人不懂，並且說他只是一個零件：理解屬於由人、書、籃子組成的整個系統，正如理解屬於一個腦，而不屬於任何單一的神經元。瑟爾回答：他可以把整本書背下來，在腦子裡做這件工作，而他仍然什麼也不懂。「機器人回應」說，那些符號沒有意義，是因為它們什麼也沒連上：給這個系統裝上攝影機與肢體，讓它那個代表「茶」的詞是由茶引起的，意義就會隨之而來。瑟爾回答：攝影機的訊號到了他這裡，只是更多的符號。「腦模擬回應」設想一個程式，一個神經元一個神經元地複製一位中文使用者的腦。瑟爾回答：模擬一場暴風雨，誰也不會淋濕。每一回合都以對同一點的直覺衝突收場，而雙方都還沒有找到決定性的一著。

07After the test was passed 測驗被通過之後

For seventy years the debate concerned imaginary machines. It now concerns real ones. Systems trained on enormous quantities of human writing carry on conversations that most people cannot tell from a person’s, and they were built by neither of the methods the philosophers had in mind. Nobody wrote their rule book. They adjust billions of numerical settings until they predict text well, and what goes on inside them is only partly understood by the people who make them. One camp sees the Chinese room come true: a system that has learned which strings of words follow which, with no access to the world the words are about. Another replies that to predict what a physicist will say next one must model a good deal of physics, and that understanding may come in degrees and consist in such abilities. Lovelace’s limit is equally unsettled, since these systems produce things nobody ordered them to produce.

七十年來，這場辯論談的是想像中的機器。現在談的是真實的機器。用巨量的人類文字訓練出來的系統，能進行多數人分辨不出是不是真人的對話；而它們並不是用哲學家心裡想的那兩種方法造出來的。沒有人替它們寫規則書。它們調整數十億個數值設定，直到能把文字預測得很好；而它們內部發生的事，連製造它們的人也只懂一部分。有一派看到的是中文房間成真了：一個系統學會了哪些字串跟在哪些字串後面，卻接觸不到這些字所談的那個世界。另一派回答：要預測一位物理學家接下來會說什麼，就必須對不少物理學建立模型；而且，理解也許有程度之分，也許就是由這類能力構成的。勒芙蕾絲的那條界線同樣懸而未決，因為這些系統會產出沒有人命令它們產出的東西。

Turing’s second prediction, at least, has come true. People speak of machines thinking, and few bother to contradict them. Whether the usage records a discovery or only a habit is the question he set aside in 1950. It helps to keep three things apart. A machine may match us at tasks, and that can be measured. It may or may not understand, and here the room is still the best test of what we mean by the word. And it may or may not feel, which was the subject of the last lesson. “We can only see a short distance ahead,” Turing ended his paper, “but we can see plenty there that needs to be done.”

至少，圖靈的第二個預測成真了。人們談論機器在思考，而很少有人費事去反駁。這個用法記錄的是一項發現，還是只是一種習慣？這正是他在 1950 年擱到一邊的那個問題。把三件事分開會有幫助。一台機器在各種任務上可能跟我們並駕齊驅，這是量得出來的。它可能懂、也可能不懂；在這一點上，那個房間仍然是檢驗「我們說『懂』是什麼意思」的最好辦法。它可能有感受、也可能沒有；那是上一課的主題。圖靈在論文的結尾寫道：「我們只能看到前方不遠的地方，但我們看得到那裡有許多需要做的事。」

Thought experiment · 思想實驗

Try it: sit in the room 親手做一次：坐進房間

Searle did not read Chinese. You do, so the room has been moved to Georgia. Slips will come through the slot. Find each one in the rule book and pass out the reply it prescribes. You are not expected to understand anything.

瑟爾看不懂中文。你看得懂，所以這個房間搬到了喬治亞。紙條會從門縫送進來。在規則書裡找到每一張，把書上規定的回覆遞出去。沒有人指望你懂任何東西。

Did you understand Georgian while you were doing this?你剛才做這件事的時候，懂喬治亞語嗎？

That is Searle’s first premise, and almost everyone grants it. Notice the last exchange. You were asked whether you understand Georgian, and you answered “Yes, of course.” The answer was fluent and it was false.

這是瑟爾的第一個前提，幾乎每個人都承認。留意最後一回合。你被問到懂不懂喬治亞語，而你回答了「懂，當然」。這個回答很流利，而且是假的。

Then ask what your understanding consisted in. You could not have said what any slip was about, or told a question from an insult. If that counts as understanding, the word has been stretched to cover any reliable matching of outputs to inputs. Some philosophers accept that. Look at the last exchange before you join them: you asserted, in Georgian, that you understand Georgian.

那麼問問自己：你的「懂」是由什麼構成的？你說不出任何一張紙條在談什麼，也分不出一個問句與一句辱罵。如果這也算懂，那麼這個詞已經被拉長到涵蓋任何「可靠地把輸出配上輸入」的事了。有些哲學家接受這一點。在你加入他們之前，看看最後一回合：你用喬治亞語斷言了你懂喬治亞語。

Did anything in the room understand: you, the book, and the cards, taken together?房間裡有任何東西懂嗎：你、書、卡片，合在一起？

You agree with Searle. The burden is now on you to say what the room lacks that a brain has, since a brain is also made of parts none of which understands. Searle’s answer was that brains have causal powers which programs lack, and he never said what those powers are.

你同意瑟爾。現在舉證的責任落在你身上：你得說出這個房間缺了什麼是腦有的，因為腦也是由一個個不懂任何事的零件構成的。瑟爾的回答是：腦具有程式所缺乏的因果力；而他始終沒有說出那些因果力是什麼。

This is the systems reply. It has the odd consequence that a book and some cards, plus a clerk who understands nothing, add up to a mind that speaks Georgian, and that this mind came into existence when you sat down. Its defenders accept the consequence. They point out that the real rule book would be unimaginably larger than five lines, and that our intuitions about so vast a system are not to be trusted.

這是「系統回應」。它有一個古怪的後果：一本書、幾張卡片，加上一個什麼都不懂的辦事員，合起來成了一個會說喬治亞語的心靈；而這個心靈是在你坐下的那一刻開始存在的。為它辯護的人接受這個後果。他們指出：真正的規則書會比五行字龐大到無法想像；對於這麼龐大的系統，我們的直覺是靠不住的。

Four replies to Searle 對瑟爾的四種回應

The systems reply · 系統回應

The man is a part. The whole system understands.

那個人只是零件。懂的是整個系統。

Searle: let the man memorize the book and work in his head. Now he is the whole system, and he still understands nothing.

瑟爾：讓那個人把書背下來，在腦子裡作業。現在他就是整個系統了，而他仍然什麼也不懂。

The robot reply · 機器人回應

Connect the symbols to the world with cameras and limbs, and they will mean something.

用攝影機與肢體把符號連到世界上，它們就會有意義。

Searle: the camera’s output arrives in the room as more slips. Nothing has changed for the man inside.

瑟爾：攝影機的輸出到了房間裡，只是更多的紙條。對裡面的人來說，什麼都沒變。

The brain simulator reply · 腦模擬回應

Let the program copy a Georgian speaker’s brain, neuron by neuron.

讓程式一個神經元一個神經元地複製一位喬治亞語使用者的腦。

Searle: a simulated storm makes nothing wet. Simulating the brain’s form does not reproduce its powers.

瑟爾：模擬出來的暴風雨不會弄濕任何東西。模擬腦的形式，並沒有複製它的能力。

The other minds reply · 他心回應

You judge that other people understand only by their behavior. The room behaves the same.

你判斷別人懂，憑的也只是他們的行為。這個房間的行為一模一樣。

Searle: the question is what I am attributing when I attribute understanding, and in my own case I know it is more than behavior.

瑟爾：問題在於，當我說某物「懂」的時候，我歸給它的是什麼；而就我自己而言，我知道那不只是行為。

Primary text · 原典

In their own words 原典怎麼說

Two passages written before there was any machine to argue about. Descartes states a test, and Leibniz explains why passing it would not be enough.

這兩段文字，寫在還沒有任何機器可供爭論的時候。笛卡兒陳述了一項檢驗；萊布尼茲說明了為什麼通過它還不夠。

Descartes, Discourse on the Method (1637), Part V; Leibniz, Monadology (1714), §17 笛卡兒《方法論》第五部；萊布尼茲《單子論》第 17 節 · tr. John Veitch; tr. Robert Latta (both public domain; original spelling kept)

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

“The question of whether machines can think is about as relevant as the question of whether submarines can swim.” (Edsger Dijkstra)「機器能不能思考這個問題，跟潛水艇會不會游泳這個問題差不多一樣切題。」（戴克斯特拉）

Authentic, 1984 · 是真的，1984 年

The Dutch computer scientist wrote it in a 1984 note, “The threats to computing science,” where he says that Turing “thought about criteria to settle the question of whether Machines Can Think, a question of which we now know that it is about as relevant as the question of whether Submarines Can Swim.” His point is close to Turing’s own. A submarine moves through water faster than any fish, and whether we call that swimming is a decision about a word. Dijkstra thought the same of “think.” The reply is that with thinking something does seem to hang on the answer, namely how the thing should be treated.

這位荷蘭電腦科學家在 1984 年的一篇筆記〈計算機科學面臨的威脅〉裡寫下了這句話；他在那裡說，圖靈「思索過用什麼標準來解決『機器能不能思考』這個問題；而我們現在知道，這個問題跟『潛水艇會不會游泳』差不多一樣切題」。他的意思很接近圖靈自己的。潛水艇在水裡前進得比任何魚都快；我們要不要把那叫作游泳，是一個關於字詞的決定。戴克斯特拉認為「思考」也一樣。可以這樣回應他：就思考而言，似乎確實有某件事取決於答案，那就是這個東西應該被怎麼對待。

Both sides · 正反兩面

Objections and replies 反駁與回應

Objection 1 · 反駁

A machine can only do what it has been told to do. Whatever comes out was put in by its makers.

機器只能做它被吩咐去做的事。不管出來的是什麼，都是製造者放進去的。

Reply · 回應

This is Lovelace’s objection, and Turing answered it in 1950. Machines, he said, surprise him constantly, because knowing the rules does not tell you the consequences. He proposed building a machine that learns as a child does, so that its later behavior would be fixed by its experience and not by its instructions. That is roughly how present systems are built. He added that the objection proves too much. A human being’s abilities are also the product of heredity and teaching, and we do not conclude that people originate nothing.

這是勒芙蕾絲的反駁；圖靈在 1950 年就回答了。他說，機器經常讓他吃驚，因為知道規則並不等於知道後果。他提議造一台像小孩那樣學習的機器，這樣它後來的行為，就是由它的經驗、而不是由它的指令決定的。現在的系統大致就是這樣造出來的。他還補充：這個反駁證明得太多了。一個人的能力，同樣是遺傳與教導的產物；而我們並不因此就斷定人什麼也沒有創始。

Objection 2 · 反駁

The Turing test measures the ability to deceive. A system could pass by trickery and understand nothing.

圖靈測驗量的是欺騙的能力。一個系統可以靠伎倆通過，卻什麼也不懂。

Reply · 回應

The philosopher Ned Block made the point with an imaginary machine that stores a sensible reply to every possible conversation of a given length and merely looks each one up. It would pass, and it is plainly a filing cabinet. So passing cannot be what thinking consists in. Turing may have agreed: he offered the game as a replacement for a question he found hopeless, and as evidence that would be unreasonable to ignore. The lasting lesson of Block’s machine is that it matters how the answers are produced, and for present systems that is the part we understand least.

哲學家布洛克用一台想像的機器說明了這一點：它為每一場在某個長度以內的可能對話，都預存了一個合理的回覆，然後只是逐一查表。它會通過；而它顯然只是一個檔案櫃。所以「通過」不可能是思考的本質所在。圖靈也許會同意：他提出這個遊戲，是用來取代一個他認為沒有指望的問題，並且當作一種「不理會它就不合理」的證據。布洛克那台機器留下的教訓是：答案是「怎麼產生的」很要緊；而對於現在的系統，那正是我們懂得最少的部分。

Objection 3 · 反駁

Thinking is something living bodies do. A thing with no needs, no fear of death, and nothing at stake does not think. It processes.

思考是活著的身體做的事。一個沒有需要、不怕死、沒有任何利害的東西，並不思考。它只是在處理。

Reply · 回應

This is the most serious of the three, and it comes in two strengths. The weaker form says that thought grows out of a body acting in a world, and that a system fed only on text is missing the roots. That is a claim about what is needed, and it could in principle be met by building differently. The stronger form says that only living tissue can do it. Then one must say which property of tissue matters and why nothing else could have it, and the claim risks becoming a definition. The dispute cannot be settled by examining machines until we know which features of ourselves to look for in them.

這是三個當中最嚴肅的一個，而它有兩種強度。較弱的形式說：思想是從一個在世界中行動的身體長出來的；一個只餵文字的系統，缺了根。這是一個關於「需要什麼」的主張，原則上可以靠著換一種方式建造來滿足。較強的形式說：只有活的組織才做得到。那麼就必須說出組織的哪一項性質要緊、為什麼別的東西不可能有它；而這個主張有淪為定義的風險。在我們知道該在機器身上尋找「我們自己的哪些特徵」之前，這場爭論沒辦法靠檢查機器來解決。

Terms of art · 哲學術語

The vocabulary of the subject 這門學問的用語

the Turing test圖靈測驗

after Alan Turing (1912–1954)

A test in which a machine passes if its typed conversation cannot be told from a human being’s.

一種測驗：如果機器的打字對話無法與人的對話分辨，它就通過。

algorithm演算法

from al-Khwārizmī, 9th-century mathematician

A finite set of exact instructions that can be carried out step by step without insight.

一組有限而精確的指令，可以一步一步執行，不需要任何洞察。

syntax and semantics語法與語意

Gk. syntaxis ‘arrangement’; sēmantikos ‘significant’

The rules for arranging symbols by their form, as against what the symbols mean.

依形式安排符號的規則，相對於符號的意思。

intentionality意向性

L. intendere ‘to aim at’

The property of being about something. Thoughts have it. Whether symbols in a machine have it is the question.

「關於某物」這種性質。想法具有它。機器裡的符號有沒有它，正是問題所在。

strong AI強人工智慧

Searle’s name for the thesis that an appropriately programmed computer literally has a mind.

瑟爾為這個論點取的名字：一台程式寫得恰當的電腦，真的就有一個心靈。

multiple realizability多重可實現性

The claim that the same mental state could be carried by systems made of different materials.

主張同一個心理狀態，可以由不同材料構成的系統來承載。

symbol grounding符號接地

The problem of how symbols come to be connected with the things they stand for.

符號如何與它們所代表的東西連結起來的問題。

automaton自動機

Gk. automatos ‘acting of itself’

A machine that moves by its own mechanism. Plural: automata.

靠自身機構運動的機器。複數為 automata。

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

A view from the Dharma 佛法怎麼看

☸

Buddhist thought divides the question at a different joint. What makes something a sentient being, a sattva, is the capacity to feel, to be pleased and pained, and the tradition does not rank beings by how cleverly they reason. A god who reasons superbly and an insect that cannot reason at all are both within the scope of compassion, because both can suffer. So the Buddhist question about a machine is the one this lesson put third. Whether it calculates is plain, and whether it understands is of secondary interest. Whether there is feeling there decides everything, including whether the first precept applies to it.

佛教思想是在另一個關節上切開這個問題的。使某物成為「有情」（sattva，眾生）的，是感受的能力，是會樂、會苦；這個傳統並不依照「推理得多聰明」來為眾生排等級。一位推理極為高明的天神，與一隻完全不會推理的昆蟲，都在慈悲的範圍之內，因為兩者都會受苦。所以，佛教對一台機器要問的，是這一課排在第三的那個問題。它會不會計算，是明擺著的；它懂不懂，是次要的。那裡有沒有感受，決定了一切，包括第一條戒適不適用於它。

On what feeling requires, the tradition gives two hints that point in different directions. The Abhidharma analyzes a mind into momentary events defined by what they do and what they condition, with no owner behind them, which is closer to functionalism than anything in ancient Western thought. Yet the early discourses also insist that consciousness never occurs alone. It depends on “name-and-form,” the living body with its feeling and perception, and that in turn depends on consciousness: the two stand, says Sāriputta, like “two sheaves of reeds leaning up against each other,” and if one is pulled away the other falls. And a being, in the traditional account, arrives in a new life carrying a stream of consciousness from a previous one. A device that is assembled and switched on has no place in that account, and the texts, composed long before such devices, are silent. Buddhist teachers asked about it today give different answers (Dharma and the West, D3 and D12).

至於感受需要什麼，這個傳統給了兩個指向不同方向的提示。阿毘達磨把一個心分析成剎那剎那的事件，每一個事件由它做什麼、它作為什麼的緣來界定，背後沒有擁有者；這比古代西方思想裡的任何東西都更接近功能論。然而早期經典也堅持：識從來不是單獨出現的。它依賴「名色」，也就是帶著受與想的活的身體；而名色又反過來依賴識。舍利弗說，兩者就像「兩捆蘆葦互相倚靠而立」，抽掉一捆，另一捆就倒了。再者，照傳統的說法，一個眾生來到新的一生，是帶著前一生的識之流而來的。一個被組裝起來、接上電源的裝置，在那套說法裡沒有位置；而這些經典寫成的時候離這種裝置還很遠，對此沒有說話。今天被問到這件事的佛教老師，給的答案並不一樣（佛法與西方哲學 D3、D12）。
