Philosophers · 哲學家 · The 1900s to Today

Peter Singer 彼得・辛格 · Peter Albert David Singer

The grandson of victims of the Holocaust who asked a fellow student why he would not eat the spaghetti, wrote the book that began the modern animal movement, told the comfortable that they were letting children drown, and has been picketed on three continents for following his arguments where they led.
納粹大屠殺受難者的外孫。他問了一位同學為什麼不吃那盤義大利麵，然後寫出了開啟現代動物運動的那本書；他告訴生活安逸的人，他們正在眼看著孩子溺水；又因為論證走到哪裡他就跟到哪裡，而在三個洲被人抗議。

Life · 生平

A life in three scenes 三幕人生

Vienna and Melbourne 維也納與墨爾本

Singer’s parents were Jews from Vienna who left for Australia in 1938, after the German annexation of Austria. Three of his grandparents did not get out. His father’s parents were deported to Łódź and were not heard of again. His mother’s father, David Oppenheim, a teacher of classics who had worked with both Freud and Adler, died in the camp at Theresienstadt. Singer was born in Melbourne in 1946 and grew up in a prosperous household without religion. Many years later he wrote a book about the grandfather he never met.

辛格的父母是維也納的猶太人；1938 年德國併吞奧地利之後，他們去了澳洲。他的祖父母、外祖父母四人當中，有三位沒能逃出來。他的祖父母被遣送到羅茲，從此音訊全無。他的外祖父大衛・奧本海姆是古典學教師，曾經與佛洛伊德、阿德勒兩人都共事過；他死在特雷津的集中營。辛格 1946 年生於墨爾本，在一個富裕而沒有宗教的家庭裡長大。許多年後，他為這位從未謀面的外祖父寫了一本書。

He studied law, history, and philosophy at the University of Melbourne and went to Oxford in 1969. His teacher there was R. M. Hare, who held that moral judgments must be universal: if I say that an act is right for me, I must accept that it is right for anyone in the same position, including when I am on the receiving end. Singer took from Hare the habit of asking, of any moral view, whether its holder would accept it from the other side.

他在墨爾本大學讀法律、歷史與哲學，1969 年到了牛津。他在那裡的老師是黑爾。黑爾主張道德判斷必須是可普遍化的：如果我說某個行為對我而言是對的，我就得接受它對任何處在同樣位置的人都是對的；包括當我是承受的那一方的時候。辛格從黑爾那裡學到一個習慣：對任何一個道德觀點都問一問，持有它的人，站到另一邊去的時候還接不接受它。

The spaghetti 那盤義大利麵

In 1970 he had lunch at Balliol College with a Canadian graduate student, Richard Keshen, who asked whether the sauce on the spaghetti contained meat and, on hearing that it did, took a salad. Singer asked why. He said later that he had never met a vegetarian who gave an answer he could understand and relate to. Within a few weeks he and his wife Renata had stopped eating meat. In 1973 he reviewed a collection of essays on animals for The New York Review of Books under the title “Animal Liberation,” and in 1975 he published the book of that name.

1970 年，他在貝里歐學院與一位加拿大研究生理查・凱申吃午飯。凱申問義大利麵的醬裡有沒有肉；聽說有，就拿了沙拉。辛格問他為什麼。他後來說，他從來沒有遇過一個素食者，給出的答案是他聽得懂、也能產生共鳴的。幾個星期之內，他與妻子蕾娜塔就不再吃肉了。1973 年，他為《紐約書評》評論了一本談動物的文集，標題是〈動物解放〉；1975 年，他出版了以此為名的書。

The book made no appeal to a love of animals, and Singer said that he had no particular fondness for them. It offered an argument and a great deal of evidence about laboratories and factory farms, and it ended with recipes. It has been continuously in print and has been translated into dozens of languages. Many of the people who founded animal organizations in the following decades have said that reading it was what set them going. Singer has said that its effects were smaller than he had hoped, since far more animals are farmed industrially now than in 1975.

這本書並不訴諸對動物的喜愛；辛格說過，他對動物並沒有特別的感情。它提出的是一個論證，以及大量關於實驗室與工廠化農場的證據；書的最後還附了食譜。它一直在印行，被譯成了幾十種語言。在後來的幾十年裡創辦動物保護組織的人，有許多都說，是讀了這本書才開始行動的。辛格說過，它的效果比他期望的小；因為現在被工業化飼養的動物，比 1975 年多得多。

The public philosopher 公共哲學家

He returned to Australia in 1977 and taught at Monash University for two decades, where he founded a center for bioethics. His textbook Practical Ethics applied the same reasoning to abortion, euthanasia, and the treatment of newborn infants with severe conditions. These chapters made him more enemies than the ones on animals. In Germany and Austria in 1989 and after, his lectures were canceled or shouted down by protesters who heard in them an echo of the Nazi killing of the disabled. He replied that his family’s history gave him every reason to take that charge seriously, and that he was arguing for the choices of parents and patients. He rejected any policy of the state. When Princeton appointed him to a chair in 1999, disability groups blocked the entrance to the building.

1977 年他回到澳洲，在蒙納許大學教了二十年書，並且在那裡創辦了一個生命倫理學中心。他的教科書《實踐倫理學》，把同樣的推理用在墮胎、安樂死，以及如何對待有嚴重病況的新生兒上。這幾章為他樹立的敵人，比談動物的那幾章更多。1989 年及其後，在德國與奧地利，他的演講被取消，或者被抗議者的吼聲壓過；抗議者在其中聽到了納粹殺害身心障礙者的回聲。他回答：他的家族史使他完全有理由認真看待這項指控；而他所主張的，是父母與病人的選擇。任何由國家推行的政策，他都反對。1999 年普林斯頓聘他擔任講座教授的時候，身心障礙者團體堵住了那棟建築的入口。

The other half of his public work began with an article written in 1971, during a famine in East Bengal, which argued that people in rich countries are doing wrong when they spend on luxuries money that could save lives. The lesson on strangers set out the argument. Forty years later it became the basis of a movement called effective altruism, which tries to find out where a donation does the most good. Singer has given a substantial part of his income away for most of his life. He gave his last lecture at Princeton in 2023.

他的公共工作的另一半，始於 1971 年東孟加拉饑荒期間寫的一篇文章。文章主張：富裕國家的人把能夠救命的錢花在奢侈品上，是在做錯事。講陌生人的那一課陳述過這個論證。四十年後，它成了一場叫作「有效利他主義」的運動的基礎；這場運動試著查明，一筆捐款捐到哪裡能做最多的好事。辛格大半輩子都把收入的相當一部分捐出去。2023 年，他在普林斯頓上了最後一堂課。

The central idea · 核心觀念

An interest is an interest 利益就是利益

Singer’s philosophy starts from one principle and applies it without exceptions. In moral deliberation, he writes, we must give equal weight “to the like interests of all those affected by our actions,” because “an interest is an interest, whoever’s interest it may be.” The principle does not say that all beings are equal in ability or should be treated alike. It says that when two beings have a similar interest, such as the interest in not being in pain, the fact that one of them is closer to me, looks like me, or belongs to my nation or species does not make its interest count for more.

辛格的哲學從一條原則出發，並且毫無例外地應用它。他寫道：在道德的思慮當中，我們必須對「所有受我們行為影響者的相似利益」給予同等的份量；因為「利益就是利益，不管它是誰的利益」。這條原則並不是說，所有的存在者能力相等，或者應該受到一樣的對待。它說的是：當兩個存在者有相似的利益，例如「不要處在痛苦之中」的利益，那麼，其中一個離我比較近、長得像我，或者屬於我的國家或物種，這些事實並不使它的利益算得比較多。

He calls the history of ethics an expanding circle. People began by counting the interests of the family and the tribe, and were brought by argument and experience to include other tribes, other races, and women on equal terms. Each expansion looked absurd to those who resisted it. Singer’s two main campaigns push the circle outward in two directions: across species, to every being that can suffer, and across distance, to the poor of other countries. In both the method is the one he learned from Hare. Find the principle that a person already accepts in a nearby case, and ask what reason there is for stopping there.

他把倫理學的歷史稱為一個「擴大中的圈子」。人們起初只計算家人與部落的利益；然後被論證與經驗帶著，以平等的條件把其他部落、其他種族，以及女性包括進來。每一次擴大，在抗拒它的人眼中都顯得荒謬。辛格的兩場主要的運動，把這個圈子朝兩個方向往外推：跨過物種，推到每一個會受苦的存在者；跨過距離，推到其他國家的窮人。在這兩件事上，方法都是他從黑爾那裡學來的那一個。找出一個人在近處的案例裡已經接受的原則，然後問：有什麼理由在那裡停下來？

The strongest objection · 最強的反駁

Who decides which lives go well? 誰來決定哪些生命過得好？

The objection that has cost Singer most comes from people with disabilities. In Practical Ethics he argued that a newborn infant is not yet a person in the sense of a being aware of itself over time, and that parents of an infant with a very severe condition should be permitted, with their doctors, to end its life. The lawyer and activist Harriet McBryde Johnson, who had a muscle-wasting disease from birth and used a wheelchair, debated him at Princeton in 2002. She wrote afterward that his argument rests on a judgment that lives like hers are worse than other lives, and that the judgment is made by people who have not lived them. Those who do live them report otherwise. A theory that counts well-being must get the facts about well-being from somewhere, and she charged that it had taken them from prejudice.

讓辛格付出最大代價的反駁，來自身心障礙者。在《實踐倫理學》裡，他主張：新生兒還不是一個「人格者」，也就是一個意識到自己在時間中持續存在的存在者；而一個病況極其嚴重的嬰兒的父母，應該被允許在醫生的協同之下結束這個嬰兒的生命。律師與運動者哈麗特・麥克布萊德・強森，生來就有肌肉萎縮的疾病，使用輪椅；2002 年她在普林斯頓與他辯論。她事後寫道：他的論證立足於一個判斷，就是像她這樣的生命比別的生命差；而作出這個判斷的，是沒有過過這種生活的人。真正過著這種生活的人，說的並不是這樣。一個計算福祉的理論，總得從某個地方取得關於福祉的事實；而她指控，它是從偏見那裡取得的。

Singer has answered that he does not deny the worth of the lives of disabled adults, and that he was writing about decisions that are already made quietly in hospitals when treatment is withheld. He has revised some of what he wrote about particular conditions as the evidence changed. The deeper objection stands as a question for any utilitarian. If the sums depend on someone’s estimate of how good a life is, then the theory is only as fair as the estimator. Johnson also reported that he had been unfailingly courteous to her, and that this had made the debate harder for her and no easier.

辛格回答：他並不否認身心障礙的成年人生命的價值；而他寫的，是醫院裡在停止治療的時候已經悄悄在做的那些決定。隨著證據的改變，他修正了自己針對某些特定病況所寫的一部分內容。更深的那個反駁，作為對任何效益主義者的提問，仍然成立。如果加總取決於某個人對「一個生命有多好」的估計，那麼這個理論的公平程度，就只跟那個估計者一樣。強森也記述道：他對她始終彬彬有禮；而這使那場辯論對她來說更難，並沒有變得容易。

How we know · 史料

His mother 他的母親

In the 1990s Singer’s mother developed Alzheimer’s disease and in time no longer recognized her children. By the standard of his own writings she had ceased to be a person in the full sense, and the money spent on her care could have done more good elsewhere. Singer and his sister paid for nurses to look after her at home until she died. Journalists put the inconsistency to him. He said that the experience had made him see that these issues are “really very difficult,” that the decision was shared with his sister, and that it is different when it is your own mother.

1990 年代，辛格的母親得了阿茲海默症；後來不再認得自己的孩子。照他自己著作裡的標準，她已經不再是完整意義上的人格者；而花在照顧她身上的錢，用在別處本來可以做更多的好事。辛格與他的姊姊出錢請護士在家裡照顧她，直到她去世。記者把這個不一致擺在他面前。他說，這段經歷使他看見這些問題「真的非常困難」；這個決定是跟姊姊一起做的；而且，當那是你自己的母親的時候，事情是不一樣的。

His critics took this as a refutation: the philosopher could not live by his philosophy. Another reading is available, and it is the one the lesson on strangers reached. A theory of what is best from the point of view of the universe, as Singer likes to say, does not have to be the rule by which each of us lives every hour. Bernard Williams had said of impartial theories that they ask a person to have one thought too many. What Singer did for his mother is what Confucius and Mencius would have expected of him, and what almost every reader would have done. The question it leaves is whether the theory should be adjusted to make room for it, or whether it shows only that we are all, as he has said of himself, less good than we could be.

他的批評者把這當作一次駁倒：這位哲學家沒辦法照自己的哲學來生活。還有另一種讀法；而那就是講陌生人的那一課所到達的讀法。一個講「從宇宙的觀點看什麼是最好的」（辛格喜歡這樣說）的理論，不一定得是我們每個人每個小時賴以生活的規則。伯納德・威廉斯談到不偏不倚的理論時說過：它們要一個人「多想了一層」。辛格為他母親所做的，是孔子與孟子會期待他做的；也是幾乎每一位讀者都會做的。它留下的問題是：是應該調整理論，為這件事留出位置；還是它只顯示了，我們全都像他說自己的那樣，沒有我們可以做到的那麼好？

Did he really say that? · 這句話真的是他說的嗎？

“Peter Singer thinks a pig’s life is worth as much as a child’s.”「彼得・辛格認為，一頭豬的命跟一個孩子的命一樣值錢。」

Not what he says · 他說的不是這樣

This is the commonest summary of Singer and it confuses two things that he keeps apart. On suffering, he holds that pains “of similar intensity and duration should be given equal weight, whether they are experienced by humans or by animals.” On killing, he holds the opposite of the claim. The wrongness of ending a life depends for him on what that life contains and would have contained. A being that is aware of itself over time, has plans, and has relationships loses more in dying than a being that lives in the present, and so he regards the death of a normal human child or adult as much the greater loss. The view that follows is harder to state on a poster. Animals may not be made to suffer for our convenience, and it is a further question, on which he has changed his mind more than once, when it is wrong to kill them painlessly.

這是對辛格最常見的概括；而它把他一直分開的兩件事混在了一起。關於受苦，他主張：「強度與時間長度相近的」痛苦「應該得到同等的份量，不管經歷它的是人還是動物」。關於殺害，他主張的與這句話相反。對他來說，結束一個生命錯在哪裡，取決於那個生命包含了什麼、本來會包含什麼。一個意識到自己在時間中持續存在、有計畫、有關係的存在者，死去的時候失去的，比一個活在當下的存在者多；所以他認為，一個正常的人類孩子或成年人的死，是大得多的損失。由此而來的看法，比較難寫在海報上。不可以為了我們的方便而使動物受苦；至於什麼情況下無痛地殺死牠們是錯的，那是另一個問題；而在這個問題上，他不只一次改變過想法。

Dharma and the West · 佛法與西方哲學

Singer and the Buddha 辛格與佛陀

☸

Singer is an atheist with no religious practice, and he has long acknowledged that Buddhism reached his main conclusion about animals some twenty-four centuries before he did. The first precept covers all sentient beings, on the ground that all fear pain and death as we do. His test for moral standing, sentience, is very nearly the Buddhist category of sentient beings. The practice of extending goodwill outward from oneself to friends, strangers, enemies, and at last all beings is an expanding circle in the form of a meditation.

辛格是無神論者，沒有任何宗教上的修持；而他早就承認，佛教比他早了大約二十四個世紀，就到達了他關於動物的主要結論。第一條戒涵蓋一切有情；根據是：牠們全都像我們一樣畏懼痛苦與死亡。他檢驗道德地位的標準「感知能力」，幾乎就是佛教的「有情」這個範疇。把善意從自己往外擴展到朋友、陌生人、仇敵，最後到一切眾生的那種修習，就是一個以禪修的形式出現的「擴大中的圈子」。

The fullest meeting took place in Taiwan. Beginning in 2016 Singer held a series of conversations with the Venerable Shih Chao-Hwei, a bhikkhunī, scholar of Buddhist ethics, and campaigner for animals and for the equality of nuns, and they were published in 2023 as The Buddhist and the Ethicist. They agree on a great deal, including that compassion must be effective and not only felt. The differences are instructive. Singer reasons from a sum of well-being, and will accept a harm when the total comes out better. The Buddhist asks first about the intention of the one who acts and what the act does to that mind, so that some acts are refused whatever the sum. On killing, on the embryo, and on the weight of karma they did not reach agreement, and neither pretended to.

最完整的一次相遇，發生在臺灣。從 2016 年起，辛格與昭慧法師進行了一系列的對談；昭慧法師是比丘尼、佛教倫理學的學者，也是為動物、為比丘尼的平等而奔走的運動者。這些對談在 2023 年出版，中文版書名是《心靈的交會：山間對話》，英文版是 The Buddhist and the Ethicist。兩人同意的地方很多，包括：慈悲必須是有效的，不能只是感受到而已。差異之處很有啟發性。辛格從福祉的總和來推理；當總數算出來比較好的時候，他願意接受一項傷害。佛教徒先問的是行為者的動機，以及這個行為對那顆心造成什麼；所以有些行為，不管總和如何，都是拒絕的。在殺生、在胚胎、在業的份量這幾件事上，兩人沒有達成一致；而雙方都沒有假裝達成了。

Terms of art · 哲學術語

Words that come with him 跟著他一起來的詞

preference utilitarianism偏好效益主義

The view that the right act is the one that best satisfies the preferences of all affected. Singer held it for most of his career.

一種看法：正確的行為，是最能滿足所有受影響者的偏好的那一個。辛格在他生涯的大部分時間裡持這個看法。

equal consideration of interests利益的平等考量

Like interests count equally, whoever has them.

相似的利益同樣算數，不管是誰的。

the expanding circle擴大中的圈子

Singer’s image for the widening of moral concern from kin to all sentient beings.

辛格的意象：道德關懷從親族擴大到一切有感知的存在者。

person (in bioethics)人格者

A being aware of itself as existing over time. In this usage not every human is a person and not every person need be human.

意識到自己在時間中持續存在的存在者。在這個用法裡，並非每個人都是人格者；而人格者也不一定是人。

effective altruism有效利他主義

The project of using evidence to find where help does the most good.

一項計畫：用證據來找出幫助用在哪裡能做最多的好事。

universalizability可普遍化

Hare’s requirement that a moral judgment apply to every relevantly similar case.

黑爾的要求：一個道德判斷必須適用於每一個在相關方面相似的案例。
