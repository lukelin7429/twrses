D13 · Dharma and the West 佛法與西方哲學

The Means of Knowledge and the West 量論與西方哲學

How do you know? The Buddha told a brahmin that faith, tradition, and careful reasoning can each turn out false, and told his own monks to examine him. A thousand years later Buddhist logicians allowed only two sources of knowledge, where their rivals allowed four. Why Hume’s two kinds of reasoning come so close, what Kant would object, and what counts as evidence on a path that asks to be tested.
你怎麼知道？佛陀告訴一位婆羅門：信仰、傳統與仔細的推理，每一樣都可能到頭來是假的；又叫他自己的比丘審察他。一千年後，佛教的邏輯學家只承認兩種知識的來源；而他們的對手承認四種。休謨的「兩類推理」為什麼這麼接近？康德會提出什麼反對？在一條要求被檢驗的道路上，什麼才算證據？

The verdict · 判定

Closest: Hume, who also allowed two and only two kinds of knowing, direct experience and reasoning, and gave tradition no independent authority. William James supplies the test of truth that the Buddhist logicians used: a cognition is knowledge if it does not let you down when you act on it. The likeness to Hume ends at the range of experience. Hume’s experience is that of the five senses and ordinary reflection. The Buddhists counted the trained observation of a meditator as perception, and they pursued the theory because the path depends on knowing what is so.

最接近的是休謨：他同樣只容許兩種、也只有兩種「知」的方式，也就是直接的經驗與推理；而且不給傳統任何獨立的權威。威廉・詹姆斯則提供了佛教邏輯學家所用的那個真理的檢驗：一個認知，如果你照著它去行動的時候它不讓你失望，它就是知識。跟休謨的相似，到「經驗的範圍」為止。休謨的經驗，是五種感官與尋常反省的經驗。佛教徒把禪修者受過訓練的觀察也算作現量；而他們之所以追究這套理論，是因為這條道路依賴於「知道什麼是如此」。

1 · What the Dharma says · 佛法怎麼說

Examine the teacher 審察老師

The early discourses are unusually explicit about grounds for belief. A young brahmin, Kāpaṭika, told the Buddha that the brahmins hold their scriptures to be the only truth. The Buddha asked whether any of them, back through seven generations of teachers, had claimed to know this for himself, and compared the tradition to a file of blind men, each holding the one in front. He then listed five grounds on which people accept things: “Faith, endorsement, oral transmission, reasoned train of thought, and acceptance of a view after deliberation.” Each can go either way. “Even though you have full faith in something, it may be vacuous, hollow, and false. And even if you don’t have full faith in something, it may be true and real.” A person who cares about truth may say “such is my faith,” and may not go on to say “this is the only truth, anything else is futile.”

早期的經典，對「相信的根據」說得異乎尋常地明白。一位年輕的婆羅門迦波提迦告訴佛陀：婆羅門們認為，他們的聖典是唯一的真理。佛陀問：他們當中，往上追溯七代的老師，有沒有任何一位宣稱過自己親自知道這一點？並且把那個傳統比作一列盲人：每一個都抓著前面的那一個。接著他列出了人們接受事情的五種根據：「信、喜好、口傳、理路的推想，以及深思之後對一個見解的接受。」每一種都可能走向兩邊。「即使你對某件事有十足的信心，它也可能是空的、虛的、假的。而即使你對某件事沒有十足的信心，它也可能是真的、實的。」一個在乎真理的人可以說「這是我的信仰」；卻不可以接著說「只有這個是真的，其餘的都是虛妄」。

He applied the standard to himself. To the Kālāmas, who were confused by rival teachers, he said not to go by oral transmission, lineage, canonical authority, logic, or the thought “the ascetic is our respected teacher,” and to rely on what they could know for themselves about what leads to harm and what to welfare. To his own monks he said that an inquirer who cannot read minds “should scrutinize the Realized One to understand whether he is a fully awakened Buddha or not,” and he described how: watch his conduct over a long time, and see whether anything in it comes from greed, hatred, or delusion. Faith has a place in the path as the confidence that makes a person willing to try. It is expected to be replaced by knowledge.

他把這個標準用在自己身上。對那些被互相競爭的導師弄糊塗了的卡拉瑪人，他說：不要因為口傳、因為傳承、因為經典的權威、因為邏輯，或者因為「這位沙門是我們尊敬的老師」這個念頭而接受；要依靠他們自己能夠知道的事：什麼導向傷害，什麼導向利益。對他自己的比丘，他說：一位不能知他心的探究者，「應當審察如來，以了知他是不是正等覺者」；並且描述了怎麼做：長時間觀察他的行為，看看其中有沒有任何東西出自貪、瞋或癡。信，在道上有它的位置：它是使一個人願意去試的那份信心。它被期待終究由「知」來取代。

About a thousand years later Buddhist philosophers turned these attitudes into a theory. Dignāga, in the fifth or sixth century, argued that there are exactly two means of knowledge, perception and inference, because there are exactly two kinds of thing to be known. A short manual of his school, translated into Chinese by Xuanzang, puts it in a line: for one’s own understanding, “there are only two means of knowledge, perception and inference.” Perception is “free of conceptual construction,” a direct awareness of a particular before any name or class is applied to it. Inference knows its object “by way of signs,” as one knows fire on a hill from smoke. The rival Nyāya school counted testimony as a third source and comparison as a fourth. The Buddhists held that what we learn from a trustworthy speaker is a case of inference: we reason from his reliability. Dharmakīrti, a century after Dignāga, added the test. A cognition is a means of knowledge if it does not deceive, that is, if the thing it shows is there to do its work when one acts.

大約一千年後，佛教的哲學家把這些態度變成了一套理論。五或六世紀的陳那主張：恰好有兩種「量」（獲得知識的途徑），也就是現量與比量；因為被認識的東西恰好有兩種。他這一派的一部簡短手冊，由玄奘譯成中文，用一句話這樣說：「為自開悟，當知唯有現、比二量。」「此中現量，謂無分別」：在任何名稱或類別被加上去之前，對一個個別之物的直接覺知。比量則是「藉眾相而觀於義」；就像人從煙知道山上有火。與他們對立的正理派，把「聖言」算作第三種來源，把「譬喻」算作第四種。佛教徒主張：我們從一位可信的說話者那裡學到的東西，是比量的一個情況：我們是從他的可靠性來推理的。在陳那之後一個世紀的法稱，補上了檢驗的標準。一個認知如果「不欺誑」，它就是量；也就是說，當人照著它去行動的時候，它所顯示的東西確實在那裡起它的作用。

3 · The nearest Western views · 西方最接近的說法

Experience, concepts, and what works 經驗、概念，與行得通的東西

Two sources and no more · 兩種來源，沒有更多

David Hume 休謨1748

The British empiricists held that the mind begins empty and is furnished by experience, and Hume gave the view its sharpest form. “All the objects of human reason or enquiry may naturally be divided into two kinds, to wit, Relations of Ideas, and Matters of Fact.” The first are known by reasoning alone and the second by experience. A book that contains neither kind of reasoning should be committed to the flames, “for it can contain nothing but sophistry and illusion.” The two-part structure, the refusal of any third source, and the consequences for religious authority all match Dignāga’s position. Hume wrote a famous chapter arguing that testimony about miracles can never outweigh experience, which is the Buddhist treatment of testimony applied to a hard case. The difference is in where each ends. Hume found that experience cannot justify even our belief in causes, and concluded with a mitigated skepticism. Dignāga and Dharmakīrti held that perception and inference, properly used, do yield knowledge, enough to walk a path by.

英國的經驗論者主張：心起初是空的，由經驗來充實；而休謨給了這個看法最尖銳的形式。「人類理性或探究的一切對象，可以自然地分成兩類：也就是『觀念的關係』與『事實』。」前一類單靠推理就能認識；後一類靠經驗。一本兩種推理都沒有的書，應該付之一炬；「因為它裡面只可能有詭辯與幻象」。這個兩分的結構、對任何第三種來源的拒絕，以及對宗教權威所造成的後果，全都與陳那的立場相符。休謨寫過有名的一章，論證「關於神蹟的證詞永遠壓不過經驗」；那正是把佛教對聖言的處理方式，用在一個困難的案例上。差別在於各自停在哪裡。休謨發現：經驗連我們對因果的信念都無法辯護；於是以一種溫和的懷疑論作結。陳那與法稱則主張：現量與比量如果用得正確，確實產生知識；足夠讓人憑著它走一條路。

The objection · 那個反駁

Immanuel Kant 康德1781

Kant agreed with the empiricists that knowledge begins with experience and denied that experience is something simply received. Two faculties are needed, one that is given objects and one that thinks them. “Thoughts without content are void; intuitions without conceptions, blind.” Raw sensation with no concept applied is not yet knowledge of anything. This is aimed at Locke and Hume, and it lands on Dignāga’s definition of perception as free of conceptual construction. The Buddhist logicians saw the difficulty themselves. They granted that bare perception does not by itself tell us what we have perceived, and that a conceptual judgment has to follow before we can act. They kept the distinction because it did work for them. It marks the point at which the mind adds names, classes, and “mine” to what is there, and the path is concerned with exactly that addition. Kant thought the contribution of concepts could not be removed. The Buddhists thought it could be watched happening.

康德同意經驗論者：知識始於經驗；卻否認經驗是單純「被接收」的東西。需要兩種能力：一種是對象被給予它的，一種是思考對象的。「思想無內容則空；直觀無概念則盲。」沒有加上任何概念的原始感覺，還不是對任何東西的知識。這是針對洛克與休謨說的；而它也落在陳那對現量的定義上：「離分別」。佛教的邏輯學家自己看到了這個困難。他們承認：光是現量，並不告訴我們自己知覺到的是什麼；必須接著有一個帶概念的判斷，我們才能行動。他們之所以保留這個區分，是因為它對他們有用。它標出了那個點：心在那裡把名稱、類別與「我的」加到現前的東西上；而道所關切的，正是那個「加上去」。康德認為概念的貢獻是拿不掉的。佛教徒認為：可以看著它發生。

The same test of truth · 同樣的真理檢驗

William James 威廉・詹姆斯1907

The pragmatists proposed that an idea be judged by what follows when we act on it. For James a true idea is one we can verify, which means that it leads us successfully through experience to what it promised. This is Dharmakīrti’s criterion almost word for word. A cognition is a means of knowledge if it is non-deceptive, and it is non-deceptive if the object it presents performs its function: the water one saw can be drunk, the fire one inferred can cook. A mirage fails the test at the moment one bends to drink. Both hold that knowledge is to be assessed by its fruit in practice and without first settling what reality is in itself. They used the criterion for different ends. James used it to defend the right to hold beliefs that make life go better where evidence is lacking. Dharmakīrti used it to exclude them. For him a belief that has not yet met the test is not yet knowledge, however much it helps.

實用主義者提議：一個觀念，要用「我們照著它去行動的時候會跟著發生什麼」來評判。對詹姆斯來說，一個真的觀念，就是我們能夠驗證的觀念；意思是：它成功地帶著我們穿過經驗，到達它所許諾的東西。這幾乎逐字就是法稱的判準。一個認知如果不欺誑，它就是量；而如果它所呈現的對象起了它的作用，它就是不欺誑的：看見的那個水可以喝；推知的那個火可以煮東西。海市蜃樓，就在人彎下腰去喝的那一刻通不過檢驗。兩者都主張：知識是要用它在實踐中的果實來評估的；而不必先確定「實在本身」是什麼。他們把這個判準用在不同的目的上。詹姆斯用它來辯護這樣的權利：在缺乏證據的地方，持有使人生過得更好的信念。法稱用它來排除這樣的信念。對他來說，一個還沒有通過檢驗的信念，不管多麼有幫助，都還不是知識。

4 · Verdict · 判定

Who is closest, and where the likeness ends 誰最接近，相似到哪裡為止

On the conclusion Hume is closest: two sources, experience first, no independent weight for scripture. On the test of truth James is closest. Kant is the critic whose objection the Buddhist tradition also raised against itself. Taken together the three show that the Buddhist theory of knowledge sits squarely within what the West calls empiricism, and that it met the standard objections to empiricism in its own history.

在結論上，最接近的是休謨：兩種來源；經驗在先；經典沒有獨立的份量。在真理的檢驗上，最接近的是詹姆斯。康德則是那位批評者；他的反駁，佛教傳統自己也對自己提出過。三者合起來顯示：佛教的知識論，端端正正地落在西方所謂「經驗論」的範圍之內；而它在自己的歷史裡，遇到過對經驗論的那些標準反駁。

The likeness ends at two points. The first is what counts as experience. The Buddhist logicians listed four kinds of perception: by the five senses, by the mind, the awareness each mental state has of itself, and the perception of a yogi, which is the direct seeing that comes from long training in attention. The claim is that a trained observer perceives what an untrained one must infer or take on trust, as a physician sees in an X-ray what a layman does not. Hume had no place for this, and a Western empiricist will ask how such perception is to be checked by those who lack it. The second is the purpose. The British empiricists wanted to map the limits of the human understanding. The Buddhists wanted to know which cognitions can be relied on by someone trying to end suffering, since acting on error keeps a person bound. Their epistemology is part of the path. It has the same job as a physician’s knowledge of how to tell a true symptom from a false one.

相似之處在兩個地方為止。第一，是什麼才算經驗。佛教的邏輯學家列出了四種現量：五根的現量、意的現量、每一個心理狀態對它自己的覺知（自證），以及瑜伽行者的現量；後者是長期訓練注意力而來的直接看見。這裡的主張是：一位受過訓練的觀察者，知覺得到未受訓練的人只能推論、或者只能憑信任接受的東西；就像醫師在一張 X 光片裡，看得到外行人看不到的東西。休謨沒有為這個留位置；而一位西方的經驗論者會問：那些沒有這種現量的人，要怎麼查核它？第二，是目的。英國的經驗論者想要畫出人類理解力的界限。佛教徒想要知道的是：對一個想要止息苦的人來說，哪些認知是靠得住的？因為照著錯誤去行動，就使人繼續被綁著。他們的知識論是道的一部分。它的工作，就像一位醫師「怎麼分辨真症狀與假症狀」的那種知識。

5 · The step the Dharma takes further · 佛法多走的一步

Perception can be trained, and the theory can be questioned 現量可以訓練；而這套理論也可以被質疑

The first further step has been named: the claim that perception is a skill. The discourses say that the Buddha’s teaching invites one to come and see, and that it is to be known by the wise each for himself. The logicians gave this a place in the theory. A meditator who has stabilized attention and examined experience closely is said to perceive impermanence and the absence of self in the way that others perceive color. This turns the central doctrines from articles of belief into things that are in principle observable, and it places the burden where the Buddha placed it for the Kālāmas, on one’s own knowing. It also creates the tradition’s hardest problem of evidence. A report from trained observation carries weight only with those who have the training or who have independent grounds for trusting the observer. The discourse on examining the teacher is the tradition’s answer: judge the observer by what can be seen of his conduct over time.

多走的第一步，前面已經說了：「現量是一項技能」這個主張。經典說，佛陀的教法是請人「來見」的；是智者各自親自了知的。邏輯學家為這一點在理論裡留了位置。一位已經使注意力穩定下來、並且仔細檢視過經驗的禪修者，被說成是知覺到無常與無我；就像別人知覺到顏色那樣。這把中心的教義，從「信條」變成了原則上可以觀察到的東西；並且把責任放在佛陀為卡拉瑪人所放的地方：放在自己的「知」上。它也造成了這個傳統在「證據」上最困難的問題。一份出自受過訓練的觀察的報告，只對那些有這種訓練的人、或者另有理由信任那位觀察者的人，才有份量。講審察老師的那部經，就是這個傳統的回答：用「長時間裡看得到的行為」來評判那位觀察者。

The second step is that Buddhism did not leave its own theory of knowledge unexamined. Nāgārjuna, three centuries before Dignāga, had asked what establishes the means of knowledge themselves. If they are established by further means, there is a regress. If they establish themselves, any claim might do the same. If they are established by the objects they reveal, the argument goes in a circle. He concluded that means and objects of knowledge depend on each other and that neither is a foundation. Western philosophy calls the same three-way trap by the name of an ancient skeptic and has used it against foundationalism ever since. So the tradition contains both projects. One builds a careful account of what counts as knowing, and the other shows that the account stands on nothing outside itself. Later Buddhist thinkers mostly held the two together, using the theory of knowledge as conventional truth: reliable in practice, like a map, and without a ground beneath it.

第二步是：佛教並沒有讓自己的知識論不受檢視。比陳那早三個世紀的龍樹問過：是什麼確立了「量」本身？如果它們是由別的量確立的，那就無窮後退了。如果它們自己確立自己，那麼任何主張都可以這樣做。如果它們是由自己所揭示的對象確立的，論證就繞成了一個圈。他的結論是：量與所量互相依待；兩者都不是地基。西方哲學用一位古代懷疑論者的名字來稱呼同樣這個三路的陷阱；並且從那時起，就拿它來對付基礎論。所以，這個傳統裡兩個計畫都有。一個計畫仔細地建立「什麼才算知道」的說明；另一個計畫顯示：這個說明並不立足於它之外的任何東西。後來的佛教思想家，多半把兩者一起持有：把知識論當作世俗諦來用：像一張地圖那樣，在實踐上可靠；而它的底下並沒有一個地基。

6 · How to say it to a Western audience · 對西方人怎麼講

Neither blind faith nor laboratory science 既不是盲信，也不是實驗室的科學

Western listeners come with one of two expectations. Those who think of Buddhism as a religion expect to be asked to believe. Those who have read popular books expect to be told that Buddhism is a science and the Buddha an empiricist ahead of his time. The texts support neither without qualification. Begin with the passage in which the Buddha tells his own students to examine him, which surprises both groups. Then describe the position plainly. Faith is where a person starts, as one trusts a doctor enough to try the treatment. The tradition’s own philosophers held that knowledge comes from direct experience and from reasoning, and that scripture must answer to both. Add the honest limit. Some of what the tradition claims, a trained mind is said to see directly, and until one has that training one is taking a specialist’s word, with reasons for trusting him.

西方的聽眾，帶著兩種期待當中的一種而來。把佛教想成一種宗教的人，預期自己會被要求去相信。讀過通俗書籍的人，預期自己會聽到：佛教是一門科學；佛陀是一位走在時代前面的經驗論者。經典對兩者都不是毫無保留地支持。從「佛陀叫自己的學生審察他」的那段經文講起；它會讓兩群人都意外。然後樸素地描述這個立場。信，是一個人的起點；就像人信任一位醫生，到願意試一試療法的程度。這個傳統自己的哲學家主張：知識來自直接的經驗，也來自推理；而經典必須對這兩者負責。再補上那個誠實的界限。這個傳統所主張的東西，有一些被說成是受過訓練的心能夠直接看見的；而在一個人有那種訓練之前，他是在接受一位專家的話；而且是有理由信任他，才接受的。

Sentences you can use 可以直接用的句子

  1. The Buddha told his own monks to examine him and see whether he was what he claimed to be.

    佛陀叫他自己的比丘審察他，看看他是不是他所宣稱的那樣。

  2. He said that faith, tradition, and even careful reasoning can each turn out true or false.

    他說：信仰、傳統，甚至仔細的推理，每一樣都可能到頭來是真的，也可能是假的。

  3. Buddhist philosophers accepted two sources of knowledge, direct experience and reasoning. Scripture has to answer to both.

    佛教的哲學家承認兩種知識的來源：直接的經驗與推理。經典必須對這兩者負責。

  4. Faith in Buddhism is like trusting a doctor enough to try the medicine. It is where you start.

    佛教裡的信，就像信任一位醫生，到願意試那帖藥的程度。它是你的起點。

  5. Their test of knowledge was practical: does it hold up when you act on it?

    他們對知識的檢驗是實際的：你照著它去行動的時候，它站得住嗎？

  6. Some things a trained meditator is said to see directly. Until I can, I am relying on someone I have reason to trust, and I should say so.

    有些東西，據說是受過訓練的禪修者直接看得見的。在我自己看得見之前，我是在依靠一位我有理由信任的人；而我應該照實這樣說。

Phrases to avoid 要避開的說法

“Buddhism requires no faith.” 「佛教不需要信仰。」

It asks for trust at the start. Say that faith is provisional and is meant to be replaced by knowing.

它在開頭是要求信任的。要說：信是暫時的；用意是由「知」來取代。

“Buddhism is a science.” 「佛教是一門科學。」

It shares the attitude of testing. It has no laboratories, and trained perception is not public in the way a measurement is.

它有「要檢驗」的那種態度。它沒有實驗室；而受過訓練的現量，並不像一項量測那樣是公開的。

“The Buddha said: believe nothing, not even me.” 「佛陀說：什麼都不要信，連我也不要信。」

A paraphrase that drops the second half. He said what to rely on: what you know for yourself to lead to harm or to welfare.

這是丟掉了後半句的轉述。他說了該依靠什麼：你親自知道會導向傷害、或者導向利益的東西。

“Meditation proves it.” 「禪修證明了它。」

To the meditator, perhaps. To others it is a report. Say what the report is and why you trust it.

對禪修者本人，也許是。對別人，那是一份報告。要說出那份報告是什麼，以及你為什麼信任它。

Reading check · 理解測驗

Did it land? 讀懂了嗎

1. What did the Buddha say about faith, oral transmission, and reasoned thought?

2. What did he tell his monks to do about himself?

3. Which two means of knowledge did Dignāga accept?

4. What is Kant’s objection to perception without concepts?

5. What is Dharmakīrti’s test for a means of knowledge?

Write · 你怎麼講？

  1. List three things you believe: one from your own experience, one by reasoning, and one on someone’s word. For the third, write down why you trust the source. About 150 words.

    列出三件你相信的事：一件出自你自己的經驗；一件出自推理；一件憑的是某個人的話。對第三件，寫下你為什麼信任那個來源。約 150 個字。

  2. A student says, “Buddhism is just blind faith like any religion.” Another says, “Buddhism is pure science.” Write three sentences in reply to each.

    一個學生說：「佛教跟任何宗教一樣，只是盲信。」另一個說：「佛教是純粹的科學。」對兩人各寫三句話的回應。

  3. The Buddha said to judge a teacher by long observation of his conduct. In 200 words, say how you would apply this to a teacher you have trusted.

    佛陀說：要用對老師行為的長期觀察來評判老師。用 200 個字說說：你會怎麼把這一點用在一位你信任過的老師身上？
