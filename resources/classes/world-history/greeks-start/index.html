World History · Unit 2 · Lesson 5 · 世界歷史 單元二 第五課

What Did the Greeks Start?希臘人開了什麼頭？

Watch the people of Attica in 3D and find out how few of them could climb the Pnyx hill to vote. Decide who could vote in Athens, and test two ancient readings of the rule for sending a man away for ten years.
在 3D 裡看看阿提卡的居民，發現能走上普尼克斯山投票的人有多少。判斷誰能在雅典投票，再試試「把一個人送走十年」那條規則的兩種古代讀法。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

From about 508 BCE, the adult male citizens of Athens met in an assembly and decided their city's affairs by voting. In the same age, some Greeks began to look for answers by asking questions and giving reasons. Both were beginnings, not finished things: most people in Athens could not vote, and other peoples also made decisions together.
大約從西元前 508 年起，雅典的成年男性公民聚在公民大會裡，用投票決定城邦的事。同一個時代，有些希臘人開始用提問和講道理的方式找答案。兩件事都是開頭，不是完成品：住在雅典的大多數人不能投票，而其他民族也有一起做決定的方式。

Reading · 英文閱讀

5

Unit 5: What Did the Greeks Start?

In Lesson 4, rulers and officials organized the first cities. Around 500 BCE, in a small Greek city-state called Athens, people tried something different. In about 508 BCE, a leader named Cleisthenes reorganized the citizens into ten new tribes and a Council of 500, chosen from all over Attica, the land around the city. From then on, the citizens themselves decided many of the city's affairs.

在第四課，最早的城市是由統治者和官員組織起來的。大約西元前 500 年，在希臘一個叫雅典的小城邦，人們試了一種不一樣的做法。大約西元前 508 年，一位叫克里斯提尼的領袖把公民重新編成十個新的部落，又設了一個從整個阿提卡（雅典城周圍的土地）選出來的五百人議事會。從那時起，城邦的很多事由公民自己決定。

They decided in the Assembly. Three or four times a month, citizens climbed a hill called the Pnyx, listened to speeches from a stone platform, and voted, usually by raising their hands. Any citizen could stand up and speak, for or against a proposal; proposals came first from the Council of 500. The Greeks called this kind of government dēmokratia, “rule by the people.” Many officials were not elected but chosen by lot, so that ordinary citizens took turns holding office.

他們在公民大會裡做決定。每個月三、四次，公民爬上一座叫普尼克斯的山丘，聽人站在石頭講台上演說，然後投票，通常是舉手。任何公民都可以站起來發言，支持或反對一項提案；提案要先由五百人議事會提出。希臘人把這種政治叫做 dēmokratia，「由人民統治」。很多官職不是選出來的，而是抽籤決定，讓一般的公民輪流擔任。

But who were “the people”? Only adult men whose parents were citizens. Women could not vote. Neither could metics, the free foreigners who lived and worked in Athens, nor the many enslaved people. By one scholar's estimate for the 300s BCE, about 30,000 citizen men lived among roughly 230,000 other people. And the earliest meeting place on the Pnyx that archaeologists can measure held only about 6,000 at a time.

但是「人民」是誰？只有父母都是公民的成年男子。女性不能投票。外籍居民（metic）也不能，也就是在雅典生活、工作的自由外邦人；為數眾多的被奴役的人也不能。依一位學者對西元前 300 年代的估計，大約三萬名男性公民，和大約二十三萬個其他的人住在一起。而考古學家能量出來的、普尼克斯山最早的會場，一次只坐得下大約六千人。

Athenian democracy also had unusual tools. Once a year, the citizens could vote to send away one powerful man for ten years. Each voter scratched a name on a broken piece of pottery called an ostrakon, which is where the word “ostracism” comes from. Archaeologists have found thousands of these ostraka. Democracy did not always last, either: in 411 and again in 404 BCE, small groups seized power for a time, before the citizens restored it.

雅典的民主還有一些特別的做法。每年一次，公民可以投票，把一位有權勢的人送走十年。每個投票的人在一塊叫 ostrakon 的破陶片上刻一個名字，「陶片放逐」這個詞就是從這裡來的。考古學家找到了好幾千塊這樣的陶片。民主也不是一直都在：西元前 411 年和 404 年，一小群人兩度奪取政權一段時間，後來公民才又把民主恢復。

In the same age, some Greeks began to explain the world with reasons instead of only with stories. Thales is said to have predicted an eclipse in 585 BCE. In Athens, Socrates questioned people about justice and goodness, and he was put on trial and sentenced to death by a jury of citizens in 399 BCE. He wrote nothing himself; we know him mostly from his student Plato.

在同一個時代，有些希臘人開始用道理、而不只是用故事來解釋世界。據說泰利斯在西元前 585 年預測了一次日食。在雅典，蘇格拉底向人追問什麼是正義、什麼是善；西元前 399 年，他被一個由公民組成的陪審團審判，判了死刑。他自己沒有寫下任何東西，我們主要是從他的學生柏拉圖那裡認識他。

Greece was not the only place where new ideas were growing. Confucius taught in China, the Buddha taught in India, and the Persian Empire ruled lands from Egypt to Central Asia. Other peoples also made decisions together: a Buddhist text praises the Vajjis of India for meeting frequently and holding many meetings. What Athens started was one beginning among several, and later peoples, including many today, have argued about what it means.

長出新想法的地方，不只希臘。孔子在中國教學，佛陀在印度說法，波斯帝國統治著從埃及到中亞的土地。其他民族也有一起做決定的方式：一部佛教經典稱讚印度的跋耆人常常集會、多次集會。雅典開的頭，是好幾個開頭中的一個；後來的人，包括今天的很多人，一直在討論它的意義。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Who could vote in the Athenian Assembly, and who could not?

Only adult men whose parents were citizens could vote. Women, metics (free foreigners living in Athens), and enslaved people could not.

Why do you think the Athenians chose many officials by lot instead of by election?

A lottery gave ordinary citizens, not only rich or famous ones, a turn at holding office. Students may also suggest that it made bribery and campaigning less useful.

The lesson calls Athens “one beginning among several.” What other beginnings does it mention?

Confucius teaching in China, the Buddha teaching in India, the Persian Empire, and the Vajjis of India meeting in assembly.

    How Do We Know? · 我們怎麼知道？

    A lost book, broken pots, and a hill 一本失而復得的書、破陶片，和一座山丘

    Athenian democracy is better documented than most ancient governments, but every kind of evidence has gaps.
    雅典的民主，比大部分古代的政治制度記錄得更清楚；但每一種證據都有缺口。

    📜

    A description of the system一份制度的說明

    The Constitution of the Athenians, written in Aristotle's school in the 300s BCE, describes how the Council, the Assembly, the courts, and the lotteries worked, and gives a short history of how they began.
    《雅典政制》是西元前 300 年代亞里斯多德學派寫的，說明議事會、公民大會、法庭和抽籤怎麼運作，也簡短地交代它們怎麼開始。

    Its limits · 它的限制It was written almost two centuries after Cleisthenes. For the early years, the author relied on older writers we cannot always check.它寫成的時候，離克里斯提尼已經將近兩百年。早期的部分，作者依靠的是更早的作者，我們不一定能查證。

    Aristotle, Constitution of the Athenians, chapters 21–22 and 42–43 ↗
    🏺

    Names on broken pottery破陶片上的名字

    Excavators in the Agora and the Kerameikos have found thousands of ostraka. One publication alone catalogues 1,145 from the Agora, and a well on the north slope of the Acropolis held 191 more, almost all naming Themistocles.
    在雅典的阿哥拉（市集廣場）和凱拉米克斯，考古學家找到了好幾千塊陶片。光是一本報告就收錄了阿哥拉的 1,145 塊；衛城北坡的一口井裡還有 191 塊，幾乎都寫著地米斯托克利。

    Its limits · 它的限制Ostraka tell us whose names were written, not how the whole vote turned out or how the 6,000 rule worked.陶片告訴我們有誰的名字被寫上去，卻看不出整場投票的結果，也看不出「6,000 票」的規則怎麼運作。

    Lang, The Athenian Agora XXV: Ostraka (1990) ↗
    ⛰️

    Measuring the meeting place量出會場的大小

    Archaeologists excavated the Pnyx and found three building phases. By allowing about 0.4 square meters for each person, one historian estimated that the first meeting place held about 6,000 citizens and the last about 13,400.
    考古學家發掘普尼克斯山，找到三個建造階段。一位歷史學家以每人大約 0.4 平方公尺來估算，認為最早的會場坐得下大約 6,000 位公民，最後一期大約 13,400 位。

    Its limits · 它的限制The dates of the phases have been revised more than once, and the space per person is an assumption, so the numbers are estimates.各期的年代已經修正過不只一次，每人占多少空間也是假設，所以這些數字都是估計。

    Hansen, Greek, Roman and Byzantine Studies 17 (1976); Rotroff and Camp, Hesperia 65 (1996) ↗

    Different Accounts · 不同的說法

    Four questions about the Athenian beginning 關於雅典這個開頭的四個問題

    Historians and political thinkers have argued about Athens for centuries. Here are four of their questions, with the views set side by side.
    歷史學家和政治思想家為雅典爭論了好幾百年。下面是其中四個問題，各種說法並列。

    Was Athens really a democracy?雅典真的是民主嗎？

    • Yes, in its own way是，用它自己的方式

      Ordinary citizens, rich or poor, voted directly on laws, war, and peace, and took turns in office by lot. In some ways that is more direct than a modern democracy, where people elect representatives.
      一般公民不論貧富，直接對法律、戰爭與和平投票，又用抽籤輪流擔任官職。在某些方面，這比現代人選出代表的民主更直接。

      Britannica, “Democracy”; Constitution of the Athenians 62–63 ↗

    • Only for a few只屬於少數人

      Women, metics, and enslaved people were shut out. By one estimate for the 300s BCE, the men with full rights were about 30,000 of some 260,000 people living in Attica, a little over one in ten.
      女性、外籍居民和被奴役的人都被排除在外。依一個對西元前 300 年代的估計，擁有完整權利的男性大約三萬人，而阿提卡大約住了二十六萬人，大約十個人裡一個多一點。

      Britannica, “Democracy”; Aristotle, Politics 3.1 ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Both can be true at once: the system was very direct for those inside it, and closed to most people.兩種說法可以同時成立：對身在其中的人，這個制度非常直接；對大多數人，它是關著的。

    Did the Greeks invent government by assembly?是希臘人發明了「集會決定事情」嗎？

    • They named it名稱是他們取的

      The word dēmokratia is Greek, and Athens left more evidence about how its system worked than any other ancient city.
      dēmokratia 這個字是希臘文；雅典留下的制度證據，也比任何一座古代城市都多。

      Britannica, “Democracy” ↗

    • Others met too別人也集會

      A Buddhist text praises the Vajjis of India for meeting often in assembly, and a historian argued in 1943 that early Mesopotamia had assemblies. Scholars debate whether these were democracies or councils of leading families.
      一部佛教經典稱讚印度的跋耆人常常集會；一位歷史學家在 1943 年主張早期的美索不達米亞也有集會。這些算不算民主，還是比較像主要家族的會議，學者仍在爭論。

      Mahāparinibbāna Sutta (DN 16); Jacobsen, JNES 2 (1943); Britannica, “Licchavi” ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Collective decision-making appears in many places; what was unusual in Athens was how much power the assembly of ordinary citizens held.一起做決定的方式在很多地方都出現過；雅典不尋常的地方，在於一般公民的大會握有多大的權力。

    How many votes did ostracism need?陶片放逐要幾票？

    • 6,000 in all全部 6,000 票

      Plutarch wrote that if fewer than 6,000 people voted, the ostracism was void. The historian Mogens Hansen follows this reading.
      普魯塔克寫道：投票的人少於 6,000，這次放逐就無效。歷史學家漢森採用這個讀法。

      Plutarch, Life of Aristides 7; Hansen, GRBS 23 (1982) ↗

    • 6,000 against one man寫同一個人 6,000 票

      A fragment of the historian Philochorus is usually read to mean that a man needed 6,000 votes against him to be sent away.
      史家菲洛科羅斯的一段殘篇，通常被讀成：一個人要被投 6,000 票，才會被放逐。

      Philochorus, fragment 30 (FGrHist 328 F30), in translation at attalus.org ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Try both rules in the game above: the same pile of ostraka can give different results.在上面的小互動試試兩種規則：同一堆陶片，結果可能不一樣。

    How many people lived in Attica?阿提卡住了多少人？

    • About 250,000大約二十五萬

      Mogens Hansen suggested that in 431 BCE Attica had at least 60,000 citizens and about 250,000 inhabitants in all.
      漢森認為，西元前 431 年阿提卡至少有六萬名公民，全部居民大約二十五萬人。

      Hansen, as summarized in sehepunkte 21 (2021) ↗

    • 400,000 or more四十萬以上

      In a 2019 book, the historian Ben Akrigg argues that the total on the eve of the Peloponnesian War was 400,000 or more.
      歷史學家阿克里格在 2019 年的書裡主張，伯羅奔尼撒戰爭前夕的總人口有四十萬以上。

      Akrigg, Population and Economy in Classical Athens (2019), reviewed in BMCR 2020.10.40 ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況No census survives, so every number is an estimate. The model in this lesson uses one estimate for the 300s BCE and says so.沒有留下任何人口普查，所以每個數字都是估計。這一課的模型用的是一組對西元前 300 年代的估計，並且寫明了。

    Notice that some disagreements are about evidence, such as numbers, and some are about words, such as what counts as a democracy.留意有些分歧是關於證據的（例如數字），有些是關於用詞的（例如怎樣才算民主）。

    Taiwan at That Time · 那時候的台灣

    Villages, rice, and standing stones 聚落、稻米與豎立的巨石

    Around 500 BCE, about 2,450 years ago, there were no city-states in Taiwan, but several cultures were flourishing, and metal was about to arrive.
    大約西元前 500 年，也就是距今大約 2,450 年，台灣沒有城邦，但好幾個文化正興盛，金屬器也快要出現了。

    🏝️

    Archaeologists in Taiwan give dates as “years ago” (before 1950). About 2,450 years ago is roughly 500 BCE.
    台灣的考古學家用「距今幾年」（以 1950 年為準）說年代。距今大約 2,450 年，差不多就是西元前 500 年。

    • Beinan culture卑南文化

      The National Museum of Prehistory says the Beinan culture was at its height about 3,500 to 2,000 years ago, and that it lived side by side with the Qilin culture for a long time. The museum also notes that it is completely different from the culture of today's Puyuma people.
      國立臺灣史前文化博物館說，卑南文化「極盛的年代大約在三千五百到二千年前，與麒麟文化並存了一段很長的時間」；博物館也說明，卑南文化和今天的卑南族文化完全不同。

      國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

    • Yuanshan culture圓山文化

      In the Taipei Basin, the Yuanshan culture lasted from about 4,500 to a little over 2,000 years ago. Its people grew rice and used shouldered stone axes and stepped stone adzes.
      在臺北盆地，圓山文化從距今大約四千五百年延續到二千多年前；那裡的人種稻，使用有肩石斧和有段石錛。

      國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

    • Standing stones豎立的巨石

      On the east coast, the Qilin culture, about 3,000 years ago, raised large standing stones, which the museum says may have been connected with ceremonies.
      在東海岸，大約距今三千年的麒麟文化豎起了巨大的石頭，博物館說它們可能和祭祀活動有關。

      國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

    • When did metal arrive?金屬什麼時候來？

      Sources differ. One recent study dates the start of Taiwan's metal age to about 2,400 years ago, though its evidence of iron smelting at Shisanhang is later; the museum says the Shisanhang culture, which smelted its own iron, rose about 2,000 years ago, and that iron-making spread to Taiwan about 1,500 years ago.
      各資料的說法不同。一份近年的研究把台灣金屬器時代的開始定在距今大約 2,400 年，不過它在十三行找到的煉鐵證據比這晚；史前館則說，會自己煉鐵的十三行文化大約在二千年前興起，製作鐵器的技術大約在一千五百年前傳入臺灣。

      國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源；國立臺灣大學學位論文（2025） ↗

    Four short stories about how the Greeks and their neighbors decided things, and how we know.
    四個小故事：希臘人和他們的鄰居怎麼做決定，以及我們怎麼知道。

    Athenian Agora · 雅典阿哥拉

    A machine for choosing by lot一台抽籤用的機器

    A marble slab with eleven rows of slots, found in 1935, is a kleroterion: citizens' tickets went into the slots, and black and white cubes, dropped through a tube, decided which rows were chosen. None of the surviving machines can be securely dated before the mid-100s BCE, long after Pericles.
    1935 年找到的一塊大理石板，上面有十一排插槽，是一台抽籤機（kleroterion）：公民的名牌插進槽裡，再從一根管子丟下黑色和白色的小方塊，決定哪幾排被選中。留下來的抽籤機，沒有一台能確定早於西元前二世紀中葉，比伯里克利的時代晚很多。

    Early 400s BCE · Athens · 雅典

    A well full of one name一口寫滿同一個名字的井

    On the north slope of the Acropolis, an archaeologist found 191 ostraka in a well, almost all naming Themistocles, the Athenian commander who planned the Greek naval victory at Salamis. Themistocles himself was later ostracized.
    在衛城的北坡，一位考古學家在一口井裡找到 191 塊陶片，幾乎都寫著地米斯托克利，也就是策劃希臘人在薩拉米斯海戰獲勝的雅典將領。後來，地米斯托克利自己也被放逐了。

    About 400s BCE · India · 印度

    The Vajjis who met often常常集會的跋耆人

    In the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta, the Buddha asks Ānanda whether the Vajjis (Pāli Vajjī, Sanskrit Vṛji) meet frequently and hold many meetings, and says that as long as they do, they can expect growth, not decline. It is the first of seven such conditions he lists. Historians debate whether their government should be called a republic or rule by leading families.
    在巴利《大般涅槃經》（Mahāparinibbāna Sutta，《長部》第 16 經；漢譯《長阿含・遊行經》）裡，佛陀問阿難：跋耆人（巴利文 Vajjī，梵文 Vṛji）是不是常常集會、多次集會？並說只要他們這樣做，就可以期待興盛，不會衰退。這是佛陀列出的七個條件中的第一個。他們的政治該叫共和，還是由主要家族統治，歷史學家仍在爭論。

    About 530 BCE · Greece · 希臘

    Statues in color有顏色的雕像

    Greek statues look white today, but many were painted. A sphinx at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, from about 530 BCE, still has extensive remains of its original colors, and researchers have studied ancient paint for more than 40 years.
    希臘雕像今天看起來是白的，但很多原本有上色。紐約大都會藝術博物館的一尊人面獅身像，大約是西元前 530 年的，至今還留著大片原本的顏料；研究者研究古代的彩繪已經超過 40 年。

    Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

    Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Every Athenian could vote.每個雅典人都能投票。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Only adult men whose parents were citizens could vote. Women, metics, and enslaved people, the great majority, could not.只有父母都是公民的成年男子能投票。女性、外籍居民和被奴役的人，也就是絕大多數的人，都不能。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Greek statues and temples were pure white.希臘的雕像和神廟是純白的。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Many were brightly painted. Traces of the original colors survive on statues such as a sphinx of about 530 BCE in the Metropolitan Museum of Art.很多原本塗著鮮豔的顏色。像大都會藝術博物館那尊大約西元前 530 年的人面獅身像，至今還留著原本的顏料。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Only the Greeks ever made decisions in assemblies.只有希臘人用集會做決定。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Other peoples, such as the Vajjis of India, also met in assemblies, though scholars debate how much power ordinary people had in them.其他民族也有集會，例如印度的跋耆人；不過一般人在這些集會裡有多少權力，學者仍有爭論。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Athens was a democracy from beginning to end.雅典從頭到尾都是民主的。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Small groups overthrew the democracy in 411 and again in 404 BCE, and the citizens restored it each time.西元前 411 年和 404 年，一小群人兩度推翻了民主，公民每一次都把它恢復。

    Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

    Hold a Class Assembly 開一場班級公民大會

    You need · 準備材料

    • A question the class really cares about, such as where to go for a field trip一個全班真的在意的問題，例如校外教學要去哪裡
    • Slips of paper for a second, secret vote第二次祕密投票用的小紙條

    Steps · 步驟

    1. Choose two “presiding officers” by drawing names from a box. Anyone may stand and speak for one minute: “I propose that …”從盒子裡抽出兩位「主席」。任何人都可以站起來發言一分鐘：「I propose that …」
    2. Vote by a show of hands. The officers must judge the result by looking only, without counting.舉手表決。主席只能用看的判斷結果，不可以數。
    3. Now vote again on paper and count every slip. Did the officers judge correctly?接著用紙條再投一次，每一張都數。主席剛剛判斷得對嗎？
    4. Finally, repeat the vote with only about one student in ten allowed to vote. How does the class feel about the result?最後，只讓大約十分之一的同學投票，再表決一次。全班對這個結果有什麼感覺？

    Step 4 is the lesson. Students feel the difference between a direct vote and a narrow one, which is exactly the double picture of Athens.
    第四步就是這堂課的重點。學生會感受到「直接投票」和「只有少數人能投」的差別，這正是雅典的兩面。

    Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

    The Socrates Game 蘇格拉底遊戲

    You need · 準備材料

    • A big question, such as “What is a good friend?” or “What is fair?”一個大問題，例如「什麼是好朋友？」或「什麼是公平？」
    • A timer一個計時器

    Steps · 步驟

    1. In pairs, one student gives a short answer: “A good friend is someone who …”兩人一組，一個人先給一個簡短的答案：「A good friend is someone who …」
    2. The partner may only ask questions, never give answers: “Is that always true? Can you think of an example where it is not?”另一個人只能問問題，不能給答案：「Is that always true? Can you think of an example where it is not?」
    3. After three minutes, the first student improves the answer. Then swap roles.三分鐘後，第一個人修改自己的答案。接著交換角色。
    4. Share one answer that changed. What question made it change?分享一個改變了的答案。是哪一個問題讓它改變的？

    Keep the questions kind. The goal, as with Socrates, is to examine ideas, not to embarrass people.
    提問要友善。就像蘇格拉底那樣，目的是檢驗想法，不是讓人難堪。
