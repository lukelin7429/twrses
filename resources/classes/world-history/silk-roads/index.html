Big idea · 一句話看懂

The “Silk Road” was not one road but a web of land and sea routes linking Asia, Africa, and Europe. Few people traveled the whole way; most goods were passed from trader to trader, one stretch at a time. Along with silk went horses, spices, paper, glass, porcelain, religions, skills, and even diseases.
「絲路」不是一條路，而是一張連起亞洲、非洲和歐洲的陸路和海路的網。很少有人從頭走到尾；大部分的貨物是一段一段、從一個商人傳到下一個商人手上。跟著絲一起走的，還有馬、香料、紙、玻璃、瓷器、宗教、技術，甚至疾病。

Unit 9: What Traveled Along the Silk Road?

Nobody in ancient times called these routes the “Silk Road.” The German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen used the name in 1877, though earlier German writers had used it too, and it spread widely only in the 1900s. UNESCO describes the Silk Roads as a network of land and sea routes, used for well over 1,500 years, along which silk and many other goods were exchanged.

古代沒有人把這些路叫做「絲路」。德國地理學家李希霍芬在 1877 年用了這個名字，更早的德國作家也用過；它在二十世紀才真正流行起來。聯合國教科文組織說，絲路是一張由陸路和海路組成的網，用了一千五百多年，絲和許多其他的貨物沿著它交換。

On land, the routes linked China with Central Asia, Persia, and the Mediterranean. Around 138 BCE the Han envoy Zhang Qian set out west, and the Han set up commanderies along the Hexi Corridor, including Dunhuang. Travelers had to cross deserts, mountains, and the lands of many different kingdoms, so caravans moved from oasis to oasis.

在陸地上，這些路把中國和中亞、波斯、地中海連起來。大約西元前 138 年，漢朝的使者張騫往西出發；漢朝也沿著河西走廊設了幾個郡，包括敦煌。旅人要穿過沙漠、高山，還有許多不同國家的土地，所以商隊是從一個綠洲走到下一個綠洲。

Few people traveled the whole way. Britannica puts it simply: goods were handled stage by stage by middlemen. Sogdian merchants from around Samarkand built a network of partners in many towns; letters they wrote around 313 CE mention silver, linen, musk, and pepper. In 97 CE a Han envoy, Gan Ying, reached the sea in the west and turned back, after sailors there warned him the crossing could take years.

很少有人從頭走到尾。Britannica 說得很簡單：貨物是由中間商一段一段轉手的。撒馬爾罕一帶的粟特商人，在許多城鎮都有夥伴；他們大約在西元 313 年寫的信，提到了銀、麻布、麝香和胡椒。西元 97 年，漢朝的使者甘英往西走到了海邊，當地的船夫告訴他渡海可能要好幾年，他就回頭了。

The sea routes were just as important. Ships sailed between China, Southeast Asia, India, the Persian Gulf, the Red Sea, and East Africa, timing their voyages to the monsoon winds. A ship that sank near the island of Belitung around the 820s or 830s carried about 60,000 objects from China, most of them painted bowls. Later, ports such as Quanzhou in China and Kilwa in East Africa grew rich on this trade.

海路一樣重要。船在中國、東南亞、印度、波斯灣、紅海和東非之間航行，看季風決定什麼時候出發。大約在 820 或 830 年代，一艘在勿里洞島附近沉沒的船，載著大約六萬件來自中國的東西，大部分是彩繪的碗。後來，中國的泉州、東非的基爾瓦等港口，都因為這些貿易而富裕起來。

Silk was only one of many things that traveled. Horses, grapes, and alfalfa came east to China; paper and the skill of making it went west, reaching Samarkand by 751 and Baghdad by 793. Glass, spices, porcelain, and coins moved too. So did beliefs: Buddhism, Manichaeism, Christianity, and Islam all spread along these routes. And so did disease: the Black Death of the 1340s probably traveled along these routes, though scholars still debate exactly how.

絲只是許多旅行的東西之一。馬、葡萄和苜蓿往東傳到中國；紙和造紙的技術往西走，751 年到了撒馬爾罕，793 年到了巴格達。玻璃、香料、瓷器和錢幣也在流動。信仰也一樣：佛教、摩尼教、基督宗教和伊斯蘭教都沿著這些路傳播。疾病也是：1340 年代的黑死病，很可能是沿著這些路傳開的，只是到底怎麼傳的，學者還在討論。

No single people controlled the routes. Chinese, Sogdian, Persian, Indian, Arab, Malay, Swahili, and many other traders each knew their own stretch. What made the network work was not one long road, but many shorter connections, each linking one place to the next. That is why ideas and goods could travel so far, even though most people did not.

沒有哪一個民族控制這些路。中國、粟特、波斯、印度、阿拉伯、馬來、斯瓦希里，還有許多其他的商人，各自熟悉自己的那一段。讓這張網運作起來的，不是一條很長的路，而是許多比較短的連結，每一段把一個地方連到下一個地方。這就是為什麼大部分的人沒有走得很遠，想法和貨物卻能傳得那麼遠。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Why do historians say “Silk Roads” or “a network” instead of “the Silk Road”?

There was no single road. Many land and sea routes connected different places at different times, and the name itself was first used in the 1800s.

Name two things that traveled east toward China and two that traveled west.

East: horses, grapes, alfalfa, Mediterranean glass, Christianity (any two). West: silk, papermaking, Changsha bowls and porcelain (any two).

If few people traveled the whole way, how did goods and ideas get so far?

They were passed from trader to trader, stage by stage. Each group knew its own stretch, and the connections between them carried things across the whole network.

    How Do We Know? · 我們怎麼知道？

    Letters, shipwrecks, and travelers' tales 信、沉船，和旅人的故事

    Merchants rarely wrote history books, so historians piece together the trade from many small clues.
    商人很少寫史書，所以歷史學家要從許多小線索拼出這些貿易。

    ✉️

    Merchants' own letters商人自己寫的信

    In 1907, in a watchtower just west of the Jade Gate, the explorer Aurel Stein found a bag of letters written in the Sogdian language. They show merchants in China writing to partners in Samarkand about silver, linen, musk, and pepper.
    1907 年，探險家斯坦因在玉門關西邊不遠的一座烽燧裡，找到一袋用粟特文寫的信。信裡是在中國的商人寫給撒馬爾罕的夥伴，談到銀、麻布、麝香和胡椒。

    Its limits · 它的限制The letters never reached their readers, and they reflect one short time of crisis. Scholars still debate their exact date.這些信從來沒有送到收信人手上，而且只反映一段短短的危機時期。它們確切的年代，學者還在討論。

    The Sogdian Ancient Letters, trans. Sims-Williams (University of Washington) ↗
    ⚓

    Shipwrecks沉船

    A sunken ship is like a time capsule. The Belitung wreck, found by fishermen in 1998, held about 60,000 objects. The Nanhai No. 1, a Song dynasty ship lifted in 2007, carried thousands of pieces of porcelain.
    一艘沉船就像一個時光膠囊。1998 年漁民發現的勿里洞沉船，裡面有大約六萬件東西。2007 年打撈起來的宋代沉船「南海一號」，載了好幾千件瓷器。

    Its limits · 它的限制A wreck shows one ship on one voyage. Studies of other wrecks suggest some ships sold their cargo at many ports, while others sailed to a single market.一艘沉船只代表一趟航程的一艘船。對其他沉船的研究顯示，有些船在許多港口一路賣貨，有些則是開往單一的市場。

    Singapore National Heritage Board; UNESCO Courier; Li, Asian Archaeology (2020) ↗
    🧭

    Histories and travelers' accounts史書和旅人的遊記

    Chinese histories record envoys such as Zhang Qian and Gan Ying. Later travelers wrote long accounts: Ibn Battuta from Morocco, from 1325, and Marco Polo from Venice, who said he spent seventeen years in China.
    中國的史書記載了張騫、甘英等使者。後來的旅人寫下了很長的遊記：從 1325 年出發的摩洛哥人伊本・巴杜達，以及說自己在中國住了十七年的威尼斯人馬可・波羅。

    Its limits · 它的限制Travelers sometimes repeated stories they had only heard, and historians debate how much of Marco Polo's book, and of Ibn Battuta's chapter on China, comes from what they saw themselves.旅人有時會把只是聽來的故事也寫進去；馬可・波羅的書，以及伊本・巴杜達寫中國的那一章，有多少是親眼看到的，歷史學家還在討論。

    《後漢書・西域傳》；Britannica, “Marco Polo” and “Ibn Battuta” ↗

    Different Accounts · 不同的說法

    Four questions about the Silk Roads 關於絲路的四個問題

    Historians agree that the routes mattered, but they disagree on some important details. Here are four questions, with the views set side by side.
    歷史學家都同意這些路很重要，但對一些重要的細節看法不同。下面是四個問題，各種說法並列。

    Who named the “Silk Road”?「絲路」這個名字是誰取的？

    • Richthofen, 1877李希霍芬，1877 年

      Many books say the German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen coined the name in 1877. UNESCO says the name is recent and dates it to the mid-1800s.
      很多書說，德國地理學家李希霍芬在 1877 年創了這個名字。聯合國教科文組織說這個名字很新，是十九世紀中期才有的。

      UNESCO, About the Silk Roads ↗

    • Used earlier更早就有人用

      A 2019 study found the German word Seidenstraße in print from 1858, and argues that Richthofen popularized the name rather than inventing it.
      2019 年的一項研究發現，德文的 Seidenstraße 在 1858 年就出現在印刷品上；研究者主張，李希霍芬是讓這個名字流行起來，而不是發明它。

      Mertens, The Silk Road 17 (2019) ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Either way, the people who used the routes had no single name for them.不管哪一種說法，當年走這些路的人，都沒有替它們取一個統一的名字。

    How big was the trade?這些貿易有多大？

    • Mostly small and local大多是小規模、短距離的

      The historian Valerie Hansen argues that most travelers moved in small circuits, and that the trade was mainly a trickle between neighboring towns, not great caravans crossing Asia.
      歷史學家韓森（Valerie Hansen）主張，大部分的旅人只在小範圍裡來回，貿易主要是鄰近城鎮之間細細的流動，而不是橫越亞洲的大商隊。

      Hansen, OUPblog (2012) ↗

    • A great expansion大大地擴張

      UNESCO says the volume of exchange increased substantially from the 2nd century BCE, and the Metropolitan Museum describes a great expansion of international trade by the end of the 1st century BCE.
      聯合國教科文組織說，從西元前二世紀起，交流的規模大幅增加；大都會博物館也說，到西元前一世紀末，國際貿易大大地擴張。

      UNESCO, Chang'an-Tianshan Corridor; Metropolitan Museum of Art ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況The two views can fit together: small steps between neighbors could still move a great deal over centuries. The sea routes, which carried bulk cargo, add another part of the picture.兩種說法可以同時成立：鄰居之間的小步伐，經過幾百年也能搬動很多東西。能載大量貨物的海路，又是另一部分的圖像。

    Did Marco Polo really go to China?馬可・波羅真的去過中國嗎？

    • Probably not可能沒有

      The historian Frances Wood argued that he probably never went much further than his family's trading posts on the Black Sea and in Constantinople, and noted that Chinese records do not mention him.
      歷史學家吳芳思（Frances Wood）主張，他大概沒有走得比家族在黑海和君士坦丁堡的貿易站更遠；她也指出，中國的紀錄沒有提到他。

      Review of Vogel, Reviews in History 1667 ↗

    • Yes去過

      The historian Hans Ulrich Vogel argued in 2012 that Polo's detailed and accurate information about Chinese money and taxes could not have come from anywhere else.
      歷史學家傅漢思（Hans Ulrich Vogel）在 2012 年主張，馬可・波羅對中國錢幣和稅收的資料那麼詳細、正確，不可能是從別的地方來的。

      Review of Vogel, Marco Polo Was in China (2012), Reviews in History 1667 ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Several scholars, including Vogel, have since argued that he did go. Everyone agrees his book mixes what he saw with what he heard.後來有不少學者，包括傅漢思，主張他確實去過。大家都同意，他的書把看到的和聽到的混在一起。

    Where did the Black Death begin?黑死病是從哪裡開始的？

    • Central Asia, 1330s中亞，1330 年代

      A 2022 study of DNA from people buried in 1338–39 near Lake Issyk-Kul found the plague bacterium and argues the strain arose in that region shortly before the pandemic.
      2022 年的一項研究，分析伊塞克湖附近 1338–39 年下葬的人的 DNA，找到鼠疫桿菌，並主張那一株病菌就是在大流行前不久，在那一帶出現的。

      Spyrou et al., Nature (2022) ↗

    • Spread earlier, in the 1200s更早，在 1200 年代就散布開

      The historian Monica Green argues that Mongol armies unknowingly moved plague across Central Eurasia in the 1200s, and that the 1300s outbreaks were later local spillovers.
      歷史學家莫妮卡・格林（Monica Green）主張，蒙古軍隊在 1200 年代就不知不覺地把鼠疫帶過歐亞大陸中部，1300 年代的爆發是後來各地的外溢。

      Green, American Historical Review (2020) ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Both views connect the plague to Central Asia and to the movement of people and goods. They differ on when it spread and how.兩種說法都把鼠疫和中亞、和人與貨物的移動連在一起；分歧在於什麼時候、怎麼傳開的。

    Notice how a single new find, such as a gravestone or a letter, can change an old story.留意一個新的發現，例如一塊墓碑或一封信，怎麼改變一個老故事。

    Taiwan at That Time · 那時候的台灣

    Beads, coins, and a nearby island 珠子、錢幣，和附近的一個島

    Taiwan was not on the main routes, but objects from overseas reached it, and later Chinese writers described the islands nearby.
    台灣不在主要的路線上，但海外的東西還是傳到了這裡；後來中國的作家也記下了附近的島。

    Archaeologists in Taiwan give dates as “years ago.” About 1,500 years ago is roughly the 5th century CE.
    台灣的考古學家用「距今幾年」說年代。距今大約一千五百年，差不多是西元五世紀。

    • Shisanhang culture, north北部的十三行文化

      The Shisanhang people worked iron, and their sites held gold ornaments, glass bracelets, and Chinese coins from the Tang and Song dynasties. The museum says these show overseas trade.
      十三行文化的人會煉鐵；遺址裡有金飾、玻璃手環，以及唐宋時代的中國古錢。史前館說，這些顯示他們有海外貿易。

    • Jingpu culture, east東部的靜浦文化

      On the east coast, the Jingpu culture, from about 1,500 to a few hundred years ago, had agate and glass ornaments that came from overseas.
      在東海岸，大約距今一千五百年到兩、三百年前的靜浦文化，有來自海外的瑪瑙和琉璃裝飾品。

    Stories Behind the Evidence · 證據背後的故事

    An envoy at the sea, a bag of letters, 60,000 objects, and a stone in two languages 海邊的使者、一袋信、六萬件東西，和一塊兩種文字的石碑

    Four short stories about people and objects on the Silk Roads.
    四個小故事：絲路上的人和東西。

    97 CE · The western sea · 西海

    The envoy who turned back回頭的使者

    Gan Ying was sent toward Daqin, the Chinese name for the Roman world. At the sea, Parthian sailors told him that with bad winds the crossing could take two years, so travelers took three years of food. He turned back. The same history says Parthia wanted to keep the silk trade with Rome for itself.
    甘英被派往大秦，也就是中國人對羅馬世界的稱呼。到了海邊，安息的船夫告訴他，風不好的話，渡海可能要兩年，所以出海的人都帶三年的糧食。他就回頭了。同一本史書也說，安息想把和羅馬的絲綢買賣留給自己。

    About 313 CE · Near the Jade Gate · 玉門關附近

    Letters that never arrived沒有寄到的信

    A Sogdian merchant wrote home that the Indians and Sogdians in Luoyang had all died of starvation, during a famine that also drove the emperor from the city. His letters were lost in a watchtower, where they waited about 1,600 years to be read.
    一位粟特商人寫信回家，說洛陽發生饑荒，那裡的印度人和粟特人都餓死了；信裡還說皇帝也因為饑荒逃出了洛陽。他的信遺落在一座烽燧裡，等了大約一千六百年才被讀到。

    1998 · Off Belitung · 勿里洞外海

    A cargo of bowls一船的碗

    Fishermen diving for sea cucumbers found the wreck. Its planks were sewn together with rope, in a style from the Persian Gulf, and it held more than 50,000 painted bowls from Changsha, packed in large jars.
    潛水抓海參的漁民發現了這艘沉船。它的船板是用繩子縫起來的，是波斯灣的造船方法；船上有五萬多個長沙窯的彩繪碗，裝在大陶罐裡。

    635 CE · Chang'an · 長安

    A stone in two languages兩種文字的石碑

    A stone stele in Chinese and Syriac records that a priest called Aluoben brought the Christian teaching of the Church of the East to Chang'an in 635. It is one of many signs that the Tang capital welcomed many religions.
    一塊用中文和敘利亞文寫的石碑記載，635 年一位叫阿羅本的教士，把東方教會的基督教帶到長安。這是唐朝都城接納許多宗教的眾多證據之一。

    Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

    Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

    ✗ Myth · 迷思The Silk Road was one long road.絲路是一條很長的路。

    ✓ Fact · 事實It was a network of many land and sea routes. The British Museum puts it this way: not the story of one road, but of many routes. Even Taiwan's Ministry of Education dictionary defines it only as an overland route, an older view.它是一張由許多陸路和海路組成的網。大英博物館說：這不是一條路的故事，而是許多路線的故事。連教育部的國語辭典也只把它解釋成陸路，那是比較舊的看法。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Merchants traveled all the way from China to Rome.商人從中國一路走到羅馬。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Few people traveled the whole way. Goods were handled stage by stage by middlemen, and the Han envoy Gan Ying turned back at the sea in 97 CE.很少有人從頭走到尾。貨物是由中間商一段一段轉手的；漢朝的使者甘英在西元 97 年到了海邊就回頭了。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Romans and Han Chinese met all the time.羅馬人和漢朝人常常見面。

    ✓ Fact · 事實The History of the Later Han records only one embassy from Daqin, in 166 CE, which came by sea. The historian noted that its gifts were nothing special and suspected that earlier stories about Daqin had been exaggerated. Each empire knew the other mostly by hearsay.《後漢書》只記載了一次大秦的使節，在西元 166 年從海路來。史家說它獻上的東西並沒有什麼珍奇，因此懷疑以前傳說大秦的人說得太誇張。兩個帝國大多只是聽說過對方。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Marco Polo brought pasta from China to Italy.馬可・波羅把麵從中國帶到義大利。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Food historians show that pasta was known in Italy before Polo came home in 1295. The story seems to have spread in the 20th century.研究飲食史的學者指出，馬可・波羅在 1295 年回家以前，義大利就已經有麵食了。這個故事似乎是二十世紀才流傳開的。

    Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

    Hand It On 一手傳一手

    You need · 準備材料

    • One “bolt of silk” (a strip of cloth or paper) and five station cards: Chang'an, Dunhuang, Kashgar, Palmyra, the Mediterranean coast一「匹絲」（一條布或紙），和五張站名卡：長安、敦煌、喀什、帕邁拉、地中海沿岸
    • A price sheet for each station每一站一張價目單

    Steps · 步驟

    1. Place five groups around the room as stations. Each group may trade only with its neighbors.把五組學生排在教室四周當五個站。每一組只能和隔壁的站交易。
    2. The first station sells the silk to the next for a price. Each station adds its costs (food, water, tolls, risk) before selling it on.第一站把絲賣給下一站。每一站要先加上自己的成本（食物、水、過路費、風險），再往下賣。
    3. At the last station, compare the final price with the first. Ask: did anyone on the Mediterranean coast ever meet anyone from Chang'an?到最後一站，比較最後的價錢和一開始的價錢。問：地中海沿岸的人有沒有見過長安的人？
    4. Discuss in English: “The price rose because …” and “Nobody traveled the whole way, but …”用英文討論：「The price rose because …」和「Nobody traveled the whole way, but …」

    The prices are made up for the game. The point is the pattern: many short links, each adding cost, and no one who sees both ends.
    價錢是遊戲自己定的。重點在於模式：許多短短的連結，每一段都加上成本，而且沒有人看得到兩端。

    Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

    The Journey of One Thing 一件東西的旅程

    You need · 準備材料

    • The 3D globe in this lesson這一課的 3D 地球儀
    • A blank map of Asia, Africa, and Europe (land only)一張只畫陸地的亞洲、非洲、歐洲空白地圖

    Steps · 步驟

    1. In pairs, choose one of the five things in “Follow one thing” and watch its journey.兩人一組，在「跟著一件東西走」裡選一件，看它的旅程。
    2. Draw its route on the map. Label each stretch with who carried it.把路線畫在地圖上，每一段寫上是誰帶的。
    3. Write three sentences using “passed through,” “from hand to hand,” and “in exchange for.”用 passed through、from hand to hand、in exchange for 寫三句英文。
    4. Share with another pair: which journey changed hands the most times?和另一組分享：哪一段旅程換手最多次？

    Remind students that the globe shows likely journeys, not the record of one object.
    提醒學生：地球儀畫的是可能的旅程，不是某一件東西的紀錄。
