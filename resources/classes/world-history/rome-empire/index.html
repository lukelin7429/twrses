World History · Unit 2 · Lesson 6 · 世界歷史 單元二 第六課

How Did Rome Hold an Empire Together?羅馬怎麼管住那麼大的地方？

Build a stone arch in 3D, take away its wooden frame, and find out when it stands and when it falls. Then watch Rome grow on a map, and sort out which Roman wonders were older than Rome.
在 3D 裡蓋一座石拱，拆掉木頭拱架，看看它什麼時候站得住、什麼時候會塌。再從地圖上看羅馬怎麼長大，分一分哪些羅馬的奇蹟其實比羅馬還早。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

Rome grew from one city into an empire that stretched from Britain to the Middle East. It was held together not only by armies, but also by roads, shared laws, the spread of citizenship, aqueducts, and cities. It was also built by conquest, and the people who were conquered paid a heavy price.
羅馬從一座城市長成一個從不列顛延伸到中東的帝國。把它連在一起的，不只是軍隊，還有道路、共同的法律、公民權的擴大、水道和城市。它也是征服來的，被征服的人付出了沉重的代價。

The arch · 這座拱

Stones in place已經放好的石頭
Thickness ÷ radius (to the middle of the stones)厚度 ÷ 半徑（量到石頭的中線）
The thinnest that can stand撐得住的最薄比例

A half-circle arch carrying only its own weight needs a thickness of at least about 0.1075 times its radius, measured to the middle of the stones (Milankovitch's solution; Heyman's simpler method gives about 0.106). This assumes the stones do not slide or crush and the abutments do not move. Real arches also carry roads, water, or walls.一座只承受自己重量的半圓拱，厚度至少要有半徑（量到石頭的中線）的大約 0.1075 倍（米蘭科維奇的解；海曼比較簡單的方法算出約 0.106）。這假設石頭不會滑動、不會被壓碎，拱座也不會移動。真實的拱上面還壓著道路、水道或牆。

Unit 6: How Did Rome Hold an Empire Together?

In Lesson 5, Athens was a small city-state. Rome began small too, as a city in central Italy. Later Romans said it was founded in 753 BCE and became a republic in 509 BCE, but these are traditions; the early centuries are poorly recorded. By about 275 BCE Rome ruled Italy. Over the next two and a half centuries it fought its way around the Mediterranean Sea, and in 27 BCE the republic became an empire ruled by emperors.

第五課的雅典是一個小城邦。羅馬一開始也很小，是義大利中部的一座城市。後來的羅馬人說，羅馬建於西元前 753 年，在西元前 509 年成為共和國；不過這些都是傳統的說法，最早的幾百年記錄很少。大約西元前 275 年，羅馬統治了義大利。接下來的兩百五十年左右，它一路打到地中海四周；西元前 27 年，共和國變成由皇帝統治的帝國。

How do you hold together lands from Britain to Egypt? One answer was roads. A recent digital atlas maps about 300,000 kilometers of roads in the Roman Empire around 150 CE, though the exact route of most of them is not known for certain. Soldiers, messengers, traders, and travelers used them. Roads let the government send orders and armies quickly, and let goods and ideas move too.

從不列顛到埃及，這麼大的地方要怎麼連在一起？一個答案是道路。一份新的數位地圖集，畫出西元 150 年前後羅馬帝國大約三十萬公里的道路；不過其中大部分的確切路線，並不能確定。士兵、信差、商人和旅人都走這些路。有了路，政府可以很快送出命令、調動軍隊，貨物和想法也跟著流動。

Another answer was building. The Romans did not invent the arch, but they used it on a huge scale, in bridges, aqueducts, and great buildings. Over about 500 years, eleven aqueducts were built to bring water to the city of Rome. One aqueduct for the city of Nîmes, in Gaul, ran about 50 kilometers and dropped only about 11 meters along the way, crossing a valley on the three-level bridge now called the Pont du Gard. Rome's water commissioner Frontinus compared the aqueducts with the “idle Pyramids.”

另一個答案是建設。拱不是羅馬人發明的，但他們把拱用到很大的規模：橋、水道和大型建築。五百年間，羅馬一共蓋了十一條把水送進城裡的水道。高盧的尼姆城有一條水道，長約 50 公里，一路上只下降大約 11 公尺，還靠一座三層的橋跨過一個山谷，就是今天所說的加爾橋。羅馬的水務官弗朗提努斯，把這些水道拿來和「閒置的金字塔」相比。

Rome also spread its laws and its citizenship. At first, Roman citizens were mainly the people of the city of Rome and the lands near it. Over time, citizenship was given to more and more people, and in 212 CE the emperor Caracalla gave it to almost all free people in the empire. Much later, in the 500s CE, Roman law was collected into one great work. Taiwan's civil law belongs to the continental European civil-law tradition, whose roots go back to Roman law.

羅馬也把它的法律和公民權傳了出去。一開始，羅馬公民主要是羅馬城和附近地方的人。後來公民權給了越來越多的人；西元 212 年，卡拉卡拉皇帝把它給了帝國裡幾乎所有的自由人。很久以後，在西元 500 年代，羅馬法被整理成一部大全。台灣的民法屬於歐陸法（大陸法）傳統，這個傳統的根源可以追溯到羅馬法。

But the empire was built by conquest. The Romans destroyed Carthage in 146 BCE and enslaved its people. Ancient writers such as Plutarch claimed that Julius Caesar's wars in Gaul killed a million people; historians doubt the exact figures, but the losses were enormous. In Britain, Queen Boudica led a revolt after Roman officials seized her family's kingdom. In 70 CE, Roman soldiers captured Jerusalem and burned its Temple.

但是這個帝國是征服來的。西元前 146 年，羅馬人摧毀了迦太基，把那裡的人變成奴隸。普魯塔克等古代的作者說，凱撒在高盧的戰爭殺了一百萬人；歷史學家懷疑確切的數字，但損失非常巨大。在不列顛，羅馬官員奪走布狄卡女王家族的王國以後，她領導了一場起事。西元 70 年，羅馬軍隊攻下耶路撒冷，燒毀了聖殿。

At the same time, at the other end of Asia, the Han dynasty ruled China. In 97 CE a Han envoy named Gan Ying traveled west toward Rome and reached a great sea, but turned back. In 166 CE, a Chinese history records, men who said they came from the king of Da Qin, the Chinese name for Rome, arrived through what is now Vietnam. The western half of the Roman Empire ended in 476 CE, while the eastern half lasted much longer. Lesson 7 looks at how the Han held their own empire together.

在同一個時代，亞洲的另一端是漢朝統治的中國。西元 97 年，一位叫甘英的漢朝使者往西朝羅馬走，到了一片大海邊，卻折返了。一部中國的史書記載，西元 166 年，自稱是大秦（中國對羅馬的稱呼）國王派來的人，經由今天的越南來到中國。羅馬帝國的西半部在西元 476 年結束，東半部則延續了更久。第七課要看漢朝怎麼把自己的帝國連在一起。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Name three things, other than armies, that helped hold the Roman Empire together.

Roads, shared laws, the spread of citizenship, aqueducts, and cities. Any three are correct.

Why does a stone arch need a wooden frame while it is being built, and what lets it stand afterward?

Until the last stone, the keystone, is in place, the two halves of the arch cannot lean on each other, so they must be held up. Once the arch is closed, each stone presses on its neighbors, the abutments hold the outward push, and if the arch is thick enough it stands on its own.

The lesson says the Romans did not invent the arch. What did they do with it instead?

They used it on a huge scale, in bridges, aqueducts, and great buildings, and combined arches in new ways, such as the groin vault.

A water commissioner's book一位水務官的書

Around 97 CE, Frontinus was put in charge of Rome's water supply and wrote a book about the aqueducts, listing them, their builders, and how much water they carried.
大約西元 97 年，弗朗提努斯負責管理羅馬的供水，寫了一本關於水道的書，列出各條水道、建造的人，以及它們送多少水。

Where things stand · 目前的情況The two numbers count different things. Asking “what was counted?” matters as much as the number.兩個數字算的東西不一樣。問「算了什麼？」和數字本身一樣重要。

How thin can a stone arch be?石拱最薄能多薄？

  • About 0.106 of the radius大約半徑的 0.106 倍

    The engineer Jacques Heyman, in the 1960s, gave a simple method that puts the minimum at about 0.106 of the radius for a half-circle arch.
    工程師海曼在 1960 年代提出一個簡單的方法，算出半圓拱最薄大約是半徑的 0.106 倍。

    Heyman, International Journal of Mechanical Sciences 11 (1969) ↗

  • About 0.1075 of the radius大約半徑的 0.1075 倍

    A more exact solution, published by Milutin Milankovitch in 1907, gives about 0.1075. A 2021 study says Heyman's method finds the main features more simply, but puts one of the hinges in the wrong place.
    米蘭科維奇在 1907 年發表了更精確的解，大約是 0.1075。2021 年的一項研究說，海曼的方法比較簡單、也抓得到主要的特徵，但有一個鉸點的位置算錯了。

    Nikolić, Acta Mechanica (2017); Cocchetti and Rizzi, Archive of Applied Mechanics (2021) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況Both are simplified models, and both measure the radius to the middle of the stones. Real arches also depend on the load above them and on their abutments. The 3D model uses 0.1075.兩個都是簡化的模型，半徑都量到石頭的中線。真實的拱還要看上面壓了什麼、拱座穩不穩。這一課的 3D 模型用 0.1075。

How many people lived in the Roman Empire?羅馬帝國住了多少人？

  • About 46 million大約四千六百萬

    The historian Bruce Frier estimated about 46 million people under Augustus.
    歷史學家弗里爾估計，奧古斯都的時候大約有四千六百萬人。

    As summarized by the University of St Andrews, “Uncertain Past” (2018) ↗

  • 59 to 72 million五千九百萬到七千二百萬

    Walter Scheidel estimated 59 to 72 million in 164 CE, just before a great epidemic.
    沙伊德爾估計，西元 164 年、一場大瘟疫之前，大約有五千九百萬到七千二百萬人。

    As summarized by the University of St Andrews, “Uncertain Past” (2018) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況The estimates are for different dates and use different methods. No full census of the empire survives.這些估計的年代不同、方法也不同。整個帝國的完整人口普查，沒有留下來。

Is “Romanization” a good way to describe what happened?「羅馬化」這個說法恰當嗎？

  • People became Roman大家變成了羅馬人

    An older picture said conquered peoples gradually took up Roman language, dress, cities, and ways of life, a process called Romanization.
    比較早的說法認為，被征服的民族慢慢採用了羅馬的語言、服裝、城市和生活方式，這個過程叫做「羅馬化」。

    Described in Mattingly, Imperialism, Power, and Identity (Princeton, 2011) ↗

  • A word that hides too much一個遮住太多事的詞

    The archaeologist David Mattingly rejects “outdated notions about Romanization” and looks at Roman rule from the point of view of the ruled, whose lives it could improve, challenge, or destroy.
    考古學家馬丁利拒絕「關於羅馬化的過時觀念」，改從被統治者的角度看羅馬的統治：它可能讓人的生活變好、受到挑戰，或被毀掉。

    Mattingly, Imperialism, Power, and Identity (Princeton, 2011) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況The debate is about whose experience counts: the people who prospered under Rome, or also those who lost land, freedom, or lives.這場爭論問的是：誰的經驗算數？是在羅馬統治下過得好的人，還是也包括失去土地、自由或生命的人？

Notice that new tools, such as digital maps and better physics, can change the numbers, not only new discoveries in the ground.留意能改變數字的，不只是地底下的新發現，也包括新的工具，例如數位地圖和更精確的力學。

Taiwan at That Time · 那時候的台灣

Glass, gold, and trade across the sea 玻璃、黃金與海上的交易

Around the time of the Roman Empire, about 2,000 years ago, people in Taiwan were also part of trade networks across the sea, though on a very different scale.
大約在羅馬帝國的時代，距今大約兩千年，台灣的人也身在海上的交易網絡裡，只是規模和形式非常不一樣。

🏝️

Archaeologists in Taiwan give dates as “years ago” (before 1950). About 2,000 years ago is roughly the first century BCE or CE.
台灣的考古學家用「距今幾年」（以 1950 年為準）說年代。距今大約兩千年，差不多是西元前一世紀到西元一世紀。

  • Shisanhang culture十三行文化

    The National Museum of Prehistory says the Shisanhang culture rose near the Taipei Basin about 2,000 years ago. Its gold ornaments and glass bracelets, from different periods, point to trade with lands overseas.
    國立臺灣史前文化博物館說，「大約二千年前，十三行文化在臺北盆地附近興起」；那裡不同時期的金飾和玻璃手環，顯示他們和海外有交易。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

  • Glass beads at Jiuxianglan舊香蘭的玻璃珠

    At Jiuxianglan in Taitung, an Iron Age site dated from about the third century BCE to the eighth century CE, archaeologists found glass beads and waste from bead-making. A 2018 study found that the beads did not match the waste, so the beads may have been made elsewhere and brought in.
    台東的舊香蘭是一處鐵器時代的遺址，年代大約從西元前三世紀到西元八世紀。考古學家在那裡找到玻璃珠和做珠子留下的廢料。2018 年的一項研究發現，珠子和廢料的成分對不上，所以這些珠子可能是在別的地方做好、再帶進來的。

    Wang et al., Archaeological and Anthropological Sciences (2018) ↗

  • When did iron-making arrive?製鐵技術什麼時候來的？

    The same museum says the technique of making iron tools reached Taiwan about 1,500 years ago, while it also describes iron in the Shisanhang culture. Dates for Taiwan's metal age differ between sources.
    同一座博物館說，「約一千五百年前，製作鐵器的技術傳入臺灣」；它也說十三行文化有鐵器。台灣金屬器時代的年代，各資料說法不一。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

  • A different kind of connection不一樣的連結

    There was no empire in Taiwan, but the Shisanhang people traded across the sea: the museum points to gold ornaments and glass bracelets from overseas. Rome was held together by roads; Taiwan's ancient connections ran over water.
    台灣沒有帝國，但十三行的人透過海洋和外地交易；博物館指出，那裡出土了金飾和玻璃手環。羅馬靠道路連在一起；台灣古代的連結，走的是水路。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

Stories Behind the Evidence · 證據背後的故事

Idle pyramids, a queen's revolt, a Chinese name for Rome, and a saying 閒置的金字塔、一位女王的起事、中國對羅馬的稱呼，和一句俗語

Four short stories about how the Romans saw themselves, and how others saw them.
四個小故事：羅馬人怎麼看自己，別人又怎麼看羅馬。

About 97 CE · Rome · 羅馬

Better than the Pyramids?比金字塔還好？

Frontinus, in charge of Rome's water, wrote that the city's many aqueducts were far more useful than “the idle Pyramids” or the famous works of the Greeks. It is a proud official's opinion, and a reminder that Lesson 4's pyramids were already ancient to him.
負責羅馬供水的弗朗提努斯寫道，羅馬那麼多的水道，比「閒置的金字塔」或希臘人有名的作品有用得多。這是一位驕傲的官員的看法；也提醒我們，第四課的金字塔對他來說已經很古老了。

60 or 61 CE · Britain · 不列顛

Boudica's revolt布狄卡的起事

When her husband, a local king, died, he left part of his wealth to the emperor, hoping to protect his family. Instead Roman officials seized the kingdom. Queen Boudica led a revolt that burned the Roman towns of Camulodunum (Colchester), Verulamium (St Albans), and Londinium (London) before it was defeated. The Roman historian Tacitus gives huge casualty figures; the editor of a standard translation calls them “equally incredible.”
她的丈夫是當地的國王，死前把一部分財產留給皇帝，希望能保護家人。結果羅馬官員卻奪走了他的王國。布狄卡女王領導起事，燒了卡姆洛杜努姆（今科爾切斯特）、維魯拉米恩（今聖奧爾本斯）和倫蒂尼恩（今倫敦）這幾座羅馬城鎮，最後被打敗。羅馬史家塔西佗寫下的死傷數字非常大，一部標準譯本的編者認為雙方的數字「同樣不可信」。

97 and 166 CE · Han China · 漢朝

Da Qin, the land west of the sea大秦，海西之國

The Book of the Later Han calls Rome Da Qin, also Lijian, a country “west of the sea.” It records that Gan Ying reached Tiaozhi but did not cross the sea, and that in 166 CE envoys from Da Qin's king Andun arrived through Rinan, on the southern frontier, with ivory, rhinoceros horn, and tortoiseshell. The same text adds that the gifts were nothing rare, and doubts the reports.
《後漢書・西域傳》說：「大秦國，一名犂鞬，以在海西，亦云海西國。」書裡記載甘英「抵條支」，沒有渡海；又說延熹九年（西元 166 年），「大秦王安敦遣使自日南徼外獻象牙、犀角、瑇瑁，始乃一通焉。」書裡也說，他們的貢品「並無珍異，疑傳者過焉」。

About 1175 CE · Europe · 歐洲

“All roads lead to Rome”「條條大路通羅馬」

The saying is not Roman. It probably goes back to a Latin line in a medieval book of proverbs by Alain de Lille: “a thousand roads lead people to Rome.” The next line speaks of seeking the Lord. English and French versions followed centuries later.
這句俗語不是羅馬人說的。它大概來自中世紀里爾的阿蘭（Alain de Lille）一本諺語集裡的一句拉丁文：「千條道路把人帶到羅馬」，下一句說的是尋求上主。英文和法文的說法，是好幾百年以後才出現的。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思The Romans invented the arch.拱是羅馬人發明的。

✓ Fact · 事實Arches were known in ancient Egypt and Greece. The Romans used them on a far larger scale and combined them in new ways, such as the groin vault.古埃及和希臘就知道拱。羅馬人把它用到大得多的規模，並用新的方式組合，例如交叉拱頂。

✗ Myth · 迷思“All roads lead to Rome” is an ancient Roman saying.「條條大路通羅馬」是古羅馬的俗語。

✓ Fact · 事實It probably comes from a medieval Latin proverb of about 1175 CE, long after the western empire ended.它大概來自大約西元 1175 年中世紀的一句拉丁諺語，那時西羅馬帝國早就結束了。

✗ Myth · 迷思Everyone in the empire was a Roman citizen from the start.帝國裡的每個人一開始就是羅馬公民。

✓ Fact · 事實Citizenship spread slowly. Only in 212 CE did it reach almost all free people, and enslaved people were still left out.公民權是慢慢擴大的。要到西元 212 年才給了幾乎所有的自由人，被奴役的人仍然不在其中。

✗ Myth · 迷思Roman concrete was just ordinary mortar.羅馬混凝土只是普通的砂漿。

✓ Fact · 事實A 2023 study found Roman builders hot-mixed quicklime with volcanic ash, and the lime lumps left inside may help it heal small cracks, one reason some Roman structures have lasted so long.2023 年的一項研究發現，羅馬的工匠把生石灰和火山灰熱拌；留在裡面的石灰塊可能幫它修補細小的裂縫，這是有些羅馬建築能撐這麼久的原因之一。

Four things besides armies that held the empire together.
除了軍隊之外，把帝國連在一起的四樣東西。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

Build a Sugar-Cube Arch 用方糖蓋一座拱

You need · 準備材料

  • Sugar cubes or small wooden blocks, a butter knife to shape them, and two stacks of books as abutments方糖或小木塊、一把奶油刀用來修形狀，以及兩疊書當拱座
  • A curved piece of cardboard as centering一片彎曲的硬紙板當拱架

Steps · 步驟

  1. Shape the cubes into wedges, a little narrower at the bottom than at the top.把方糖修成楔形，下面比上面窄一點。
  2. Stand the cardboard between the two book stacks and lay the wedges over it from both sides. Put the keystone in last.把硬紙板立在兩疊書中間，從兩邊把楔形塊放上去，最後放拱心石。
  3. Slide the cardboard out. Does the arch stand? Now slide one book stack a little outward. What happens?把硬紙板抽出來。拱站得住嗎？接著把一疊書稍微往外推。會發生什麼事？
  4. Explain in English: “The arch stands because …” and “It fell when …”用英文說明：「The arch stands because …」和「It fell when …」

Step 3 shows why abutments matter: an arch pushes outward, and if its ends can move, it falls.
第三步讓學生看到拱座為什麼重要：拱會往外推，兩端一動，拱就塌了。

Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

Two Empires, One Table 兩個帝國，一張表

You need · 準備材料

  • A large sheet of paper divided into two columns: Rome and Han China一張大紙，分成兩欄：羅馬和漢朝
  • This lesson, and Lesson 7 when it is ready這一課，以及之後的第七課

Steps · 步驟

  1. List what held each empire together: roads, laws, writing, money, armies, officials.列出把兩個帝國各自連在一起的東西：道路、法律、文字、錢、軍隊、官員。
  2. Mark what was similar with a ✓ and what was different with a ✗.相似的打 ✓，不同的打 ✗。
  3. Add one row for the people who paid the price of each empire.加一列：寫下每個帝國裡付出代價的人。
  4. Share one sentence using “whereas”: “Rome used …, whereas the Han used …”用 whereas 分享一句：「Rome used …, whereas the Han used …」

Comparing without ranking is the goal: two different ways to hold a huge land together, each with its own costs.
目標是比較而不排名：兩種把一大片土地連在一起的方法，各有各的代價。
