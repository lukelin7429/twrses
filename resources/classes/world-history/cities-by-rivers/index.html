World History · Unit 1 · Lesson 4 · 世界歷史 單元一 第四課

Why Did the First Cities Grow Beside Rivers?最早的城市為什麼都在河邊？

Raise the Great Pyramid course by course in 3D and test four ideas about how its blocks were lifted. Then find on a map the rivers where the first cities grew, and sort out what a river gave a city, and what trouble it brought.
在 3D 裡一層一層蓋起大金字塔，試試四種「石頭怎麼運上去」的想法。再從地圖上找出最早的城市長在哪些河邊，分一分河帶給城市什麼、又帶來什麼麻煩。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

The first cities grew where rivers made it possible to farm enough food for many people who did not farm. But every river behaved differently, and every city had to learn to live with its own river. Rivers made cities possible, not certain.
最早的城市長在河流讓人能種出足夠糧食的地方，多到可以養活許多不種田的人。但每一條河的脾氣都不一樣，每一座城市都得學會跟自己的河相處。河讓城市成為可能，卻不保證一定會有城市。

One long straight ramp一條長長的直坡道

A single ramp runs straight up to one face, and it is raised and lengthened as the pyramid grows.一條坡道直直通到金字塔的一面，金字塔長高，它就跟著加高、加長。

For · 支持的理由It is the simplest idea, and small ramps of this kind were probably used for the lowest courses.這是最簡單的想法；最下面幾層，很可能真的用過這種小坡道。

Problems · 難題At a slope of 1 in 10 it would have been about 1.5 kilometers long. One estimate says it would have needed more than three times the material of the pyramid itself, and no such mound has been found.以每升高 1 公尺走 10 公尺的坡度計算，它會長達大約 1.5 公里。有一個估計認為，它需要的材料是金字塔本身的三倍以上，而且沒有找到這樣的土堆。

Edwards, Technology and Culture 44 (2003); Brier, Archaeology (2007) ↗

A zigzag ramp on one face在一面上來回折返的坡道

The ramp climbs one face of the pyramid, turning back and forth like a mountain path.坡道沿著金字塔的一面往上爬，像山路一樣來回轉彎。

For · 支持的理由It needs far less material than a long straight ramp and fits on a crowded building site.它需要的材料比長長的直坡道少得多，也擠得進施工的工地。

Problems · 難題Turning a sledge with a heavy block at every switchback is slow and dangerous, and the ramp covers the face that builders needed to measure.每一次折返都要把載著大石頭的雪橇轉過來，又慢又危險；坡道還會蓋住工人需要測量的那一面。

Zigzag ramps are described in Arnold, Building in Egypt (1991), and Lehner, The Complete Pyramids (1997), as cited by Edwards (2003); the drawbacks follow Rosell Roig, arXiv 2511.06112 (2025), a preprint that argues for the author's own model ↗

A ramp that spirals around the outside繞著外面盤旋上去的坡道

The ramp wraps around all four faces, the way a mountain road spirals upward.坡道繞著四個面盤旋往上，就像繞山的公路。

Problems · 難題It would hide the corners and edges that builders needed to measure, and teams would have to haul blocks around tight corners.它會遮住工人需要測量的角和邊，拖石頭的隊伍也得在很窄的轉角轉彎。

Brier, Archaeology (2007), quoting Dieter Arnold, Building in Egypt; Edwards (2003) ↗

A ramp hidden inside the pyramid藏在金字塔裡面的坡道

Unit 4: Why Did the First Cities Grow Beside Rivers?

In Lesson 2, villages stored grain. In Lesson 3, people in Uruk wrote down who owed what. Uruk was more than a village. It was a city, with temples, workshops, and at least 25,000 people, many of whom did not farm at all. A city needs a lot of extra food, more than its farmers eat. Where could that much food come from?

第二課的村子會儲存穀子。第三課的烏魯克人會記下誰欠了什麼。烏魯克不只是個村子，它是一座城市，有神廟、作坊，至少兩萬五千人，其中很多人根本不種田。一座城市需要很多多出來的糧食，比種田的人自己吃的還多。這麼多糧食，要從哪裡來？

In most of the places where the first cities grew, the answer was a river. Southern Mesopotamia gets almost no rain, but the Euphrates and Tigris bring water from the mountains. Egypt is mostly desert, but every summer the Nile rose and left fresh mud on its fields. Rivers gave water for crops, soil that stayed rich, fish and reeds, and a road for boats. Heavy loads, from grain to stone, traveled far more easily by water than over land.

在大多數最早長出城市的地方，答案是一條河。美索不達米亞南部幾乎不下雨，但幼發拉底河和底格里斯河從山上帶來了水。埃及大部分是沙漠，但尼羅河每年夏天漲水，在田裡留下新的泥土。河流給了灌溉作物的水、保持肥沃的土、魚和蘆葦，還有一條給船走的路。重的東西，從穀子到石頭，走水路比走陸路容易得多。

Yet the rivers were not alike. The Nile's flood came at about the same time every year, and Egyptian farmers could plan around it. In Mesopotamia the high water came at the wrong time for the crops, so people had to lead water to the fields with canals and dikes. The rivers of the Indus Valley, fed by the summer monsoon, changed over the centuries as their floods and the monsoon rains grew weaker. Along the Yellow River in China, great floods could break through and change the river's course. Each river shaped its city in its own way.

可是這些河並不一樣。尼羅河每年漲水的時間差不多，埃及的農夫可以照著它安排。在美索不達米亞，漲水的時間跟作物的季節對不上，人們只好用運河和堤防把水引到田裡。印度河流域的河靠夏季的季風供水，幾百年下來，洪水和季風雨都慢慢變弱了。在中國的黃河，大洪水可能沖破河岸，讓河改道。每一條河都用自己的方式塑造它的城市。

Many textbooks speak of four great river civilizations: Mesopotamia, Egypt, the Indus, and China's Yellow River. The list is a useful start, but it leaves a lot out. Liangzhu, beside the Yangtze, built dams and a walled city more than 5,000 years ago. Caral, in Peru, grew in a desert valley beside the small Supe River at about the same time as the first Egyptian pyramids. Cities began in more places than four, and none of them is the “first” in every way.

很多課本會說有四大河流文明：美索不達米亞、埃及、印度河，和中國的黃河。這份清單是一個好的起點，但漏掉了很多。長江邊的良渚，在五千多年前就建了水壩和有城牆的城市。祕魯的卡拉爾，長在一個沙漠河谷裡，旁邊是小小的蘇佩河，時間和埃及最早的金字塔差不多。城市開始的地方不只四個，也沒有哪一個在每一方面都是「第一」。

Cities could organize huge projects. In Egypt, the Great Pyramid of Khufu rose more than 4,500 years ago, originally about 146 meters tall. It was not built by slaves. Archaeologists have found the town that housed the builders of the Giza pyramids, with bakeries and storerooms. A Harvard project describes the workers as Egyptians paid in food, mainly bread and beer; some researchers think many came in rotating teams from their home districts. Boats carried its fine limestone down the Nile. How the blocks were raised to the top is still not fully known.

城市能組織巨大的工程。在埃及，胡夫的大金字塔在四千五百多年前蓋起來，原本大約 146 公尺高。它不是奴隸蓋的。考古學家找到了吉薩金字塔建造者住的城鎮，有麵包坊和倉庫。哈佛大學的一個計畫說，這些工人是埃及人，領的是食物，主要是麵包和啤酒；也有研究者認為，很多人是從家鄉輪班被徵調來的。船沿著尼羅河運來細緻的石灰岩。石頭是怎麼運到頂端的，到現在還沒有完全弄清楚。

Living beside a river had risks. Floods could destroy fields, and a river that changed its path could leave a city without water. The Indus cities declined slowly after about 1900 BCE, and changing river flows are one of the main explanations. A river did not make a city by itself. People did, by learning how their river behaved and working together to manage it.

住在河邊也有風險。洪水會毀掉田地，河一改道，城市就可能沒有水。印度河的城市在大約西元前 1900 年以後慢慢衰落，河水流量的改變是主要的解釋之一。河並不會自己變出一座城市。是人學會了自己的河怎麼流、一起合作管理它，城市才出現。
Read aloud · 課文朗讀

Questions · 閱讀理解

Why does a city need more food than a village, and how did a river help?

Many people in a city did not farm, such as priests, craftspeople, and officials, so farmers had to grow more than they ate. A river gave water in dry lands, rich soil, and an easy way to move food by boat.

How was the Nile's flood different from the floods in Mesopotamia, and why did that matter to farmers?

The Nile rose at about the same time every year, so farmers could plan around it. In Mesopotamia the high water came at the wrong time for the crops, so people had to build canals and dikes to bring water to the fields when it was needed.

The lesson says rivers made cities possible, not certain. What else did people need to do?

They had to learn how their river behaved, work together to dig canals or build dams, store food, and deal with floods and droughts. Without organization, a river alone did not create a city.

Buried towns, river mud, and a boatman's diary 埋起來的城鎮、河底的泥，和一位船隊隊長的日誌

最早的城市留下了牆、街道和垃圾。養活它們的河，也留下了線索。

The workers' town工人的城鎮

Near the pyramids, archaeologists have dug up the town that housed the builders of the Giza pyramids (probably including Khufu's), with workshops, bakeries, storerooms, and officials' houses. The workers' cemetery was found in 1990.
在金字塔附近，考古學家挖出了吉薩金字塔建造者住的城鎮（很可能也包括蓋胡夫金字塔的人），有作坊、麵包坊、倉庫，和官員的房子。工人的墓地是 1990 年發現的。

Its limits · 它的限制The town shows how workers lived, but not exactly how they were recruited. Researchers still debate how much was paid work and how much was a duty owed to the state.這座城鎮讓我們看到工人怎麼生活，但看不出他們確切是怎麼被找來的。有多少是領酬勞的工作、有多少是對國家的義務，研究者還在討論。

Ancient Egypt Research Associates; National Geographic History (2017) ↗
📜

The diary of Merer梅勒的日誌

In 2013, papyri were found at an ancient harbor on the Red Sea. They are the logbook of an official named Merer, whose team carried limestone by boat from the quarries at Tura to the Great Pyramid while it was being built. They are the oldest inscribed papyri yet found in Egypt.
2013 年，在紅海邊一座古代港口找到了一批莎草紙。它們是一位叫梅勒的官員的工作日誌：大金字塔施工的時候，他的隊伍用船把石灰岩從圖拉的採石場運到工地。這是目前在埃及找到最古老、寫了字的莎草紙。

Its limits · 它的限制Merer tells us how stone traveled, not how it was lifted. The logbook says nothing about ramps.梅勒告訴我們石頭怎麼運來，卻沒說怎麼抬上去。日誌裡沒有提到坡道。

Tallet and Marouard, Near Eastern Archaeology 77 (2014); Tallet, Les papyrus de la mer Rouge I (IFAO, 2017) ↗
🛰️

Rivers that moved會搬家的河

Rivers leave traces. Satellite images and drill cores show an old branch of the Nile, now dried up, that once ran close to the pyramids. Studies of old river beds in the Indus region show how its floods weakened over time.
河會留下痕跡。衛星影像和鑽探取出的土芯顯示，有一條現在已經乾掉的尼羅河古支流，以前離金字塔很近。對印度河一帶古河床的研究，讓我們看到它的洪水怎麼隨時間變弱。

Its limits · 它的限制Dating old river channels is hard, and linking a change in a river to a change in people's lives is an interpretation, not a direct observation.古河道很難定年；把河的改變和人的生活改變連在一起，是一種解讀，不是直接看到的事。

Ghoneim et al., Communications Earth & Environment (2024); Giosan et al., PNAS (2012) ↗

Different Accounts · 不同的說法

Four questions about rivers and cities 關於河與城市的四個問題

The questions below have been argued over for a long time. The views are set side by side; this lesson does not choose between them.
下面這些問題已經爭論很久了。各種說法並列，這一課不替誰下結論。

Did managing water create the first states?是管水的需要，造出了最早的國家嗎？

  • Yes是

    The historian Karl Wittfogel argued in 1957 that large irrigation works needed strong central rulers, and that this was how powerful states arose in river valleys.
    歷史學家魏復古（Karl Wittfogel）在 1957 年主張，大型的灌溉工程需要強大的中央統治者，大河流域的強大國家就是這樣出現的。

    Wittfogel, Oriental Despotism (1957); see “Oriental Despotism” ↗

  • Not at first一開始不是

    Later researchers found that in Mesopotamia large-scale irrigation is clearly attested only from about 2500 BCE, long after the first cities. A 2025 study suggests that the tides on the delta helped water the earliest fields.
    後來的研究者發現，在美索不達米亞，大規模灌溉要到大約西元前 2500 年才有明確的證據，比最早的城市晚了很多。2025 年的一項研究認為，三角洲上的潮水幫最早的田灌了溉。

    Giosan and Goodman, PLOS ONE (2025) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況Water mattered everywhere, but the order of events differs from place to place.水在每個地方都重要，但事情發生的先後，每個地方不一樣。

Are there “four great ancient civilizations”?真的有「四大文明古國」嗎？

  • A useful list一份好用的清單

    In 1900 Liang Qichao wrote of four ancient homelands of civilization and tied them to great rivers. Lists like this help students compare a few well-studied places.
    1900 年，梁啟超寫到地球上古代的「文明祖國有四」，並把它們和大河連在一起。像這樣的清單，可以幫學生比較幾個研究得比較多的地方。

    梁啟超〈二十世紀太平洋歌〉(1900) ↗

  • Too few太少了

    Liangzhu on the Yangtze and Caral in Peru were early and complex, and the Olmec of Mexico followed a little later, about 1200 to 500 BCE, yet none is on the list. Even Liang's own four were China, India, Egypt, and Asia Minor, not quite today's version.
    長江的良渚、祕魯的卡拉爾都很早、也很複雜，墨西哥的奧爾梅克稍晚一些（大約西元前 1200 到 500 年），卻都不在清單上。就連梁啟超自己列的四個，也是中國、印度、埃及和小亞細亞，跟今天的版本並不完全一樣。

    UNESCO, Liangzhu and Caral-Supe; National Geographic Education, “Olmec Civilization” ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況The number four is a teaching habit, not a discovery.「四」是一種教學習慣，不是一項發現。

Who built the pyramids?金字塔是誰蓋的？

  • Slaves (an old claim)奴隸（一個古老的說法）

    The Greek writer Herodotus, about 2,000 years after the pyramids were built, claimed they were built by forced labor, and the idea lasted into modern times.
    希臘作家希羅多德在金字塔蓋好大約兩千年之後，說它們是用強迫的勞力蓋的；這個想法一直流傳到現代。

    National Geographic History (2017) ↗

  • Egyptian workers埃及的工人

    The workers' town and cemetery show Egyptian workers who were fed and cared for. Some researchers stress that they were paid in food; others suggest that many were called up in rotating teams from their home districts.
    工人的城鎮和墓地顯示，蓋金字塔的是有人供應食物、照顧的埃及工人。有些研究者強調他們領的是食物；也有研究者認為，很多人是從家鄉輪班被徵調來的。

    Harvard University, Digital Giza; Ancient Egypt Research Associates ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況The evidence rules out the slave story but still leaves room to discuss how free the workers were.證據排除了「奴隸蓋的」這個故事，但工人有多自由，還有討論的空間。

Why did the Indus cities decline?印度河的城市為什麼衰落？

  • Invaders (an older view)入侵者（比較早的看法）

    Early in the 20th century some scholars thought newcomers had conquered the cities. As early as 1964, an archaeologist writing for the Penn Museum disputed this.
    二十世紀初，有些學者認為是外來的人征服了這些城市。早在 1964 年，就有一位為賓州大學博物館撰文的考古學家反駁這種說法。

    Dales, “The Mythical Massacre at Mohenjo-Daro,” Expedition 6 (1964) ↗

  • Changing rivers河流的改變

    Today researchers point to changing river flows and weakening floods and monsoon rains, which disrupted farming. One study argues that weaker floods first encouraged cities, and that as the monsoon kept weakening, settlements shrank.
    今天的研究者指出，河水的流量改變、洪水和季風雨變弱，打亂了農業。有一項研究認為，洪水變弱一開始促成了城市；後來季風繼續變弱，聚落就縮小了。

    Giosan et al., PNAS (2012); Kenoyer, harappa.com ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況The decline was slow and differed from region to region, and its causes are still debated.衰落是慢慢發生的，各地區也不一樣，原因到現在還有爭論。

Notice how often a new kind of evidence, such as river mud or a satellite image, changes the question rather than ending it.留意新的證據（例如河底的泥或衛星影像）有多常改變問題本身，而不是結束討論。

History English · 歷史英語

Eight sentences for comparing places 比較不同地方的八句英文

Historians compare places without ranking them: what was alike, what was different, and what we still do not know. Listen, repeat, then make your own sentence with the words in bold.
歷史學家比較不同的地方，但不替它們排名：哪裡一樣、哪裡不一樣、還有什麼不知道。先聽、再跟著唸，然後用粗體的字自己造一句。

  1. Both Egypt and Mesopotamia grew up beside great rivers.

    埃及和美索不達米亞都在大河旁邊發展起來。

    both … and …: two things that are alike兩者都……

  2. The Nile rose at about the same time every year, whereas Mesopotamia's floods came at the wrong time for the crops.

    尼羅河每年差不多同一個時候漲水，而美索不達米亞的洪水卻在作物不需要的時候來。

    whereas: sets two different things side by side而……（對照兩件不同的事）

  3. Unlike the farmers of the Yellow River, the people of Liangzhu grew rice.

    和黃河邊的農夫不同，良渚的人種的是稻米。

    unlike: different from和……不同

  4. The streets of Harappa were similar to those of Mohenjo-daro.

    哈拉帕的街道和摩亨約達羅的很像。

    similar to: almost the same as和……相似

  5. A food surplus made it possible for some people not to farm.

    多出來的糧食，讓一些人可以不種田。

    made it possible for + person + to: allowed讓某人能夠

  6. No one knows for certain how the blocks were raised.

    沒有人能確定石塊是怎麼運上去的。

    no one knows for certain: it is still unknown沒有人能確定

  7. Changing river flows may have led to the decline of the Indus cities.

    河水流量的改變，可能導致了印度河城市的衰落。

    may have led to: possibly caused可能導致了

  8. Rivers made cities possible, but not certain.

    河讓城市成為可能，卻不保證一定會有。

    possible, but not certain: a condition, not a cause可能，但不一定

Taiwan at That Time · 那時候的台灣

Villages by rivers and the sea 河邊與海邊的村落

While Uruk and Memphis were growing, people in Taiwan lived in villages near rivers, estuaries, and the coast. There were no cities, but there were farms, fishing, and, later, a very large settlement at Beinan.
烏魯克和孟斐斯在長大的時候，台灣的人住在河邊、河口和海邊的村落。那時沒有城市，但有農田、有漁獵，後來還有卑南一處很大的聚落。

🏝️

Archaeologists in Taiwan usually give dates as “years ago” (before 1950), not as BCE. About 5,000 years ago is roughly 3000 BCE, and 3,500 years ago is roughly 1550 BCE.
台灣的考古學家通常用「距今幾年」（以 1950 年為準）來說年代，不用西元前。距今大約 5,000 年，差不多是西元前 3000 年；距今 3,500 年，差不多是西元前 1550 年。

  • Dapenkeng: by the water大坌坑文化：靠水而居

    The National Museum of Prehistory dates the Dapenkeng culture to between a little over 6,000 and 5,000 years ago. Its sites lie mostly on coasts, at river mouths, and by lakes. Many scholars think these people may be among the earliest ancestors of the Austronesian peoples.
    國立臺灣史前文化博物館把大坌坑文化定在「大約在六千多年前到五千年前之間」。它的遺址大多在海邊、河口和湖邊。許多學者認為，他們可能是南島語族最早的祖先之一。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

  • Grain farming種穀子

    In the cord-marked red pottery cultures, about 4,500 to 3,500 years ago, the same museum says life was based mainly on grain farming, while hunting and fishing stayed important.
    在大約距今 4,500 到 3,500 年的細繩紋陶文化，同一座博物館說，生活以穀類農業為主，打獵和捕魚也還是很重要。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

  • Beinan: a large settlement卑南：很大的聚落

    At Beinan in Taitung, on a river terrace beside the Beinan River, people built a village covering more than 50 hectares, with stone buildings, standing stone pillars, and about 1,600 slate coffins. The Beinan culture appeared between about 3,500 and 3,000 years ago and lasted to about 2,300 years ago. It is not the same as today's Puyuma (Beinan) people.
    在台東卑南大溪旁的河階上，人們建了一個面積超過 50 公頃的聚落，有石頭砌的建築、豎立的石柱，還留下大約 1,600 具石板棺。卑南文化大約在距今 3,500 到 3,000 年之間出現，延續到距今 2,300 年前後。它和今天的卑南族並不是同一回事。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館電子報第 360 期；文化部 國家文化記憶庫「卑南考古遺址」 ↗

  • Why no cities?為什麼沒有城市？

    A village does not need to become a city. Archaeologists find settled villages of farmers and fishers in Taiwan, but no cities from this time. Why is not known for sure. One idea is that Taiwan's rivers are short and steep, without a wide floodplain like the Nile's, but that is an idea, not a finding.
    村落不一定要變成城市。考古學家在台灣找到許多定居的農耕、漁獵村落，但那個時代沒有找到城市。為什麼，沒有人能確定。有一種想法是：台灣的河又短又陡，沒有尼羅河那樣寬廣的平原；但這只是一種想法，不是發現。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源（定居的聚落）；「河短又陡」是一種可能的解釋，不是研究結論 ↗

Stories Behind the Evidence · 證據背後的故事

A gift, a boatman, a lost wonder count, and a city without pottery 一份禮物、一位船隊隊長、只剩一個的奇觀，和一座沒有陶器的城

Four short stories about rivers and the cities beside them.
四個小故事，關於河，以及河邊的城市。

5th century BCE · Egypt · 埃及

The gift of the Nile尼羅河的禮物

The Greek writer Herodotus, more than 2,000 years after the first pyramids, wrote that much of Egypt was “the gift of the river”: land built up from the mud the Nile carried down. He said the Egyptian priests told him so, and he agreed.
在最早的金字塔出現兩千多年後，希臘作家希羅多德寫道，埃及很多土地是「河的禮物」：是尼羅河帶下來的泥土堆出來的。他說這是埃及的祭司告訴他的，他也同意。

Khufu's reign · Egypt · 埃及

A boatman's logbook一位船隊隊長的日誌

Merer's team rowed and sailed between the quarries at Tura and Giza, carrying limestone for the Great Pyramid. His logbook, found in 2013 by the Red Sea, records their trips day by day.
梅勒的隊伍在圖拉的採石場和吉薩之間來回划船、揚帆，替大金字塔運石灰岩。他的日誌 2013 年在紅海邊被找到，一天一天地記下了他們的航程。

Today · Giza · 吉薩

The last wonder standing唯一還在的奇觀

Greek writers listed seven wonders of the world. Of those, UNESCO notes, the Great Pyramid of Khufu is the only one that still survives.
希臘人列過世界七大奇觀。聯合國教科文組織指出，其中只有胡夫的大金字塔還留存到今天。

About 3000–1800 BCE · Peru · 祕魯

A city in the desert沙漠裡的城市

Caral stands on a dry terrace above the green valley of the Supe River, near the Pacific coast. Its builders raised pyramids and sunken plazas there, and UNESCO calls it the oldest center of civilization in the Americas.
卡拉爾蓋在蘇佩河綠色河谷上方一塊乾燥的台地上，離太平洋海岸不遠。建造它的人在那裡築起金字塔和下沉式廣場；聯合國教科文組織稱它是美洲最古老的文明中心。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思Slaves built the pyramids.金字塔是奴隸蓋的。

✓ Fact · 事實The workers' town and cemetery show Egyptian workers who were fed with bread and beer. Many may have been called up in rotating teams, but they were not slaves.工人的城鎮和墓地顯示，蓋金字塔的是吃麵包、喝啤酒的埃及工人。很多人可能是輪班被徵調來的，但他們不是奴隸。

✗ Myth · 迷思Every pharaoh was buried in a pyramid.每一位法老都葬在金字塔裡。

✓ Fact · 事實Pyramids were the royal tombs of the Old Kingdom and a few later periods. Many later kings were buried in rock-cut tombs in the Valley of the Kings, near Thebes.金字塔是古王國和後來少數時期的王陵。很多後來的國王葬在底比斯附近的帝王谷，墓是在岩壁上鑿出來的。

✗ Myth · 迷思There were only four ancient civilizations.古代文明只有四個。

✓ Fact · 事實Liangzhu and Caral were early and complex too, and the Olmec came a little later. “Four” is a teaching habit.良渚和卡拉爾也都很早、也很複雜，奧爾梅克稍晚一些。「四」是一種教學習慣。

✗ Myth · 迷思The Indus cities were destroyed suddenly by invaders.印度河的城市是被入侵者一下子毀掉的。

✓ Fact · 事實There is no evidence of an invasion. The cities declined slowly, and changing rivers are a leading explanation.沒有入侵的證據。這些城市是慢慢衰落的，河流的改變是主要的解釋之一。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

Plan a River City 規劃一座河邊的城市

You need · 準備材料

  • A large sheet of paper and colored pencils for each group每組一張大紙和色鉛筆
  • The map from “Where Did the First Cities Grow?”「最早的城市長在哪裡？」那張地圖

Steps · 步驟

  1. Draw a river crossing your paper. Decide where the high ground is and where the flood reaches.在紙上畫一條穿過去的河。決定哪裡是高地，洪水會淹到哪裡。
  2. Place the fields, the houses, a granary, a workshop, and a harbor. Explain in English why each goes where it does: “The fields are near the river because…”放進田地、房子、穀倉、作坊和碼頭。用英文說明每一樣為什麼放在那裡：「The fields are near the river because…」
  3. Your teacher announces a problem: a very high flood, a year with no flood, or the river moving 2 kilometers east. Change your plan.老師宣布一個問題：一場超大的洪水、一年沒有氾濫，或是河往東搬了兩公里。修改你們的規劃。
  4. Compare plans with another group. Which city would survive best, and why?和另一組比較。哪一座城市最能撐下去？為什麼？

Step 3 is the lesson. Students see that living by a river means planning for its moods, which is what every early river city had to do.
第三步就是這堂課的重點。學生會發現，住在河邊就得為它的脾氣做準備，這正是每一座早期的河邊城市都得做的事。

Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

How Much Is Half? 一半是多少？

You need · 準備材料

  • About 30 small building blocks or sugar cubes per group每組大約 30 個小積木或方糖
  • The 3D model in this lesson這一課的 3D 模型

Steps · 步驟

  1. Build a stepped pyramid: a bottom layer of 4 × 4 blocks, then 3 × 3, then 2 × 2, then 1. That is 30 blocks.蓋一座階梯金字塔：最下層 4 × 4 個，接著 3 × 3、2 × 2，最上面 1 個。一共 30 個。
  2. Count how many blocks are in the bottom two layers. Is it more or less than half of 30? (It is 25.)數一數最下面兩層有幾個。比 30 的一半多還是少？（是 25 個。）
  3. Now open the 3D model and stop at half the height. Compare the gold number with your count.接著打開 3D 模型，停在一半的高度，比一比金色的數字和你數的結果。
  4. Say it in English: “At half the height, most of the stone is already in place.”用英文說：「At half the height, most of the stone is already in place.」

This turns a surprising number into something students can hold. The top of a pyramid is small but hard to reach, which is why the ramp question matters.
這個活動把一個讓人意外的數字變成摸得到的東西。金字塔的頂端很小卻很難到達，這就是坡道的問題重要的原因。
