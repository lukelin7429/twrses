Who Kept Knowledge Alive?誰把知識保存下來？

Follow seven books on a 3D globe as they are copied and translated from Greek, Sanskrit, and Arabic into Syriac, Arabic, Latin, and Chinese, from Alexandria and Baghdad to Toledo, Pisa, and Chang'an. Find out where words like algebra and zero came from.

Big idea · 一句話看懂

Knowledge did not stay in one place. Greek, Persian, and Indian books were translated into Syriac and Arabic in Baghdad, studied and improved there, and later translated into Latin in Spain and Italy; Buddhist books went from Sanskrit into Chinese. The people who kept knowledge alive spoke many languages and followed different religions.
知識沒有一直待在同一個地方。希臘、波斯和印度的書在巴格達被譯成敘利亞文和阿拉伯文，在那裡被研究、被改進，後來又在西班牙和義大利被譯成拉丁文；佛經則從梵文譯成中文。保存知識的人說很多種語言，也信不同的宗教。

Reading · 英文閱讀

11

Unit 11: Who Kept Knowledge Alive?

Before printing spread, every book had to be copied by hand. A book survived only if people kept making new copies, and only if someone could read its language. When nobody copied a book for a few hundred years, it could be lost forever. So the question “who kept knowledge alive?” has many answers, in many languages.

在印刷普及以前，每一本書都要用手抄。一本書要留下來，就要有人不斷抄新的，也要有人讀得懂它的語言。如果幾百年都沒有人抄，它就可能永遠失傳。所以「誰把知識保存下來？」有很多答案，用很多種語言。

Some of the most famous books were written in Greek. Plato taught at the Academy in Athens, and Euclid taught geometry in Alexandria about 300 BCE. Around 150 CE Ptolemy wrote his great book of astronomy there, and the physician Galen, born in Pergamum, wrote many books on medicine. In Constantinople, Byzantine scribes went on copying Greek books for centuries.

有些最有名的書是用希臘文寫的。柏拉圖在雅典的學園教書，歐幾里得大約西元前 300 年在亞歷山卓教幾何。大約西元 150 年，托勒密在那裡寫了他的天文學大書；生於帕加馬的醫生蓋倫，寫了許多醫書。在君士坦丁堡，拜占庭的抄寫員好幾百年都繼續抄寫希臘文的書。

In India, scholars wrote in Sanskrit. In 628 the astronomer Brahmagupta treated zero as a number with its own rules. The numerals we use today, from 0 to 9, began in India in the 6th or 7th century. At the monastery of Nalanda, the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang learned Sanskrit; back in Chang'an from 645, he translated the scriptures he had brought into Chinese. In China, too, a Buddhist text printed in 868 is the oldest printed book that carries a date.

在印度，學者用梵文寫作。628 年，天文學家婆羅摩笈多把零當成一個數，替它定了運算規則。我們今天用的 0 到 9，六或七世紀起源於印度。中國的朝聖者玄奘在那爛陀寺學梵文；645 年回到長安以後，他把帶回來的佛經譯成中文。也是在中國，一部 868 年印的佛經，是現存有日期的印刷書裡最古老的。

In Baghdad, the Abbasid caliphs kept a royal library called the House of Wisdom, and their court supported many scholars. Translators turned Persian, Indian, Syriac, and Greek works into Arabic, some of them close to the court. The mathematician al-Khwārizmī wrote about Indian numerals and about algebra. A generation later, Hunayn ibn Ishaq, a Christian physician, gathered Greek manuscripts and translated Galen into Syriac and Arabic.

在巴格達，阿拔斯王朝的哈里發有一座王室圖書館，叫「智慧宮」，宮廷也支持許多學者。譯者把波斯、印度、敘利亞和希臘的著作譯成阿拉伯文，有些和宮廷關係密切。數學家花拉子密寫了印度的數字和代數。一個世代以後，基督徒醫生侯奈因・伊本・伊斯哈格收集希臘文抄本，把蓋倫譯成敘利亞文和阿拉伯文。

Scholars did not only copy. Avicenna's Canon of Medicine, Ibn al-Haytham's book on optics, and al-Khwārizmī's algebra added new ideas. In Seville and Córdoba, Averroes wrote commentaries on Aristotle, which also survive in Hebrew translation. Then, in the 12th century, translators in Toledo, a city taken by the Christian king Alfonso VI in 1085, turned many of these Arabic books into Latin. Gerard of Cremona went there to learn Arabic just to read Ptolemy.

學者不只是抄寫。阿維森納的《醫典》、伊本・海什木的光學、花拉子密的代數，都加進了新的想法。在塞維亞和科爾多瓦，阿威羅伊替亞里斯多德寫注釋，這些注釋也有希伯來文譯本留下來。到了十二世紀，在 1085 年被基督徒國王阿方索六世攻下的托雷多，譯者把許多阿拉伯文的書譯成拉丁文。克雷莫納的傑拉德就是為了讀托勒密，才到那裡學阿拉伯文。

In Latin Europe, monks had been copying books in monastery writing rooms all along, and the new universities after 1100 were stimulated by Arabic learning. In West Africa, Timbuktu became a center of Islamic culture and learning. Knowledge survived because people in many places, speaking Greek, Syriac, Arabic, Persian, Sanskrit, Chinese, Hebrew, and Latin, kept copying, translating, and asking new questions.

在說拉丁文的歐洲，修士一直在修道院的抄寫室裡抄書；1100 年以後新的大學，也受到阿拉伯學術的刺激。在西非，廷巴克圖成為伊斯蘭文化與學術的中心。知識能留下來，是因為許多地方的人，說著希臘文、敘利亞文、阿拉伯文、波斯文、梵文、中文、希伯來文和拉丁文，一直抄寫、翻譯，也一直提出新的問題。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Why could a book be lost forever before printing?

Every copy was made by hand. If nobody copied a book, or nobody could read its language, the last copies could wear out or be destroyed.

Name two languages that Galen's books passed through on their way to Latin Europe.

Greek (the original), Syriac, Arabic, and then Latin (any two).

Give one example of a scholar who did more than copy older books.

Al-Khwārizmī (algebra), Ibn al-Haytham (optics), Avicenna (the Canon of Medicine), Averroes (commentaries on Aristotle), or Brahmagupta (rules for zero).

    How Do We Know? · 我們怎麼知道？

    Old copies, book lists, and hidden words 古老的抄本、書目，和藏起來的字

    Historians trace a book's journey by looking at the copies that survive and the lists people made of books.
    歷史學家看留下來的抄本，以及人們編的書目，來追查一本書的旅程。

    📜

    Notes at the end of a book書末的題記

    Many manuscripts end with a note, called a colophon, saying who made the copy and when. The Diamond Sutra from Dunhuang has one: it was made for free distribution by Wang Jie on behalf of his parents, on a day equal to May 11, 868.
    很多抄本在最後有一段題記，寫著誰抄的、什麼時候抄的。敦煌的《金剛經》就有一段：一位叫 Wang Jie 的人，為了父母而印、讓大家免費取得，日期相當於 868 年 5 月 11 日。

    Its limits · 它的限制Many copies have no note at all, and many early printed editions of translations leave out the translator's name, so historians have to argue about who translated what.很多抄本根本沒有題記；許多早期印刷的譯本也沒寫譯者是誰，所以歷史學家還要爭論誰譯了什麼。

    British Library, International Dunhuang Programme; Britannica, “Gerard of Cremona” ↗
    📚

    Book lists書目

    In 988 a bookseller, Ibn al-Nadim, compiled the Fihrist, an index of the Arabic books of his time, from language study to alchemy.
    988 年，書商伊本・納迪姆編了《書目》（Fihrist），列出他那個時代的阿拉伯文書，從語言學到煉金術都有。

    Its limits · 它的限制Most of the books on the list have since been lost. A list tells us a book existed, not what was in it.書目上的書，後來大多已經失傳。書目告訴我們有過這本書，但不告訴我們書裡寫了什麼。

    Britannica, “Ibn al-Nadim” ↗
    🔬

    Words hidden under words藏在字底下的字

    Parchment was expensive, so old writing was sometimes scraped off and the page reused. A copy of works by Archimedes made in the 900s was turned into a prayer book in 1229 at a monastery near Bethlehem. After it was sold in 1998, scientists used ultraviolet light and X-rays to read the Archimedes text underneath.
    羊皮紙很貴，所以舊的字有時會被刮掉，紙再拿來用。一部 900 年代的阿基米德抄本，1229 年在伯利恆附近的修道院被改成祈禱書。1998 年它被賣出以後，科學家用紫外光和 X 光，讀出了底下阿基米德的字。

    Its limits · 它的限制Most scraped-off texts are lost for good. We only know about the ones that happened to survive and be studied.大部分被刮掉的字，已經永遠消失了。我們只知道剛好留下來、又被研究過的那些。

    Smithsonian Magazine, “Reading Between the Lines” ↗

    Different Accounts · 不同的說法

    Four questions about keeping knowledge 關於保存知識的四個問題

    Historians agree that translation mattered enormously, but they disagree on some details. Here are four questions, with the views set side by side.
    歷史學家都同意翻譯非常重要，但對一些細節看法不同。下面是四個問題，各種說法並列。

    What was the House of Wisdom?智慧宮是什麼樣的地方？

    • A great translation center一座大型的翻譯中心

      Britannica's short summary at the top of its article calls it a “royal library and translation center” of the Abbasid caliphs.
      Britannica 條目開頭的簡介，把它叫做阿拔斯王朝哈里發的「王室圖書館兼翻譯中心」。

      Britannica, “Bayt al-Hikmah” ↗

    • A royal library; translation happened elsewhere王室圖書館；翻譯在別的地方

      Britannica's full article says translation, almost all from Persian, was not one of its main jobs, and that the translation of Greek works, including Hunayn's, took place elsewhere. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy agrees that it cannot be viewed as an institutional center for translating Greek works, though the first translators had close ties to the court.
      Britannica 的正文說，翻譯（幾乎都是從波斯文）不是它主要的工作；希臘著作的翻譯，包括侯奈因的，是在別的地方進行的。《史丹佛哲學百科》也說，它不能看成翻譯希臘著作的正式機構，不過最早的那些譯者和宮廷關係很密切。

      Britannica, “Bayt al-Hikmah”; Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Greek Sources in Arabic and Islamic Philosophy” ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Britannica's full article and the Stanford Encyclopedia agree on the second view. Either way, the caliphs' court and Baghdad's elites supported translators.Britannica 的正文和《史丹佛哲學百科》都支持第二種看法。不管哪一種，哈里發的宮廷和巴格達的上層人士都支持譯者。

    Why did the Abbasid court support books and translation?阿拔斯王朝的宮廷為什麼支持書和翻譯？

    • To carry on Persian traditions延續波斯的傳統

      For the royal library, Britannica points to Persian heritage: possibly modeled on Sasanian practice, it specialized in Persian knowledge and showed the dynasty continuing that heritage.
      就王室圖書館來說，Britannica 指向波斯的傳統：它可能仿照薩珊王朝的做法，專精波斯的知識，表現出這個王朝延續那份傳統。

      Britannica, “Bayt al-Hikmah” ↗

    • New ideas and new interests新的想法和新的興趣

      For the translations of Greek works, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy points to al-Maʾmūn's new view of Islam and of the caliph's role, and to the growing interest of educated elites in the secular sciences.
      就希臘著作的翻譯來說，《史丹佛哲學百科》指出，是馬蒙對伊斯蘭和哈里發角色的新看法，加上受過教育的上層人士對世俗學問越來越有興趣。

      Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Greek Sources in Arabic and Islamic Philosophy” ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況The two answers are about different kinds of books, and they can overlap: rulers, physicians, astronomers, and officials all had their own uses for these books.兩個答案談的是不同種類的書，也可以同時成立：統治者、醫生、天文學家和官員，對這些書各有各的用處。

    When was the Library of Alexandria destroyed?亞歷山卓圖書館是什麼時候被毀的？

    • In 48 BCE西元前 48 年

      One account in Britannica says the main library was destroyed in a civil war in 48 BCE, and a smaller “daughter library” in 391 CE.
      Britannica 的一種說法是，主圖書館在西元前 48 年的內戰中被毀，另一座較小的「分館」在西元 391 年被毀。

      Britannica, “Library of Alexandria” ↗

    • In the late 3rd century CE西元三世紀後期

      Another passage in the same encyclopedia says the library was destroyed in a civil war under the Roman emperor Aurelian, in the late 3rd century CE.
      同一部百科的另一段說，圖書館是在三世紀後期羅馬皇帝奧勒良時的內戰中被毀的。

      Britannica, “Library of Alexandria” ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況The sources do not agree, and the library probably declined in more than one stage rather than vanishing in a single fire.資料彼此不一致；圖書館大概是分好幾個階段衰落的，不是一場大火就消失。

    When did the Hindu-Arabic numerals reach Europe?印度—阿拉伯數字什麼時候傳到歐洲？

    • Around 1000大約 1000 年

      Britannica says the scholar Gerbert, later Pope Sylvester II, introduced an abacus with counters bearing Arabic numerals around 1000 CE, though very few people knew of it.
      Britannica 說，學者熱爾貝（後來的教宗西爾維斯特二世）大約在西元 1000 年用了一種算盤，算子上刻著阿拉伯數字；不過知道的人很少。

      Britannica, “Sylvester II” ↗

    • About the 12th century and after大約十二世紀以後

      Britannica's article on the numerals says they reached Europe through Middle Eastern mathematicians, especially al-Khwārizmī and al-Kindī, about the 12th century, and Fibonacci's book of 1202 did much to spread them.
      Britannica 談數字的條目說，它們大約在十二世紀，透過中東的數學家（特別是花拉子密和金迪）傳到歐洲；費波那契 1202 年的書，大大幫助了它們的傳播。

      Britannica, “Hindu-Arabic numerals” and “Fibonacci” ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Both can be true: a few scholars knew the numerals early, but most people in Europe went on using Roman numerals for a long time.兩種說法可以同時成立：少數學者很早就知道這些數字，但歐洲大多數人還用了很久的羅馬數字。

    Notice that even good encyclopedias sometimes give more than one answer.留意：連很好的百科全書，有時也會給出不只一個答案。

    These sentences help you describe how ideas were saved and passed on. Listen, repeat, then make your own sentence with the words in bold.
    這八句幫你描述想法怎麼被保存、怎麼傳下去。先聽、再跟著唸，然後用粗體的字自己造一句。

    Taiwan at That Time · 那時候的台灣

    Knowledge without books 沒有書的知識

    Before 1600 the peoples of Taiwan kept and passed on knowledge through speech, skills, and customs rather than books.
    1600 年以前，台灣的人群不是用書，而是用語言、技術和習俗，保存、傳下知識。

    🏝️

    Archaeologists in Taiwan give dates as “years ago.” About 2,000 years ago is roughly the beginning of the Common Era.
    台灣的考古學家用「距今幾年」說年代。距今大約兩千年，差不多是西元紀年開始的時候。

    • Shisanhang culture, north北部的十三行文化

      The museum says the Shisanhang people had the skill to smelt iron themselves. A skill like this lives only if it is taught to the next generation.
      史前館說，十三行文化的人「有自行煉鐵的技術」。這樣的技術，要教給下一代才能留下來。

      國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

    • Counting by the moon看月亮算時間

      In the winter of 1602–1603 the Ming scholar Chen Di visited the southwestern coast and wrote about the Indigenous peoples he met there. He noted that they counted a month from full moon to full moon and ten months as a year. He called them by an outsider's word, 夷, and found it strange that they kept no written calendar; but a calendar kept by the moon and by memory is a way of knowing too.
      1602 年冬到 1603 年初，明朝的陳第到過台灣西南海岸，寫下他遇到的原住民族。他記下他們「計月圓為一月，十月為一年」。他用明代外人的字眼「夷」稱呼他們，也覺得他們沒有寫下來的曆法很奇怪；可是看月亮、靠記憶的曆法，也是一種知識。

      陳第〈東番記〉 ↗

    • Knots for trade打結記帳

      Chen Di also wrote that the people he met kept track of trade by tying knots in cord. He was an outsider, and his record shows what he noticed, not everything they knew.
      陳第也寫到，他遇到的人「交易，結繩以識」。他是外來的人，他的紀錄只代表他看到的，不是他們知道的全部。

      陳第〈東番記〉 ↗

    • A language written down一種語言被寫下來

      In the 1600s missionaries tried to teach the Siraya, a Pingpu people, to write their own language with the Roman alphabet. The Siraya kept using it for contracts; the last known document dates from 1818 (Lesson 10).
      1600 年代，傳教士嘗試教平埔族群的西拉雅人用羅馬字母寫自己的語言。西拉雅人一直用它寫契約，留下的「新港文書」最晚的一件寫於 1818 年（第十課）。

      中央研究院臺灣史研究所檔案館〈研究成果問與答〉 ↗

    Stories Behind the Evidence · 證據背後的故事

    A manuscript hunter, a scholar who moved for one book, a hidden Archimedes, and a printed sutra 找抄本的人、為一本書搬家的學者、藏起來的阿基米德，和一部印出來的經

    Four short stories about people who kept books alive.
    四個小故事：讓書活下來的人。

    9th century · Baghdad · 巴格達

    The manuscript hunter找抄本的人

    Hunayn ibn Ishaq, a Christian of the Church of the East, became chief physician to the caliph al-Mutawakkil. He traveled to Syria, Palestine, and Egypt looking for Greek manuscripts, and ran a school of translators in Baghdad with his students.
    侯奈因・伊本・伊斯哈格是東方教會的基督徒，後來成為哈里發穆塔瓦基勒的首席御醫。他到敘利亞、巴勒斯坦和埃及找希臘文的抄本，和學生在巴格達辦了一所翻譯學校。

    12th century · Toledo · 托雷多

    Moving for one book為了一本書搬家

    Gerard of Cremona, from northern Italy, wanted to read Ptolemy's Almagest, which did not exist in Latin. He went to Toledo, learned Arabic, finished his translation in 1175, and stayed there for the rest of his life. About 80 translations are credited to him.
    義大利北部的克雷莫納的傑拉德，想讀托勒密的《天文學大成》，可是那時沒有拉丁文本。他到托雷多學阿拉伯文，1175 年譯完，然後一輩子留在那裡。大約有 80 部譯作歸在他名下。

    900s → 1229 → 1998 · Byzantine world · 拜占庭世界

    Archimedes under a prayer book祈禱書底下的阿基米德

    In the 900s a Byzantine scribe copied works of Archimedes onto parchment. In 1229 a priest at a monastery near Bethlehem scraped the pages and reused them for a prayer book. Modern scientists used ultraviolet light and X-rays to read the faint lines underneath.
    900 年代，一位拜占庭的抄寫員把阿基米德的著作抄在羊皮紙上。1229 年，伯利恆附近一座修道院的教士把紙頁刮乾淨、拿去寫祈禱書。現代的科學家用紫外光和 X 光，讀出了底下淡淡的字跡。

    868 CE · Dunhuang · 敦煌

    A printed sutra一部印出來的經

    A man named Wang Jie had the Diamond Sutra printed from carved wooden blocks, on behalf of his parents and for free distribution. One scroll, about five meters long, was sealed in a cave with tens of thousands of other scrolls and found again in the early 20th century.
    一位叫 Wang Jie 的人，為了父母，也為了讓大家免費取得，用雕刻的木版印《金剛經》。其中一卷長約五公尺，和好幾萬件其他的卷子一起被封在洞窟裡，到二十世紀初才又被發現。

    Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

    Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Europe forgot all its learning in the “Dark Ages.”歐洲在「黑暗時代」把學問全忘了。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Monks kept copying books in monastery writing rooms, and Byzantine scribes kept copying Greek. Britannica notes that European universities after 1100 were stimulated by Arabic scholars.修士一直在修道院的抄寫室裡抄書，拜占庭的抄寫員也一直抄希臘文的書。Britannica 指出，1100 年以後歐洲的大學，受到阿拉伯學者的刺激。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Arabic numerals were invented by Arabs.阿拉伯數字是阿拉伯人發明的。

    ✓ Fact · 事實They began in India in the 6th or 7th century and reached Europe through Arabic writers. That is why they are also called Hindu-Arabic numerals.它們在六或七世紀起源於印度，再透過阿拉伯文的作者傳到歐洲。所以它們也叫「印度—阿拉伯數字」。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Scholars in the Islamic world only kept Greek books safe.伊斯蘭世界的學者只是把希臘的書保管好。

    ✓ Fact · 事實They also added new work: al-Khwārizmī's algebra in Baghdad, Ibn al-Haytham's correct model of how we see, and Avicenna's Canon of Medicine, which was a textbook in Europe until the mid-17th century.他們也做出新的研究：花拉子密在巴格達寫的代數、伊本・海什木對視覺的正確解釋，以及阿維森納的《醫典》（在歐洲一直當教科書，到十七世紀中期）。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思The Library of Alexandria burned in one great fire.亞歷山卓圖書館是在一場大火裡燒光的。

    ✓ Fact · 事實The sources disagree. Britannica itself gives a civil war in 48 BCE, another in the late 3rd century CE, and the loss of a daughter library in 391 CE. A story that appeared in the 13th century blamed the Arab general ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ, but Britannica says it is now considered a fabrication.資料彼此不一致。Britannica 自己就寫了西元前 48 年的內戰、西元三世紀後期的另一場內戰，以及 391 年一座分館的毀滅。十三世紀出現一個故事，把責任推給阿拉伯將領阿慕爾・伊本・阿斯；Britannica 說，這個說法現在被認為是捏造的。

    Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

    Pass It Along 傳下去

    You need · 準備材料

    • One short paragraph about a scientific idea, in English一小段英文的科學說明
    • Paper and pencils; one group per “language” (English, Chinese, simple English)紙筆；每一種「語言」一組（英文、中文、簡單英文）

    Steps · 步驟

    1. Group 1 copies the paragraph by hand in two minutes and passes it on.第一組在兩分鐘內用手抄寫這段話，然後傳下去。
    2. Group 2 translates it into Chinese. Group 3 translates the Chinese back into simple English.第二組把它譯成中文；第三組再把中文譯回簡單的英文。
    3. Compare the last version with the first. What was lost, and what was made clearer?比較最後一版和第一版：少了什麼？哪裡變得更清楚？
    4. Discuss in English: “The idea was translated from … into …” and “Something was lost when …”用英文討論：「The idea was translated from … into …」和「Something was lost when …」

    Real translators like Hunayn compared several manuscripts before translating, to avoid copying mistakes. Ask students how they could do the same.
    像侯奈因這樣的譯者，會先比對好幾份抄本，避免抄錯。問學生：他們可以怎麼做到一樣的事？

    Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

    Word Detectives 字的偵探

    You need · 準備材料

    • The 3D globe and the word game in this lesson這一課的 3D 地球儀和字的小遊戲
    • A dictionary that gives word origins一本有字源的字典

    Steps · 步驟

    1. In pairs, choose two words from the word game.兩人一組，從字的小遊戲裡選兩個字。
    2. On the globe, find a city where each word's language was spoken.在地球儀上，找出這個字的語言當時在哪一個城市說。
    3. Look up one more English word that came from Arabic or Greek.再查一個從阿拉伯文或希臘文來的英文字。
    4. Write two sentences with “borrowed … from” and “was translated from … into.”用 borrowed … from 和 was translated from … into 寫兩句英文。

    Remind students that the stops on the globe show where a book changed language, as one likely path.
    提醒學生：地球儀上的每一站，表示一本書換語言的地方，是一條可能的路。
