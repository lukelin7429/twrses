World History · Unit 1 · Lesson 3 · 世界歷史 單元一 第三課

Why Did People Invent Writing?人為什麼發明文字？

Press number signs into a clay tablet in 3D, the way a scribe in ancient Mesopotamia kept accounts. Then find on a map the few places where writing began on its own, and discover the trick that let pictures write down names and speech.
在 3D 泥板上壓出數字記號，像古代美索不達米亞的書吏那樣記帳。再從地圖上找出文字各自開始的幾個地方，並發現讓圖畫能寫下名字和說話的那個訣竅。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

Writing was invented from scratch in only a few places. Its first uses were mostly practical: keeping accounts in Mesopotamia, asking questions of the spirits in China, recording dates in Mesoamerica. Over time, people learned to use signs for sounds, and writing could record anything people said.
文字從無到有被發明出來的地方只有少數幾個。它最早的用途大多很實際：美索不達米亞拿來記帳，中國拿來向神靈提問，中部美洲拿來記日子。後來，人學會用記號代表聲音，文字就能記下人說的任何話。

Unit 3: Why Did People Invent Writing?

Think back to the granary in Lesson 2. A village has hundreds of sheep, many families, and a store of grain that has to last until the next harvest. Who brought how much? Who still owes some? Memory is not enough for questions like these. In the cities of southern Mesopotamia, people began to answer them with marks pressed into small pieces of soft clay.

回想第二課的穀倉。一個村子有好幾百隻羊、很多戶人家，還有一倉要吃到下一次收成的穀子。誰交了多少？誰還欠著？這種問題光靠記憶是不夠的。在美索不達米亞南部的城市裡，人們開始用壓在一小塊軟泥上的記號來回答這些問題。

The oldest known writing comes from the city of Uruk, about 3350 to 3000 BCE. A scribe pressed the end of a cut reed into wet clay, and the tablet hardened as it dried in the hot, dry air. Most of these early tablets are accounts: grain, animals, workers, and the food paid to them. A smaller number are lists of words, probably used to train scribes. Writing here began as a tool for keeping track of things.

已知最早的文字來自烏魯克城，大約是西元前 3350 到 3000 年。書吏把削過的蘆葦一端壓進濕泥裡，泥板在又熱又乾的空氣中乾了就變硬。這些早期的泥板大多是帳目：穀物、牲畜、工人，以及發給他們的食物。比較少的一部分是單字的清單，大概是用來訓練書吏的。在這裡，文字一開始是用來記住東西的工具。

Writing was not invented only once. Many scholars count four places where it began from scratch: Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica. In Egypt, labels from early royal tombs date from about the same time as the Uruk tablets. In China, the earliest surviving writing was carved on shells and bones more than 3,000 years ago, recording the Shang kings' questions and predictions about the future, addressed to their ancestors and spirits. In Mesoamerica, writing recorded dates in a calendar, and later the names and deeds of rulers.

文字不只被發明了一次。許多學者認為它從無到有出現在四個地方：美索不達米亞、埃及、中國和中部美洲。在埃及，早期王室墓葬裡的標籤和烏魯克的泥板差不多同一個時期。在中國，留存下來最早的文字刻在三千多年前的龜甲和獸骨上，記錄商王向祖先和神靈提出的、關於未來的問題與預測。在中部美洲，文字記下曆法上的日子，後來也記統治者的名字和事蹟。

A picture can show a sheep or a jar of grain, but how do you draw a name, or a word like “and”? The answer was a clever trick: use a picture for its sound. In English, a drawing of an eye could stand for the word “I.” Egyptian hieroglyphs used both kinds of signs, some for meanings and some for sounds. Most Chinese characters joined a part that hints at the meaning with a part that hints at the sound.

圖畫可以畫出一隻羊、一甕穀子，可是名字要怎麼畫？像「和」這樣的字要怎麼畫？答案是一個很聰明的辦法：借用圖畫的聲音。在英文裡，畫一隻眼睛（eye）可以代表「我」（I）。埃及的聖書體兩種記號都用，有的代表意思，有的代表聲音。大多數的漢字，則是把提示意思的部分和提示聲音的部分合在一起。

Perhaps as early as 1800 BCE, people who spoke a Semitic language took a few Egyptian signs and used each one for a single sound. Exactly where and when this happened is still argued over. This was the beginning of the alphabet, which later passed to the Phoenicians, the Greeks, and the Romans. An alphabet needs far fewer signs. But that does not make it better than other scripts. Chinese characters, Japanese kana, and many other systems serve the languages they write very well.

可能早在西元前 1800 年，說閃語的人拿了幾個埃及的記號，讓每一個只代表一個聲音。這件事確切發生在哪裡、什麼時候，至今仍有爭論。這就是字母的開始，後來傳給了腓尼基人、希臘人和羅馬人。字母需要的記號少得多。但這並不表示它比別的文字好。漢字、日文的假名和許多其他的文字系統，都把它們所寫的語言服務得很好。

Writing changed what people could do. A promise, a law, or a list could now outlast the person who made it. For most of its history, though, writing was the skill of a few, such as trained scribes. Most people passed on their history by telling it. In Taiwan, the long age before written records is called prehistory. The National Museum of Prehistory names Chen Di's account of 1603 as the earliest record of Taiwan's customs; older Chinese texts may mention the island, but scholars disagree.

文字改變了人能做的事。一個承諾、一條法律、一份清單，現在可以比說出它的人留得更久。不過在文字歷史上大部分的時間裡，讀寫是少數人的技能，例如受過訓練的書吏。大多數的人是靠口耳相傳，把自己的歷史傳下去。在台灣，沒有文字記錄的漫長年代稱為史前時代。國立臺灣史前文化博物館指出，陳第 1603 年寫成的記述是最早關於台灣風俗的紀錄；更早的中文典籍可能提過這座島，但學者看法不一。

Questions · 閱讀理解

What were the earliest tablets from Uruk mostly used for, and why does that make sense after Lesson 2?

Mostly for accounts: grain, animals, workers, and the food paid to them. Lesson 2 showed that farming villages stored grain and kept herds, which raised questions of who owned what and who owed what. Those questions are hard to answer from memory alone.

Why couldn't early writers simply draw everything they wanted to say?

Many things cannot be drawn: names, small words like “and” or “if,” and ideas. Using a picture for its sound let writers spell out any word, including names.

The lesson says an alphabet is not “better” than other writing systems. What reasons does it give?

An alphabet uses fewer signs, but other scripts, such as Chinese characters and Japanese kana, also serve their languages well. How good a script is depends on the language it writes and the people who use it, not on the number of its signs.

When 1 means 60當 1 代表 60

Place value · 位值

In the Babylonian way, used from about 2000 BCE, 61 is written with two upright wedges, and the left one means sixty. Its place tells you its value, as the 1 in 10 means ten. There was no zero sign, so 1 and 60 on their own looked the same, and readers relied on the context.
在大約西元前 2000 年起使用的巴比倫寫法裡，61 寫成兩個直楔，左邊那一個代表六十。它的位置決定它的值，就像 10 裡面的 1 代表十。那時沒有代表零的記號，所以單獨的 1 和 60 看起來一樣，要靠上下文判斷。

Clay that lasts留得住的泥

Most clay tablets were only dried in the sun, yet in the dry climate of Mesopotamia a great many survived in the ground. More than half a million are now kept in collections around the world.
大多數的泥板只是曬乾而已，但在美索不達米亞乾燥的氣候裡，很多都在地底下保存了下來。現在世界各地的收藏裡有超過五十萬塊。

Reading a lost script讀懂失傳的文字

When a script stops being used, it has to be deciphered. Michael Ventris announced his decipherment of the Greek script called Linear B in 1952, and in the same year the Russian scholar Yuri Knorozov announced that he could match some Maya signs to Maya words.
一種文字不再有人使用之後，就得重新「解讀」。1952 年，文屈斯（Michael Ventris）宣布解讀了一種叫「線形文字 B」的希臘文字；同一年，俄國學者克諾羅佐夫（Yuri Knorozov）宣布他能把一些馬雅的記號和馬雅語的字對起來。

Its limits · 它的限制Some scripts have never been deciphered, such as the Indus signs. Without a key, or a language we know, the marks stay silent.有些文字至今沒有被解讀，例如印度河的記號。沒有鑰匙，也不知道它記的是哪一種語言，這些記號就一直沉默。

Different Accounts · 不同的說法

Four questions about the first writing 關於最早的文字的四個問題

Even the beginning of writing, the point where history is said to start, is argued over. Here are four questions. The views are set side by side; this lesson does not choose between them.
就連文字的開端——常被說是「歷史開始」的地方——也有爭論。下面是四個問題。各種說法並列，這一課不替誰下結論。

Was writing invented once, or several times?文字是被發明一次，還是好幾次？

  • Several times好幾次

    A curator at the University of Chicago's museum, describing the scholarship of the last few decades, counts four places where writing was invented from scratch: Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica.
    芝加哥大學博物館的一位策展人整理近幾十年的研究，認為文字在四個地方從無到有地被發明：美索不達米亞、埃及、中國和中部美洲。

    University of Chicago News (2010); University of Chicago Magazine (2011) ↗

  • Fewer than that沒有那麼多次

    An older view held that writing began in Mesopotamia and the idea spread from there. Egypt is still the case most debated, because its writing appeared at almost the same time, not far away.
    比較早的看法認為文字從美索不達米亞開始，這個點子再從那裡傳開。至今爭論最多的還是埃及，因為它的文字幾乎同時出現，距離又不遠。

    University of Chicago Magazine (2011) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況Most scholars think Chinese and Mesoamerican writing began independently, although a few have argued that the idea of writing reached China from the west. The liveliest debate is about Egypt.多數學者認為中國和中部美洲的文字是各自開始的，不過也有少數學者主張，「文字」這個點子是從西方傳到中國的。爭論最熱烈的是埃及。

Did writing grow out of small clay tokens?文字是從小小的陶籌演變來的嗎？

  • Yes是

    The archaeologist Denise Schmandt-Besserat argues that small clay tokens, used for counting goods from about 8000 to 3500 BCE, were the forerunners of the first written signs.
    考古學家施曼特－貝瑟拉（Denise Schmandt-Besserat）主張，大約西元前 8000 到 3500 年用來計算貨物的小陶籌，是最早的文字記號的前身。

    Schmandt-Besserat, “The Evolution of Writing,” University of Texas ↗

  • Not so simply沒有那麼簡單

    Reviewers have criticized her methods and pointed out that the tokens are too varied, and found over too wide an area, to fit one neat story. Other specialists accept that some simple tokens were forerunners of writing, but not all of her readings.
    書評者批評她的研究方法，並指出這些陶籌種類太多、分布太廣，放不進一個整齊的故事裡。也有專家接受其中一部分簡單的陶籌是文字的前身，但不接受她全部的解讀。

    Zimansky (1993); Michalowski (1993); Englund (c. 2011) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況The theory has been partly accepted, not simply proved or disproved.這個理論部分被接受，並沒有被簡單地證明或推翻。

Are the Indus signs writing?印度河的記號是文字嗎？

  • No不是

    Three scholars argued in 2004 that the Indus signs were a system of symbols that did not record a spoken language.
    三位學者在 2004 年主張，印度河的記號是一套符號系統，並沒有記錄口說的語言。

    Farmer, Sproat, and Witzel, Electronic Journal of Vedic Studies 11 (2004) ↗

  • Possibly可能是

    A 2009 study measured how the signs follow one another and found the patterns closer to those of languages than of non-linguistic systems.
    2009 年的一項研究測量記號前後相接的規律，發現它比較像語言，而不像非語言的符號系統。

    Rao et al., Science 324 (2009); a reply: Sproat, Computational Linguistics 36 (2010) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況Until the signs are deciphered, if they ever are, the question stays open.在這些記號被解讀之前（如果有那一天），這個問題都不會有答案。

Was writing invented for keeping accounts?文字是為了記帳而發明的嗎？

  • In Mesopotamia, mostly yes在美索不達米亞，大致是

    About 85 percent of the early tablets from Uruk are administrative records, by one specialist's count. He puts it plainly: cuneiform began with number signs.
    依一位專家的統計，烏魯克早期的泥板大約百分之八十五是行政記錄。他說得很直接：楔形文字是從數字記號開始的。

    Englund, “Accounting in Proto-cuneiform” (c. 2011) ↗

  • Not everywhere不是每個地方都這樣

    The earliest Chinese writing records divination, and one of the earliest Maya texts includes a calendar date. Even in Mesopotamia, word lists appear from the start, and one specialist suggests they may be the earliest known literature.
    中國最早的文字記的是占卜；最早的馬雅文字裡，有一段記的是曆法上的日子。就算在美索不達米亞，單字的清單也是一開始就有，有一位專家認為它們可能是已知最早的文學。

    University of Chicago Magazine (2011); Stuart et al. (2022) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況“Why writing began” may have a different answer in each place it began.「文字為什麼開始」，在每一個它開始的地方，答案可能都不一樣。

Notice that the disagreements are not only about dates. They are also about definitions: what counts as writing at all.留意這些分歧不只是年代的問題，也是定義的問題：怎樣才算是「文字」？

History English · 歷史英語

Eight sentences for talking about old writing 談古老文字的八句英文

These sentences help you talk about writing the way historians do: what is the earliest known, what a sign stands for, and what is still unread. Listen, repeat, then make your own sentence with the words in bold.
這八句幫你用歷史學家的方式談文字：已知最早的是什麼、一個記號代表什麼、什麼還沒有被讀懂。先聽、再跟著唸，然後用粗體的字自己造一句。

  1. The earliest known writing comes from Uruk.

    已知最早的文字來自烏魯克。

    the earliest known: the oldest found so far目前找到最早的

  2. Most of the first tablets were used to keep accounts.

    最早的泥板大多是拿來記帳的。

    were used to + verb: what something was for說某樣東西的用途

  3. This sign stands for the number ten.

    這個記號代表數字十。

    stands for: means, represents代表

  4. A picture can stand for a sound as well as a thing.

    一個圖畫可以代表一個聲音，也可以代表一個東西。

    stand for a sound: be used for how it is said借用它的聲音

  5. The Indus signs have not been deciphered.

    印度河的記號還沒有被解讀。

    have not been deciphered: no one can read them yet還沒有人讀得懂

  6. Writing was invented independently in several places.

    文字在好幾個地方被各自獨立地發明。

    was invented independently: began separately各自開始

  7. Scholars generally agree that China invented its own writing.

    學者一般都同意，中國的文字是自己發明的。

    scholars generally agree that: most experts think大多數專家認為

  8. Writing made it possible to keep a promise after the speaker was gone.

    文字讓一個承諾在說話的人離開之後還能留下來。

    made it possible to: allowed something new讓某件事變得可能

Taiwan at That Time · 那時候的台灣

From spoken history to written records 從口傳的歷史到文字的記錄

When the first tablets were being pressed at Uruk, Taiwan's people were living through the age the museum calls prehistory. Their history was carried in memory and speech.
烏魯克的第一批泥板被壓出來的時候，台灣的住民正生活在博物館所說的史前時代。他們的歷史，是靠記憶和口語傳下來的。

🏝️

History does not begin with writing; only written records do. Taiwan's story shows how much was passed on by speaking, and how writing arrived late, from outside, and was then taken up by the people themselves.
歷史不是從文字開始的，開始的只是文字的記錄。台灣的故事讓我們看到，有多少東西是靠說話傳下來的；也看到文字是很晚才從外面來的，後來又被這裡的人拿來自己用。

  • Prehistory, in the museum's words博物館怎麼說史前

    The National Museum of Prehistory explains that prehistory is the age without written records, when people passed down what had happened by telling it, from one generation to the next.
    國立臺灣史前文化博物館說明：「史前時期是指沒有文字紀錄的年代，史前人們用口語流傳的方式，把過去發生的事一代傳一代地說給後代子孫聽。」

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館「從史前走進歷史」 ↗

  • The earliest written descriptions最早的文字描述

    In 1603 Chen Di wrote Dongfan ji, which the same museum calls the earliest record of customs in Taiwan. An older Chinese history, the Book of Sui, describes a place called Liuqiu, but scholars have long disagreed about whether it means Taiwan.
    1603 年，陳第寫下《東番記》，同一座博物館稱它留下了「最早關於臺灣風俗的紀錄」。更早的《隋書》描寫過一個叫「流求」的地方，但它指的是不是台灣，學者之間一直有不同的看法。

    林鶴亭〈隋書流求傳之研究〉，《臺南文化》(1959) ↗

  • A language written in Latin letters用羅馬字寫下的語言

    In the 17th century Dutch missionaries taught Siraya communities to write their own language in Latin letters. The so-called Sinckan (or Sinkang) manuscripts, mostly land contracts and loans, kept being written long after the Dutch left: the earliest known is from 1683 and the latest from 1813 or 1818, depending on the source. More than 140 survive.
    十七世紀，荷蘭傳教士教西拉雅族的社群用羅馬字書寫自己的語言。所謂的「新港文書」大多是土地契約，荷蘭人離開以後還繼續有人寫：已知最早的是 1683 年，最晚的是 1813 年或 1818 年（兩份資料不同）。現存大約一百四十多件。

    中央研究院民族學研究所〈新港文書〉；中央研究院臺灣史研究所檔案館 ↗

  • Today今天

    Since 2005, Taiwan's Indigenous languages have had official writing systems in Latin letters, set by the Council of Indigenous Peoples and the Ministry of Education. The announcement itself names the Sinckan manuscripts as part of that history.
    2005 年起，臺灣的原住民族語言有了由原住民族委員會和教育部公告、以羅馬字書寫的書寫系統。公告本身就提到，「新港文書」是這段歷史的一部分。

    原住民族委員會、教育部《原住民族語言書寫系統》(2005) ↗

Stories Behind the Evidence · 證據背後的故事

A last tablet, an architect, a deer, and some old bones 最後一塊泥板、一位建築師、一隻鹿，和幾塊舊骨頭

Four short stories about how writing ended, and how it was found again.
四個小故事：文字怎麼結束，又怎麼被重新找到。

79 or 80 CE · Mesopotamia · 美索不達米亞

The last tablet最後一塊泥板

Cuneiform was written for more than three thousand years. The latest datable tablet found so far is an astronomical text from 79 or 80 CE. After that, the script was forgotten until scholars in the 19th century learned to read it again.
楔形文字被使用了三千多年。目前找到最晚、可以定年的一塊泥板，是西元 79 或 80 年的天文記錄。在那之後，這種文字被遺忘了，直到十九世紀的學者才又重新學會讀它。

1952 · Greece · 希臘

An architect cracks a code一位建築師解開密碼

Clay tablets from ancient Crete and mainland Greece were written in a script called Linear B. For decades no one could read them. Michael Ventris, an architect, showed that they recorded an early form of Greek, and his decipherment was broadcast to the world on July 1, 1952.
古代克里特島和希臘本土出土的泥板，寫的是一種叫「線形文字 B」的文字。幾十年來沒有人讀得懂。建築師文屈斯證明它記的是一種早期的希臘語，他的解讀在 1952 年 7 月 1 日透過廣播公諸於世。

About 300–200 BCE · Mesoamerica · 中部美洲

A day called 7 Deer一個叫「七鹿」的日子

At San Bartolo in Guatemala, painted fragments of a wall carry the oldest dated Maya calendar sign found so far: a day called 7 Deer, in a 260-day calendar. It shows that keeping track of time was already part of the earliest Maya writing.
在瓜地馬拉的聖巴托洛，一面牆的彩繪殘片上，有目前所知最早、可以定年的馬雅曆法記號：260 天曆法裡一個叫「七鹿」的日子。這說明記錄時間在最早的馬雅文字裡就已經出現了。

1899 · China · 中國

Writing on old bones舊骨頭上的字

Shells and bones from Xiaotun village near Anyang were recognized as ancient writing in 1899. One standard reference lists 4,672 different characters on them, of which 1,723 can be transcribed into later standard characters; the meanings of many are still unclear. Lesson 5 of the Chinese Calligraphy series looks at them closely.
1899 年，安陽小屯村一帶出土的龜甲和獸骨被認出是古代的文字。一部常用的工具書收錄了上面 4,672 個不同的字，其中 1,723 個可以隸定成後來的楷字；很多字的意思到現在還不清楚。書法系列第五課會仔細看它們。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思Writing was invented to record stories, poems, and history.文字是為了記錄故事、詩和歷史而發明的。

✓ Fact · 事實About 85 percent of the early tablets from Uruk are administrative records; in China the earliest writing records divination. Stories and poems came later, although word lists appear from the very start.烏魯克早期的泥板大約百分之八十五是行政記錄；在中國，最早的文字記的是占卜。故事和詩是後來的事，不過單字的清單從一開始就有了。

✗ Myth · 迷思Egyptian hieroglyphs are just pictures.埃及的聖書體就只是圖畫。

✓ Fact · 事實They mixed picture signs with signs that stood for sounds, using the same trick as a picture of an eye standing for the word “I.”它們把圖畫記號和代表聲音的記號混在一起用，用的就是「畫一隻眼睛代表 I」那種辦法。

✗ Myth · 迷思Chinese characters are pictures of things.漢字都是畫東西的圖。

✓ Fact · 事實A few began as pictures, but about nine in ten of the characters in the Shuowen jiezi, a dictionary from about 2,000 years ago, combine a meaning part with a sound part. In the much older oracle bone script, such characters were still a small share, perhaps 10 to 20 percent by one estimate.有一些字一開始是圖畫，但在大約兩千年前的字典《說文解字》裡，大約十個字有九個是把表意的部分和表音的部分合在一起的形聲字。在更古老的甲骨文裡，這種字還只占一小部分，有一種估計是百分之十到二十左右。

✗ Myth · 迷思The alphabet is the most advanced kind of writing.字母是最先進的文字。

✓ Fact · 事實An alphabet uses fewer signs, but a museum essay on its history notes that widespread literacy does not require an alphabet. Many historians of writing would add that each script suits the language it was made for, so none is simply better than another.字母用的記號比較少，但一篇談字母歷史的博物館文章指出，要讓很多人識字，並不一定需要字母。許多研究文字史的學者也會補充：每一種文字都適合它所寫的語言，所以沒有哪一種是單純比較好的。

In Mesopotamia, writing began with numbers and lists. Literature came much later.
在美索不達米亞，文字是從數字和清單開始的。文學是很久以後的事。

Eye for “I.” When a picture can stand for a sound, anything that can be said can be written.
用 eye 寫 I。一旦圖畫能代表聲音，說得出口的話就都寫得下來。

No winner among scripts文字不分高下

Alphabet or characters: each fits the language it writes.
字母也好、漢字也好：每一種都適合它所寫的語言。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

Keep the Class Accounts in Clay 用黏土替全班記帳

You need · 準備材料

  • A lump of clay or play dough for each pair, about the size of a large cookie每兩人一塊黏土或紙黏土，大約一片大餅乾那麼大
  • A chopstick or a craft stick with one end cut at a slant, to press wedges一支一端斜切的筷子或冰棒棍，用來壓出楔形

Steps · 步驟

  1. Flatten the clay into a tablet that fits in your palm.把黏土壓成可以放在掌心的泥板。
  2. Count something real in the classroom: chairs, windows, people who walk to school. Write each count in Babylonian numbers: an upright wedge for each one, a corner wedge for each ten.數一數教室裡真的有的東西：椅子、窗戶、走路上學的人。用巴比倫的數字寫下每個數：一個直楔代表一，一個角楔代表十。
  3. Swap tablets with another pair. Read their numbers out in English: “There are twenty-six chairs.”和另一組交換泥板，用英文唸出他們的數字：「There are twenty-six chairs.」
  4. Now try to record what was counted, without using any letters or characters. How would you show “chairs”? How would you show your teacher's name?接著試著記下「數的是什麼」，但不可以用任何字母或漢字。「椅子」要怎麼表示？老師的名字又要怎麼表示？

Step 4 is the lesson. Students usually find that things are easy to draw and names are not, which is exactly the problem early writers solved by borrowing sounds.
第四步就是這堂課的重點。學生通常會發現：東西很好畫，名字卻畫不出來——這正是早期的書寫者借用聲音所解決的問題。

Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

Write Your Name in Rebus 用畫謎寫你的名字

You need · 準備材料

  • Paper and colored pencils紙和色鉛筆
  • The examples from the “A Picture, or a Sound?” game「是圖，還是聲音？」遊戲裡的例子

Steps · 步驟

  1. Say your English name slowly and break it into sounds. “Ka-ren,” “Ben,” “Lu-key.”慢慢唸出你的英文名字，把它拆成幾個聲音。例如 Ka-ren、Ben、Lu-key。
  2. For each sound, find a word that sounds like it and is easy to draw: a car, a bee, a key.每一個聲音，找一個唸起來很像、又好畫的字：car（車）、bee（蜜蜂）、key（鑰匙）。
  3. Draw your name as a row of pictures. Trade with a classmate and read each other's names aloud.把名字畫成一排圖。和同學交換，把對方的名字唸出來。
  4. Try a Chinese name the same way. Is it easier or harder? Why?用同樣的方法試試中文名字。比較容易還是比較難？為什麼？

Some names will not work well. That is the point: picture-sounds are clumsy, which is one reason writing systems grew sets of signs used mainly for their sounds.
有些名字會畫得很勉強，這正是重點：用圖畫借聲音很笨拙，這也是文字系統後來發展出一套主要用來表音的記號的原因之一。
