Big idea · 一句話看懂

In 221 BCE the kingdom of Qin conquered the other Warring States and tied them together with one script, one set of weights and measures, one coin, and new roads. The Qin fell within fifteen years, but the Han dynasty kept many of its standards and lasted about four centuries. Unification brought connection, and also heavy forced labor and harsh punishments.
西元前 221 年，秦國攻下了其他戰國的國家，用同一套文字、度量衡、錢和新的道路把它們連在一起。秦朝十五年就亡了，但漢朝保留了很多標準，延續了大約四百年。統一帶來了連結，也帶來了沉重的勞役和嚴厲的刑罰。

Reading · 英文閱讀

Unit 7: How Did Qin and Han Unite China?

For more than two centuries before 221 BCE, China was divided among rival kingdoms, a time known as the Warring States. Each kingdom had its own coins, its own weights and measures, and its own laws. A trader from Qi in the east paid with heavy bronze knives; in Chu in the south, people used small coins shaped like shells. Between 230 and 221 BCE, the western kingdom of Qin conquered the other six, one after another.

西元前 221 年以前的兩百多年，中國分成好幾個互相競爭的國家，這個時代叫做「戰國」。每個國家都有自己的錢、自己的度量衡和自己的法律。東邊齊國的商人用沉重的青銅刀幣付錢；南方的楚國，人們用像貝殼一樣的小錢。從西元前 230 年到 221 年，西邊的秦國一個接一個地攻下了其他六國。

The king of Qin took a new title, Huangdi, “emperor,” and is remembered as the First Emperor. His government ordered one standard for laws, weights and measures, the width of cart axles, and writing. An old Qin measuring cup in the Shanghai Museum, made in 344 BCE, also carries the edict of 221 BCE. The round Qin coin with a square hole became the only legal bronze coin, and that shape was used in East Asia for about 2,000 years.

秦王採用了一個新的稱號「皇帝」，後來被稱為秦始皇。他的政府下令，法令、度量衡、車軸的寬度和文字都用同一個標準。上海博物館有一個秦國的舊量器，做於西元前 344 年，上面還刻有西元前 221 年的詔書。秦朝圓形方孔的錢成了唯一合法的銅錢，這個形狀在東亞用了大約兩千年。

The Qin also built roads, including the Straight Road toward the northern frontier, and joined sections of earlier walls into one line of defense. All this took the forced labor of huge numbers of people. Qin law was strict, and in 213 BCE the government ordered private copies of many books to be burned, sparing books on medicine, divination, and farming. The next year, the historian Sima Qian writes, more than 460 scholars were put to death.

秦朝也修築道路，包括通往北方邊境的直道，並把早先各國的城牆連成一條防線。這些都要靠非常多人的強迫勞役。秦朝的法律很嚴；西元前 213 年，政府下令燒掉民間收藏的許多書，醫藥、卜筮、種樹的書不燒。史家司馬遷寫道，第二年有四百六十多個讀書人被處死。

The Qin dynasty did not last. Soon after the First Emperor died in 210 BCE, rebellions broke out, and by 207–206 BCE the Qin had fallen. The Han dynasty that followed lasted, with one interruption, until 220 CE. The Han kept many Qin standards and its centralized government, while turning toward Confucian ideas of moderate rule. Emperor Wu appointed scholars for the Confucian classics and asked each region to recommend honest men for office. In 118 BCE he introduced the wuzhu coin, used for centuries afterward.

秦朝沒有撐多久。西元前 210 年秦始皇死後不久，各地起兵反抗；到了西元前 207 至 206 年，秦朝就亡了。接下來的漢朝，中間被打斷過一次，一直延續到西元 220 年。漢朝保留了很多秦朝的標準和中央集權的制度，同時轉向儒家溫和治理的理想。漢武帝設立五經博士，又讓各地推薦孝順廉潔的人做官。西元前 118 年，他推行五銖錢，之後用了好幾百年。

The Han empire also reached out to the world. Around 138 BCE, the envoy Zhang Qian set out west and returned about thirteen years later with news of distant lands; Lesson 9 follows the roads he opened. A census recorded in the History of the Former Han counts almost 60 million people. In 105 CE, an official named Cai Lun presented a new way of making paper from bark, hemp, rags, and old fishing nets; archaeologists have also found older pieces of paper.

漢朝也向外面的世界伸出手。大約西元前 138 年，使者張騫往西出發，大約十三年後帶著遠方的消息回來；第九課會沿著他打通的路走。《漢書》記載的一次人口普查，算出將近六千萬人。西元 105 年，一位叫蔡倫的官員，提出用樹皮、麻、破布和舊魚網造紙的新方法；考古學家也找到過更早的紙片。

At the same time, at the other end of Eurasia, the Roman Empire also ruled tens of millions of people. Both empires built roads, made laws, and issued coins. Among their differences, Rome spread its citizenship, while the Han relied on a shared script and on officials chosen for their learning and good name. Each empire knew very little about the other. Comparing them shows two different ways of holding a huge land together, each with its own costs.

在同一個時代，歐亞大陸的另一端，羅馬帝國也統治著好幾千萬人。兩個帝國都修道路、訂法律、發行錢幣。不同的地方之一是：羅馬擴大公民權，而漢朝靠共同的文字，以及因學問和好名聲被選出來的官員。兩個帝國對彼此都所知很少。把它們放在一起比較，可以看到兩種把一大片土地連在一起的方法，各有各的代價。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Name three things the Qin made the same across its empire.

Laws, weights and measures, the width of cart axles, writing, and coins. Any three are correct.

Why might a single coin and single set of measures help hold an empire together?

People in different regions could trade, pay taxes, and check amounts in the same way, and the government could count and collect everywhere using one standard.

The lesson says unification brought connection and also costs. Give one example of each.

Connection: one script, coins, measures, and roads linked distant regions. Costs: forced labor on roads and walls, harsh punishments, and the burning of books.

How Do We Know? · 我們怎麼知道？

A historian, a measuring cup, and slips of bamboo 一位史家、一個量器，和一片片竹簡

Much of the story of the Qin comes from one historian who wrote under the Han, so archaeology matters a great deal.
秦朝的故事，很多來自一位在漢朝寫書的史家，所以考古的發現特別重要。

📚

The Records of the Grand Historian《史記》

Sima Qian, a Han court historian, wrote the Shiji about a century after the Qin fell. It tells of the unification, the standards, the roads, the burning of books, and the rebellions.
漢朝的史官司馬遷，在秦亡大約一百年後寫成《史記》。書裡記載了統一、各種標準、道路、焚書，以及各地的起兵。

Its limits · 它的限制Sima Qian wrote for the dynasty that replaced the Qin, and readers have long asked how fair he was to it. Newly excavated Qin texts let historians check parts of his story.司馬遷是為取代秦朝的漢朝寫書，讀者一直在問他對秦朝是否公平。新出土的秦朝文書，讓歷史學家能查核他的一部分說法。

《史記・秦始皇本紀》 ↗

Its limits · 它的限制Objects show what the law ordered, not how strictly it was followed in every village.器物告訴我們法令下了什麼命令，卻看不出每個村子執行得多嚴格。

Qin legal texts written on bamboo slips, found at Shuihudi in Hubei, show the actual rules. One statute on labor service fines people who arrive late and excuses them if it rains.

Different Accounts · 不同的說法

Four questions about the Qin and Han 關於秦漢的四個問題

Ancient histories and new discoveries do not always agree. Here are four questions, with the views set side by side.
古代的史書和新的發現，說法不一定一樣。下面是四個問題，各種說法並列。

Was being late really punished by death?遲到真的要殺頭嗎？

  • Yes, says the Shiji《史記》說是

    Sima Qian writes that Chen Sheng's band, held up by rain, knew that under the law “those who miss the deadline are all beheaded,” so they rebelled.
    司馬遷寫道，陳勝的隊伍被大雨耽誤，知道依法「失期，法皆斬」，所以起兵反抗。

    《史記・陳涉世家》 ↗

  • Not in this law這條法律不是這樣

    A Qin statute on labor service found at Shuihudi punishes lateness with a reprimand or fines, and excuses it in rain. But Chen Sheng's men were garrison soldiers, who fell under different rules.
    睡虎地出土的秦朝〈徭律〉，對遲到的處罰是斥責或罰錢，下雨還可以免除。不過陳勝那群人是去戍守邊境的，適用的可能是另一套規定。

    睡虎地秦簡〈徭律〉；郭建，澎湃新聞（2014） ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況The new evidence shows that one story may be too simple, but it does not settle the question.新的證據說明這個故事可能太簡單了，但還不能下定論。

What was the burning of books really about?焚書到底是怎麼回事？

  • Destroying learning毀掉學問

    The burning of books and the deaths of scholars became a lasting symbol of a harsh ruler attacking learning, especially Confucian learning.
    焚書和處死讀書人，成了暴君打壓學問、特別是儒家學問的長久象徵。

    《史記・秦始皇本紀》 ↗

  • Controlling private learning控制民間的學問

    The Shiji itself says that books on medicine, divination, and farming were spared, and the court's own scholars kept their copies. One scholar argues that Confucianism as such was not the target.
    《史記》自己就說，醫藥、卜筮、種樹的書不燒，朝廷博士官的藏書也保留。有學者主張，針對的並不是儒家本身。

    Pines, “Legalism in Chinese Philosophy,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況Both views agree that many books were lost; they differ on what the Qin intended.兩種說法都同意很多書因此失傳；分歧在於秦朝的用意是什麼。

Is every terracotta warrior's face different?每一個兵馬俑的臉都不一樣嗎？

  • All different都不一樣

    UNESCO's description of the First Emperor's tomb says that the figures “are all different.”
    聯合國教科文組織對秦始皇陵的說明寫道，這些陶俑「都不一樣」。

    UNESCO World Heritage List, Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor ↗

  • Molded, then finished by hand用模子做，再用手修

    A Metropolitan Museum essay explains that parts were made with molds and then finished by hand, and that most scholars do not think the statues were modeled on real soldiers.
    大都會藝術博物館的一篇文章說明，陶俑的部分是用模子做的，再用手修整；大多數學者也不認為它們是照著真的士兵做的。

    Metropolitan Museum of Art, “Making the Warrior” (2017) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況The faces are individualized, but made with a mix of molds and handwork. Scholars still debate exactly how.臉是有個別差異的，但是用模子加上手工做出來的；確切的做法學者還在討論。

Which empire was bigger, Rome or the Han?羅馬和漢朝，哪一個比較大？

  • The same order of size同一個規模

    A census in the History of the Former Han counts 59,594,978 people. A 2025 study accepts about 57.7 million for the Han around 2 CE and about 75 million for Rome around 165 CE, and says each ruled 60 to 70 million people at its peak.
    《漢書》記載的人口普查是 59,594,978 人。2025 年的一項研究採用的估計是：西元 2 年前後的漢朝約 5,770 萬，西元 165 年前後的羅馬約 7,500 萬；並說兩者全盛時都統治了六、七千萬人。

    《漢書・地理志》；Alfani, Bolla, and Scheidel, Nature Communications (2025) ↗

  • The wrong question問錯了問題

    Historians who compare the two, such as Michael Loewe, look at how each was governed, and note that each empire was largely ignorant of the other.
    比較這兩個帝國的歷史學家，例如魯惟一（Michael Loewe），看的是它們怎麼治理，並指出兩個帝國對彼此大多一無所知。

    Loewe, Imperial Institutions in Ancient Rome and Early China (2025) ↗

Where things stand · 目前的情況Size alone does not tell us how well people lived. Lesson 6 gave other estimates for Rome; every number here is an estimate for a different year.光看大小，說明不了人們過得好不好。第六課對羅馬的人口有其他的估計；這裡每一個數字，都是不同年份的估計。

Notice how a single excavated text can challenge a story told for two thousand years, and how it can also be over-read.留意一份出土的文書，怎麼挑戰一個流傳了兩千年的故事；也留意它可能被讀得太過頭。

Taiwan at That Time · 那時候的台灣

Many cultures, no kingdoms 很多文化，沒有王國

When the Qin united China, about 2,200 years ago, Taiwan was home to several prehistoric cultures, each in its own region.
秦統一中國的時候，大約距今兩千兩百年，台灣有好幾個史前文化，各自分布在不同的地區。

🏝️

Archaeologists in Taiwan give dates as “years ago” (before 1950). About 2,200 years ago is roughly the third century BCE.
台灣的考古學家用「距今幾年」（以 1950 年為準）說年代。距今大約兩千兩百年，差不多是西元前三世紀。

  • Yuanshan culture, north北部的圓山文化

    In the Taipei Basin, the Yuanshan culture lasted from about 4,500 to a little over 2,000 years ago.
    在臺北盆地，圓山文化從距今約四千五百年延續到二千多年前。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

  • Dahu and Yingpu cultures大湖文化與營埔文化

    In the south, the Dahu culture, and in central Taiwan, the Yingpu culture, both lasted from about 3,500 to a little over 2,000 years ago.
    南部的大湖文化，以及中部的營埔文化，都是從距今約三千五百年延續到二千多年前。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

  • Beinan and Qilin, east東部的卑南與麒麟

    On the east coast, the Beinan culture was at its height about 3,500 to 2,000 years ago, and lived side by side with the Qilin culture of standing stones. The museum notes that the Beinan culture is completely different from the culture of today's Puyuma people.
    在東海岸，卑南文化極盛的年代大約在三千五百到二千年前，和豎立巨石的麒麟文化並存。博物館也說明，卑南文化和今天的卑南族文化完全不同。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

  • No single standard沒有統一的標準

    These cultures had different pottery and tools, and there was no kingdom to make them the same. A little later, about 2,000 years ago, the Shisanhang culture rose near the Taipei Basin.
    這些文化各有不同的陶器和工具，也沒有一個王國把它們變成一樣。稍晚，大約二千年前，十三行文化在臺北盆地附近興起。

    國立臺灣史前文化博物館 考古學習資源 ↗

Stories Behind the Evidence · 證據背後的故事

A measuring cup, a farmers' well, a wall that is not one wall, and a view from space 一個量器、一口農夫挖的井、一道不只一道的牆，和從太空看下來

Four short stories about how we know what the Qin and Han built.
四個小故事：我們怎麼知道秦漢蓋了什麼。

344 and 221 BCE · Shanghai Museum · 上海博物館

A cup with two dates一個有兩個年代的量器

The Shang Yang measure was made in 344 BCE, when Shang Yang reformed the kingdom of Qin. More than a century later, a new edict was carved on its base: the emperor had united the land, and all measures that were not the same were to be made the same.
商鞅方升做於西元前 344 年，那時商鞅正在秦國變法。一百多年後，器物的底部又刻了新的詔書：皇帝已經統一天下，「法度量則不壹嫌疑者，皆明壹之」。

1974 · Xi'an · 西安

Farmers digging a well挖井的農夫

In 1974, farmers sinking a well near the First Emperor's tomb found pieces of clay soldiers. The first pit alone is about 210 meters long and holds more than 3,000 figures.
1974 年，在秦始皇陵附近挖井的農夫，挖到了陶俑的碎片。光是第一號坑就長大約 210 公尺，裡面有三千多個陶俑。

220 BCE to 1600s CE · Northern China · 中國北方

Not one wall不只一道牆

Around 220 BCE, the Qin joined sections of earlier walls into one line of defense. Walls were then built and rebuilt until the 1600s. A national survey published in 2012 measured the walls of all periods at 21,196.18 kilometers; the Ming walls are the longest single part, about 8,850 kilometers.
大約西元前 220 年，秦把各國早先的城牆連成一條防線。之後一直有人修、有人重建，直到十七世紀。2012 年公布的全國調查，量出歷代長城總長 21,196.18 公里；其中明朝的長城最長，大約 8,850 公里。

2003 · In orbit · 太空中

“I didn't see the Great Wall”「我沒有看到長城」

When Yang Liwei became China's first astronaut in 2003, he was asked whether he had seen the Great Wall. He said he had not. NASA explains that the wall is about the same color as the land around it, and certainly cannot be seen from the Moon.
2003 年，楊利偉成為中國第一位太空人。被問到有沒有看到長城，他說沒有。美國太空總署解釋，長城和周圍土地的顏色差不多，從月球上當然更看不到。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思The Great Wall can be seen from space with the naked eye.從太空用肉眼看得到長城。

✓ Fact · 事實China's first astronaut said he did not see it, and NASA explains that from low orbit it generally cannot be seen without help, and certainly not from the Moon.中國第一位太空人說他沒有看到；美國太空總署也說明，從低軌道上一般看不到，從月球更不可能。

✗ Myth · 迷思The Great Wall is one continuous wall built by the Qin.長城是一道連續的牆，是秦朝蓋的。

✓ Fact · 事實The Qin joined earlier walls, and walls were built and rebuilt for nearly 2,000 years. The Ming walls are the longest single part.秦是把早先的城牆連起來；之後將近兩千年一直有人修建。其中明朝的長城最長。

✗ Myth · 迷思The First Emperor burned all the books.秦始皇把所有的書都燒了。

✓ Fact · 事實The order targeted private copies of certain books. Books on medicine, divination, and farming were spared, and the court kept its own copies.命令針對的是民間收藏的某些書。醫藥、卜筮、種樹的書不燒，朝廷也保留了自己的藏書。

✗ Myth · 迷思Unification meant everyone spoke the same language.統一以後，大家都說同一種話。

✓ Fact · 事實The Qin standardized writing, not speech. People in different regions could read the same characters while still speaking very differently.秦統一的是文字，不是說話。不同地方的人可以讀同樣的字，說起話來卻還是很不一樣。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

The Class Market 班級市集

You need · 準備材料

  • Paper “coins” cut into four shapes: spades, knives, round coins, and small shells剪成四種形狀的紙錢：鏟形、刀形、圓形和小貝殼形
  • Cards for goods: salt, cloth, rice, horses商品卡：鹽、布、米、馬

Steps · 步驟

  1. Divide the class into four “kingdoms.” Each kingdom gets only one shape of coin and sets its own prices.把全班分成四個「國家」。每個國家只拿到一種形狀的錢，自己定價格。
  2. Trade between kingdoms for five minutes. How do you agree how many knives equal one spade?國家之間交易五分鐘。幾把刀等於一把鏟，要怎麼談？
  3. Now “unify”: everyone exchanges their coins for one round coin and uses one price list. Trade again.接著「統一」：大家把錢換成同一種圓錢，用同一張價目表，再交易一次。
  4. Discuss in English: “Trading was easier because …” and “Some kingdoms lost because …”用英文討論：「Trading was easier because …」和「Some kingdoms lost because …」

Step 4 matters most. A single coin helps trade, but changing over is not fair to everyone, which is part of why unification was hard.
第四步最重要。同一種錢讓交易方便，但換錢的過程對每個人不一定公平，這也是統一不容易的原因之一。

Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

Compare Two Empires 比較兩個帝國

You need · 準備材料

  • The comparison table from Lesson 6's activity, or a new sheet with two columns: Rome and Han第六課活動的比較表，或一張新的紙，分成羅馬和漢朝兩欄
  • This lesson and Lesson 6這一課和第六課

Steps · 步驟

  1. Fill in: How did each empire hold itself together? Roads, law, citizenship, script, coins, measures, officials.填寫：兩個帝國各用什麼方法把自己連在一起？道路、法律、公民權、文字、錢、度量衡、官員。
  2. Add one row: Who paid the price?加一列：誰付出了代價？

  4. Write two sentences: one with “both … and,” one with “while.”寫兩句英文：一句用 both … and，一句用 while。

Remind students that the goal is to compare, not to decide which empire was “better.”
提醒學生：目的是比較，不是決定哪一個帝國「比較好」。
