Big idea · 一句話看懂

Paper, movable clay type, and gunpowder were developed in China, and printing grew up across East Asia; sailors in China and Europe found the compass apparently on their own. Other peoples improved these inventions, and they changed the world. From 1405 to 1433, Zheng He led seven great fleets across the Indian Ocean to ports that were already busy with trade.
紙、膠泥活字和火藥在中國發展出來，印刷在整個東亞成長；指南針則是中國和歐洲的水手似乎各自發現的。其他民族也改良了這些發明，它們改變了世界。從 1405 到 1433 年，鄭和帶領七支大船隊橫越印度洋，到達那些本來就很熱鬧的貿易港口。

Unit 15: What Did China Invent, and Where Did Zheng He Sail?

The oldest known piece of paper, a map found in a tomb in Gansu, was made in the 2nd century BCE. About 105 CE an official named Cai Lun recorded a way of making paper from bark, rags, hemp, and old fishnets. Paper reached Samarkand by 751 and Baghdad by 793, and by the 1300s there were paper mills in Europe.

已知最古老的紙，是在甘肅一座墓裡找到的一張地圖，做於西元前二世紀。大約西元 105 年，一位叫蔡倫的官員記下了一種用樹皮、破布、麻和舊漁網造紙的方法。紙在 751 年傳到撒馬爾罕，793 年到巴格達；到了十四世紀，歐洲已經有了造紙廠。

Printing came next. Buddhist charms were printed from wood blocks in Japan about 764–770, and the Diamond Sutra, printed in China in 868, is the oldest known printed book. About 1041–48 Bi Sheng made movable type from baked clay. In Korea, movable type appeared by the 1200s, and in 1403 the king ordered 100,000 pieces of bronze type. Britannica says this knowledge does not seem to have reached Europe, where a printing press was invented about 1450.

接下來是印刷。日本大約在 764–770 年用木版印了佛教的咒文；868 年在中國印的《金剛經》，是已知最古老的印刷書。大約 1041–48 年，畢昇用燒過的膠泥做活字。在朝鮮，活字在十三世紀已經出現；1403 年，國王下令鑄造十萬個銅活字。Britannica 說，這些知識似乎沒有傳到歐洲；歐洲大約在 1450 年發明了印刷機。

The magnetic compass has a shared story. Late in the 11th century Shen Kuo wrote that a needle rubbed with lodestone points south, though slightly east. Britannica says that in the 12th century sailors in China and Europe found, apparently on their own, that lodestone could show them north. Gunpowder was used in China for fireworks and signals by the 10th century; knowledge of it later reached Europe, possibly through the Mongols or the Arab world.

指南針的故事是大家一起寫的。十一世紀後期，沈括寫道，用磁石磨過的針會指向南方，不過「常微偏東」。Britannica 說，十二世紀時，中國和歐洲的水手似乎各自發現，磁石可以告訴他們北方在哪裡。火藥在十世紀時已經在中國用來做煙火和信號；後來它的知識傳到歐洲，可能是經由蒙古人或阿拉伯世界。

In 1402 the Yongle emperor took the throne of the Ming dynasty. He chose Zheng He, a man from a Muslim family in Yunnan who had been captured as a boy and served the Prince of Yan, who became the Yongle emperor, to command great fleets to the “Western Oceans.” Between 1405 and 1433 Zheng He led seven voyages to Southeast Asia, India, the Persian Gulf, Arabia, and the coast of East Africa.

1402 年，永樂皇帝登上明朝的皇位。他選了鄭和來指揮前往「西洋」的大船隊。鄭和出身雲南的穆斯林家庭，小時候被俘，後來在燕王（後來的永樂皇帝）府中做事。從 1405 到 1433 年，鄭和七次航行，到過東南亞、印度、波斯灣、阿拉伯半島和東非海岸。

The fleets carried silk, porcelain, and other goods, traded at the ports, and brought envoys and tribute back to China, including a giraffe. Education About Asia says the fleet was by and large meant for peaceful diplomacy and trade, though the History of Ming says force was used against those who would not submit, and the fleet did fight: it defeated a pirate leader and captured a king of Ceylon. Calicut and Hormuz were already great trading cities; Malacca, founded only a few years earlier, grew into a major hub in that century (Lesson 9 tells of the older sea roads).

船隊載著絲綢、瓷器和其他貨物，在各港口交易，再把使節和貢品帶回中國，其中還有一隻長頸鹿。Education About Asia 說，船隊大體上是為了和平的外交和貿易；不過《明史》說，對不服的國家會「以武懾之」，船隊也確實打過仗：它打敗了一個海盜首領，也俘虜了錫蘭的一位國王。古里和忽魯謨斯本來就是大貿易城市；滿剌加才剛建立不久，在那個世紀裡成長為重要的貿易樞紐（更早的海路見第九課）。

After the Yongle emperor died in 1424, the voyages were suspended; one last fleet sailed in 1431. Zheng He died in 1433, and the great fleets never sailed again. Chinese merchants and migrants, however, kept sailing to Southeast Asia, and Chinese communities grew there. About 65 years later, Portuguese ships reached India by sailing around Africa.

1424 年永樂皇帝死後，航行就停了下來；1431 年又出航最後一次。鄭和在 1433 年過世，大船隊從此沒有再出海。不過，中國的商人和移民繼續航向東南亞，那裡的華人社群越來越大。大約 65 年後，葡萄牙的船繞過非洲到了印度。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Name two inventions that were developed in China.

Paper, movable clay type (Bi Sheng), gunpowder (any two).

Where did Zheng He's fleets sail between 1405 and 1433?

Southeast Asia, India, the Persian Gulf, Arabia, and the coast of East Africa.

Why do historians disagree about the size of the treasure ships?

The biggest number was written down more than 300 years later, and it does not match most shipwrecks, though some scholars say a giant rudderpost points to large ships.

    How Big Were the Ships? 船有多大？

    The size of Zheng He's largest ships is one of the great debates of this story. Here the numbers from different sources are drawn to the same scale, so you can compare them. Read where each number comes from.
    鄭和最大的船有多大，是這段歷史裡最有名的爭論之一。這裡把不同資料的數字，用同樣的比例畫出來，讓你比一比。看看每一個數字是從哪裡來的。

    A number written down long after很久以後才寫下的數字A modern reading of that number現代對那個數字的解讀Something archaeologists found考古找到的東西Another ship, for comparison拿來比較的另一艘船

    1. Largest treasure ship, as the History of Ming records it《明史》記載的最大寶船44 zhang ≈ 447 ft (136 m)44 丈，約 447 英尺（136 公尺）

      The History of Ming, finished in 1739, more than 300 years after the voyages, says 62 great ships were 44 zhang long and 18 zhang wide. Sally Church gives this as 447 by 183 feet. Some scholars think the figure may come from a novel published in 1597.《明史》在 1739 年完成，比下西洋晚了三百多年。它說 62 艘大船「修四十四丈、廣十八丈」；學者丘絲麗（Sally Church）換算成 447 × 183 英尺。有些學者認為，這個數字可能來自 1597 年出版的一本小說。 明史 卷304; Church (2005)

    2. Treasure ship, as one modern article reads the records一篇現代文章對紀錄的解讀390–408 ft (119–124 m)390–408 英尺（119–124 公尺）

      An article in Education About Asia (2014) accepts ships of this size, pointing to a giant rudder found at the shipyard and to dry docks wide enough for them.《Education About Asia》2014 年的一篇文章接受這麼大的船，理由是船廠找到的巨大舵桿，以及寬到容得下它們的船塢。 Education About Asia (2014)

    3. Chinese ships of the 1300s and 1400s found by archaeologists考古找到的十四、十五世紀中國船usually not over 100 ft (30 m)通常不超過 100 英尺（30 公尺）

      National Geographic says most marine archaeological finds suggest ships of this time were usually no longer than this, and that the claims of a 1597 adventure tale sound exaggerated.國家地理說，大多數海底考古的發現顯示，這個時代的船通常不比這更長；1597 年一本冒險故事裡的說法聽起來太誇張。 National Geographic

    4. Rudderpost found at the Nanjing shipyard南京船廠找到的舵桿about 11 m (36 ft)大約 11 公尺（36 英尺）

      Found in 1957. Scholars disagree about what size of ship it fits: Education About Asia says it supports the records; National Geographic says it raises the possibility; Sally Church examined how it has been interpreted.1957 年出土。它配得上多大的船，學者看法不同：Education About Asia 說它支持記載；國家地理說它讓「那麼大」有可能；丘絲麗則檢討大家怎麼解讀它。 Education About Asia; National Geographic; Church (2005)

    5. Columbus's Santa María (1492)哥倫布的聖瑪利亞號（1492）85–117 ft (26–36 m)85–117 英尺（26–36 公尺）

      Even this ship's size is uncertain: Britannica says about 117 feet; the Education About Asia article says 85 feet.連這艘船有多大也不確定：Britannica 說大約 117 英尺；Education About Asia 那篇文章說 85 英尺。 Britannica, “Santa María”; Education About Asia

    No plan or picture of a treasure ship from the time of the voyages gives its size. That is why historians argue: they weigh records written later, shipwrecks, the rudderpost, and what wooden ships can do.下西洋那個時代，沒有一張寶船的圖或設計圖寫下它的大小。所以歷史學家才會爭論：他們要把後來寫的紀錄、沉船、舵桿，以及木船能做到什麼，放在一起衡量。

    The fleet's own inscription (1431)船隊自己的石碑（1431）

    Before the last voyage, Zheng He and his officers set up a stone at Changle in Fujian. It lists all seven voyages, the countries reached, and the gifts sent back, such as lions, zebras, and a giraffe.
    最後一次出航前，鄭和和部屬在福建長樂立了一塊石碑，列出七次航行、到過的國家，以及送回來的禮物，例如獅子、斑馬和長頸鹿。

    Its limits · 它的限制It was written by the fleet's leaders to praise the emperor and thank the sea goddess Tianfei. It tells the fighting in Ceylon and Sumatra only from the fleet's side.它是船隊的領導者為了頌揚皇帝、感謝海神天妃而寫的；錫蘭和蘇門答剌的戰事，只寫了船隊這一方的說法。

    Tianfei lingying zhi ji (1431), Wikisource ↗

    Ma Huan's book馬歡的書

    Ma Huan, an interpreter who had studied Arabic and Persian, sailed on three of the voyages and described the lands he visited in the Yingya shenglan. National Geographic calls him perhaps the most reliable of the three men who wrote about the voyages.
    通譯馬歡學過阿拉伯文和波斯文，參加了三次航行，把造訪過的地方寫成《瀛涯勝覽》。國家地理說，在寫下下西洋經過的三個人裡，他大概是最可靠的。

    Its limits · 它的限制He saw the world as a member of the fleet, and sometimes exaggerated; National Geographic notes that he described a giraffe's neck as more than 16 feet long.他是用船隊一員的眼光看世界，有時也會誇張；國家地理指出，他說長頸鹿的脖子有十六英尺長。

    National Geographic ↗

    The History of Ming《明史》

    The official history of the Ming gives the famous numbers: 27,800 men and 62 great ships 44 zhang long. It also says the emperor wanted to search for the emperor he had overthrown, and to show off China's wealth and power.
    明朝的正史寫下了有名的數字：二萬七千八百多人、六十二艘長四十四丈的大船。它也說，皇帝想追查被他推翻的前一位皇帝的下落，並且「耀兵異域，示中國富強」。

    Its limits · 它的限制It was finished in 1739, under the next dynasty, more than 300 years after the voyages. Some scholars think its ship size came from a novel of 1597.它在 1739 年、下一個朝代才完成，比下西洋晚了三百多年。有些學者認為，它的寶船尺寸來自 1597 年的一本小說。

    • Truly enormous真的非常巨大

      The History of Ming gives 44 zhang, about 447 feet. An article in Education About Asia (2014) accepts ships of about 390–408 feet, pointing to an 11-meter rudderpost found at the shipyard in 1957 and to dry docks wide enough for them.
      《明史》寫四十四丈，大約 447 英尺。《Education About Asia》2014 年的一篇文章接受大約 390–408 英尺的說法，理由是 1957 年在船廠找到的 11 公尺舵桿，以及寬到容得下它們的船塢。

      明史 卷304; Education About Asia (2014) ↗

    • Probably much smaller大概小得多

      National Geographic says the claim of a 1597 adventure tale sounds exaggerated, and that most finds suggest ships of the time were usually no longer than 100 feet. Sally Church tested the 447-foot figure against the earlier sources, shipwrecks, the rudderpost, and the wood such ships would need, and notes the suggestion that the number came from that novel.
      國家地理說，1597 年那本冒險故事的說法聽起來太誇張，大部分的發現顯示那個時代的船通常不超過 100 英尺。丘絲麗拿更早的資料、沉船、舵桿，以及造船要用的木材，來檢驗 447 英尺這個數字，並提到有人認為這個數字來自那本小說。

      National Geographic; Church, Monumenta Serica (2005) ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Both sides agree the fleets were very large for their time. The question is how large the biggest ship really was.兩邊都同意，以那個時代來說船隊非常龐大；爭的是最大的那一艘到底有多大。

    How big was the fleet?船隊有多大？

    • The fleet's own words船隊自己的說法

      The inscription of 1431, written by Zheng He and his officers, speaks of the voyages in general: “tens of thousands” of officers and soldiers on “more than a hundred great ships.”
      1431 年鄭和和部屬立的石碑，概括地說整個下西洋：「統率官校旗軍數萬人，乘巨舶百餘艘」。

      Tianfei lingying zhi ji (1431) ↗

    • Later counts後來的數字

      The History of Ming gives 27,800 men and 62 great ships for the first voyage; Britannica gives 62 ships. National Geographic gives 255 ships, 62 of them treasure ships, and Education About Asia gives 317 ships.
      《明史》說第一次有二萬七千八百多人、六十二艘大船；Britannica 寫 62 艘船。國家地理說 255 艘，其中 62 艘是寶船；Education About Asia 說 317 艘。

      明史; Britannica; National Geographic; Education About Asia ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Part of the difference is what is counted: only the great treasure ships, or every supply ship, horse ship, and warship too.差別有一部分在於算的是什麼：只算大寶船，還是連補給船、馬船、戰船都算進去。

    Why did the voyages happen, and why did they stop?為什麼要下西洋？又為什麼停了？

    • Power and legitimacy權力和正當性

      The History of Ming says the emperor wanted to find the emperor he had overthrown and to show off China's power. Education About Asia says most historians doubt the search story, and lists other motives: showing power, gaining influence and trade, finding allies, and winning foreign recognition for an emperor who had seized the throne.
      《明史》說，皇帝想找出被他推翻的前一位皇帝，並且炫耀中國的力量。Education About Asia 說，多數歷史學家不太相信「尋人」的說法，另外列出其他動機：展示武力、擴大影響和貿易、尋找盟友，以及讓外國承認這位奪位的皇帝。

      明史; Education About Asia (2014) ↗

    • What did they achieve?它們達成了什麼？

      Britannica says some historians see no achievement beyond flattering the emperor's vanity, while the voyages did extend China's political influence over maritime Asia for half a century. After 1424 the court suspended them, and after 1433 they ended. Education About Asia points to the cost and to objections from Confucian officials; National Geographic says the problem was political, not economic: concern with the northern frontier and suspicion from powerful officials.
      Britannica 說，有些歷史學家認為，除了滿足皇帝的虛榮以外沒有什麼成就；不過這些航行確實讓中國在海上亞洲的政治影響力維持了半個世紀。1424 年以後朝廷暫停了航行，1433 年以後就結束了。Education About Asia 指出花費太大，以及儒家官員的反對；國家地理則說問題不在經濟而在政治：朝廷把注意力轉向北方邊境，有權勢的官員也對航行有疑慮。

      Britannica, “Zheng He”; National Geographic ↗

    Where things stand · 目前的情況Britannica also notes that, unlike later European voyages, these did not lead to trading empires, though Chinese migration to Southeast Asia grew after them.Britannica 也指出，和後來歐洲人的航行不同，下西洋沒有建立起貿易帝國；不過在那之後，中國人移居東南亞的越來越多。

    Ask who wrote each source, and when.問一問：每一份資料是誰寫的？什麼時候寫的？

    這八句幫你描述誰發明或改良了什麼，以及一次航行去了哪裡。先聽、再跟著唸，然後用粗體的字自己造一句。

    Taiwan at That Time · 那時候的台灣

    Legends of a eunuch, and a visit by the Ming navy 三保太監的傳說，和明朝水師的一次來訪

    Taiwan is not among the places that the inscription of 1431 or Britannica lists for the voyages. Yet stories about Zheng He grew up here later.
    1431 年的石碑和 Britannica 列出的下西洋地點裡，沒有台灣。可是後來，台灣也長出了關於鄭和的故事。

    🏝️

    Separate what a source says happened from what it says people believed. Taiwan's oldest gazetteer marks some of these stories with the words “it is said.”
    要分清楚：資料說「發生了什麼」，和資料說「人們相信什麼」。台灣最早的方志，在其中一些故事前面寫著「相傳」。

    • 1685: a well and a ginger1685 年：一口井和一種薑

      Taiwan's first gazetteer, compiled in 1685, says that in the Xuande years (1426–35) the eunuch “Wang Sanbao” took water from a great well in Tainan, and that ginger he planted on a mountain could cure any illness. It begins the ginger story with “it is said.”
      1685 年編成的台灣第一部府志說，明宣德年間（1426–35），「太監王三保」曾到台南的大井取水；又說「相傳」三保太監在山上種的薑，「百病食之皆瘳」。

      蔣毓英《臺灣府志》卷10（1685） ↗

    • Why “Wang” Sanbao?為什麼是「王」三保？

      The History of Ming says Zheng He was the one known as the Sanbao eunuch, and his fellow envoy was Wang Jinghong. The gazetteer does not explain the “Wang.” One guess is that two names got mixed, but no source says so. These stories were written down more than 250 years after the voyages.
      《明史》說，鄭和就是「世所謂三保太監者」，和他一起出使的是王景弘。府志沒有解釋這個「王」字。一種猜測是兩個人的名字被弄混了，但沒有任何資料這樣說。這些故事是在下西洋兩百五十多年後才寫下來的。

      明史 卷304; 天妃靈應之記 ↗

    • 1603: the Ming navy at Tayouan1603 年：明朝水師到大員

      In the winter of 1602–03 a Ming general, General Shen of Wuyu (usually identified as Shen Yourong), sailed to Taiwan to fight pirates, and Chen Di went with him. His Dongfan ji is an early, detailed record of Taiwan's customs (Lesson 11). It also repeats an older story that early in the Yongle years a eunuch named Zheng sailed to instruct foreign peoples; like the gazetteer's stories, no record of the voyages confirms that he reached Taiwan.
      1602 年冬，「浯嶼沈將軍」（一般認為是沈有容）到台灣剿滅倭寇，陳第跟著去。他的《東番記》是一份早期、詳細的台灣風俗紀錄（第十一課）。它也轉述了一個更早的故事：永樂初年，「鄭內監航海諭諸夷」；和府志的故事一樣，下西洋的紀錄裡找不到他到過台灣的證據。

      陳第《東番記》（1603） ↗

    Stories Behind the Evidence · 證據背後的故事

    A needle that leans east, clay type, a goddess of the sea, and a qilin with long legs 偏東的針、膠泥活字、海上的女神，和長腿的麒麟

    Four short stories from the books and stones of this lesson.
    這一課的書和石碑裡的四個小故事。

    Late 11th century · Shen Kuo · 沈括

    A needle that leans a little east有點偏東的針

    Shen Kuo wrote that a needle rubbed on lodestone points south, “but always slightly east, not exactly south.” He compared floating it on water, balancing it on a fingernail or a bowl's rim, and hanging it from a thread, which he found best.
    沈括寫道，用磁石磨過的針會指南，「然常微偏東，不全南也」。他比較了把針浮在水上、放在指甲或碗邊上，以及用絲線吊起來，結論是「不若縷懸為最善」。

    1041–48 · Bi Sheng · 畢昇

    Type as thin as a coin's edge薄如錢唇的活字

    Shen Kuo also recorded that a commoner named Bi Sheng carved characters in clay, each one a separate piece “as thin as the edge of a coin,” and baked them hard. Britannica says the idea was not followed up in China, while Korea developed movable type further.
    沈括也記下，平民畢昇「用膠泥刻字，薄如錢唇，每字為一印，火燒令堅」。Britannica 說，這個方法在中國沒有繼續發展，朝鮮則把活字發展得更遠。

    1431 · Changle · 長樂

    A goddess for sailors水手的女神

    On their stone, Zheng He and his officers wrote that in storms “a divine lantern” appeared on the masts, and thanked Tianfei, the sea goddess known in Taiwan today as Mazu, for every safe return.
    鄭和和部屬在石碑上寫道，遇到風濤時，「有神燈燭於帆檣」；他們感謝天妃（今天台灣熟悉的媽祖）保佑每一次平安歸來。

    1417–19 · Aden · 阿丹

    A qilin called zulafa叫做祖剌法的麒麟

    The inscription lists the gifts of the fifth voyage: lions, leopards, and horses from Hormuz; a qilin, “called zulafa in the foreign tongue,” from Aden; zebras from Mogadishu; and ostriches from Brava.
    石碑列出第五次航行帶回的禮物：忽魯謨斯的獅子、金錢豹和大馬；阿丹的「麒麟，番名祖剌法」；木骨都束的花福祿（斑馬）；卜剌哇的駝雞（鴕鳥）。

    Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

    Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

    ✗ Myth · 迷思China invented everything first, and the rest of the world just copied.什麼都是中國最先發明的，別人只是照抄。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Japan printed charms before the Diamond Sutra; Korea developed bronze movable type; Britannica says sailors in China and Europe found the lodestone compass apparently independently, and that movable type does not seem to have reached Europe from China. Inventions often have more than one home.日本印咒文比《金剛經》還早；朝鮮發展了銅活字；Britannica 說，中國和歐洲的水手似乎各自發現了磁石指北，活字的知識似乎也沒有從中國傳到歐洲。很多發明不只有一個家鄉。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Zheng He discovered new lands.鄭和發現了新的土地。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Great ports such as Calicut and Hormuz were already busy. Britannica says Indian and Muslim visitors had long sailed to China, and Chinese seaborne trade had been growing for some 300 years. The fleet even brought interpreters who knew Arabic and Persian.古里、忽魯謨斯這些大港本來就很熱鬧。Britannica 說，印度人和穆斯林早就航行到中國，中國的海上貿易也已經成長了大約三百年。船隊甚至帶了懂阿拉伯文和波斯文的通譯。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思The fleets never fought anyone.船隊從來沒有打過仗。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Education About Asia says the fleet was mostly meant for peaceful diplomacy and trade, but the fleet's own inscription tells of defeating a pirate leader, capturing a king of Ceylon, and capturing a rival ruler in Sumatra. The fleet carried soldiers.Education About Asia 說船隊大體上是為了和平的外交和貿易，但船隊自己的石碑寫著：打敗海盜首領、俘虜錫蘭國王、在蘇門答剌活捉一位爭位的統治者。船隊是載著軍人的。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思After 1433 China turned its back on the sea.1433 年以後，中國就背對大海了。

    ✓ Fact · 事實The great state fleets stopped, but Britannica says Chinese emigration to Southeast Asia grew, along with tributary trade that lasted into the 19th century.國家派出的大船隊是停了，但 Britannica 說，移居東南亞的中國人越來越多，朝貢貿易也一直延續到十九世紀。

    Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

    Make a Floating Compass 做一個浮在水上的指南針

    You need · 準備材料

    • A sewing needle, a magnet, a small piece of cork or leaf, and a bowl of water一根縫衣針、一塊磁鐵、一小片軟木或葉子、一碗水

    Steps · 步驟

    1. Stroke the needle with one end of the magnet about 30 times, always in the same direction.用磁鐵的一端，朝同一個方向把針摩擦大約三十次。
    2. Lay the needle on the cork and float it in the bowl. Wait until it stops turning.把針放在軟木上，浮在碗裡，等它停下來。
    3. Compare with a phone compass. Does it point north–south? Is it a little off?和手機的指南針比一比：它指著南北嗎？是不是有一點偏？
    4. Read Shen Kuo's words aloud and say in English: “The needle points …”讀一讀沈括的話，再用英文說：「The needle points …」

    Shen Kuo noticed his needle pointed slightly east of south. A magnetic needle does not point exactly to true north; how far off it is depends on where and when you are.
    沈括發現他的針「常微偏東」。磁針不會正好指著真正的北方；偏多少，要看你在哪裡、在什麼時候。

    Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

    Who Says So? 這是誰說的？

    You need · 準備材料

    • The ship-size chart and the three “How Do We Know?” cards in this lesson這一課的船長度圖，和「我們怎麼知道？」的三張卡

    Steps · 步驟

    1. In groups, take one number each: 447 feet, 390–408 feet, 100 feet, the 11-meter rudderpost.分組，每組拿一個數字：447 英尺、390–408 英尺、100 英尺、11 公尺的舵桿。
    2. Find out who gave the number, when, and what evidence they used.查出這個數字是誰說的、什麼時候說的、用了什麼證據。
    3. Measure the length on the school field with a tape or by counting steps.在操場上用捲尺或數步伐，量出那個長度。
    4. Discuss in English: “Historians disagree about …” and “This number comes from …”用英文討論：「Historians disagree about …」和「This number comes from …」

    Let students stand at the end of each length. The point is not to decide the answer but to see why the evidence matters.
    讓學生站在每一個長度的終點。重點不是決定答案，而是看出證據為什麼重要。
