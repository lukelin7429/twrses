Why Does Clerical Script Have Silkworm Heads and Swallow Tails?隸書：為什麼是蠶頭燕尾？

Clerical script made writing quicker: curves became straight lines, tall shapes became flat, and one stroke in each character got a head like a silkworm's and a tail like a swallow's. Big idea · 一句話看懂

Clerical script is a simpler form of seal script, made to be quick to write. It began in the Qin dynasty and was the common script of the Han dynasty. Its shapes are flat and wide, its curves have become straight lines and turns, and its long horizontal stroke begins like a silkworm's head and ends like a swallow's tail.
隸書是篆書的簡化體，為了寫得快而出現。它創始於秦、通行於漢。字形扁而寬，彎曲的線變成直線和轉折；長長的橫畫，起筆像蠶頭、收筆像燕尾。

Unit 6: Why Does Clerical Script Have Silkworm Heads and Swallow Tails?

Small seal script looks neat, with smooth, even lines, but it takes time to write. Xu Shen, a scholar of the Han dynasty, wrote that in the Qin dynasty the government offices and prisons had so much work that a new kind of writing appeared to make things quick and simple. That writing was clerical script.

小篆看起來很整齊，線條圓滑、粗細一樣，可是寫起來很花時間。東漢學者許慎說，秦代官府和監獄的事務太多了，於是出現一種新的字體，讓書寫又快又簡單——那就是隸書。

Clerical script changed the shapes of characters. Round turns became square corners. Long curves became straight lines. Characters that stood tall in seal script became flat and wide. This change is called libian (隸變), and after it, characters began to look like the ones we write today.

隸書改變了字的樣子：圓圓的轉彎變成方方的轉角，長長的彎線變成直線，小篆裡高高的字形變得又扁又寬。這個變化叫做「隸變」；隸變之後，漢字開始像我們今天寫的樣子。

The most famous clerical stroke is the long horizontal line. It starts with a silkworm head: the brush touches down moving backward, then turns and travels to the right, so the start looks round. It ends with a swallow tail: the brush presses down, then lifts as it flicks up to the right.

隸書最有名的一筆是長長的橫畫。起筆是「蠶頭」：筆尖先逆著入紙，再回過頭往右走，所以起筆是圓的。收筆是「燕尾」：毛筆先往下按，再一邊提起、一邊往右上挑出去。

A clerical character usually has only one swallow tail. Look at 三: only the long bottom line has a tail, and the two lines above end round. Later calligraphers summed up this habit in a saying: wild geese do not fly in pairs, and a silkworm head is not set down twice.

一個隸書字通常只有一個燕尾。看看「三」：只有最下面那條長橫有燕尾，上面兩條的收筆都是圓的。後來的書法家把這個習慣說成一句口訣：「雁不雙飛，蠶不二設」（燕尾也叫雁尾）。

Clerical script was the common script of the Han dynasty. People wrote it with a brush on narrow slips of bamboo and wood, and tied the slips together with cords to make a book. More than 10,000 Han dynasty slips were found near Juyan, on the northwest frontier, in 1930 and 1931. Today they are kept at Academia Sinica in Taipei.

隸書是漢代通行的字體。人們用毛筆把字寫在窄窄的竹片和木片上，再用繩子編成一冊。1930 到 1931 年，西北邊疆的居延附近出土了一萬多枚漢代的簡，現在收藏在台北的中央研究院。

In this lesson, replay the three parts of the long horizontal stroke in 3D, watch seal script change into clerical script, and unroll a scroll of bamboo slips. Then drag the line between the two scripts, find the swallow tails, and trace the strokes yourself.

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to Xu Shen, why did clerical script appear in the Qin dynasty?

The government offices and prisons had so much work that people needed a way to write that was quick and simple.

How does the brush make a silkworm head and a swallow tail?

For the head, the brush touches down moving backward, then turns and goes right. For the tail, it presses down, then lifts as it flicks up to the right.

In the clerical character 三, which line has the swallow tail?

Only the long bottom line. The two lines above end round.

Stories of Clerical Script · 隸書的故事

Busy offices, slips from a tomb, slips from the frontier, and a famous stone 忙碌的官府、秦墓裡的竹簡、邊疆的木簡，和一塊有名的石碑

Four short stories about where clerical script came from and where we can still see it.
關於隸書從哪裡來、今天在哪裡還看得到的四個小故事。

Why It Began · 由來

Too much work, not enough time事情太多，來不及寫

Xu Shen wrote that in the Qin dynasty the offices and prisons had so much work that clerical script appeared, to make writing quick and simple. A later history, the Book of Jin, says that seal script was hard to finish, so lowly clerks were told to help with the writing, and the script was named after them. It adds that clerical script is the quick form of seal script.
許慎說，秦代「官獄職務繁，初有隸書，以趣約易」：官府和監獄的事情太多，才有了寫起來又快又簡單的隸書。《晉書》說，篆字難成，於是「令隸人佐書，曰隸字」——讓地位低的小吏幫忙抄寫，所以叫隸書；又說「隸書者，篆之捷也」，隸書就是寫得快的篆書。

Older Than the Story · 更早的隸書

Slips from a Qin tomb秦墓裡的竹簡

A famous story says a prisoner named Cheng Miao invented clerical script for the First Emperor. But even the old histories begin that story with “some say.” In 1975, 1,155 bamboo slips were found in a Qin tomb at Shuihudi in Yunmeng, Hubei. They were written by Qin people around the time China was united, in an early clerical script that still looks a little like seal script. Clerical script grew little by little.
有個有名的故事說，囚犯程邈為秦始皇創造了隸書；不過連古代的史書講這個故事時，都先說「或曰」（有人說）。1975 年，湖北雲夢睡虎地的秦墓出土了 1,155 枚竹簡，是秦統一六國前後秦人的墨跡，用早期的隸書（秦隸）書寫，結構還帶著篆書的樣子。可見隸書是一點一點慢慢形成的。

Taiwan · 台灣

Slips from the frontier, kept in Taipei台北的居延漢簡

In 1930 and 1931, an expedition found more than 10,000 Han dynasty slips near Juyan, on the northwest frontier: soldiers' reports, lists, and writing practice, most of them in clerical script. During the war years they were moved to Hong Kong and then to the Library of Congress in the United States, and in November 1965 they arrived in Taiwan. Academia Sinica's museum in Taipei shows more than 200 of them, including a national treasure: a book of 77 wooden slips from the years 93 to 95, still tied together with its cords.
1930 到 1931 年，西北科學考察團在西北邊疆的居延附近發現一萬多枚漢代的簡：士兵的報告、名冊和練字的簡，大量使用隸書。戰爭期間，這批簡先運到香港，再運到美國國會圖書館保存，1965 年 11 月運抵台灣。位在台北的中央研究院歷史文物陳列館展出其中兩百多支，包括一件國寶：東漢永元五年到七年（西元 93–95 年）的一份簡冊，77 支木簡還用編繩連在一起。

A Model for Learners · 範本

The Cao Quan Stele曹全碑

The Cao Quan Stele is a stone tablet carved in clerical script in the year 185, in the Eastern Han dynasty. It was dug up many centuries later, in the Wanli years of the Ming dynasty, and now stands in the Forest of Steles in Xi'an. The Ministry of Education's dictionary calls its writing graceful and strong, and many people who learn clerical script use it as their model. The National Palace Museum in Taipei has an ink rubbing of it.
曹全碑是東漢中平二年（西元 185 年）的隸書碑刻，到明朝萬曆年間才出土，現在存放在西安碑林。教育部辭典說它「書法秀麗而有骨力，學習隸書者，多用為範本」。台北的國立故宮博物院藏有它的墨拓本。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思Clerical script is older than seal script.隸書比篆書古老。

✓ Fact · 事實It is the other way around. Clerical script is a simpler form of seal script. It began in the Qin dynasty and was common in the Han and Wei dynasties.正好相反。隸書是篆書的簡化體，創始於秦，通行於漢、魏。

✗ Myth · 迷思Every horizontal line in clerical script has a swallow tail.隸書的每一條橫線都有燕尾。

✓ Fact · 事實Usually only one stroke in a character has it. In 三, the two short lines end round, and only the bottom line flicks up.通常一個字只有一筆有燕尾。「三」上面兩條短橫收筆是圓的，只有最下面那條往上挑。

✗ Myth · 迷思To make a silkworm head, you draw a little circle.蠶頭是畫一個小圓圈畫出來的。

✓ Fact · 事實The brush does not draw a circle. It touches down moving backward, then turns and goes right. The turn is hidden inside the stroke, and that makes the start look round.毛筆不是畫圓圈，而是先逆著入紙、再回頭往右走。回鋒藏在筆畫裡面，起筆才看起來圓圓的。

✗ Myth · 迷思One man invented clerical script in prison.隸書是一個人在監獄裡發明的。

✓ Fact · 事實That is the old story about Cheng Miao, and even old histories tell it with “some say.” Bamboo slips found at Shuihudi in 1975 show an early clerical script already in use around the time China was united. It grew over many years, in many hands.那是關於程邈的老故事，連古代史書都用「或曰」來講。1975 年睡虎地出土的竹簡顯示，秦統一前後就有人用早期的隸書寫字了。隸書是很多人、很多年慢慢寫出來的。

In the Classroom · 教室提醒

Before you pick up a brush 拿起毛筆之前

  • Ink stains clothes and is very hard to wash out. Wear old clothes or an apron, and roll up your sleeves.墨沾到衣服很難洗掉：穿舊衣服或圍裙，袖子捲起來。
  • Cover the desk with old newspaper, and keep the ink in a heavy, steady dish so it will not tip over.桌上先鋪舊報紙；墨要裝在重一點、放得穩的碟子裡，免得打翻。
  • Press gently. Crushing the brush against the paper squashes the hairs and can split or break them.輕輕地按就好：把筆用力壓在紙上，筆毛會擠扁，也可能分岔或折斷。
  • Do not put the brush, the ink, or the ink stick in your mouth, and do not lick the brush tip to shape it.毛筆、墨汁、墨條都不要放進嘴裡，也不要用嘴巴舔筆尖。
  • After writing, wash the brush in clean water until the water runs clear, gently shape it into a point, and hang it tip-down or lay it flat to dry. Wash your hands, too.寫完後用清水洗筆，洗到水變清為止；輕輕把筆毛理成尖形，筆尖朝下掛起來或平放晾乾。也要洗手。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

Heads and Tails with Water 清水練蠶頭燕尾

You need · 準備材料

  • A brush and a cup of clean water for each student每人一枝毛筆、一杯清水
  • Old newspaper, or a small blackboard舊報紙，或一塊小黑板

Steps · 步驟

  1. Say the five moves out loud: left, turn, go, press, flick.先大聲唸五個動作：往左、回頭、走、按、挑。
  2. Write a long horizontal line with water, saying each move as you do it.用清水寫一條長橫，邊寫邊唸每一個動作。
  3. Look quickly before the water dries: is the head round? Does the tail point up to the right?趁水還沒乾趕快看：頭是圓的嗎？尾巴有沒有往右上翹？
  4. Now write 三. Remember: only the bottom line gets a tail.接著寫「三」。記得：只有最下面那一橫有燕尾。

Water costs nothing and leaves no stain, so students can try the same stroke twenty times.
清水不用錢、也不會弄髒，同一筆可以練二十次。

Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

Make a Scroll of Slips 做一卷竹簡

You need · 準備材料

  • Eight to ten craft sticks, or strips of thick paper, for each student每人八到十根冰棒棍，或一樣大小的厚紙條
  • Two pieces of string, each about as long as your arm兩條繩子，每條大約一隻手臂長
  • A marker or a small brush一枝麥克筆或小毛筆

Steps · 步驟

  1. Lay the sticks side by side. Tie them together with the two strings, one near the top and one near the bottom, weaving over and under.把冰棒棍一根根並排，用兩條繩子一上一下穿過去綁好：一條靠近上面，一條靠近下面。
  2. Write one character on each stick from top to bottom, starting with the stick on the right.從最右邊那一根開始，在每一根上由上往下寫字。
  3. Roll up your scroll, then unroll it and read it to a classmate.把竹簡捲起來，再攤開唸給同學聽。
  4. Look at the character 冊. Can you see the slips and the two cords?看看「冊」這個字：看得出竹片和兩道繩子嗎？

Writing from the right-hand stick first follows the old way of reading: top to bottom, right to left.
從最右邊那一根寫起，是照古書的讀法：由上到下、由右到左。
