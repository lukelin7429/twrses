U.S. Government · Unit 1 · Lesson 3 · 美國政治 單元一 第三課

Why Does Every State Have Its Own Laws?為什麼每一州的法律不一樣？

Choose a power, such as coining money, running schools, or collecting taxes, and see which layer of a 3D map lights up: the national government, the states, or both. Then compare all fifty states on one question at a time, read Federalist No. 45 and McCulloch v. Maryland, and weigh local choice against national standards.
選一項權力——鑄幣、辦學校、徵稅——看 3D 地圖上亮起來的是哪一層：全國政府、各州，還是兩層都亮。接著一次一個題目比較五十州，讀《聯邦論》第四十五篇與 McCulloch v. Maryland，再想想該由地方決定，還是該有全國一致的標準。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

Federalism divides power between two levels of government. Some powers belong to the national government, some remain with the states, and some are shared. That is why one question can have fifty different answers across the country, and why, when the two levels collide, the Constitution's Supremacy Clause decides which law prevails.
聯邦制把權力分給兩層政府：有些權力屬於全國政府，有些留給各州，有些兩邊共有。所以同一個問題，在全國可以有五十種不同的答案；而兩層衝突時，由憲法的最高條款決定哪一邊的法律優先。

Who decides? · 這件事誰管？

National government · 全國政府

Coining money鑄造貨幣

Only the national government issues money. The Constitution gives the power to Congress and, in a separate clause, forbids the states to coin money. That is why a dollar is the same in every state.只有全國政府發行貨幣。憲法把這項權力交給國會，又在另一條明文禁止各州鑄幣。所以一塊錢在每一州都一樣。

No State shall enter into any Treaty, Alliance, or Confederation; grant Letters of Marque and Reprisal; coin Money; …Article I, Section 10

National government · 全國政府

Declaring war and raising armies宣戰與建軍

Defense belongs to the national government. Congress declares war and raises the army and navy, and the states may not keep troops or ships of war in peacetime without Congress's consent.國防屬於全國政府。宣戰、招募陸海軍都由國會負責；平時各州未經國會同意，不得維持軍隊或戰艦。

To declare War, grant Letters of Marque and Reprisal, and make Rules concerning Captures on Land and Water;Article I, Section 8

National government · 全國政府

Making treaties締結條約

The President makes treaties with the consent of two thirds of the senators present. The states are forbidden to enter into “any Treaty, Alliance, or Confederation.”總統經出席參議員三分之二同意締結條約；各州則被禁止締結「任何條約、同盟或邦聯」。

No State shall enter into any Treaty, Alliance, or Confederation; …Article I, Section 10

National government · 全國政府

Setting the rules for becoming a citizen訂定入籍的規則

The rules for becoming a citizen are national and the same everywhere. A person naturalized in one state is a citizen of the United States and of whatever state he or she lives in.入籍的規則是全國性的，各地都一樣。在任何一州歸化的人，都是美國公民，也是他所居住那一州的公民。

To establish an uniform Rule of Naturalization, …Article I, Section 8

National government · 全國政府

Post offices and post roads郵局與郵路

The Constitution gives Congress the power to establish post offices. A letter mailed in one state carries the same national stamp in every other.憲法把設立郵局的權力交給國會。在任何一州寄出的信，貼的都是同一種全國通用的郵票。

To establish Post Offices and post Roads;Article I, Section 8

The states · 各州

Running public schools辦公立學校

Education is not among the powers the Constitution gives the national government, so it falls under the powers “reserved to the States.” States, and the school districts they create, set graduation requirements and run the schools.教育不在憲法交給全國政府的權力之列，所以屬於「保留給各州」的權力。畢業的條件由各州——以及各州設立的學區——決定，學校也由他們辦。

The powers not delegated to the United States by the Constitution, nor prohibited by it to the States, are reserved to the States respectively, or to the people.Tenth Amendment

The states · 各州

Licenses for driving and for marriage駕照與結婚登記

Driver's licenses are issued by each state's motor vehicle agency, and marriage licenses by the states as well, under state law. That is why the requirements and the paperwork are not the same everywhere.駕照由各州的監理機關核發，結婚證書也由各州依州法核發。所以各地的條件與手續並不相同。

Reserved powers (Tenth Amendment) · 保留給各州的權力（第十條修正案）

The states · 各州

Defining most crimes規定大部分的罪行

Most criminal cases, like most family-law cases, are heard in state courts under state law. Federal criminal law covers matters tied to the national government's powers, such as counterfeiting money.大部分的刑事案件——和大部分的家事案件一樣——是在州法院依州法審理的。聯邦刑法管的是和全國政府權力有關的事，例如偽造貨幣。

Reserved powers (Tenth Amendment) · 保留給各州的權力（第十條修正案）

Both · 兩層都管

Collecting taxes徵稅

Both levels tax the same people at the same time. Congress may “lay and collect Taxes,” and the states keep their own power to tax, apart from a few limits, such as the rule that a state may not tax imports or exports without Congress's consent.兩層政府同時向同一批人課稅。國會可以「課徵各種稅捐」，各州也保有自己的徵稅權，只受少數限制——例如未經國會同意，州不得對進出口貨物課稅。

The Congress shall have Power To lay and collect Taxes, Duties, Imposts and Excises, …Article I, Section 8

Both · 兩層都管

Running courts設立法院

There are two court systems side by side: federal courts under Article III, and the courts of each state under its own constitution. State courts hear most criminal, contract, and family-law cases. Lesson 14 follows a case up both ladders.有兩套法院並排存在：依第三條設立的聯邦法院，以及各州依自己的憲法設立的法院。大部分的刑事、契約與家事案件由州法院審理。第十四課會跟著一個案子，沿著兩道階梯往上走。

The judicial Power of the United States, shall be vested in one supreme Court, and in such inferior Courts as the Congress may from time to time ordain and establish.Article III, Section 1

Both · 兩層都管

Running elections for Congress辦國會議員選舉

The states set the times, places, and manner of elections for Congress, and Congress may change those rules by law. In practice, the states organize the voting.國會議員選舉的時間、地點與方式由各州規定，國會則可以用法律加以修改。實際上，投票是由各州在辦理。

The Times, Places and Manner of holding Elections for Senators and Representatives, shall be prescribed in each State by the Legislature thereof; but the Congress may at any time by Law make or alter such Regulations, …Article I, Section 4

Unit 3: Why Does Every State Have Its Own Laws?

An American lives under two governments at the same time. One is the national government in Washington, usually called the federal government. The other is the government of the state where the person lives, with its own constitution, legislature, governor, and courts. Dividing power between two levels in this way is called federalism. It explains why the rate of sales tax, the requirements for a driver's license, and the punishment for many crimes change at a state line, while the dollar in a wallet, the stamp on a letter, and the rules for becoming a citizen are the same everywhere. In Madison's words, the two levels are “different agents and trustees of the people.”

一個美國人同時生活在兩個政府之下。一個是在華盛頓的全國政府，通常叫做聯邦政府（federal government）；另一個是他居住那一州的政府，有自己的憲法、議會、州長和法院。像這樣把權力分給兩層政府，叫做聯邦制（federalism）。它說明了為什麼銷售稅的稅率、考駕照的條件、許多罪行的刑罰，一跨過州界就不一樣；而皮夾裡的一塊錢、信封上的郵票、入籍的規則，卻到處都相同。用麥迪遜的話說，兩層政府是「人民不同的代理人與受託人」。

State governments came first. When the Constitution was written, the thirteen states already had governments of their own, and few Americans wanted to see them swallowed by a distant capital. The Constitution therefore gave the national government a list of specific powers and left the rest where it was. James Madison put it plainly in The Federalist No. 45. The powers given to the national government, he wrote, “are few and defined,” while those that remain with the states “are numerous and indefinite.” The national government would deal mainly with matters such as war, peace, and foreign commerce, and the states with the ordinary affairs of daily life.

州政府比聯邦政府先存在。寫憲法的時候，十三個州早已有了自己的政府，很少美國人願意看到它們被一個遙遠的首都吞掉。因此，憲法給全國政府一張列明的權力清單，其餘的權力則留在原處。麥迪遜在《聯邦論》第四十五篇講得很清楚：交給全國政府的權力「少而明確」，留給各州的則「多而不定」。全國政府主要處理戰爭、和平、對外貿易這一類的事，各州處理的是日常生活的一般事務。

Powers under the Constitution fall into a few groups. Enumerated powers are the ones listed for Congress, such as coining money, declaring war, and running the post office. Reserved powers are the ones left to the states by the Tenth Amendment, which says that powers not delegated to the United States “are reserved to the States respectively, or to the people.” Education, family law, and most criminal law belong here. Concurrent powers, such as taxing, borrowing, and running courts, are held by both levels at once. Finally, some powers are denied to the states outright. No state may coin money or make a treaty, and no state may tax imports without the consent of Congress.

憲法之下的權力可以分成幾類。列舉的權力（enumerated powers）是明列給國會的，例如鑄幣、宣戰、經營郵政。保留的權力（reserved powers）是第十條修正案留給各州的——條文說，沒有授予合眾國的權力，「保留給各州，或保留給人民」；教育、家庭法和大部分的刑法屬於這一類。共有的權力（concurrent powers），例如徵稅、舉債、設立法院，則由兩層政府同時擁有。最後，有些權力是明文禁止各州行使的：任何一州都不得鑄幣、不得締結條約；未經國會同意，也不得對進口貨物課稅。

The boundary between the levels has never stayed still. In 1818 Maryland passed a law taxing a branch of the Bank of the United States, which Congress had created even though the Constitution says nothing about banks; the dispute reached the Supreme Court in 1819. In McCulloch v. Maryland, Chief Justice John Marshall upheld the bank under the clause that lets Congress make all laws “necessary and proper” for carrying out its listed powers, and he held that a state could not tax it, since “the power to tax involves the power to destroy.” Maryland had argued for reading the national government's powers narrowly. The Court read them to include the means of carrying them out, and held that a state may not obstruct a valid federal law.

兩層之間的界線從來沒有停在原地。1818 年，馬里蘭州通過法律，對美國銀行設在當地的分行課稅——這家銀行是國會設立的，雖然憲法對銀行一個字也沒提；這場爭議在 1819 年打到最高法院。在 McCulloch v. Maryland 案中，首席大法官馬歇爾援引容許國會制定一切「必要且適當」法律以執行其列舉權力的條款，判定這家銀行合憲；他也判定州不得對它課稅，因為「課稅的權力包含毀滅的權力」。馬里蘭主張對全國政府的權力作狹義的解讀；最高法院則把這些權力解讀為包括執行它們的手段，並判定州不得阻撓有效的聯邦法律。

Two other tools have widened the national government's reach. The first is the Commerce Clause, which lets Congress regulate commerce “among the several States.” In Gibbons v. Ogden in 1824, the Supreme Court held that commerce includes navigation, so that steamboats licensed by Congress could not be shut out by a monopoly that New York had granted. Over time Congress has relied on that clause for a growing share of national laws, and how far it reaches is still argued. The second tool is money. Congress can attach conditions to the funds it sends the states. In 1984 Congress passed a law that withheld part of a state's federal highway funds unless it made twenty-one the minimum age for buying alcohol, and by 1988 every state had done so.

另外有兩項工具擴大了全國政府的範圍。第一項是商業條款（Commerce Clause），讓國會管理「各州之間的貿易」。1824 年的 Gibbons v. Ogden 案，最高法院判定「貿易」包括航運，所以領有國會執照的汽船，不能被紐約州授予的獨占權擋在門外。隨著時間過去，國會越來越常援引這項條款來制定全國性的法律，而它能延伸到多遠，至今仍有爭論。第二項工具是錢：國會可以在撥給各州的款項上附加條件。1984 年，國會通過一項法律：州若不把購買酒類的最低年齡訂為二十一歲，就扣下它一部分的聯邦公路經費；到 1988 年，每一州都照做了。

Federalism has been used for good and ill. Before the Civil War, claims of “states' rights” were used to defend slavery, and later to defend racial segregation. Yet national power was also turned to slavery's ends: the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850 required the return of people who had escaped, and some Northern states passed laws to resist it. After the war, the Fourteenth Amendment forbade any state to deny a person “the equal protection of the laws.” In 1957, when the governor of Arkansas used the state's National Guard to keep Black students out of Central High School in Little Rock, President Eisenhower sent federal troops to enforce desegregation.

聯邦制被用來做過好事，也做過壞事。內戰之前，「州權」（states' rights）的主張被用來為奴隸制辯護，後來又被用來為種族隔離辯護。可是全國政府的權力也曾被用來替奴隸制服務：1850 年的《逃亡奴隸法》要求交還逃走的人，而北方有些州通過法律加以抵制。內戰之後，第十四條修正案禁止任何一州拒絕給予任何人「法律的平等保護」。1957 年，阿肯色州州長動用州的國民警衛隊（National Guard），阻止黑人學生進入小岩城的中央中學；艾森豪總統派出聯邦部隊，強制執行廢除種族隔離。

The same division also lets states try ideas that the nation has not adopted. Justice Louis Brandeis wrote in 1932 that “a single courageous state may, if its citizens choose, serve as a laboratory; and try novel social and economic experiments without risk to the rest of the country.” History offers examples. Nine western states had adopted woman suffrage by 1912, years before the Nineteenth Amendment secured the vote for women across the country in 1920. Nebraska has run its legislature with a single chamber since 1937, while every other state has two. An idea that works in one state can be copied by others, and one that fails stays contained.

同樣的分權，也讓各州可以嘗試全國還沒採用的做法。布蘭戴斯大法官在 1932 年寫道：「一個勇敢的州，只要它的公民願意，就可以充當一間實驗室，嘗試新的社會與經濟實驗，而不會危及國家的其他部分。」歷史上有例子：到 1912 年，西部已經有九個州通過了婦女投票權，比 1920 年第十九條修正案替全國的女性確保投票權早了好幾年。內布拉斯加從 1937 年起，州議會只有一院，其他每一州都是兩院。一州行得通的做法，別州可以照著學；行不通的，也只限於那一州。

The result is a country in which the answer to many questions depends on where you live. That variety is one of the purposes of the system, and it is also one of its costs. People who move from one state to another must learn new rules, businesses must follow fifty sets of laws, and a right may seem broader in one state than in the next. Americans have argued since 1787 about which matters should be settled close to home and which deserve one national standard, and the line has moved many times. The map in this lesson shows what that division looks like on a handful of questions that are not matters of party dispute.

結果是：一個國家裡，很多問題的答案取決於你住在哪裡。這種多樣性是這套制度的用意之一，也是它的代價之一。從一州搬到另一州的人得重新學一套規則，企業得遵守五十套法律，同一種權利在這一州和鄰州看起來也可能寬窄不同。從 1787 年起，美國人就一直在爭論哪些事該在離家近的地方決定、哪些事該有全國一致的標準，這條線也移動過很多次。這一課的地圖，用幾個不屬於政黨爭議的題目，讓你看看這種分權是什麼樣子。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Explain the difference between enumerated, reserved, and concurrent powers, and give one example of each.

Enumerated powers are the ones the Constitution lists for Congress, such as coining money. Reserved powers are left to the states by the Tenth Amendment, such as running public schools. Concurrent powers belong to both levels at the same time, such as collecting taxes.

In McCulloch v. Maryland, what two questions did the Court answer, and why does each matter for the balance between the national government and the states?

First, could Congress create a bank when the Constitution does not mention one? Yes, because the Necessary and Proper Clause lets Congress choose the means of carrying out its listed powers. This allowed national power to grow beyond the bare list. Second, could Maryland tax the bank? No, because a state may not obstruct a valid federal law, and “the power to tax involves the power to destroy.” This confirmed that valid federal law is supreme over state law.

The reading says that federalism “has been used for good and ill.” Use two examples from the reading, one for each, and explain what they suggest about whether either level of government is automatically the protector of rights.

Sample answer: “States' rights” arguments were used to defend slavery and segregation, but the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850 used national power on slavery's side, while some Northern states resisted it. In 1957 federal troops enforced desegregation against a state governor. The examples suggest that neither level is automatically the protector of rights; it depends on what each level does with its power.

The Design Debate · 制度的爭論

Close to home, or one national standard? 離家近的地方決定，還是全國一個標準？

Every federal system has to decide which matters are settled locally and which nationally. Americans have argued about the line since 1787, and it has moved many times. This card states the strongest case for each direction and does not choose between them. It is about the design, not about any particular issue.
每一個聯邦制度都得決定：哪些事在地方決定，哪些事在全國決定。從 1787 年起美國人就在爭論這條線，它也移動過很多次。這張卡把兩個方向各用最強的版本寫出來，不替它們分高下；談的是制度的設計，不是任何一個具體的議題。

When people disagree about how a matter should be handled, should each state be free to decide it for itself, or should there be one rule for the whole country?當人們對一件事該怎麼處理意見不同時，應該讓每一州自己決定，還是應該全國用同一套規則？

One direction · 一個方向

Decide it close to home在離家近的地方決定

  • People differ from place to place in their circumstances and their values. Fifty decisions can fit fifty states, while one national rule will fit some places badly, and those who dislike a state's choice can move to another.各地的人處境不同、價值觀也不同。五十個決定可以配合五十個州；一條全國的規則，總會讓某些地方很不合身。不喜歡某一州做法的人，也可以搬到別州去。
  • A state government is closer to the people it governs. Citizens can see it, reach it, and change it more easily than a government far away, so decisions made there are easier to hold to account.州政府離它所治理的人比較近。比起一個遙遠的政府，公民比較看得見它、找得到它，也比較容易改變它，所以在那裡作成的決定比較容易被問責。
  • States can experiment. A policy tried in one state shows what works before the whole country commits to it, and a mistake made in one state does not become a mistake everywhere.各州可以做實驗。一項政策先在一州試行，可以在全國投入之前看出它行不行得通；一州犯的錯，也不會變成全國都犯的錯。
It is one of the happy incidents of the federal system that a single courageous state may, if its citizens choose, serve as a laboratory; and try novel social and economic experiments without risk to the rest of the country.Justice Louis Brandeis, dissenting in New State Ice Co. v. Liebmann, 285 U.S. 262 (1932)聯邦制度有一個可喜的好處：一個勇敢的州，只要它的公民願意，就可以充當一間實驗室，嘗試新的社會與經濟實驗，而不會危及國家的其他部分。——布蘭戴斯大法官，1932 年（不同意見書）

Another direction · 另一個方向

Set one national standard訂一個全國的標準

  • Some things should not depend on where a person happens to live. A citizen's basic rights, and the rules of a market that crosses every state line, call for one standard, applied the same way everywhere.有些事不應該取決於一個人剛好住在哪裡。公民的基本權利，以及一個跨越所有州界的市場的規則，需要一個標準，在每個地方用同樣的方式適用。
  • A smaller community can make it easier for a local majority to override the rights of a local minority. A national majority is broader and more varied, so it is less likely to share one narrow aim.一個比較小的社群，比較容易讓當地的多數壓過當地少數的權利。全國性的多數範圍更廣、組成更多元，比較不會共有某一個狹隘的目標。
  • Fifty sets of rules impose real costs. People who move must start over, businesses must comply many times, and states may compete with each other in ways that leave everyone worse off.五十套規則會帶來實實在在的成本：搬家的人得從頭來過，企業得重複遵守很多次，各州之間的競爭也可能讓大家的處境都變差。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思The states are local branch offices of the government in Washington.各州是華盛頓那個政府在地方的分支機構。

✓ Fact · 事實Each state has its own constitution, elects its own officials, and holds powers that do not come from Congress.每一州都有自己的憲法、自己選出的官員，以及不是來自國會的權力。

✗ Myth · 迷思Whenever federal and state law conflict, the federal law always wins.聯邦法律和州法律衝突時，一定是聯邦法律贏。

✓ Fact · 事實Only a valid federal law wins. The Supremacy Clause covers laws made “in Pursuance” of the Constitution, so a federal law that goes beyond the national government's powers does not displace state law.只有有效的聯邦法律才會贏。最高條款涵蓋的是「依本憲法」制定的法律；超出全國政府權力範圍的聯邦法律，不能取代州法律。

✗ Myth · 迷思The Constitution created the states.各州是憲法創造出來的。

✓ Fact · 事實The thirteen original states existed before the Constitution and ratified it. The other thirty-seven were admitted later by Congress under Article IV.最初的十三州在憲法之前就存在，憲法還是由它們批准的。其餘三十七州是後來由國會依第四條接納加入的。

✗ Myth · 迷思Every state government is built like the federal one.每一州的政府都和聯邦政府一個樣。

✓ Fact · 事實Most are similar, with a governor, two legislative chambers, and courts, but not all. Nebraska's legislature has had only one chamber since 1937, and state constitutions differ widely in length and detail.大多數很像——有州長、兩院制的議會和法院——但不是全部。內布拉斯加的州議會從 1937 年起只有一院；各州憲法的長短與詳細程度也差得很多。
