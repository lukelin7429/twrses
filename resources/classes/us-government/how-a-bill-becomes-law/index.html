U.S. Government · Unit 2 · Lesson 6 · 美國政治 單元二 第六課

How Does a Bill Become a Law?法案怎麼變成法律？

Follow a bill through nine stations, from the hopper to the President, and see where most bills stop.
跟著一個法案走過九站，從提案箱到總統，看大多數法案停在哪裡。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

A bill becomes law only after it passes both chambers in the same words and the President signs it, or Congress overrides a veto. Each stage is a chance to improve a bill and a chance to stop it, which is why most bills never become law.
法案必須以相同的字句通過兩院，再經總統簽署——或由國會推翻否決——才成為法律。每一關都是改進法案的機會，也是擋下法案的機會；這就是為什麼大多數法案都沒有成為法律。

The bill's journey · 法案的旅程

The whole road走完全程

The bill clears both committees and both chambers, the two versions are reconciled into one text, and the President signs it.法案通過兩院的委員會與院會，兩院版本整合成一個，總統簽署。

The most common ending最常見的結局

The committee never acts on the bill. No vote is taken; when the Congress ends, the bill dies.委員會一直沒有處理這個法案。沒有任何表決；這一屆國會結束時，法案就自動失效。

Passed one chamber only只過了一院

The House passes the bill, but in the Senate supporters cannot reach the sixty votes needed to end debate, so the bill never gets a final vote.眾議院通過了法案，但在參議院，支持者湊不到結束辯論所需的六十票，法案始終沒能進入最後表決。

The veto holds否決維持

Congress passes the bill and the President vetoes it. The override vote falls short of two thirds (in this example, 260 of 435 in the House and 62 of 100 in the Senate), so the bill dies.國會通過法案，總統否決。推翻否決的票數不到三分之二（這個例子是眾議院 435 票中的 260 票、參議院 100 票中的 62 票），法案失效。

Two thirds of each chamber兩院各三分之二

The President vetoes the bill, but two thirds of each chamber vote to pass it again (in this example, 310 in the House and 70 in the Senate). It becomes law without the President's signature.總統否決，但兩院各以三分之二再次通過（這個例子是眾議院 310 票、參議院 70 票）。法案不經總統簽署成為法律。

No signature, no return不簽也無從退回

Congress sends the bill and then adjourns for good at the end of its term. The President neither signs it nor can it be returned within ten days. Under Article I, Section 7, it does not become law, and there is nothing for Congress to override.國會送出法案後，在一屆任期結束時休會。總統既沒有簽署，十天內法案也無從退回。依憲法第一條第七項，法案不成為法律，國會也沒有可以推翻的對象。

Step 1 · 第 1 站 · House · 眾議院

A member introduces the bill議員提出法案

Only a member of Congress can introduce a bill; in the House that includes the delegates who do not have a full vote. In the House, the member places it in a wooden box called the hopper, and the Speaker, with the help of the Parliamentarian, refers it to a committee. A bill may start in either chamber, except that bills for raising revenue must start in the House. This model follows a bill that starts in the House.只有國會議員可以提出法案；在眾議院，沒有完整表決權的代表也可以。在眾議院，議員把法案放進一個叫做 hopper 的木箱，由議長在議事專家協助下交付委員會。法案可以從任何一院開始，只有增加歲入的法案必須從眾議院開始。這個模型跟著一個從眾議院開始的法案走。

Step 2 · 第 2 站 · House · 眾議院

House committee眾議院委員會

The committee chair decides which bills get attention. The committee, often through a subcommittee, may hold hearings, rewrite the bill line by line in a session called a markup, and vote to report it to the full House. It may also simply never act on it, and many bills end here.委員會主席決定哪些法案會被處理。委員會（常常透過小組委員會）可以舉行聽證、在稱為 markup 的會議裡逐條修改法案，再表決是否提報給全院。它也可以一直不處理——許多法案就停在這裡。

Step 3 · 第 3 站 · House · 眾議院

House Rules Committee眾議院規則委員會

For most major bills, the Rules Committee writes a special rule that sets how long the House will debate and which amendments may be offered. The House votes on the rule before it debates the bill.大多數重要法案，由規則委員會訂出一個特別規則，決定全院辯論多久、可以提出哪些修正案。全院先表決這個規則，才辯論法案本身。

Step 4 · 第 4 站 · House · 眾議院

The full House votes眾議院院會表決

Members debate the bill under the rule, vote on amendments, and then vote on passage. A majority of those voting passes it, with a quorum present. If a committee refuses to act, a majority of the whole House, 218 members, can sign a discharge petition to bring the bill to the floor anyway.議員依規則辯論、表決修正案，再表決是否通過。在法定人數出席下，投票者過半數就通過。如果委員會拒絕處理，全院過半數——218 位議員——可以連署「解除審查」的請願，把法案直接帶到院會。

Step 5 · 第 5 站 · Senate · 參議院

Senate committee參議院委員會

In the Senate the bill goes to a committee again, which may hold its own hearings and markup and may report the bill, change it, or set it aside.到了參議院，法案再交付一個委員會；委員會可以自己舉行聽證與逐條審查，可以提報法案、修改法案，或把它擱置。

Step 6 · 第 6 站 · Senate · 參議院

The full Senate votes參議院院會表決

Many bills pass the Senate by unanimous consent, with little debate. But if any senator objects to ending debate, supporters of most bills need cloture, three fifths of the senators chosen and sworn (sixty when every seat is filled), before the final vote, which needs only a simple majority.許多法案在參議院以無異議同意通過，幾乎不經辯論。但只要有參議員反對結束辯論，大多數法案的支持者就需要通過「終結辯論」——已就任參議員的五分之三（席位全滿時是六十票）——才能進入最後表決，而最後表決只需要簡單多數。

Step 7 · 第 7 站 · Both chambers · 兩院

One text for both chambers兩院版本一致

Both chambers must agree to exactly the same text. If their versions differ, they trade amendments back and forth (officially, “amendments between the houses”), or appoint a conference committee whose compromise, the conference report, both chambers then vote on. The final text is printed as the enrolled bill.兩院必須同意完全相同的條文。版本不同時，兩院把修正案送來送去（正式的說法是「兩院之間的修正案」），或組成協商委員會，寫出妥協版本（協商報告），再由兩院各自表決。最後的條文印成正式文本（enrolled bill）。

Step 8 · 第 8 站 · The President · 總統

The President decides總統決定

The President has ten days, Sundays excepted, to act. Signing makes the bill law. A veto sends it back with objections. If the President does nothing, it becomes law without a signature, unless Congress's adjournment has prevented its return: then it does not, which is called a pocket veto. Congress reads this narrowly, as applying when a Congress ends.總統有十天（星期日不算）可以處理。簽署，法案就成為法律；否決，就附上反對理由退回。總統什麼都不做，法案不經簽署也成為法律——除非國會休會使法案無從退回，這時它就不成為法律，稱為口袋否決。國會對此採狹義解釋，認為只適用於一屆國會結束時。

If he approve he shall sign it, but if not he shall return it, with his Objections to that House in which it shall have originated, …Article I, Section 7

Step 9 · 第 9 站 · Both chambers · 兩院

Congress tries to override國會推翻否決

A vetoed bill becomes law if two thirds of the members voting in each chamber, a quorum being present, vote to pass it again. Since 1789 presidents have vetoed 2,599 bills, and Congress has overridden 112 of those vetoes.被否決的法案，若兩院各有出席投票議員的三分之二（且達法定人數）再次通過，就成為法律。自 1789 年以來，總統共否決 2,599 個法案，國會推翻了其中 112 次。

… if approved by two thirds of that House, it shall become a Law.Article I, Section 7

Unit 6: How Does a Bill Become a Law?

Any member of Congress may introduce a bill, and thousands do every term. In the 117th Congress, which met in 2021 and 2022, members introduced 15,058 bills and 176 joint resolutions. During the 117th Congress, 362 public laws were enacted. Most bills that are introduced never become law, and many of those never reach a vote on the floor. The road from an idea to a law is long, and the Constitution, the rules of each chamber, and the calendar of a two-year Congress all shape where a bill stops.

任何一位國會議員都可以提出法案，每一屆都有數以千計的法案被提出。2021 到 2022 年的第 117 屆國會，議員共提出 15,058 件法案和 176 件聯合決議；第 117 屆國會期間，制定了 362 條公法。大多數被提出的法案都沒有成為法律，其中許多從未進入院會表決。從一個想法到一條法律的路都很長；憲法、兩院各自的議事規則，以及一屆國會兩年的時程，都決定了法案會停在哪裡。

The first and most common stopping place is a committee. Each chamber divides its work among standing committees: in the 119th Congress, the House has twenty and the Senate has twenty permanent committees. The committee chair decides which bills receive attention. A committee may hold hearings, rewrite a bill in a markup session, and vote to report it. It may also never act at all. In 1885 the political scientist Woodrow Wilson wrote that “Congress in session is Congress on public exhibition, whilst Congress in its committee-rooms is Congress at work.” He was describing the House of his day, but committees still decide which bills receive formal attention.

第一個、也是最常見的停留地點，是委員會。兩院各自把工作分給常設委員會：第 119 屆國會，眾議院有二十個、參議院有二十個常設委員會。委員會主席決定哪些法案會被處理。委員會可以舉行聽證、在逐條審查（markup）會議裡改寫法案，再表決是否提報；它也可以完全不處理。1885 年，政治學者威爾遜寫道：「開會中的國會是公開展示的國會，而委員會會議室裡的國會才是在工作的國會。」他描述的是當時的眾議院，但直到今天，仍是由委員會決定哪些法案會被正式處理。

In the House, a reported bill usually needs a special rule from the Rules Committee, which sets the length of debate and decides which amendments may be offered. The House then debates and votes; a majority of those voting is enough to pass it. A committee's silence is not always final. If 218 members, a majority of the whole House, sign a discharge petition, the bill can be brought to the floor without the committee's approval. Gathering that many signatures is difficult, but the possibility is part of the balance between a committee and the full House.

在眾議院，被提報的法案通常需要規則委員會訂出一個特別規則，決定辯論多久、可以提出哪些修正案。接著全院辯論、表決；投票者過半數就通過。委員會不處理，也不一定就是終點：如果有 218 位議員——全院過半數——連署「解除審查」的請願，法案就可以不經委員會同意直接進入院會。要湊到這麼多連署並不容易，但這個可能性是委員會與全院之間權力平衡的一部分。

The Senate works differently. Many bills pass by unanimous consent, with little or no debate, when no senator objects. But Senate rules allow debate to continue for a long time. To end debate on most bills over objection, supporters need cloture, which requires three fifths of the senators chosen and sworn: sixty votes when every seat is filled. Lesson 5 explained how that rule developed. In practice it means that many bills need support from more than a simple majority of senators before they can reach a final vote, even though the final vote itself needs only a majority.

參議院的運作不一樣。沒有參議員反對時，許多法案以無異議同意通過，幾乎不經辯論。但參議院的規則允許辯論持續很久。有人反對時，大多數法案要結束辯論，支持者需要通過「終結辯論」，也就是已就任參議員的五分之三：席位全滿時是六十票。第五課講過這條規則的由來。實際上，這表示許多法案需要超過簡單多數參議員的支持才能進入最後表決，雖然最後表決本身只需要過半數。

Passing both chambers is not enough. The House and the Senate must agree to exactly the same text, down to the last word. When their versions differ, the chambers may send amendments back and forth, which is officially called “amendments between the houses,” or appoint a conference committee of members from both chambers to write a compromise, called a conference report. Each chamber then votes on that report without changing it. Only when both have agreed to identical text is the final version printed as the enrolled bill, signed by the presiding officers of both chambers, and sent to the President.

通過兩院還不夠。眾議院和參議院必須同意完全相同的條文，一字不差。兩院版本不同時，可以把修正案送來送去——正式的說法是「兩院之間的修正案」——或組成由兩院議員組成的協商委員會，寫出妥協版本，稱為協商報告；兩院再各自表決這份報告，不能再修改。只有兩院都同意了完全相同的條文，最後的版本才會印成正式文本（enrolled bill），由兩院的主席（眾議院議長與參議院的主持議事者）簽署，送交總統。

The President then has ten days, Sundays excepted, to act. Signing the bill makes it law. Returning it with objections is a veto. If the President does neither, the bill becomes law without a signature, unless Congress has adjourned and so prevented its return. In that case it does not become law, which is called a pocket veto, and Congress cannot override it. The House's own guide notes that Congress reads this power narrowly, as applying only when a Congress ends, and that the courts have not definitively decided how far it reaches.

接著，總統有十天（星期日不算）可以處理。簽署，法案就成為法律；附上反對理由退回，就是否決。總統兩樣都不做，法案不經簽署也成為法律——除非國會已經休會，使法案無從退回；這時它就不成為法律，稱為口袋否決，國會也無從推翻。眾議院自己的說明指出，國會對這項權力採狹義解釋，認為只適用於一屆國會結束時；法院則還沒有明確判定它的範圍。

A vetoed bill goes back to the chamber where it started. If two thirds of the members voting in each chamber, with a quorum present, vote to pass it again, it becomes law over the President's objections. That margin is hard to reach. According to the Senate's records, presidents have vetoed 2,599 bills since 1789, and Congress has overridden only 112 of those vetoes. Because overrides are rare, members who want a bill to become law usually try to address the President's objections earlier in the process, before the bill is sent to the White House.

被否決的法案退回提出它的那一院。如果兩院各有出席投票議員的三分之二（且達法定人數）再次通過，它就不顧總統的反對成為法律。這個門檻很難達到。依參議院的紀錄，自 1789 年以來總統共否決 2,599 個法案，國會只推翻了其中 112 次。因為推翻否決很少見，想讓法案成為法律的議員，通常會在送交白宮之前，就先設法回應總統的反對意見。

The difficulty is deliberate. The framers built a system in which a law must win a majority in two chambers chosen in different ways and then win the President's signature or a supermajority. Supporters of the design say each stage catches mistakes and keeps laws stable. Critics say it lets a small number of people block what most want, and that problems go unaddressed while bills wait. Both sides agree on the facts: most bills never become law. Whether that is a strength or a weakness is the question in the design debate below.

這種困難是刻意設計的。制憲者打造的制度，要求一條法律必須在兩個以不同方式選出的議院各自過半，再得到總統簽署或絕對多數的支持。支持這個設計的人說，每一關都能抓出錯誤、讓法律保持穩定。批評的人說，它讓少數人能擋住多數人想要的事，問題也在法案等待時一直沒有處理。兩邊對事實沒有爭議：大多數法案都沒有成為法律。這是優點還是缺點，正是下面「制度的爭論」要討論的問題。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Why might a member introduce a bill that has little chance of passing?

A bill can signal a position to voters, start a public conversation, attract cosponsors over time, or stake out ground for a later compromise. Introducing a bill costs little, so members use it for purposes other than passing a law in the current Congress.

What does the discharge petition reveal about the balance of power between committees and the whole House?

Committees have great power to stop bills, but that power is borrowed from the House. If a majority of the whole House insists, it can take a bill away from a committee. The high threshold of 218 signatures means the House usually lets committees decide, but not without limit.

Why do overrides being rare give the President influence over bills that are never vetoed?

Because two thirds of each chamber is so hard to reach, members expect a veto to stand. So they adjust a bill in advance to avoid one, negotiating with the President's administration while the bill is still in Congress. The threat of a veto shapes laws even when no veto is cast.

    What Does the Text Say? · 條文怎麼寫？

    The Constitution's recipe for a law 憲法裡制定法律的配方

    Article I, Section 7 is the main part of the Constitution that describes the path of a bill. The words are copied from the National Archives transcription, with its spelling and capitalization.
    第一條第七項是憲法裡描述法案路線的主要地方。原文照美國國家檔案館的謄本逐字抄錄，保留原本的拼法與大寫。

    • Article I, Section 7 (first sentences of the second paragraph)第一條第七項（第二段的開頭）

      Every Bill which shall have passed the House of Representatives and the Senate, shall, before it become a Law, be presented to the President of the United States; If he approve he shall sign it, but if not he shall return it, with his Objections to that House in which it shall have originated, who shall enter the Objections at large on their Journal, and proceed to reconsider it.

      每一個經眾議院與參議院通過的法案，在成為法律之前，都應送交合眾國總統；總統若批准，就簽署；若不批准，就附上反對理由，退回原提出的那一院；該院應把反對理由全文記入議事錄，並重新審議。

      “Passed the House of Representatives and the Senate”: both, not either. The veto goes back to the chamber where the bill started.「經眾議院與參議院通過」：兩院都要，不是任一院。否決退回的是法案最初提出的那一院。

      National Archives: The Constitution of the United States, a transcription ↗

    • Article I, Section 7 (the override)第一條第七項（推翻否決）

      If after such Reconsideration two thirds of that House shall agree to pass the Bill, it shall be sent, together with the Objections, to the other House, by which it shall likewise be reconsidered, and if approved by two thirds of that House, it shall become a Law. But in all such Cases the Votes of both Houses shall be determined by yeas and Nays, and the Names of the Persons voting for and against the Bill shall be entered on the Journal of each House respectively.

      重新審議之後，若該院三分之二同意通過該法案，就連同反對理由送交另一院，由另一院同樣重新審議；若經該院三分之二批准，即成為法律。但在所有這類情形中，兩院的表決都應以贊成與反對記名決定，投票贊成與反對該法案者的姓名，應分別記入各院的議事錄。

      An override vote must be a recorded vote: every member's name goes on the record. The two thirds is of the members voting, with a quorum present.推翻否決必須記名表決：每位議員的名字都留在紀錄上。三分之二指的是出席投票的議員，而且要達法定人數。

      National Archives: The Constitution of the United States, a transcription ↗

    • Article I, Section 7 (ten days and the pocket veto)第一條第七項（十天與口袋否決）

      If any Bill shall not be returned by the President within ten Days (Sundays excepted) after it shall have been presented to him, the Same shall be a Law, in like Manner as if he had signed it, unless the Congress by their Adjournment prevent its Return, in which Case it shall not be a Law.

      任何法案送交總統後十日內（星期日除外）未經退回，即與經總統簽署一樣成為法律；除非國會因休會而使其無從退回，在此情形下，該法案不成為法律。

      The pocket veto is not a separate power the Constitution names. It is what this sentence produces when Congress has adjourned.憲法並沒有另外寫一項叫「口袋否決」的權力；它是國會休會時，這一句話所造成的結果。

      National Archives: The Constitution of the United States, a transcription ↗

    One position · 一種立場

    Difficulty is a safeguard困難是一種保障

    • Every stage is another chance to catch mistakes, unclear wording, and unintended effects before a law binds everyone.每一關都是另一次機會，在法律拘束所有人之前，抓出錯誤、含糊的字句和意料之外的後果。
    • A law must satisfy majorities chosen in different ways, in two chambers and the presidency, so it reflects broad agreement.一條法律必須讓以不同方式選出的多數都滿意——兩院加上總統——所以它反映的是廣泛的共識。
    • Laws that change slowly let people plan, and keep each new majority from reversing the last one's work.變化緩慢的法律讓人能夠規劃，也避免每一個新的多數把上一個多數的成果推翻。

      行政院對於立法院決議之法律案、預算案、條約案，如認為有窒礙難行時，得經總統之核可，於該決議案送達行政院十日內，移請立法院覆議。……覆議時，如經全體立法委員二分之一以上決議維持原案，行政院院長應即接受該決議。

      全國法規資料庫 ↗Laws & Regulations Database: Additional Articles (English) ↗

    One thing to notice: reconsideration in Taiwan is not the same as an American veto. The request comes from the Executive Yuan, the threshold to uphold the bill is more than half of all legislators rather than two thirds, and if the Legislative Yuan does not decide within fifteen days, the original resolution lapses. The texts were checked against the Laws and Regulations Database in October 2026.有一點可以注意：台灣的覆議和美國的否決不是同一回事。提出覆議的是行政院；維持原案的門檻是全體立委二分之一以上，不是三分之二；而且立法院若在十五日內沒有作成決議，原決議失效。條文於 2026 年 10 月對過全國法規資料庫。

    News English · 新聞英語

    Eight terms for following a bill 追法案新聞用得到的八個詞

    Listen to each term and its example sentence, then try one of your own. The examples describe the process, not any current bill.
    先聽每個詞和例句，再試著自己造一句。例句只描述程序，不涉及任何現在的法案。

    1. to introduce a bill提出法案

      to formally present a bill in one chamber of Congress在國會的一院正式提出法案

      A senator introduced a bill to rename a post office.一位參議員提出法案，要替一間郵局改名。

    2. a sponsor提案人

      the member who introduces a bill; others who join are cosponsors提出法案的議員；加入支持的其他議員是共同提案人

      The bill has one sponsor and forty cosponsors.這個法案有一位提案人和四十位共同提案人。

    3. a committee委員會

      a group of members that studies bills in one subject area審查某一領域法案的一群議員

      The bill is still in committee.這個法案還在委員會。

    4. a markup逐條審查

      a committee meeting where members amend a bill line by line委員會逐條修改法案的會議

      The committee scheduled a markup for Thursday.委員會把逐條審查排在星期四。

    5. a floor vote院會表決

      a vote by the whole chamber全院的表決

      Leaders hope to hold a floor vote next week.議會領袖希望下星期舉行院會表決。

    6. a conference committee協商委員會

      a temporary committee of members from both chambers that writes one version of a bill由兩院議員組成、寫出單一版本法案的臨時委員會

      A conference committee settled the differences between the two versions.協商委員會解決了兩個版本之間的差異。

    7. to veto否決

      (of the President) to refuse to sign a bill and return it to Congress with objections (a pocket veto is the exception: nothing is returned)（總統）拒絕簽署法案，並附上反對理由退回國會（口袋否決例外：不退回任何東西）

      The President vetoed the bill and explained why in a message.總統否決了法案，並在咨文中說明理由。

    8. to override推翻（否決）

      to pass a vetoed bill again by two thirds of each chamber兩院各以三分之二再次通過被否決的法案

      Congress failed to override the veto.國會未能推翻這項否決。

    Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

    Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Most bills get a vote.大多數法案都會被表決。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Committees do not formally consider every bill referred to them, and many are never brought to the floor. In the 117th Congress, more than 15,000 bills were introduced and 362 public laws were enacted.委員會不會正式處理每一個交付給它的法案，許多法案也從未進入院會。第 117 屆國會提出了超過一萬五千件法案，制定了 362 條公法。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思The President can propose a bill directly to Congress.總統可以直接向國會提出法案。

    ✓ Fact · 事實The President can recommend legislation, but only a member of Congress can introduce a bill.總統可以建議立法，但只有國會議員可以提出法案。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思If the President does nothing, the bill dies.總統什麼都不做，法案就會失效。

    ✓ Fact · 事實If Congress is in session, the bill becomes law after ten days (Sundays excepted) without a signature. It dies only if Congress's adjournment prevents its return, a pocket veto.如果國會還在開會，十天（星期日不算）後法案不經簽署就成為法律。只有在國會休會使法案無從退回時才會失效，也就是口袋否決。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思A conference committee can rewrite a bill and send it straight to the President.協商委員會可以改寫法案，直接送給總統。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Its conference report must be approved by both chambers before the bill can be enrolled and presented to the President.它的協商報告必須經兩院同意，法案才能印成正式文本、送交總統。

    Activity 1 · 課堂或自學活動一

    Follow a Bill You Invent 跟著你發明的法案走

    You need · 準備材料

    • The model above and the nine station cards上面的模型和九站的說明
    • Paper for a one-page bill寫一頁法案的紙

    Steps · 步驟

    1. In small groups, write a short, uncontroversial bill (for example, creating a national library week).分小組，寫一個簡短、沒有爭議的法案（例如設立「全國圖書館週」）。
    2. Assign roles: sponsor, committee chair, Rules Committee, senators, conferees, President.分配角色：提案人、委員會主席、規則委員會、參議員、協商代表、總統。
    3. Move the bill through each station. At each one, the role-player decides whether it moves on, changes, or stops, and gives a reason.讓法案走過每一站。每一站由扮演的人決定：往前、修改，還是停下來，並說明理由。
    4. Compare where each group's bill ended with the scenarios in the model.把各組法案的結局和模型裡的情境比一比。

    Choose a topic no one feels strongly about, so the discussion stays on the process.
    選一個大家都沒有強烈立場的題目，讓討論集中在程序上。

    Activity 2 · 課堂或自學活動二

    Read a Bill's History 讀一個法案的履歷

    You need · 準備材料

    • The public bill-status pages on congress.gov (or a printed bill history your teacher provides)congress.gov 上公開的法案進度頁（或老師準備的法案履歷影本）

    Steps · 步驟

    1. Pick a bill from a past Congress that became law.挑一個過去某屆國會已經成為法律的法案。
    2. List each action in its history and match it to a station in the model.列出它履歷上的每一個動作，對應到模型裡的某一站。
    3. Note any station it skipped, and find out why (for example, unanimous consent in the Senate).注意它跳過了哪一站，並找出原因（例如在參議院以無異議同意通過）。

    Older, settled laws work best; the goal is to read the procedure, not to debate the policy.
    選年代較早、已有定論的法律最好；目標是讀懂程序，不是辯論政策。
