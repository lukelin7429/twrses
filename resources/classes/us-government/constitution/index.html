Big idea · 一句話看懂

A written constitution can be read as a contract in which the people authorize a government. It first creates the institutions and divides power among them, and then it draws lines that the government may not cross. The U.S. Constitution is short and very hard to change, so for more than two centuries it has been applied to a changing country through interpretation and a small number of amendments.
成文憲法可以看成一份「人民授權政府」的書面契約：它先設立機關、分配權力，再劃出政府不能越過的線。美國憲法很短、很難改，所以兩百多年來，同一份文字是靠解釋和少數幾條修正案，用在一個不斷改變的國家上。

In the document · 文件裡寫的

The Preamble序言

One sentence that says who is speaking and why. It names the people as the authors and lists six purposes. The Supreme Court has said that the Preamble is not itself a source of power (1905); the powers are in the articles that follow.只有一句話，說明是誰在說話、為什麼要寫這份文件：它以「人民」為作者，列出六個目的。最高法院說過，序言本身不是權力的來源（1905 年）；權力寫在後面各條。

We the People of the United States … do ordain and establish this Constitution for the United States of America.Preamble (the full sentence is in “Read the Source” below)

Article I: Congress第一條：國會（立法）

Creates a Congress of two chambers, the House of Representatives and the Senate, and lists what it may do. It is by far the longest article.設立由眾議院與參議院組成的兩院制國會，並列出國會可以做什麼。這是全文最長的一條。

  • Lay and collect taxes; borrow and coin money徵稅、舉債、鑄造貨幣
  • Regulate commerce among the states and with foreign nations管理州與州之間、以及與外國的貿易
  • Declare war; raise and support armies宣戰；招募並維持軍隊
  • Make all laws “necessary and proper” for carrying out these powers制定為執行上述權力所「必要且適當」的一切法律
  • The House impeaches; the Senate tries impeachments眾議院提出彈劾，參議院審判彈劾案
All legislative Powers herein granted shall be vested in a Congress of the United States, which shall consist of a Senate and House of Representatives.Article I, Section 1

Article II: The President第二條：總統（行政）

Creates the office of President, chosen for a four-year term by electors, and vests the executive power in that office.設立總統一職，任期四年，由選舉人選出；行政權交給總統。

  • Commander in chief of the army and navy陸海軍的統帥
  • Make treaties, if two thirds of the senators present agree締結條約（須經出席參議員三分之二同意）
  • Nominate judges and other officers, with the Senate's advice and consent提名法官與其他官員（須經參議院同意）
  • Grant pardons for federal offenses, except in cases of impeachment赦免聯邦罪行（彈劾案除外）
  • “take Care that the Laws be faithfully executed” (Section 3)「注意使法律切實執行」（第三項）
The executive Power shall be vested in a President of the United States of America.Article II, Section 1

Article III: The Courts第三條：法院（司法）

Creates one Supreme Court and lets Congress set up lower courts. It is the shortest of the three, and it does not say how many justices there are; Congress decides that by law.設立一個最高法院，並讓國會設立下級法院。這是前三條裡最短的一條，也沒有寫大法官有幾位——人數由國會以法律決定。

  • Decide cases arising under the Constitution, federal laws, and treaties審理因憲法、聯邦法律與條約而生的案件
  • Judges keep their offices “during good Behaviour,” and their pay cannot be cut法官「品行端正」即得繼續任職，薪俸不得削減
  • Crimes are tried by jury刑事案件由陪審團審判
The judicial Power of the United States, shall be vested in one supreme Court, and in such inferior Courts as the Congress may from time to time ordain and establish.Article III, Section 1

Article IV: The States第四條：州與州的關係

Sets the rules among the states and between the states and the Union.規定州與州之間、各州與聯邦之間的關係。

  • Each state gives “Full Faith and Credit” to the public acts, records, and court rulings of the others各州對他州的法令、紀錄與司法程序給予「充分的信任與尊重」
  • Congress may admit new states國會得接納新的州
  • The United States guarantees every state a republican form of government合眾國保證各州實行共和政體
  • In 1787 it also required that people who escaped from slavery be returned (ended by the Thirteenth Amendment, 1865)1787 年的原文還規定逃亡的被奴役者必須交還（1865 年第十三條修正案廢除奴隸制後失效）

Article V: Amending第五條：修憲程序

Explains how to change the Constitution: two ways to propose an amendment and two ways to ratify one. Try them in “How Hard Is It to Change One Word?” below.規定怎麼修改憲法：兩種提案方式、兩種批准方式。下面的「改一個字有多難？」可以走一次。

  • Proposed by two thirds of both houses of Congress, or by a convention called when two thirds of the state legislatures apply由國會兩院各三分之二提出；或經三分之二的州議會申請，召開會議提出
  • Ratified by the legislatures of three fourths of the states, or by conventions in three fourths of them由四分之三的州議會批准；或由四分之三的州的會議批准
  • No state may lose its equal vote in the Senate without its consent未經該州同意，不得剝奪任何一州在參議院的平等表決權

Article VI: The Supreme Law第六條：最高條款

Makes the Constitution, and federal laws and treaties made under it, the supreme law of the land, and binds every state judge to it.宣告憲法以及依據憲法制定的聯邦法律與條約是全國的最高法律，各州法官都受其拘束。

  • State constitutions and laws give way when they conflict with valid federal law州的憲法與法律牴觸有效的聯邦法律時，以聯邦法律為準
  • All federal and state officials swear to support the Constitution聯邦與各州的官員都要宣誓擁護憲法
  • No religious test may be required for any federal office擔任聯邦公職不得以宗教信仰為條件
This Constitution, and the Laws of the United States which shall be made in Pursuance thereof … shall be the supreme Law of the Land.Article VI, Clause 2

Article VII: Ratifying第七條：批准

Says how the Constitution itself would take effect: when conventions in nine of the thirteen states approved it. Under the Articles of Confederation, any change had needed all thirteen.規定憲法本身怎麼生效：十三州裡有九個州的會議批准即可。在《邦聯條例》之下，任何修改都要十三州全部同意。

The Ratification of the Conventions of nine States, shall be sufficient for the Establishment of this Constitution between the States so ratifying the Same.Article VII

The Amendments修正案

Twenty-seven changes added at the end of the document. The original text is not rewritten; an amendment simply overrides the words it replaces.附在文件最後面的二十七條修改。原文不改寫，被取代的字句仍留在原處，只是以修正案為準。

  • 1 to 10 (1791): the Bill of Rights第一至十條（1791）：權利法案
  • 13, 14, 15 (1865–1870): abolish slavery; citizenship and equal protection; voting not to be denied by race第十三、十四、十五條（1865–1870）：廢除奴隸制；公民身分與平等保護；不得因種族剝奪投票權
  • 19 (1920): voting not to be denied on account of sex第十九條（1920）：不得因性別剝奪投票權
  • 27 (1992): the most recent, first proposed in 1789第二十七條（1992）：最近的一條，1789 年就已提出

Unit 1: How Can One Document Run a Country?

In most of life, laws come from the government. A constitution works the other way around: it is the law that creates the government in the first place. The Constitution of the United States, written in 1787, opens with the words “We the People,” and that opening is a claim about where power comes from. Political thinkers call the idea popular sovereignty: the people are the source of the government's authority, and officials hold power only because the people have granted it. In this sense the Constitution is a written contract. It sets up the institutions of government, hands each of them certain powers, and marks limits that none of them may cross.

在日常生活裡，法律是政府訂的。憲法卻反過來：它是先把政府造出來的那一部法。美國憲法寫於 1787 年，開頭是「我們人民」（We the People）——這個開頭本身就是一個主張，講的是權力從哪裡來。政治思想家把這個想法叫做人民主權（popular sovereignty，法學上也譯作國民主權）：政府的權威來自人民，官員之所以握有權力，只因為人民把權力交給了他們。從這個意思來說，憲法是一份書面契約：它設立政府的機關，交給每個機關某些權力，並且劃出任何機關都不能越過的界線。

The Constitution was the country's second attempt. After declaring independence in 1776, the thirteen states joined together under the Articles of Confederation, which took effect in 1781 and described the union as “a firm league of friendship.” Each state kept its sovereignty and had one vote in a Congress with a single chamber. That Congress could not collect taxes; it could only ask the states for money. It could not regulate trade between the states, and there was no separate president or national court system to carry out its decisions. Changing the Articles required every state legislature to agree, so their weaknesses were nearly impossible to repair.

憲法是這個國家的第二次嘗試。1776 年宣布獨立之後，十三個州依《邦聯條例》（Articles of Confederation）結合在一起；條例在 1781 年生效，把這個聯盟稱為「堅定的友好同盟」。每一州保有自己的主權，在只有一院的國會裡各有一票。這個國會不能徵稅，只能請各州出錢；不能管理州與州之間的貿易；也沒有獨立的總統或全國性的法院體系來執行它的決定。要修改條例，必須每一個州的議會都同意，所以它的弱點幾乎沒辦法補救。

In May 1787, delegates began to gather in Philadelphia. Twelve states sent them; Rhode Island refused. They had been called together to revise the Articles. Meeting in secret through the summer, they ended up drafting an entirely new plan of government. Fifty-five delegates took part, and thirty-nine signed the finished document on September 17, 1787. The plan was built on compromises. Large and small states disagreed about representation, so the two chambers of Congress were filled in different ways: a House of Representatives based on population and a Senate in which every state has two seats. The delegates also decided that the plan would take effect once nine states approved it, rather than all thirteen.

1787 年 5 月，代表們陸續來到費城。十二個州派了人，羅德島拒絕。召開這場會議的本意，是修改《邦聯條例》。代表們整個夏天閉門開會，最後起草出來的，是一套全新的政府方案。先後有五十五位代表出席，其中三十九位在 1787 年 9 月 17 日簽署了定稿。這套方案是由一連串妥協搭起來的：大州和小州對代表權的算法意見不合，於是國會的兩院用不同的方式組成——眾議院按人口分配席次，參議院每州兩席。代表們還決定，只要九個州批准，方案就生效，不必等十三州全部同意。

The document itself is brief. A one-sentence Preamble states its purposes, and seven articles follow. Article I creates Congress and lists its powers, such as taxing, regulating commerce, and declaring war. Article II creates the presidency, and Article III creates the Supreme Court and lets Congress set up lower courts. Article IV deals with relations among the states, Article V explains how to amend the Constitution, and Article VI declares the Constitution and the federal laws made under it “the supreme Law of the Land.” Article VII describes ratification, the formal approval that turned the proposal into law. Twenty-seven amendments have been added since. The original text, signatures included, runs to about 4,500 words.

這份文件本身很短。一句話的序言（Preamble）說明目的，接著是七條。第一條設立國會並列出它的權力，例如徵稅、管理貿易、宣戰；第二條設立總統；第三條設立最高法院，並讓國會設立下級法院。第四條處理州與州之間的關係，第五條說明怎麼修憲，第六條宣告憲法以及依據憲法制定的聯邦法律是「全國的最高法律」。第七條規定批准（ratification）——也就是讓這份提案正式成為法律的那一道同意程序。此後又加上了二十七條修正案。原始的文本連簽名在內，大約 4,500 個英文字。

Several principles run through these few pages. The first is limited government: the national government has only the powers the Constitution gives it, which are called enumerated powers, and the Tenth Amendment adds that powers not delegated to it “are reserved to the States respectively, or to the people.” The second is the separation of powers among three branches, each able to check the others; that is the subject of the next lesson. The third is federalism, the division of authority between the national government and the states. Finally, the Supremacy Clause of Article VI settles what happens when the two collide: valid federal law prevails, and judges in every state are bound by it.

這短短幾頁裡貫穿著幾個原則。第一是有限政府：全國政府只擁有憲法交給它的權力，這叫做列舉的權力（enumerated powers）；第十條修正案又補了一句——沒有授予合眾國的權力，「保留給各州，或保留給人民」。第二是三個部門之間的權力分立（separation of powers），每一個部門都能牽制另外兩個，這是下一課的主題。第三是聯邦制（federalism），也就是全國政府與各州之間的權限劃分。最後，第六條的最高條款（Supremacy Clause）決定了兩者衝突時怎麼辦：有效的聯邦法律優先，各州的法官都受它拘束。

Ratification was far from certain. Supporters of the Constitution, known as Federalists, argued that the union would not survive without a stronger national government; Alexander Hamilton, James Madison, and John Jay made the case in eighty-five newspaper essays now called The Federalist. Opponents, known as Anti-Federalists, warned that a distant national government would overwhelm the states, and they pointed out that the document contained no bill of rights. Massachusetts ratified only after the Federalists agreed to recommend amendments, and other states followed that example. New Hampshire became the ninth state to ratify in June 1788, and the Constitution took effect in March 1789. Congress then proposed twelve amendments, and ten were ratified in 1791. That Bill of Rights was in large part the product of a bargain.

批准的過程一點也不篤定。支持憲法的人稱為聯邦派（Federalists），他們主張沒有一個更強的全國政府，這個聯盟撐不下去；漢彌爾頓、麥迪遜和傑伊在報紙上寫了八十五篇文章來說明這個主張，後人稱為《聯邦論》（The Federalist，又譯《聯邦黨人文集》）。反對的人稱為反聯邦派（Anti-Federalists），他們警告：一個遙遠的全國政府會壓過各州，而且這份文件裡沒有權利法案。麻薩諸塞是在聯邦派同意建議增列修正案之後才批准的，其他幾個州也照這個做法。1788 年 6 月，新罕布夏成為第九個批准的州；1789 年 3 月，憲法開始施行。接著國會提出十二條修正案，其中十條在 1791 年完成批准。這部權利法案（Bill of Rights），在很大的程度上是一場交換的產物。

The contract also had deep flaws, and they were written into its text. “We the People” did not mean everyone. The Constitution never used the word “slavery,” yet several of its clauses protected the institution. In dividing up seats in the House, three fifths of a state's enslaved population was added to its free population, which gave slaveholding states more seats than their free population alone would have. Congress could not ban the importation of enslaved people before 1808, and people who escaped to another state had to be returned. Twenty-five of the delegates held people in slavery themselves. Voting rules were left to the states, which at the time shut out nearly all women and many others. Slavery was abolished only in 1865, by the Thirteenth Amendment, after a civil war.

這份契約也有很深的缺陷，而且就寫在條文裡。「我們人民」並不是指所有的人。憲法從頭到尾沒有用「奴隸制」這個詞，卻有好幾個條款保護了這個制度：分配眾議院席次時，一州被奴役人口的五分之三要加進自由人口裡計算，蓄奴的州因此比只算自由人口時多出一些席次；國會在 1808 年以前不得禁止輸入被奴役的人；逃到別州的人必須被交還。與會代表當中，有二十五位自己就蓄奴。誰可以投票則交給各州決定，而當時各州把幾乎所有的女性和其他許多人排除在外。奴隸制要到 1865 年、打過一場內戰（南北戰爭）之後，才由第十三條修正案廢除。

Because Article V sets a very high bar, formal change is rare. An amendment normally needs a two-thirds vote in both houses of Congress and then the approval of three fourths of the states. Of the roughly twelve thousand amendments proposed in Congress since 1789, only thirty-three have been sent to the states, and twenty-seven have been ratified. Most of the work of applying the text to new situations has therefore been done through interpretation, as courts, Congress, and presidents apply eighteenth-century phrases such as “Commerce … among the several States” to railroads, radio, and the internet. How far interpretation may go is one of the oldest arguments in American law. It is also the reason a document of a few pages can still govern a country of more than 330 million people (2020 census).

因為第五條把門檻訂得非常高，正式的修改很少發生。一條修正案通常需要國會兩院各三分之二通過，再經四分之三的州批准。1789 年以來在國會裡提出的修憲案大約一萬兩千件，只有三十三件送交各州，其中二十七件完成批准。所以把條文用到新情況上的工作，大部分是靠解釋完成的：法院、國會和總統把十八世紀的字句——例如「各州之間的貿易」——套用到鐵路、無線電和網際網路上。解釋可以走多遠，是美國法律裡最古老的爭論之一。這也是為什麼一份只有幾頁的文件，到今天還能治理一個三億三千多萬人（2020 年人口普查）的國家。

Questions · 閱讀理解

The reading calls the Constitution “a written contract.” Who are the parties to this contract, and what does each side give or receive?

On this reading, the people are one party and the government they create is the other. The people grant certain powers to the institutions set up in Articles I, II, and III. In return, the government is bound by the limits in the document: it may use only the powers it was given, and it must respect the rights the amendments protect. One fair objection is that in 1787 “the people” who actually agreed were a small part of the population.

Choose two weaknesses of the Articles of Confederation and explain which part of the Constitution was written to fix each one.

Sample answer: Congress under the Articles could not tax, so Article I, Section 8 gives Congress the power “To lay and collect Taxes.” There was no separate executive or national court system, so Article II creates a President and Article III creates a Supreme Court. Other good answers: trade between states (the commerce power in Article I), or the need for all thirteen states to agree to any change (Article V requires three fourths).

The Constitution has been formally amended only twenty-seven times. Is a constitution that is very hard to change a strength, a weakness, or both? Give one reason on each side.

A strength: the basic rules and rights cannot be rewritten by a temporary majority, so the system is stable and people can rely on it. A weakness: an amendment can fail even when a majority of citizens might favor it, so the text can fall behind the times and the work of updating shifts to judges and other officials who interpret it. Many answers are possible, as long as each side gets a reason.

    The Design Debate · 制度的爭論

    Fixed meaning or a living constitution? 意思固定，還是「活的憲法」？

    A written constitution that is very hard to amend raises a question the text does not answer: how should later generations read it? Two broad approaches have competed for a long time. Each is stated here in its strongest form, and the card does not choose between them.

    One approach · 一種取向

    Original meaning (originalism)原意（原意主義）

    • A written constitution exists to fix the rules. If the meaning of its words can shift without an amendment, the limits on power are only as firm as the views of whoever is interpreting them.成文憲法的用處，就是把規則固定下來。如果不經修憲，文字的意思就可以移動，那麼對權力的限制有多牢靠，就只取決於解釋者當下的看法。
    • The text draws its authority from the people who ratified it, and what they agreed to is what the words meant at the time. A fixed meaning can still be applied to new facts: a rule about “searches” reaches devices that nobody had imagined in 1791.條文的權威來自批准它的人民，而他們同意的，是那些文字在當時的意思。意思固定，並不妨礙把它用到新的事實上：一條關於「搜索」的規定，照樣管得到 1791 年沒有人想像得到的裝置。
    • Article V already says how to update the Constitution. When judges update it by interpretation, they make choices that the document gives to Congress and the states. A fixed meaning leaves changes of policy to elected lawmakers.憲法要怎麼更新，第五條已經寫了。如果由法官用解釋來更新，就是由法官做出憲法交給國會與各州去做的選擇。意思固定，政策要不要改，才會留給民選的立法者決定。
    I entirely concur in the propriety of resorting to the sense in which the Constitution was accepted and ratified by the nation. In that sense alone it is the legitimate Constitution. And if that be not the guide in expounding it, there can be no security for a consistent and stable, more than for a faithful exercise of its powers.James Madison, letter to Henry Lee, June 25, 1824我完全贊同這樣的做法：回到全國接受並批准這部憲法時所理解的意思。只有照那個意思，它才是正當的憲法。如果解釋憲法時不以此為準，憲法所授予的權力就既不能保證被忠實地行使，也不能保證被前後一致而穩定地行使。——麥迪遜，1824 年

    Another approach · 另一種取向

    A living constitution活的憲法

    • The authors chose broad phrases such as “due process of law,” “equal protection,” and “unreasonable searches.” Words like these state principles, and applying a principle to a world the authors never saw requires judgment in every generation.寫憲法的人選用了很寬的字句，像「正當法律程序」「平等保護」「不合理的搜索」。這樣的文字宣示的是原則；把原則套用到作者沒見過的世界，每一代人都得自己判斷。
    • Amendment is so difficult that it cannot keep the document current. If meaning were fixed, each generation would be bound by understandings formed in 1787, when a large part of the people then living had no voice in the matter.修憲太難，沒辦法靠它讓這份文件跟上時代。如果意思固定，每一代人就都得受 1787 年形成的理解拘束——而當時活著的人裡，有很大一部分在這件事上沒有發言權。
    • The historical record is often thin or divided, so the original meaning cannot always be found. Reasoning openly from precedent, practice, and present consequences is more candid than claiming a certainty the record does not give.歷史紀錄常常很單薄，或彼此矛盾，「原意」不一定找得到。與其宣稱一種史料給不出來的確定，不如公開地從判例、慣例與現在的後果來推理，還比較坦白。

    Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

    Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

    ✗ Myth · 迷思The Constitution gives Americans their rights.美國人的權利是憲法給的。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Most of its rights clauses are written as limits on government: “Congress shall make no law …” The Ninth Amendment adds that listing certain rights “shall not be construed to deny or disparage others retained by the people.” How far that sentence reaches is still argued.憲法裡談權利的條文，大多是寫成對政府的限制：「國會不得制定法律……」第九條修正案還補上一句：列舉某些權利，「不得解釋為否定或貶低人民所保有的其他權利」。這句話的效力到底有多大，到現在還有爭論。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思“All men are created equal” is in the Constitution.「人人生而平等」寫在憲法裡。

    ✓ Fact · 事實That sentence is in the Declaration of Independence (1776). The Constitution of 1787 gave no guarantee of equality among persons. The guarantee of “the equal protection of the laws” was added by the Fourteenth Amendment in 1868.那句話出自 1776 年的《獨立宣言》。1787 年的憲法沒有保障人與人之間的平等；「法律的平等保護」是 1868 年第十四條修正案才加進去的。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思The Constitution created a democracy in which the people elected their leaders directly.憲法建立了一個由人民直接選出領導人的民主國家。

    ✓ Fact · 事實In the original design, voters chose only the House of Representatives directly. State legislatures chose senators until the Seventeenth Amendment (1913), electors chose the President, and judges were appointed. Who could vote at all was left to each state.照原來的設計，選民直接選的只有眾議院。參議員由州議會選出，直到 1913 年第十七條修正案才改為直選；總統由選舉人選出；法官是任命的。至於誰有資格投票，則交給各州決定。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思The U.S. Constitution is the oldest constitution in the world.美國憲法是全世界最古老的憲法。

    ✓ Fact · 事實The U.S. Senate describes it as “the world’s longest surviving written charter of government,” and that is a claim about national governments. The constitution of the state of Massachusetts was adopted in 1780, seven years before the U.S. Constitution was written, and it is still in use.美國參議院的說法是：它是「全世界存續最久的成文政府憲章」——這講的是國家層級。麻薩諸塞「州」的憲法在 1780 年通過，比美國憲法的起草早七年，而且到現在還在使用。

    4. Write two sentences that begin: “Both constitutions …” and “One difference in how they are organized is …”寫兩句話，開頭分別是：「兩部憲法都……」和「它們在編排上的一個不同是……」。

    Keep to description. The task is to see how two documents are organized, not to decide which one is better.
    只做描述。這個活動是要看清楚兩份文件各自怎麼編排，不是要判斷哪一部比較好。
