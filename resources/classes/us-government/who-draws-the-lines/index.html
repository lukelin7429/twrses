Who Draws the Lines?選區的線是誰畫的？

One invented map, the same voters, three ways to draw the lines, and three different results.
一張虛構的地圖、同樣的選民、三種畫線的方式，得到三種不同的結果。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

After every census, states redraw the boundaries of their House districts. The Constitution leaves the method mostly to the states, courts require districts of nearly equal population, and where the lines fall can change who wins, even when every vote is counted the same way.
每次人口普查之後，各州都要重劃眾議院的選區界線。憲法大致把方法交給各州，法院要求各選區人口大致相等；而線畫在哪裡，即使每一票都照同樣的方式計算，也可能改變誰會贏。

Same Voters, Different Lines 同樣的選民，不同的線

The map is invented. Every square has the same number of people, and its color shows which of two invented groups the voters there belong to; to keep it simple, each square is all one group. Orange squares also carry stripes, so the two groups can be told apart without color. The colors were chosen to avoid the red and blue that American news uses for the two parties. Choose a way to divide the map into eight districts of equal population. White walls mark the boundaries, and a pillar in each district takes the color of the group that wins it; the labels give each district's vote in squares, green first.
這張地圖是虛構的。每一格人數相同，顏色代表那裡的選民屬於兩群虛構選民中的哪一群（為了簡單起見，每一格都只有一群）；橘色格子另外加上斜紋，不靠顏色也分得出來；顏色刻意避開美國新聞替兩大黨用的紅色與藍色。選一種把地圖切成八個人口相等選區的方法：白色的牆是邊界，每一區中間的柱子是贏下那一區的那一群的顏色；標籤寫出每一區的格數，綠在前、橘在後。

Same voters, different lines · 同樣的選民，不同的線

An invented map: 64 squares with the same number of people in each. Green voters are the majority in 40 squares (62.5 percent), orange voters in 24 (37.5 percent). Eight districts of eight squares each. To keep it simple, every voter in a square belongs to the same group.一張虛構的地圖：64 格，每格人數相同。綠色選民在 40 格占多數（62.5%），橘色選民在 24 格占多數（37.5%）。要切成八個選區，每區 8 格。為了簡單起見，每一格裡的選民都屬於同一群。

Columns照欄切

Each column is a district. Five columns are green and three are orange, so green wins 5 districts and orange wins 3, matching their shares of the voters.每一欄是一個選區。五欄是綠、三欄是橘，所以綠贏 5 區、橘贏 3 區，和兩群選民的比例一致。

Rows照列切

Each row is a district. Every row has five green squares and three orange ones, so green wins all 8 districts. Orange voters are spread so thin that they win nowhere. When lines are drawn this way on purpose, it is called cracking.每一列是一個選區。每一列都有五格綠、三格橘，所以綠贏下全部 8 區。橘色選民被分散得太薄，哪一區都贏不了。如果是刻意這樣畫，就叫做「分散」（cracking）。

Boxes切成方塊

Each district is a neat block of two rows and four columns. The four blocks on the left are all green; the four on the right have six orange squares and two green ones. Green voters are concentrated in four districts that they win by huge margins, so the result is 4 to 4 even though green has most of the voters. When lines are drawn this way on purpose, it is called packing.每一區是兩列四欄的整齊方塊。左邊四塊全是綠；右邊四塊各有六格橘、兩格綠。綠色選民被集中在四個選區、以極大的差距勝出，所以雖然綠色選民比較多，結果卻是 4 比 4。如果是刻意這樣畫，就叫做「集中」（packing）。

Unit 7: Who Draws the Lines?

Every ten years the census counts the people, and the 435 House seats are divided among the states. Then comes a second step, redistricting: each state with more than one seat redraws the boundaries of its districts so that they match the new population. After the 2020 census, the apportionment counts were released on April 26, 2021, and the detailed data that states use to draw districts were first released on August 12, 2021. Six states had only one seat, so the whole state is a single district. The other forty-four had lines to draw.

每十年，人口普查清點人口，眾議院的 435 席依此分配給各州。接著是第二步——重劃選區：每一個有兩席以上的州，都要重劃選區界線，讓它們符合新的人口分布。2020 年普查之後，分配席次用的人口數在 2021 年 4 月 26 日公布，各州劃選區用的詳細資料則在 2021 年 8 月 12 日首度公布。有六個州只有一席，整個州就是一個選區；其餘四十四州都有線要畫。

The Constitution says nothing about districts. It says that the “Times, Places and Manner” of electing representatives shall be set by each state's legislature, but that Congress may change those rules by law. Congress has used that power at times. An 1842 law required districts of contiguous territory, later laws added equal population and compactness, and those standards lapsed in 1929. Since a law of 1967, every state with more than one representative must elect them from single-member districts, one representative for each district, instead of electing several statewide.

憲法沒有提到選區。它規定，選舉眾議員的「時間、地點與方式」由各州議會訂定，但國會可以用法律更改這些規定。國會有時會行使這項權力：1842 年的法律要求選區的土地要相連，後來的法律又加上人口相等與緊湊，這些標準在 1929 年失效。1967 年的一條法律規定，凡是有兩席以上眾議員的州，都必須以單一選區選出——每區選出一位，而不是全州一起選出好幾位。

Who draws the lines depends on the state. According to the Congressional Research Service, in the redistricting that followed the 2020 census, 33 states gave the job to their state legislatures, where in most cases the governor can veto the plan like any other bill. In 11 states, a redistricting commission was primarily responsible. In six of the legislature states, a commission also gave advice. Commissions differ: some include members of the public chosen by methods meant to be nonpartisan or bipartisan, while others include officials or political appointees.

誰來畫線，各州不同。依國會研究服務處的資料，在 2020 年普查之後的重劃中，有 33 州把這項工作交給州議會，其中大多數州的州長可以像否決其他法案一樣否決選區圖；有 11 州主要由選區劃分委員會負責；在由州議會畫圖的州裡，另有六州設有提供意見的委員會。委員會也各不相同：有些包括以力求無黨派或兩黨均衡的方式選出的一般民眾，有些則包括官員或政治任命的人。

For much of American history, district populations could differ widely. Tennessee, for example, had not reapportioned its legislature since a 1901 law. In 1962, in Baker v. Carr, the Supreme Court held that federal courts could hear claims that unequal districts denied the equal protection of the laws. Two years later, in Wesberry v. Sanders, it held that congressional districts within a state must be drawn so that “as nearly as is practicable one man's vote in a congressional election is to be worth as much as another's.” Reynolds v. Sims applied a similar rule to state legislatures.

美國歷史上很長一段時間，各選區的人口可以相差很多。例如田納西州，自從 1901 年的一條法律之後就沒有重新分配州議會的席次。1962 年的 Baker v. Carr 案，最高法院判定聯邦法院可以受理「選區不均等剝奪了法律的平等保護」這類主張。兩年後的 Wesberry v. Sanders 案，法院判定一州之內的國會選區必須劃分得「在可行的範圍內盡量讓國會選舉中每個人的一票都和別人的一票同樣有分量」。Reynolds v. Sims 案則把類似的規則適用到州議會。

The word for drawing lines to favor one side is older than most of these rules. In 1812, Massachusetts redrew its state senate districts, and Governor Elbridge Gerry signed the plan, although, according to the Library of Congress, he found it “highly disagreeable.” The word “Gerry-mander” first appeared in the Boston Gazette on March 26, 1812. One district in Essex County had such a twisted shape that cartoons drew it as a winged dragon. The best-known cartoon was drawn by Elkanah Tisdale; who first coined the word has never been identified.

用畫線來偏袒某一方，這個詞比上面大多數的規則都還要早。1812 年，麻薩諸塞州重劃州參議院的選區，州長傑利（Elbridge Gerry）簽署了這個方案——依美國國會圖書館的說法，他其實覺得這個方案「極不愉快」。「Gerry-mander」這個字最早在 1812 年 3 月 26 日的《波士頓公報》出現。艾塞克斯郡的一個選區形狀非常扭曲，漫畫把它畫成一條有翅膀的龍。最有名的那幅漫畫出自 Elkanah Tisdale 之手；至於是誰最先造出這個字，一直無法確定。

The Congressional Research Service describes gerrymandering as redistricting “intended to unfairly favor one group's interests over another's,” and names two common techniques. Packing concentrates voters who are expected to vote alike into a few districts, where they win by far more than they need. Cracking spreads them across many districts, so that they fall short of a majority in each. The model above uses invented voters to show the arithmetic: with the same voters and districts of equal population, three ways of drawing the lines produce results of 5 to 3, 8 to 0, and 4 to 4.

國會研究服務處把「傑利蠑螈」（gerrymandering）描述為「意圖不公平地偏袒某一群人的利益，勝過另一群人」的重劃，並舉出兩種常見的手法。「集中」（packing）是把預期會投相同的選民集中在少數幾個選區，讓他們以遠超過所需的票數獲勝；「分散」（cracking）是把他們分散到許多選區，讓他們在每一區都差一點過半。上面的模型用虛構的選民示範這筆算術：同樣的選民、人口相等的選區，三種畫法得到 5 比 3、8 比 0 和 4 比 4 三種結果。

Boundary lines have also been drawn to shut people out of voting because of their race, according to claims the courts have heard. In 1957, Alabama's legislature redrew the boundaries of the city of Tuskegee into what the Supreme Court described as “an uncouth twenty-eight-sided figure.” According to the complaint, the change removed all but four or five of the city's 400 Black voters and not a single white voter. In Gomillion v. Lightfoot in 1960, the Court held that such a claim could proceed under the Fifteenth Amendment. Five years later, the Voting Rights Act of 1965 barred voting practices that deny or abridge the right to vote on account of race or color.

根據法院審理過的主張，界線也曾被用來因種族而把人擋在投票之外。1957 年，阿拉巴馬州議會把塔斯基吉市的邊界重劃成最高法院形容的「一個粗陋的二十八邊形」。依起訴書的說法，這次變更把該市 400 位黑人選民中除了四、五人以外全部劃出市外，卻沒有劃出任何一位白人選民。1960 年的 Gomillion v. Lightfoot 案，法院判定這樣的主張可以依第十五條修正案進行審理。五年後，1965 年的《投票權法》禁止以種族或膚色為由，剝奪或限縮投票權的投票作法。

Beyond equal population and the Voting Rights Act, many states add their own rules. CRS reported in 2026 that 29 states require congressional districts to be compact and 33 have language requiring them to be contiguous; many also ask mapmakers to respect county and city lines or communities of interest. These goals can pull against each other, and none of them guarantees a particular result. The model's neat boxes are compact and contiguous, yet they give a group with most of the voters only half of the seats. Who should draw the lines is the question in the design debate below.

除了人口相等與《投票權法》，許多州還加上自己的規則。國會研究服務處 2026 年的報告指出，有 29 州要求國會選區要緊湊、33 州有要求選區相連的規定；許多州也要求劃選區的人尊重郡市界線或「利益相近的社群」。這些目標可能互相拉扯，而且沒有一項能保證某種結果。模型裡整齊的方塊既緊湊又相連，卻讓一群占多數的選民只拿到一半的席次。誰應該畫線，正是下面「制度的爭論」要討論的問題。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Why does the Constitution's silence about districts matter for how redistricting works today?

Because the Constitution leaves the manner of elections to state legislatures, subject to change by Congress, the rules come from a mix of state law, federal statutes, and court decisions. That is why practices differ so much from state to state and why the rules have changed over time.

In the model, the neat boxes are compact and contiguous but give a majority group only half the seats. What does that suggest about using shape rules alone to judge a map?

Shape rules prevent some obvious abuses, but they do not by themselves guarantee any particular result. The same voters can produce very different seat counts under maps that all look neat, so people who judge maps also look at other things, such as how the results compare with the votes.

Why might the Supreme Court have treated unequal district populations as a question of equal protection?

If one district has twice as many people as another, each voter in the larger district has about half the influence on choosing a representative. The Court saw that as treating citizens unequally, which the Fourteenth Amendment forbids the states to do.

    Three texts behind every district map 每一張選區圖背後的三段文字

    The constitutional words are copied from the National Archives transcription; the statute is from the United States Code.
    憲法原文照美國國家檔案館的謄本逐字抄錄；法律條文出自《美國法典》。

    • Article I, Section 4第一條第四項

      The Times, Places and Manner of holding Elections for Senators and Representatives, shall be prescribed in each State by the Legislature thereof; but the Congress may at any time by Law make or alter such Regulations, except as to the Places of chusing Senators.

      選舉參議員與眾議員的時間、地點與方式，由各州議會規定；但國會得隨時以法律制定或更改這些規定，唯選舉參議員的地點除外。

      The first move belongs to the states, the last word to Congress. “Chusing” is the parchment's spelling.第一步屬於各州，最後的決定權屬於國會。「chusing」是羊皮紙原件的拼法。

    • Article I, Section 2 (the census)第一條第二項（人口普查）

      The actual Enumeration shall be made within three Years after the first Meeting of the Congress of the United States, and within every subsequent Term of ten Years, in such Manner as they shall by Law direct.

      實際的人口清點，應於合眾國國會第一次集會後三年內進行，此後每十年進行一次，方式依國會以法律指示。

    The Design Debate · 制度的爭論

    Elected legislators, or a separate commission? 民選的州議會，還是另設的委員會？

    Every state with more than one House seat must draw districts of nearly equal population. The open question is who should hold the pen. Americans have argued about state control of election rules since 1787, and states today answer the question differently. Each side is stated here in its strongest form, and the card does not choose between them.
    每一個有兩席以上的州，都必須畫出人口大致相等的選區；還沒有定論的是：筆該握在誰手上？美國人從 1787 年就在爭論各州對選舉規則的掌控，而今天各州的答案也不同。這裡把兩邊各用最強的版本寫出來，不替它們分高下。

    Let the elected legislature draw由民選的州議會來畫

    Let a separate commission draw由另設的委員會來畫

    News English · 新聞英語

    Eight terms for reading about redistricting 讀選區劃分新聞用得到的八個詞

    Listen to each term and its example sentence, then try one of your own. The examples describe the process, not any state's current map.

    Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

    Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

    ✗ Myth · 迷思The Constitution says how districts must be drawn.憲法規定了選區要怎麼畫。

    ✓ Fact · 事實It does not mention districts. Single-member districts come from a 1967 federal law, equal population from Supreme Court decisions, and most other rules from state law.憲法沒有提到選區。單一選區來自 1967 年的聯邦法律，人口相等來自最高法院的判決，其他大部分規則來自州法。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Elbridge Gerry drew the first gerrymander and invented the word.傑利親手畫出了第一個傑利蠑螈，也發明了這個字。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Gerry signed a plan he reportedly found “highly disagreeable.” The cartoon was drawn by Elkanah Tisdale, and the person who coined the word has never been identified.傑利簽署了一個據說讓他覺得「極不愉快」的方案。漫畫是 Elkanah Tisdale 畫的，而造出這個字的人一直無法確定。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思If every district has the same population, the map must be fair to everyone.只要每個選區人口一樣，這張地圖就對每個人都公平。

    ✓ Fact · 事實Equal population is required, but as the model shows, maps with equal populations can produce very different results from the same voters.人口相等是必要的，但正如模型所示，人口相等的地圖，用同樣的選民也可能得到非常不同的結果。

    ✗ Myth · 迷思Congress draws the districts for every state.國會替每一州畫選區。

    ✓ Fact · 事實The states draw them, through their legislatures or commissions. Congress sets a few national rules, such as single-member districts.選區是各州畫的，由州議會或委員會負責。國會只訂幾條全國性的規則，例如單一選區。
