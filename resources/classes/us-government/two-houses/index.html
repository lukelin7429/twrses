Why Are There Two Houses?國會為什麼有兩院？

Choose a state and see its seats light up in both chambers: many in the House if it is large, always two in the Senate.
選一州，看它在兩個議院的座位亮起來：大州在眾議院有很多席，參議院永遠是兩席。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

Congress has two chambers built on two different ways of counting the states, because the framers could not agree on one. The House gives seats by population; the Senate gives every state an equal vote. The compromise of 1787 built that disagreement into the government, and every law must now win a majority in both.
國會的兩院，用的是兩種不同的方式來算各州的分量，因為制憲者對該用哪一種談不攏。眾議院依人口分配席次，參議院每州表決權平等。1787 年的妥協把這個分歧寫進了政府，從此每一條法律都必須在兩院各自過半。

1789: the Constitution's list for thirteen states. Unit 5: Why Are There Two Houses?

In the Congress of the Articles of Confederation, each state's delegation cast one vote, whatever the state's size. When delegates met in Philadelphia in 1787, the large states wanted that rule gone. The Virginia Plan, presented on May 29, proposed a legislature of two branches, with votes in proportion to each state's wealth or free population. On June 15 the New Jersey Plan answered by proposing to strengthen the existing Congress, where each state kept its single vote. For weeks the convention argued over this one question, and several delegates feared it would break up the meeting before a constitution could be written.

在《邦聯條例》下的國會，每一州的代表團不論州的大小，都只投一票。1787 年各州代表在費城開會時，大州想廢掉這個規則。5 月 29 日提出的維吉尼亞方案，主張立法機關分成兩院，表決權依各州的財力或自由人口比例分配。6 月 15 日的紐澤西方案則回應：加強現有的國會，讓每州保有自己的一票。好幾個星期，會議就為這一個問題爭論不休；好幾位代表擔心，憲法還沒寫出來，會議就會因此破局。

The answer, adopted on July 16, 1787, is usually called the Great Compromise or the Connecticut Compromise, after its architects, the Connecticut delegates Roger Sherman and Oliver Ellsworth. One chamber, the House of Representatives, would be apportioned by population. The other, the Senate, would give each state an equal vote; a week later, on July 23, the delegates settled on two senators per state. The vote was five states to four, with Massachusetts divided, and it was taken by state delegations, not by individuals. The large states had won one chamber; the small states had kept equality in the other.

1787 年 7 月 16 日通過的解答，通常叫做「大妥協」或「康乃狄克妥協」，以紀念它的主要設計者——康乃狄克的代表雪曼與艾斯華斯。一院——眾議院——依人口分配；另一院——參議院——每州表決權平等；一週後的 7 月 23 日，代表們才定下每州兩名參議員。表決結果是五州對四州，麻薩諸塞代表團內部意見分歧；而且是以州代表團為單位投票，不是一人一票。大州贏得了一院；小州在另一院保住了平等。

The two chambers were also designed to differ in character. Representatives serve two-year terms, so they must face the voters often. Senators serve six-year terms, and only about a third of the Senate is elected every two years, so the chamber changes slowly. A representative must be at least 25 years old and a citizen for seven years; a senator, at least 30 and a citizen for nine. The framers expected the House to reflect the mood of the people quickly and the Senate to be steadier. Both must pass the same bill, in the same words, before it can become law.

兩院的性格也刻意設計得不一樣。眾議員任期兩年，所以必須常常面對選民；參議員任期六年，而且每兩年只改選大約三分之一，所以這一院變化得慢。眾議員至少 25 歲、成為公民至少七年；參議員至少 30 歲、成為公民至少九年。制憲者期待眾議院很快反映民意，參議院則比較穩定。兩院必須通過同一個法案、而且字句完全相同，它才可能成為法律。

Counting people for the House raised a harder question. The Constitution counted each enslaved person as three fifths of a person when dividing seats, and it excluded “Indians not taxed.” The three-fifths rule gave the slaveholding states more seats in the House, and therefore more electoral votes, than their free population alone would have earned, while the people counted had no vote at all. After the Civil War, the Fourteenth Amendment replaced the rule: seats would be based on “the whole number of persons in each State,” though it still excluded “Indians not taxed.” The original compromise over representation cannot be told honestly without this part.

為了眾議院而計算人口，引出一個更難的問題。憲法在分配席次時，把每一個被奴役的人算作五分之三個人，並排除「不納稅的印第安人」。五分之三條款讓蓄奴州在眾議院得到的席次——因而在選舉人團得到的票——比只算自由人口時更多，而那些被計算的人自己根本沒有投票權。內戰之後，第十四條修正案取代了這個規則：席次依「各州的總人數」計算，但仍排除「不納稅的印第安人」。不講這一段，就不可能誠實地講那場關於代表權的妥協。

Senators were not at first chosen by the voters. The Constitution gave that power to the state legislatures, partly so that the states as governments would have a voice in the national government. Over time, deadlocked legislatures left Senate seats empty, and many Americans came to see the system as indirect and open to corruption. The Seventeenth Amendment, ratified in 1913, provided that senators would be “elected by the people” of each state. The equal vote of every state in the Senate did not change, and Article V protects it: no state may lose that equality without its own consent.

參議員起初不是由選民選出的。憲法把這個權力交給州議會，部分原因是讓各州以政府的身分在全國政府裡有發言權。後來，州議會陷入僵局而讓參議員席位空著的情形時有發生，許多美國人也認為這個制度太間接、容易舞弊。1913 年批准的第十七條修正案規定，參議員由各州「人民選出」。每州在參議院的平等表決權沒有改變，而且受第五條保護：任何一州未經自己同意，都不得被剝奪這份平等。

The size of the House is set by law, not by the Constitution. The first House had 65 members. It generally grew with the population, though it shrank once, after the 1840 census, until 1911, when Congress set the number at 433, with one seat added for each of the two territories expected to become states. Since 1913 the House has had 435 voting members, except from 1959 to 1963, when it briefly had 437 after Alaska and Hawaii joined the Union. After each census the 435 seats are divided among the states by a formula, and every state receives at least one. After the 2020 census, California had 52 seats, and six states had one each.

眾議院的人數是法律定的，不是憲法定的。第一屆眾議院有 65 位議員。人數大致隨人口增加（1840 年普查後曾減少一次），直到 1911 年國會把人數定為 433 席，並為預計將成為州的兩個領地各加一席。從 1913 年起，眾議院一直有 435 位有表決權的議員，只有 1959 到 1963 年例外：阿拉斯加和夏威夷加入聯邦後，暫時有 437 席。每次普查之後，這 435 席依公式分給各州，每州至少一席。2020 年普查之後，加利福尼亞有 52 席，有六個州各只有一席。

The two chambers also hold different powers. Bills for raising revenue must start in the House. The House alone can impeach a federal official, and the Senate alone can try the case, with conviction requiring two thirds of the senators present. The Senate also gives its “advice and consent” to treaties, which need two thirds of the senators present, and to the president's nominations of judges and other officers. Each chamber writes its own rules. For example, under Senate rules, ending debate on most bills takes sixty votes, so more than forty senators can keep a bill that a simple majority supports from coming to a vote.

兩院的權力也不同。增加歲入的法案必須從眾議院提出。只有眾議院能彈劾聯邦官員，只有參議院能審判，定罪需要出席參議員的三分之二。參議院也對條約給予「建議與同意」——條約需要出席參議員的三分之二——並對總統提名的法官和其他官員行使同意權。兩院各自訂定議事規則。例如依參議院的規則，大多數法案要結束辯論需要六十票，所以超過四十位參議員，就能讓一個得到簡單多數支持的法案無法進入表決。

How the Senate's equal vote should be judged is still argued. Both sides start from the same facts: in 2020 the most populous state had more than 68 times as many residents as the least populous, and each has two senators. Supporters say the design protects smaller states, makes laws win support across many regions, and reflects a union of states as well as of people. Critics say it gives some voters far more weight than others, lets senators representing a minority of Americans block a majority, and applies a rule made for thirteen states to fifty. This lesson presents both arguments without deciding between them.

參議院的平等表決權該怎麼評價，至今仍有爭論。兩邊的出發點是同一組事實：2020 年，人口最多的州居民是人口最少的州的 68 倍以上，兩州各有兩位參議員。支持的人說，這個設計保護小州、讓法律必須得到許多地區的支持，也反映美國既是人民的聯合、也是各州的聯合。批評的人說，它讓某些選民的分量遠大於其他人、讓只代表少數美國人的參議員能擋住多數，也把一條為十三州訂的規則原封不動用在五十州。這一課把兩邊的論點都呈現出來，不替它們分高下。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Why did the large states and the small states each think their own plan was fair?

The large states argued that a government acting on people should count people, so more residents should mean more seats. The small states argued that the states had entered the union as equals and would be outvoted on everything if votes followed population. Each side was applying a different idea of whom Congress represents: individual people or member states.

The framers gave the House two-year terms and the Senate six-year terms. What did they hope each choice would achieve?

Short terms keep representatives close to the voters, who can replace them every two years. Long, staggered terms make the Senate change slowly, so it can resist passing moods and take a longer view. The cost of the first is that members are always campaigning; the cost of the second is that the Senate may respond more slowly to public opinion.

Why can the original compromise over representation not be told honestly without the three-fifths clause?

The compromise decided how to count people for the House, and the three-fifths clause was part of that count. It gave the slaveholding states extra seats and electoral votes based on enslaved people who could not vote. Leaving it out would make the compromise look like a simple bargain between large and small states, when it also settled power between slave and free states.

What Does the Text Say? · 條文怎麼寫？

Four clauses that built two chambers 蓋出兩個議院的四段條文

The words are copied from the National Archives transcriptions, which keep the spelling and capitalization of the parchment.
原文照美國國家檔案館的謄本逐字抄錄，保留羊皮紙原件的拼法與大寫。

  • Article I, Section 2, Clause 1第一條第二項第一款

    The House of Representatives shall be composed of Members chosen every second Year by the People of the several States, and the Electors in each State shall have the Qualifications requisite for Electors of the most numerous Branch of the State Legislature.

    眾議院由各州人民每兩年選出的議員組成；各州選舉人應具備該州議會中人數最多一院之選舉人所需的資格。

    “Every second Year” and “by the People”: from the start, the House was the chamber closest to the voters.「每兩年」「由人民」：眾議院從一開始就是離選民最近的一院。

  • Article I, Section 2, Clause 3 (1787; later changed)第一條第二項第三款（1787；後來修改）

    Representatives and direct Taxes shall be apportioned among the several States which may be included within this Union, according to their respective Numbers, which shall be determined by adding to the whole Number of free Persons, including those bound to Service for a Term of Years, and excluding Indians not taxed, three fifths of all other Persons.

Notes of the convention, 1787 · 1787 年的制憲會議筆記

Ellsworth and Franklin on a compromise (excerpts)艾斯華斯與富蘭克林談妥協（節錄）

James Madison's notes: Oliver Ellsworth of Connecticut on June 29, 1787, and Benjamin Franklin of Pennsylvania on June 30, 1787.麥迪遜的筆記：康乃狄克的艾斯華斯（1787 年 6 月 29 日）與賓夕法尼亞的富蘭克林（1787 年 6 月 30 日）。

… He hoped it would become a ground of compromise with regard to the 2d. branch. We were partly national; partly federal. The proportional representation in the first branch was conformable to the national principle & would secure the large States agst. the small. An equality of voices was conformable to the federal principle and was necessary to secure the Small States agst. the large. He trusted that on this middle ground a compromise would take place. He did not see that it could on any other. And if no compromise should take place, our meeting would not only be in vain but worse than in vain.

The diversity of opinions turns on two points. If a proportional representation takes place, the small States contend that their liberties will be in danger. If an equality of votes is to be put in its place, the large States say their money will be in danger. When a broad table is to be made, and the edges of planks do not fit, the artist takes a little from both, and makes a good joint.

他希望這能成為第二院方面妥協的基礎。我們一部分是全國性的，一部分是聯邦性的。第一院依比例代表，符合全國性的原則，能保護大州不受小州侵害；表決權平等，符合聯邦性的原則，是保護小州不受大州侵害所必需的。他相信妥協會在這個中間的立場上達成，看不出在別的立場上能夠達成。如果達不成妥協，我們這次開會不但白費，而且比白費還糟。

意見的分歧在於兩點。如果採用比例代表，小州主張它們的自由會有危險；如果改用平等表決，大州說它們的錢會有危險。要做一張寬桌子，木板的邊緣接不攏時，工匠會從兩邊各削掉一點，做出一個好的接縫。

Argument for ratification, 1788 · 1788 年支持批准的論述

The Federalist No. 62 (excerpts)《聯邦論》第 62 篇（節錄）

Publius. The Library of Congress lists the author as Alexander Hamilton or James Madison.署名 Publius。美國國會圖書館列出的作者是漢彌爾頓或麥迪遜。

The equality of representation in the Senate is another point, which, being evidently the result of compromise between the opposite pretensions of the large and the small States, does not call for much discussion. … In this spirit it may be remarked, that the equal vote allowed to each State is at once a constitutional recognition of the portion of sovereignty remaining in the individual States, and an instrument for preserving that residuary sovereignty.

In this point of view, a senate, as a second branch of the legislative assembly, distinct from, and dividing the power with, a first, must be in all cases a salutary check on the government. It doubles the security to the people, by requiring the concurrence of two distinct bodies in schemes of usurpation or perfidy, where the ambition or corruption of one would otherwise be sufficient.

The Design Debate · 制度的爭論

Equal votes for states, or equal weight for people? 每州平等，還是每人平等？

The House settles representation by population. The argument is about the Senate: should every state have the same voice, however many people live there? Delegates argued it in 1787, and Americans still do. Each side is stated here in its strongest form, and the card does not choose between them.
眾議院依人口分配代表，沒有爭議；爭的是參議院：不論住了多少人，每一州都該有一樣的發言權嗎？1787 年的代表為此爭論，美國人到今天也還在爭論。這裡把兩邊各用最強的版本寫出來，不替它們分高下。

Should the Senate give every state the same two votes?參議院應該讓每一州都有相同的兩票嗎？

One position · 一種立場

Keep an equal vote for every state保留每州平等的一票

  • The United States is a union of states as well as of people. An equal vote recognizes that each state keeps part of its own authority.美國既是人民的聯合，也是各州的聯合。平等的一票承認每一州仍保有自己的一部分權力。
  • Without it, a handful of populous states could outvote the rest on every question. Laws must instead win support across many states and regions.沒有它，少數人口多的州就能在每個問題上壓過其他各州；有了它，法律必須在許多州、許多地區都得到支持。
  • Two chambers chosen on different principles make every law pass two different majorities, which slows hasty or harmful legislation.兩院依不同的原則組成，每一條法律都要通過兩種不同的多數，這能減緩倉促或有害的立法。
He said as the States would remain possessed of certain individual rights, each State ought to be able to protect itself: otherwise a few large States will rule the rest.Roger Sherman of Connecticut, as recorded in Madison's notes, June 11, 1787他說，既然各州仍保有某些各自的權利，每一州就應該能保護自己；否則少數幾個大州就會統治其餘各州。——康乃狄克的雪曼，麥迪遜筆記，1787 年 6 月 11 日

Another position · 另一種立場

Give every person equal weight讓每個人的分量相等

  • Government acts on individuals, not on states. Each person's voice in making the laws should count the same, wherever that person lives.政府作用的對象是個人，不是州。在制定法律時，每個人的聲音都應該一樣重，不論他住在哪裡。
  • Equal state votes let senators who together represent a minority of Americans block what senators representing a majority want.各州平等的表決權，讓合起來只代表少數美國人的參議員，能擋住代表多數美國人的參議員想做的事。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思The Constitution says the House has 435 members.憲法規定眾議院有 435 位議員。

✓ Fact · 事實It does not. The Constitution sets only a ceiling of one representative for every thirty thousand people and at least one per state. The number 435 comes from laws passed in 1911 and 1929.沒有。憲法只規定每三萬人最多一位眾議員、每州至少一位。435 這個數字來自 1911 年與 1929 年的法律。

✗ Myth · 迷思Senators have always been elected by the voters.參議員一直都是選民選出來的。

✓ Fact · 事實Until the Seventeenth Amendment was ratified in 1913, the Constitution gave the choice to the state legislatures.直到 1913 年第十七條修正案批准之前，憲法把這個選擇交給州議會。

✗ Myth · 迷思Larger states have more senators.大州的參議員比較多。

✓ Fact · 事實Every state has exactly two. Population matters only in the House.每一州剛好兩位。人口只在眾議院才有影響。

✗ Myth · 迷思The House and the Senate do the same job twice.眾議院和參議院只是把同一件事做兩遍。

✓ Fact · 事實Both must pass every law, but each also has powers the other lacks: the House starts revenue bills and impeaches, and the Senate tries impeachments, approves treaties, and confirms nominees.每一條法律都要兩院通過，但兩院也各有對方沒有的權力：眾議院提出歲入法案與彈劾，參議院審判彈劾、同意條約與人事。

Activity 1 · 課堂或自學活動一

Rerun the Convention 重開一次制憲會議

You need · 準備材料

  • The 1789 House seats from the model (or Article I, Section 2)模型裡 1789 年的眾議院席次（或憲法第一條第二項）
  • Thirteen cards, one per state十三張卡片，每州一張

Steps · 步驟

  1. Divide the class into state delegations, large and small, using the 1789 seats as a guide to size. (Rhode Island never sent delegates to the convention.)把全班分成大大小小的州代表團，依 1789 年的席次決定大小。（羅德島從未派代表出席制憲會議。）
  2. Each delegation argues for the Virginia Plan or the New Jersey Plan, depending on its interest.每個代表團依自己的利益，主張維吉尼亞方案或紐澤西方案。
  3. Vote by delegation, one vote per state, as the convention did. Then try to find a compromise that can pass.照制憲會議的方式，以代表團為單位投票、每州一票。接著試著找出一個能通過的妥協。
  4. Compare your result with the July 16, 1787 compromise.把你們的結果和 1787 年 7 月 16 日的妥協比一比。

The point is to feel why one vote per delegation made the small states hard to outvote at the convention itself.
重點是體會：為什麼在制憲會議上「每州一票」讓小州很難被壓過。

Activity 2 · 課堂或自學活動二

One Seat, How Many People? 一席，代表多少人？

You need · 準備材料

  • The “How Much Is One Seat Worth?” table above上面的「一票的分量」表格
  • A calculator計算機

Steps · 步驟

  1. Choose five states of different sizes and record residents per representative and per senator.選五個大小不同的州，記下每位眾議員與每位參議員代表的居民數。
  2. Make two bar charts, one for each chamber, using the same scale.用同一個刻度畫兩張長條圖，一院一張。
  3. Write two sentences: one describing what the charts show, and one stating an argument from either side of the design debate that the charts could support.寫兩句話：一句描述圖表呈現的事實，一句寫出「制度的爭論」任一邊可以用這張圖支持的論點。

Keep the description and the argument separate: the first should be something everyone can agree on.
把描述和論點分開：第一句應該是每個人都能同意的事實。
