Why Split the Power Three Ways?權力為什麼要分成三份？

Pass a bill, veto it, override the veto: choose a move in a 3D model of Washington and watch an arrow fly from the branch that acts to the branch it checks. Read Montesquieu and Federalist No. 47 in the original, then work out what happens to a bill after a veto.
通過法案、否決、推翻否決——在華盛頓的 3D 模型裡選一招，看箭頭從出手的部門飛向被牽制的部門。讀孟德斯鳩與《聯邦論》第四十七篇的原文，再算算一項法案被否決之後還有幾條路。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

The Constitution divides the work of government among three branches so that no single person or group holds all of it. Then it gives each branch a limited share in the work of the others, so that each can slow or stop the other two. The first idea is called the separation of powers. The second is called checks and balances.
憲法把政府的工作分給三個部門，讓任何一個人或一群人都拿不到全部的權力；接著又讓每個部門在別的部門的工作裡各有一小份，於是每一個都能拖慢或擋下另外兩個。前一個想法叫權力分立，後一個叫制衡。

This move · 這一招

Congress ▸ The President · 國會 ▸ 總統

Congress passes a bill國會通過法案

A bill must pass both the House and the Senate in the same form, which is already a check inside Congress. It is then presented to the President. This is an ordinary step, not a check on another branch, but it is where most exchanges begin.法案必須以相同的內容在眾議院和參議院都通過——這已經是國會內部的一道牽制。接著法案送交總統。這是平常的步驟，不算牽制別的部門，但大多數的往來從這裡開始。

Every Bill which shall have passed the House of Representatives and the Senate, shall, before it become a Law, be presented to the President of the United States;Article I, Section 7

What can the other side do? · 對方可以怎麼回應？

Congress ▸ The President · 國會 ▸ 總統

Congress overrides a veto國會推翻否決

If two thirds of the House and then two thirds of the Senate vote for the bill again, it becomes law without the President's signature.如果眾議院三分之二、接著參議院三分之二再次表決通過，法案不必總統簽署就成為法律。

If after such Reconsideration two thirds of that House shall agree to pass the Bill, it shall be sent, together with the Objections, to the other House, by which it shall likewise be reconsidered, and if approved by two thirds of that House, it shall become a Law.Article I, Section 7

In history · 歷史上Congress first overrode a veto on March 3, 1845, more than half a century after the first veto. It has done so 112 times in all (Senate records, read in October 2026).國會第一次推翻否決是在 1845 年 3 月 3 日，距離第一次否決已經超過半個世紀。到現在一共推翻過 112 次（參議院紀錄，2026 年 10 月查閱）。

Congress ▸ The President · 國會 ▸ 總統

Congress decides what money may be spent國會決定錢可以怎麼花

The executive branch runs the departments and the armed forces, but it may spend only what Congress has approved by law. This is often called the power of the purse.行政部門管理各部會和軍隊，但只能花國會以法律核准的錢。這常被稱為「錢包的權力」（power of the purse）。

No Money shall be drawn from the Treasury, but in Consequence of Appropriations made by Law;Article I, Section 9

Congress ▸ The President · 國會 ▸ 總統

The Senate refuses its consent參議院不同意

The President's nominees for the courts and for the top executive posts take office only if the Senate consents, and a treaty needs two thirds of the senators present. The Senate can vote no, or simply not act.總統提名的法官與行政部門高階官員，要參議院同意才能就任；條約則需要出席參議員三分之二同意。參議院可以表決否決，也可以乾脆不處理。

… he shall nominate, and by and with the Advice and Consent of the Senate, shall appoint Ambassadors, other public Ministers and Consuls, Judges of the supreme Court, and all other Officers of the United States …Article II, Section 2

In history · 歷史上In December 1795 the Senate voted 10 to 14 against George Washington's choice for chief justice, John Rutledge. It was the first Supreme Court nomination the Senate rejected; there have been twelve such rejections by vote.1795 年 12 月，參議院以 10 票對 14 票否決了華盛頓提名的首席大法官人選拉特利奇（John Rutledge）。這是參議院第一次否決最高法院的提名；經表決否決的一共有十二次。

What can the other side do? · 對方可以怎麼回應？

Congress ▸ The President · 國會 ▸ 總統

Congress impeaches and tries the President國會彈劾並審判總統

The House may impeach, which means to bring formal charges. The Senate then holds the trial, with the chief justice presiding when a President is tried, and conviction takes two thirds of the members present. The only penalties are removal from office and a bar on holding office again.眾議院可以提出彈劾，也就是正式提出指控。接著由參議院審判；受審的是總統時，由首席大法官主持。定罪需要出席議員三分之二同意。處罰只有免職，以及不得再擔任公職。

The President, Vice President and all civil Officers of the United States, shall be removed from Office on Impeachment for, and Conviction of, Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors.Article II, Section 4

In history · 歷史上In 1868 the vote in the Senate to convict President Andrew Johnson was 35 to 19, one vote short of two thirds, so he was acquitted and stayed in office. No President has ever been removed by conviction.1868 年，參議院以 35 票對 19 票表決是否將總統安德魯．詹森定罪——離三分之二只差一票，所以他留任。從來沒有一位總統因為被定罪而去職。

Congress ▸ The Courts · 國會 ▸ 法院

Congress impeaches and removes a judge國會彈劾法官並將其免職

Federal judges keep their offices “during good Behaviour,” which in practice means for life. The one way to remove a judge is the same process used for a President: impeachment by the House and conviction by two thirds of the Senate.聯邦法官「品行端正」即得繼續任職，實際上就是終身職。要把法官免職只有一條路，和對總統用的是同一套程序：眾議院彈劾，參議院三分之二定罪。

The Judges, both of the supreme and inferior Courts, shall hold their Offices during good Behaviour …Article III, Section 1

In history · 歷史上The Senate has convicted eight officials in impeachment trials since 1789. All eight were federal judges (Senate records, read in October 2026).1789 年以來，參議院在彈劾審判中一共將八名官員定罪，八人全是聯邦法官（參議院紀錄，2026 年 10 月查閱）。

Congress ▸ The Courts · 國會 ▸ 法院

Congress shapes the courts and can answer a ruling國會決定法院的編制，也可以回應判決

The Constitution creates only the Supreme Court. Congress creates the lower courts and sets the number of justices by law: six in 1789, and nine since 1869. When Congress and the states disagree with a ruling on the Constitution, they can amend the Constitution.憲法只設立了最高法院。下級法院由國會設立，大法官的人數也由國會以法律決定：1789 年是六位，1869 年以來是九位。國會與各州不同意法院對憲法的某項判決時，可以修憲。

To constitute Tribunals inferior to the supreme Court;Article I, Section 8

In history · 歷史上In Chisholm v. Georgia (1793) the Supreme Court let a citizen of one state sue another state in federal court. The Eleventh Amendment, ratified in 1795, reversed that result.1793 年的 Chisholm v. Georgia 案，最高法院准許一州的公民在聯邦法院控告另一個州。1795 年批准的第十一條修正案推翻了這個結果。

The President ▸ Congress · 總統 ▸ 國會

The President vetoes a bill總統否決法案

The President may refuse to sign a bill and send it back to Congress with written objections. The word “veto” is not in the Constitution; the text speaks of returning the bill.總統可以拒絕簽署，把法案連同書面的異議退回國會。憲法裡沒有 veto 這個字，條文說的是「退回」法案。

If he approve he shall sign it, but if not he shall return it, with his Objections to that House in which it shall have originated, who shall enter the Objections at large on their Journal, and proceed to reconsider it.Article I, Section 7

In history · 歷史上George Washington issued the first veto on April 5, 1792. Presidents have vetoed 2,599 bills and joint resolutions since then (Senate records, read in October 2026).華盛頓在 1792 年 4 月 5 日行使了第一次否決。從那時起，歷任總統一共否決了 2,599 項法案與聯合決議案（參議院紀錄，2026 年 10 月查閱）。

What can the other side do? · 對方可以怎麼回應？

The President ▸ The Courts · 總統 ▸ 法院

The President nominates the judges總統提名法官

Judges do not choose their own successors. Every federal judge is nominated by the President, so over time the elected branches decide who sits on the courts. The nomination then needs the Senate's consent.法官不能自己挑接班人。每一位聯邦法官都由總統提名，所以時間一久，是民選的部門在決定誰坐上法官席。提名之後還要參議院同意。

… he shall nominate, and by and with the Advice and Consent of the Senate, shall appoint … Judges of the supreme Court …Article II, Section 2

In history · 歷史上Presidents have sent the Senate 165 nominations for the Supreme Court since 1789 (Senate table, which runs through 2022).1789 年以來，歷任總統一共向參議院送出 165 次最高法院的提名（參議院的統計表，列到 2022 年）。

What can the other side do? · 對方可以怎麼回應？

The President ▸ The Courts · 總統 ▸ 法院

The President grants a pardon總統赦免

The President may set aside the punishment for a federal crime, usually after a court has convicted and sentenced the person. The power does not reach impeachment, and it does not reach crimes under state law. It is sometimes described as a check on the courts, although it works case by case.總統可以免除聯邦罪行的刑罰，通常是在法院定罪、判刑之後。這項權力不及於彈劾案，也不及於州法上的罪行。有人把它看成對法院的一種牽制，不過它是一個案子一個案子行使的。

… he shall have Power to grant Reprieves and Pardons for Offences against the United States, except in Cases of Impeachment.Article II, Section 2

The Courts ▸ Congress · 法院 ▸ 國會

The courts refuse to apply an unconstitutional law法院拒絕適用違憲的法律

When a case turns on a law that conflicts with the Constitution, a court follows the Constitution and treats the law as void. This is judicial review. The Constitution does not provide for it in so many words; the Supreme Court set out the reasoning in 1803.當一個案子牽涉到牴觸憲法的法律時，法院依據憲法判決，把那項法律當作無效。這就是司法審查。憲法並沒有明文規定這項權力；最高法院在 1803 年說明了它的道理。

It is emphatically the province and duty of the judicial department to say what the law is.Marbury v. Madison (1803), Chief Justice John Marshall

In history · 歷史上In Marbury v. Madison the Court found that part of the act that had organized the federal courts gave the Court a power the Constitution did not allow, and it declined to use that power.在 Marbury v. Madison 案裡，最高法院認定當初設立聯邦法院的那部法律，有一部分給了最高法院憲法所不允許的權力，因此拒絕行使那項權力。

What can the other side do? · 對方可以怎麼回應？

The Courts ▸ The President · 法院 ▸ 總統

The courts rule against an executive order法院判定行政命令不合法

Courts also decide whether the President and the departments have acted within the Constitution and the laws. If they have not, the court can order the action stopped.法院也判斷總統與各部會的作為有沒有超出憲法與法律的範圍。超出的話，法院可以下令停止。

In the framework of our Constitution, the President's power to see that the laws are faithfully executed refutes the idea that he is to be a lawmaker.Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (1952), Justice Hugo Black for the Court

In history · 歷史上In 1952 President Harry Truman ordered the Secretary of Commerce to take possession of most of the nation's steel mills. The Supreme Court held that neither a law nor the Constitution gave him that power, and that the order could not stand.1952 年，杜魯門總統下令商務部長接管全國大部分的鋼鐵廠。最高法院判定，沒有任何法律、憲法也沒有給他這項權力，這道命令不能成立。

Unit 2: Why Split the Power Three Ways?

In 1788, while the states were deciding whether to ratify the Constitution, James Madison named the danger it was built to avoid. “The accumulation of all powers legislative, executive and judiciary in the same hands,” he wrote in The Federalist No. 47, “… may justly be pronounced the very definition of tyranny.” The thought was not his own. Forty years earlier the French writer Montesquieu had argued that liberty cannot last where the power to make laws, the power to carry them out, and the power to judge are joined in one person or one body. Madison called him “the oracle who is always consulted and cited on this subject.” The practical question was how to keep those three powers apart.

1788 年，各州還在決定要不要批准憲法的時候，麥迪遜點出了這部憲法想要避開的危險。他在《聯邦論》第四十七篇寫道：「把立法、行政、司法的一切權力集中在同一批人手裡……完全可以說就是暴政的定義。」這個想法不是他發明的。四十年前，法國作家孟德斯鳩（Montesquieu）就主張：制定法律的權力、執行法律的權力和審判的權力，如果合在同一個人或同一個機構手上，自由就保不住。麥迪遜稱他是「談到這個題目，人人都要請教、都要引用的權威」。真正的難題是實際上要怎麼把這三種權力分開。

The first answer was separation. The Constitution gives the legislative power to Congress in Article I, the executive power to a President in Article II, and the judicial power to the courts in Article III. Each branch is chosen in a different way and for a different length of time: representatives for two years, senators for six, a President for four, and federal judges for as long as they show “good Behaviour.” The branches are also kept apart in their membership. No one who holds a federal office may sit in Congress at the same time, so a cabinet secretary cannot also be a senator. In a parliamentary system, by contrast, the ministers who run the government are usually members of the legislature.

第一個答案是「分開」。憲法在第一條把立法權交給國會，第二條把行政權交給總統，第三條把司法權交給法院。每個部門產生的方式不同，任期也不同：眾議員兩年、參議員六年、總統四年，聯邦法官則是「品行端正」就一直做下去。三個部門連成員也是分開的：擔任聯邦官職的人不能同時當國會議員，所以內閣部長不可能兼任參議員。相對地，在內閣制（parliamentary system）裡，負責施政的閣員通常就是國會議員。

Separation alone was not expected to hold. Madison warned in The Federalist No. 48 that “power is of an encroaching nature,” and he doubted that boundaries written into a document, which he called “parchment barriers,” would stop a determined branch. So the Constitution adds a second device. Rather than sealing the branches off from one another, it gives each a limited share in the work of the others. The President takes part in lawmaking through the veto. The Senate takes part in appointments. Congress can remove a President or a judge. This arrangement is called checks and balances. Madison argued that it keeps faith with Montesquieu, whose rule forbids one branch to hold the whole power of another.

光是分開，制憲者並不認為撐得住。麥迪遜在《聯邦論》第四十八篇警告：「權力具有侵略的本性」；他也懷疑，寫在文件裡的界線——他稱之為「羊皮紙上的柵欄」（parchment barriers）——擋不擋得住一個鐵了心要擴權的部門。於是憲法加上第二個設計：不是把三個部門彼此封死，而是讓每個部門在別的部門的工作裡各有一小份。總統靠否決參與立法；參議院參與人事任命；國會可以把總統或法官免職。這套安排叫做制衡（checks and balances）。麥迪遜主張，這樣並沒有違背孟德斯鳩的原則——那條原則禁止的，是一個部門握有另一個部門的「全部」權力。

Lawmaking shows how the checks work. A bill must pass both the House and the Senate, which is already a check inside Congress. It then goes to the President, who may sign it or return it with objections. That return is the veto, although the word never appears in the Constitution. Congress can still make the bill law if two thirds of each house vote to override. That is hard to do. According to the Senate's records, presidents have vetoed 2,599 bills and joint resolutions since 1789. More than a thousand were pocket vetoes, which cannot be overridden; of the 1,533 others, Congress has overridden 112. The first veto came in 1792, and the first override not until 1845.

從立法可以看出制衡怎麼運作。法案必須在眾議院和參議院都通過，這已經是國會內部的一道牽制。接著法案送到總統手上，總統可以簽署，也可以附上異議退回。這個「退回」就是否決（veto），雖然憲法裡從頭到尾沒有這個字。國會如果兩院各有三分之二表決推翻（override），法案仍然成為法律——但這很難做到。依參議院的紀錄，1789 年以來歷任總統一共否決了 2,599 項法案與聯合決議案，其中一千多項是不能推翻的「口袋否決」（pocket veto，學術名詞作「袋中否決」）；其餘 1,533 項裡，國會推翻了 112 項。第一次否決在 1792 年，第一次推翻要等到 1845 年。

Other checks run through appointments and money. The President chooses ambassadors, department heads, and federal judges, but only “by and with the Advice and Consent of the Senate,” and a treaty needs the agreement of two thirds of the senators present. The Senate has voted down a nomination to the Supreme Court twelve times, the first of them in 1795. Congress also holds what is often called the power of the purse. In the words of Article I, “No Money shall be drawn from the Treasury, but in Consequence of Appropriations made by Law.” A President commands the armed forces and directs the departments, but cannot lawfully spend money that Congress has not approved.

另外一些牽制走的是人事和錢。大使、各部會首長和聯邦法官由總統挑選，但必須「經參議院的建議與同意」；條約則需要出席參議員三分之二同意。參議院曾經十二次表決否決最高法院的提名，第一次在 1795 年。國會還握有常被稱為「錢包的權力」的那項權力——第一條的原文是：「除依法律所定的撥款外，不得從國庫提取任何款項。」總統統率軍隊、指揮各部會，但不能合法地花國會沒有核准的錢。

The most drastic check is removal. The House of Representatives may impeach, that is, bring formal charges against, the President, other civil officers, and judges for “Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors.” The Senate then holds a trial, and conviction requires two thirds of the members present. Removal has been rare. As of 2026 the House has impeached officials twenty-two times, and the Senate has convicted eight of them, every one a federal judge. No President has been removed in this way. In 1868 the vote in the Senate to convict President Andrew Johnson was 35 to 19, one vote short of two thirds, so he was acquitted.

最激烈的一道牽制是免職。眾議院可以對總統、其他文職官員和法官提出彈劾（impeach）——也就是正式提出指控——罪名是「叛國、賄賂或其他重罪與輕罪」。接著由參議院審判，定罪需要出席議員三分之二同意。真正被免職的很少。到 2026 年為止，眾議院一共彈劾過二十二次，參議院將其中八人定罪，八人全是聯邦法官。沒有一位總統是這樣去職的。1868 年，參議院表決是否將安德魯．詹森總統定罪，結果是 35 票對 19 票，離三分之二只差一票，所以他沒有被定罪。

The courts hold a check that the Constitution does not spell out. In Marbury v. Madison, decided in 1803, Chief Justice John Marshall wrote: “It is emphatically the province and duty of the judicial department to say what the law is.” The same opinion reasoned that a law contrary to the Constitution is void. This power, called judicial review, lets courts refuse to apply laws and executive actions that violate the Constitution. Yet the courts are checked as well. Judges are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate, and Congress decides how many justices sit on the Supreme Court. As Alexander Hamilton observed, the judiciary has “neither FORCE nor WILL, but merely judgment”: it depends on the executive branch to carry out its decisions.

法院握有的那道牽制，憲法並沒有明寫。1803 年判決的 Marbury v. Madison 案裡，首席大法官馬歇爾寫道：「說明法律是什麼，斷然是司法部門的職權與責任。」同一份意見書推論：牴觸憲法的法律是無效的。這項權力叫做司法審查（judicial review），法院可以據此拒絕適用違反憲法的法律和行政作為。不過法院自己也受到牽制：法官由總統提名、參議院同意；最高法院有幾位大法官由國會決定。而且正如漢彌爾頓所說，司法部門「既沒有武力，也沒有意志，只有判斷」——它的判決要靠行政部門去執行。

All of this has a cost. A system in which every branch can say no tends to be slow, and it can stall when the branches disagree, a condition that news reports call gridlock. Critics since the nineteenth century have argued that dividing power also divides responsibility, so that voters cannot easily tell whom to hold to account. Defenders answer that the delay is deliberate. Justice Louis Brandeis wrote in 1926 that the separation of powers was adopted “not to promote efficiency but to preclude the exercise of arbitrary power.” Whether that trade is worth making is a real argument about how governments should be built, and the Design Debate card in this lesson sets out both sides.

這一切是有代價的。每個部門都能說「不」的制度往往走得很慢，各部門意見不合時還可能停擺——新聞裡把這種狀況叫做僵局（gridlock）。從十九世紀起就有批評者主張：分散權力也就分散了責任，選民不容易看出該找誰負責。辯護的人則回答：慢是刻意的。布蘭戴斯大法官（Louis Brandeis）在 1926 年寫道，採用權力分立「不是為了提高效率，而是為了防止權力被恣意行使」。這筆交換值不值得，是關於政府該怎麼設計的一場真正的爭論；這一課的「制度的爭論」卡會把兩邊都寫出來。

Questions · 閱讀理解

Explain the difference between “separation of powers” and “checks and balances.” Why did Madison think the first would not work without the second?

Separation of powers means giving the three kinds of power to three different branches with different members. Checks and balances means giving each branch a small part in the others' work, such as the veto or the Senate's consent to appointments, so that it can resist them. Madison thought that written boundaries alone (“parchment barriers”) would not stop a branch from reaching for more power, because “power is of an encroaching nature.” Each branch needed its own means of defense.

Choose one check from the reading and trace a complete exchange: who acts first, how the other branch can respond, and what finally decides the outcome.

Sample answer: Congress passes a bill. The President vetoes it by returning it with objections. Congress can respond by voting again; if two thirds of the House and two thirds of the Senate approve, the bill becomes law anyway. If either house falls short, the veto stands. Other good answers: nomination and Senate consent; impeachment by the House and trial in the Senate; a court ruling answered by a constitutional amendment.

The reading says that a system in which every branch can say no “has a cost.” What is the cost, and what do defenders of the system say it buys?

The cost is speed and sometimes action itself: laws are harder to pass, and when the branches disagree the government can reach gridlock. Critics add that it becomes harder for voters to know who is responsible. Defenders say the delay protects liberty, because no one branch can act alone; in Brandeis's words, the purpose was “not to promote efficiency but to preclude the exercise of arbitrary power.”

Political theory, 1748 · 1748 年的政治理論

The Spirit of Laws, Book XI, Chapter 6 (excerpt)《論法的精神》第十一卷第六章（節錄）

Charles de Secondat, Baron de Montesquieu. First published in French in 1748; English translation by Thomas Nugent, first published in the early 1750s.孟德斯鳩（Montesquieu）著，1748 年以法文出版；英譯者為 Thomas Nugent，英譯本在 1750 年代初出版。

When the legislative and executive powers are united in the same person, or in the same body of magistrates, there can be no liberty; because apprehensions may arise, lest the same monarch or senate should enact tyrannical laws, to execute them in a tyrannical manner.

Again, there is no liberty, if the power of judging be not separated from the legislative and executive powers. Were it joined with the legislative, the life and liberty of the subject would be exposed to arbitrary controul; for the judge would be then the legislator. Were it joined to the executive power, the judge might behave with all the violence of an oppressor.

There would be an end of every thing, were the same man, or the same body whether of the nobles or of the people, to exercise those three powers, that of enacting laws, that of executing the public resolutions, and that of judging the crimes or differences of individuals.

當立法權與行政權集中在同一個人或同一個官員團體手上時，自由就不存在了；因為人們會擔心，同一位君主或同一個元老院會制定暴虐的法律，再用暴虐的方式去執行。

同樣地，如果審判權不和立法權、行政權分開，自由也不存在。如果審判權和立法權合在一起，人民的生命與自由就會受到恣意的支配，因為法官就是立法者。如果審判權和行政權合在一起，法官就可能擁有壓迫者的全部暴力。

如果同一個人，或是同一個機構——不論是貴族的還是人民的——行使這三種權力，也就是制定法律的權力、執行公共決議的權力，以及審判罪行或私人爭訟的權力，那就一切都完了。

Essay, 1788 · 1788 年的文章

The Federalist, No. 47 (excerpts)《聯邦論》第四十七篇（節錄）

By James Madison, signed “Publius.” Printed in New York newspapers at the end of January 1788; the editors of Madison's papers date it January 30.麥迪遜著，署名「普布利烏斯」（Publius）。1788 年 1 月底刊登於紐約的報紙；《麥迪遜文集》的編者把日期定在 1 月 30 日。

The accumulation of all powers legislative, executive and judiciary in the same hands, whether of one, a few or many, and whether hereditary, self appointed, or elective, may justly be pronounced the very definition of tyranny.

From these facts by which Montesquieu was guided it may clearly be inferred, that in saying, “there can be no liberty where the legislative and executive powers are united in the same person, or body of magistrates,” or, “if the power of judging be not separated from the legislative and executive powers,” he did not mean that these departments ought to have no partial agency in, or no control over the acts of each other. His meaning, as his own words import, and still more conclusively as illustrated by the example in his eye, can amount to no more than this, that where the whole power of one department is exercised by the same hands which possess the whole power of another department, the fundamental principles of a free constitution, are subverted.

把立法、行政、司法的一切權力集中在同一批人手裡——不論是一個人、少數人還是多數人，也不論是世襲的、自己任命的還是選舉產生的——完全可以說就是暴政的定義。

從孟德斯鳩所依據的這些事實，可以清楚地推斷：當他說「立法權與行政權集中在同一個人或同一個官員團體時，自由便不存在」，或者「審判權如果不和立法權、行政權分開」的時候，他的意思並不是說這些部門對彼此的作為不應該有部分的參與，或者不應該有任何牽制。他的意思——從他自己的話就看得出來，從他眼前的那個實例看得更加確定——只不過是：當一個部門的全部權力，由握有另一個部門全部權力的同一批人行使時，自由憲政的基本原則就被顛覆了。

The Design Debate · 制度的爭論

Is friction the flaw or the design? 摩擦是缺陷，還是設計？

Democracies have built their governments in two broad ways. Some, like the United States, keep the executive and the legislature apart and let each check the other. Others join them, in what the English writer Walter Bagehot called in 1867 “the nearly complete fusion” of the two powers: the executive is chosen by the legislature and lasts only while it keeps the legislature's support. Each design is stated here in its strongest form, and the card does not choose between them.
民主國家搭建政府的方式大致有兩種。一種像美國，把行政和立法分開，讓它們互相牽制；另一種把兩者結合起來——英國作家白芝浩（Walter Bagehot）在 1867 年稱之為兩種權力「幾乎完全的融合」：行政首長由立法機關產生，只有在保住立法機關支持的時候才能繼續執政。這裡把兩種設計各用最強的版本寫出來，不替它們分高下。

Should the power to make laws and the power to carry them out be kept in separate hands that must agree before the government acts, or joined so that one majority, answerable to the voters, can govern?制定法律的權力和執行法律的權力，應該放在必須彼此同意才能行動的不同人手上，還是結合起來，讓一個要向選民負責的多數能夠施政？

One design · 一種設計

Separate the powers and let them check each other分開，並且互相牽制

  • Concentrated power is the greater danger. A government that needs the agreement of several independent bodies will sometimes fail to act, but it is less likely to act against people's rights, because no one of those bodies can do so alone.集中的權力才是比較大的危險。要好幾個獨立機構都同意才能行動的政府，有時候會做不成事，但也比較不會侵害人民的權利——因為沒有哪一個機構能單獨辦到。
  • Bodies chosen in different ways, at different times, by different electorates must each be persuaded. A law that survives all of them rests on broader agreement than one passed by a single majority, and it is less likely to be reversed after the next election.用不同方式、在不同時間、由不同選民選出來的機構，必須一個一個被說服。通得過這些關卡的法律，共識比單一多數通過的更廣，下次選舉後也比較不會被翻掉。
  • Each branch has both a motive and the means to expose what the others do wrong. Rivalry between institutions does some of the work that would otherwise depend on the virtue of the people in office.每個部門都有動機、也有手段去揭露別的部門做錯的事。機構之間的競爭，分擔了一部分原本只能指望在位者品德的工作。
The doctrine of the separation of powers was adopted by the convention of 1787 not to promote efficiency but to preclude the exercise of arbitrary power. The purpose was not to avoid friction, but, by means of the inevitable friction incident to the distribution of the governmental powers among three departments, to save the people from autocracy.Justice Louis Brandeis, dissenting in Myers v. United States, 272 U.S. 52 (1926)1787 年的制憲會議採用權力分立的原則，不是為了提高效率，而是為了防止權力被恣意行使。目的不在避免摩擦，而是要藉著把政府權力分配給三個部門所必然產生的摩擦，使人民免於專制。——布蘭戴斯大法官，1926 年（不同意見書）

Another design · 另一種設計

Join the powers and make one majority answerable結合，並且讓一個多數負責

  • A government has to be able to govern. When the executive is drawn from the majority in the legislature, the people who promise a program are the same people who can pass it and carry it out, so decisions get made.政府總得要能施政。當行政首長出自立法機關的多數時，提出政綱的人就是有辦法通過它、執行它的同一批人，事情因此做得了決定。
  • Clear power means clear responsibility. If things go badly, voters know whom to remove. Where power is divided, each side can blame the other, and it can be hard to say who should answer for the result.權力清楚，責任才清楚。事情辦壞了，選民知道該把誰換掉。權力分散的地方，每一邊都可以怪另一邊，很難說清楚該由誰為後果負責。
  • A deadlock needs an exit. Officials elected for fixed terms can remain at odds for years. A legislature that can dismiss the executive, or be sent back to the voters in a new election, can end a stalemate in weeks.僵局需要出口。任期固定的民選官員可以對峙好幾年；而一個可以把行政首長換掉、或者可以被解散重選的立法機關，幾個星期就能結束僵持。
If there be one principle clearer than another, it is this: that in any business, whether of government or of mere merchandising, somebody must be trusted, in order that when things go wrong it may be quite plain who should be punished. … Power and strict accountability for its use are the essential constituents of good government.Woodrow Wilson, Congressional Government (1885), written when he was a young scholar; some words are in italics in the original如果有哪一條原則比別的更清楚，那就是：任何事業，不論是政府還是單純的買賣，總得信任某一個人，這樣事情出錯時，才能清清楚楚知道該罰誰。……權力，加上對如何使用權力的嚴格問責，是良好政府的根本要素。——威爾遜，《國會政體》（1885，當時他還是一位年輕的學者）

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思Separation of powers means that each branch works alone in its own area.權力分立的意思是：每個部門各管各的，互不相干。

✓ Fact · 事實The branches are separate in their membership, but their powers overlap on purpose. The President takes part in lawmaking, the Senate in appointments, and the courts in judging what the other two have done. Madison's phrase for this was “partial agency.”三個部門的成員是分開的，權力卻刻意重疊：總統參與立法，參議院參與人事任命，法院則審查另外兩個部門做的事。麥迪遜把這叫做「部分的參與」。

✗ Myth · 迷思To impeach an official is to remove that official from office.彈劾一名官員，就是把他免職。

✓ Fact · 事實Impeachment is only the accusation, made by the House. Removal requires a trial in the Senate and a conviction by two thirds of the members present. As of 2026 the House has impeached officials twenty-two times, and eight have been convicted.彈劾只是指控，由眾議院提出。要免職，還得經過參議院審判，並由出席議員三分之二定罪。到 2026 年為止，眾議院彈劾過二十二次，被定罪的有八人。

✗ Myth · 迷思The Constitution says that the Supreme Court may strike down laws.憲法明文規定最高法院可以宣告法律無效。

✓ Fact · 事實It does not say so in words. The Supreme Court's own account is that “the function of judicial review is not explicitly provided in the Constitution,” though “it had been anticipated before the adoption of that document.” The Court set out the reasoning in Marbury v. Madison in 1803.條文裡沒有這樣的字句。最高法院自己的說明是：司法審查這項功能「在憲法中並無明文規定」，不過「在憲法通過之前就已經有人預期到它」。這項權力的道理，是最高法院在 1803 年的 Marbury v. Madison 案裡說明的。

✗ Myth · 迷思A veto kills a bill, and a bill cannot become law without the President's signature.否決就是法案的終點；沒有總統簽署，法案不可能成為法律。

✓ Fact · 事實There are two ways around the signature. Congress can override a veto by two thirds of each house, and a bill that the President neither signs nor returns within ten days becomes law if Congress is still in session. Only the pocket veto cannot be overridden.繞過簽署的路有兩條：國會可以用兩院各三分之二推翻否決；總統在十天內既不簽署也不退回的法案，只要國會還在開會，就自動成為法律。只有口袋否決是國會無法推翻的。

Activity 1 · 課堂或自學活動一

Find the Checks in the Text 到條文裡找牽制

You need · 準備材料

  • Articles I, II, and III of the Constitution, from the National Archives transcription (linked in the sources below)憲法第一、二、三條（國家檔案館的謄本，連結在下面的出處裡）
  • A sheet of paper with a large triangle drawn on it, the corners labeled Congress, President, and Courts一張紙，上面畫一個大三角形，三個角分別寫上國會、總統、法院
  • Three colored pens, one for each branch三枝不同顏色的筆，一個部門一種顏色

Steps · 步驟

  1. Skim the three articles and mark every sentence in which one branch takes part in, approves, or can undo the work of another. Aim for at least six.瀏覽這三條，把「一個部門參與、同意，或者可以推翻另一個部門的工作」的句子都標出來。目標是至少找到六句。
  2. For each sentence, draw an arrow on the triangle from the branch that acts to the branch it checks, in the color of the acting branch, and write the article and section beside it.每一句都在三角形上畫一道箭頭，從出手的部門指向被牽制的部門，用出手那個部門的顏色，並在旁邊寫上是第幾條第幾項。
  3. Count the arrows leaving each corner. Which branch has the most ways to check the others? Which has the fewest?數一數從每個角出發的箭頭。哪個部門牽制別人的辦法最多？哪個最少？
  4. Compare your triangle with the grid in the 3D model. Is there a direction for which you found no clause? Explain why that might be.把你的三角形和 3D 模型裡的方格比一比。有沒有哪個方向你找不到條文？說說可能的原因。

Many readers find no clause that plainly says a court may set a law aside; the nearest is Article III, Section 2, which extends the judicial power to cases “arising under this Constitution.” That is the point of the third myth card above.
很多人會發現：找不到哪一條明白寫著法院可以宣告法律無效；最接近的是第三條第二項，它讓司法權及於「因本憲法而生」的案件。這正是上面第三張迷思卡要說的。

Activity 2 · 課堂或自學活動二

One Bill, Three Endings 一項法案，三種結局

You need · 準備材料

  • The “After the Veto, What Then?” tool above上面的「否決之後，還有幾條路？」小工具
  • Paper for a table with four columns: House votes, Senate votes, the President's choice, and the result一張紙，畫一個四欄的表：眾議院票數、參議院票數、總統的選擇、結果

Steps · 步驟

  1. Invent a harmless bill, for example one that names a new national park. Decide how many votes it gets in each house and write the numbers down.自己想一項無害的法案，例如替一座新的國家公園命名。決定它在兩院各得幾票，把數字寫下來。
  2. Using the tool, find a set of votes and a presidential choice that produces each ending: signed into law, law after an override, and a veto that stands. Record each in the table.用小工具分別找出會產生這三種結局的票數與總統的選擇：簽署成為法律、推翻否決後成為法律、否決成立。每一種都記在表裡。
  3. Now find the two unusual endings: a bill that becomes law with no signature and no override, and a veto that no number of votes can overturn.再找出兩種少見的結局：沒有簽署也沒有推翻否決，法案卻成了法律；以及不管有多少票都推翻不了的否決。
  4. Write two sentences: one explaining why the override threshold is higher than the threshold for passing the bill, and one giving a reason someone might think it is too high or too low.寫兩句話：一句說明為什麼推翻否決的門檻比通過法案高；一句寫出有人可能覺得這個門檻太高或太低的一個理由。

In a class, three groups can play the House, the Senate, and the President, and pass a paper “bill” between them. Keep the subject of the bill light, so that the discussion stays on the procedure.
在班上可以分三組，分別扮演眾議院、參議院和總統，把一張紙做的「法案」傳來傳去。法案的主題選輕鬆一點的，討論才會留在程序上。
