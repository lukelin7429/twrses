Unit 88: The Five Spirits Housed by the Five Organs

This unit's lesson on the six fu organs and its later lesson on qi, blood, and body fluids described the organs as processing physical substances. Chinese medicine adds a less physical layer on top of that framework: each of the five zang organs is said to house a specific aspect of mind, together called the "five spirits" — the heart houses shen (the presiding spirit, roughly overall consciousness), the liver houses hun (an ethereal soul linked to planning and dreams), the spleen houses yi (intention, tied to thought and memory), the lung houses po (a corporeal soul linked to instinctive, physical sensation), and the kidney houses zhi (willpower).

這個單元講六腑的那一課，跟稍後講氣血津液的那一課，把臟腑描述成處理物質的機構。中醫在這個框架上，加了一層比較不物質性的層次：五臟每一個都被說成藏著一種特定面向的心神，合稱「五神」——心藏神（主導的心神，大致上是整體的意識）、肝藏魂（一種跟計劃與作夢連結的、比較飄渺的魂）、脾藏意（意念，跟思考與記憶綁在一起）、肺藏魄（一種跟本能、身體感覺連結的、比較物質性的魄）、腎藏志（意志力）。

This layered structure explains a claim that otherwise sounds strange in translation: that a physical organ can house something as intangible as "soul" or "will." The point isn't that Chinese medicine considers the liver a literal seat of dreaming in an anatomical sense, but that each organ was traditionally credited with a distinct psychological function alongside its physical one, so that disturbances traditionally linked to mood, memory, willpower, or sleep could each be traced to a specific organ rather than treated as a single undifferentiated category of "mental symptom."

這個分層的結構，解釋了一個翻譯起來原本聽起來很奇怪的說法：一個物質性的臟腑，怎麼能藏著像「魂」或「志」這樣無形的東西。重點不是說中醫認為肝在解剖學意義上真的是作夢的座位，而是每個臟腑，除了物質性的功能之外，傳統上都被賦予了一種獨特的心理功能，讓傳統上跟情緒、記憶、意志力或睡眠連結在一起的困擾，都能各自追溯回一個特定的臟腑，而不是被當成單一、無差別的一大類「精神症狀」來處理。

This is also why the heart is granted a coordinating role above the other four: shen, housed in the heart, was traditionally described as overseeing the other four spirits the way a ruler oversees officials — a role that maps directly onto an idea from elsewhere in this unit, that the heart functions as the body's presiding organ. A disturbance to any one of the five spirits could, under this view, eventually register at the level the heart is responsible for overseeing, which is one reason sleep and emotional disturbances are so often discussed together in this framework rather than as separate categories.

這也是為什麼心被賦予一個高於其他四者的協調角色：藏在心裡的神，傳統上被描述成用君主監督官員的方式，來監督其他四神——這個角色，直接對應到這個單元其他地方提過的一個概念：心作為身體主導性的臟腑在運作。在這個觀點下，五神當中任何一個受到擾動，最終都可能反映到心負責監督的那個層次上，這也是為什麼在這個框架裡，睡眠跟情緒方面的困擾，這麼常被放在一起討論，而不是當成分開的類別。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what are the five spirits, and which organ houses each one?

Shen (heart), hun (liver), yi (spleen), po (lung), and zhi (kidney) — a specific aspect of mind housed by each of the five zang organs.

According to the passage, what is the actual point of saying a physical organ "houses" something like soul or will?

Not that the organ is a literal anatomical seat of that faculty, but that each organ was traditionally credited with a distinct psychological function alongside its physical one, so mental disturbances could be traced to specific organs.

According to the last paragraph, why is the heart granted a coordinating role over the other four spirits?

Because shen, housed in the heart, was traditionally described as overseeing the other four spirits the way a ruler oversees officials, mapping onto the heart's role as the body's presiding organ.
