Unit 115: Why Anger Makes You Flushed and Light-Headed

An earlier lesson in this unit described fear as driving qi sharply downward. Anger is traditionally described as producing the opposite directional effect: "anger makes qi rise," sending qi surging sharply upward, away from its normal, evenly distributed circulation, in the same way fear sends it sharply down.

這個單元稍早的一課，描述過恐懼把氣猛烈地往下推。怒傳統上被描述成產生相反方向的效果：「怒則氣上」，把氣猛烈地往上衝，脫離它正常、平均分布的循環，就跟恐懼把它猛烈往下送的方式一樣。

This upward surge is offered as the traditional explanation for a cluster of symptoms familiar to almost anyone who has been genuinely furious: a flushed, reddened face, a headache concentrated at the temples or the top of the head, and in more intense cases, a spell of dizziness or light-headedness as qi rushes upward faster than the body can accommodate it. The liver, covered extensively elsewhere in this unit as the organ most closely tied to mood and blood storage, is traditionally identified as the organ most directly affected by this upward surge, since anger is its associated emotion.

這股向上的衝擊，被拿來當成幾乎任何一個真正大發雷霆過的人都熟悉的一組症狀的傳統解釋：泛紅的臉、集中在太陽穴或頭頂的頭痛，在比較劇烈的案例裡，還有一陣暈眩或頭昏眼花，因為氣衝得比身體能承受的速度更快。肝，這個單元其他地方大量講過、是跟情緒與藏血關係最密切的臟腑，傳統上被認定是這股向上衝擊最直接影響的臟腑，因為怒是它對應的情緒。

Reading anger through this framework also explains why traditional advice for anger has so often centered on physically calming or grounding actions — slow breathing, sitting down, stepping away — rather than only mental techniques aimed at the emotion itself: if anger's primary physical effect is a qi surge moving upward, actions that encourage qi to settle back downward address the same mechanism directly, working with the body's own qi mechanism rather than only trying to talk the emotion down.

透過這個框架來讀怒，也解釋了為什麼傳統上處理怒氣的建議，這麼常圍繞著身體上讓人平靜或接地的動作——緩慢的呼吸、坐下來、走開——而不只是針對情緒本身的心理技巧：如果怒最主要的生理效果，是一股往上衝的氣，那麼鼓勵氣安頓回向下的動作，就是直接針對同一個機制，跟身體自己的氣機一起運作，而不是只試著用言語把情緒安撫下去。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does "anger makes qi rise" mean, and how does it contrast with fear's effect on qi?

Anger sends qi surging sharply upward, away from its normal circulation — the opposite of fear, which sends qi sharply downward.

According to the passage, what symptoms does this upward surge traditionally explain?

A flushed, reddened face, a headache at the temples or top of the head, and in intense cases, dizziness or light-headedness.

According to the last paragraph, why does traditional advice for anger often center on physically calming or grounding actions?

Because if anger's primary physical effect is an upward qi surge, actions that help qi settle back down address the same mechanism directly, rather than only addressing the emotion mentally.
