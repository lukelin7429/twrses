Unit 3: When Overexertion Drains Qi

The Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic, one of the oldest texts in Chinese medicine, states the idea in four characters: overexertion drains qi. The same chapter goes even further, claiming that a hundred diseases arise from qi. What makes this idea useful today is what counts as "overexertion." It is not limited to physical labor. Thinking too much, carrying responsibility for too long, and never quite letting go of a worry are all treated as forms of overexertion in Chinese medicine — the same category as working a double shift.

《黃帝內經》——中醫最古老的典籍之一——用四個字講完了這個概念：勞則氣耗。同一篇甚至講得更重：百病生於氣。這個概念到今天還有用的地方，在於什麼算是「勞」。它不只限於體力勞動。思慮過度、長期承擔責任、放不下一個擔心，在中醫裡都被當成「勞」的一種——跟連上兩班一樣算在同一個範疇裡。

Qi behaves like any resource that gets spent. If it is drawn on constantly and never replenished, the result is depletion. And the classic signs of depleted qi line up closely with the five functions from the last lesson: a person feels tired and doesn't want to talk, catches colds easily, and may sweat with the smallest amount of exertion.

氣的行為就像任何會被花掉的資源。如果持續被支出、卻從來沒有回補，結果就是耗竭。而氣耗竭的典型徵象，剛好對上上一課的五個功能：人會累、不想講話、容易感冒，稍微用一點力就可能流汗。

None of this is a diagnosis. It is a description of a mechanism — spending without replenishing — that anyone can recognize in their own life, long before it becomes a medical problem.

這些都不是診斷，而是一種機制的描述——支出而不回補——任何人都能在自己的生活裡認出來，早在它變成醫療問題之前。

Questions · 閱讀理解

What does the phrase "overexertion drains qi" mean, according to the passage?

It means that qi is used up when a person is overexerted — whether the exertion is physical, mental, or emotional.

According to the passage, why does worrying for a long time count as a kind of "overexertion," even without physical labor?

Because Chinese medicine treats sustained worry and responsibility as a drain on the same resource — qi — that physical labor draws on.

How do the symptoms described in this passage connect to the five functions of qi from the previous lesson?

The symptoms — tiredness, frequent colds, and sweating with little exertion — are what happens when qi's five functions (moving, warming, defending, holding, transforming) are no longer being carried out with full force.
