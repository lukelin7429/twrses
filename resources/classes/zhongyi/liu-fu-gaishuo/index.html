Unit 18: The Six Fu Organs

Alongside the five zang organs, Chinese medicine describes six fu organs: the gallbladder, small intestine, stomach, large intestine, bladder, and a sixth called the "triple burner," which has no single equivalent in Western anatomy. The zang organs are traditionally described as solid and as storing vital substances — qi, blood, and fluids. The fu organs are traditionally described as hollow, and their job is to receive, transform, and pass things along, rather than to store them long-term.

除了五臟之外，中醫還描述了六腑：膽、小腸、胃、大腸、膀胱，還有第六個叫做「三焦」，在西方解剖學裡沒有單一的對應。五臟傳統上被描述為實心的，負責儲存重要的物質——氣、血、津液。六腑傳統上被描述為中空的，它們的工作是接收、轉化、傳遞東西，而不是長期儲存。

The stomach receives food and begins breaking it down, passing it to the small intestine, which further separates what the body will absorb from what it will discard. The large intestine receives that waste and governs its final transformation and elimination. The gallbladder stores and releases bile to aid digestion, and the bladder stores and releases urine. The triple burner is the odd one out: rather than a single organ, it describes three functional zones — upper, middle, and lower — that together manage the movement of fluids and qi through the entire torso.

胃接收食物並開始分解，再傳給小腸，小腸進一步把身體要吸收的部分跟要排掉的部分分開。大腸接收那些廢物，主導最後的轉化與排出。膽儲存並釋放膽汁來幫助消化，膀胱儲存並釋放尿液。三焦是比較特別的一個：它不是單一的臟器，而是描述三個功能區域——上焦、中焦、下焦——合起來管理津液與氣在整個軀幹裡的運行。

The pattern across all six is "movement and passage" rather than "storage." A useful way to remember the difference: the zang organs are like a warehouse, holding onto valuable resources; the fu organs are like a series of loading docks, built for things to keep moving through them. Neither type of organ is more important than the other — a warehouse with no loading docks has nothing coming in or going out, and loading docks with no warehouse have nothing worth moving.

這六個共同的模式是「運行與傳遞」，而不是「儲存」。有一個好記的方法：五臟像倉庫，保存著珍貴的資源；六腑像一連串的裝卸碼頭，是為了讓東西持續流通而建的。這兩種臟腑沒有哪一種比較重要——沒有裝卸碼頭的倉庫，什麼都進不來也出不去；沒有倉庫的裝卸碼頭，也沒有值得搬運的東西。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what are the six fu organs, and what is the "odd one out"?

The gallbladder, small intestine, stomach, large intestine, bladder, and the triple burner — the triple burner is the "odd one out" because it isn't a single organ but three functional zones (upper, middle, lower).

According to the passage, what is the key difference between how zang organs and fu organs are traditionally described?

Zang organs are traditionally described as solid and as storing vital substances (qi, blood, fluids), while fu organs are described as hollow, receiving, transforming, and passing things along rather than storing them long-term.

According to the last paragraph, what comparison does the passage use to help remember the difference between zang and fu organs?

It compares zang organs to a warehouse (holding valuable resources) and fu organs to a series of loading docks (built for things to keep moving through).
