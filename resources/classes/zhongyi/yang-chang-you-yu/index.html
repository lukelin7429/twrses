Unit 104: A Historical Debate: Is Yang Usually in Excess, or Yin Usually Deficient?

This unit's lessons have mostly presented yin-yang theory as a single, settled framework. It's worth noting explicitly that Chinese medicine has never been one uniform voice on every point, and one well-known historical disagreement illustrates this directly: a physician named Zhu Danxi argued that in the human body, "yang is often in excess, yin is often insufficient," proposing that yin, being harder to build and easier to deplete through overactivity and emotional strain, deserved more consistent protective attention than yang.

這個單元的課程，大多把陰陽學說呈現成一個單一、已經定案的框架。值得明確指出的是，中醫從來就不是在每一點上都眾口一致的單一聲音，而一場知名的歷史爭論，直接說明了這一點：一位叫朱丹溪的醫家主張，人體「陽常有餘，陰常不足」，提出陰比較難建立、也比較容易因為過度活動與情緒耗損而流失，因此值得比陽更持續一致的保護性關注。

This wasn't a universally accepted claim; other physicians in the same broad tradition argued the opposite emphasis, treating yang as the more fragile side worth protecting, given yang's role as the active, warming force that has to be continuously generated rather than simply stored. The disagreement wasn't a minor footnote — it shaped entire schools of practice, each favoring treatments and habits aligned with whichever side its founders considered more vulnerable to being worn down.

這不是一個被普遍接受的說法；同一個廣義傳統裡的其他醫家，主張相反的側重，把陽當成比較脆弱、值得保護的一方，理由是陽作為主動、溫煦的力量，必須持續被生成，而不是單純被儲存起來。這場爭論不是一個無關緊要的小註腳——它塑造了整個整個學派的實踐方式，各自偏好跟自己創始者認為比較容易被耗損的那一方相符的治療與生活習慣。

The value of knowing this debate exists isn't picking a winner; it's recognizing that Chinese medicine, like most substantial intellectual traditions, contains internal argument rather than a single voice repeating the same conclusion. A framework built entirely around avoiding excess in general and protecting whatever tends to run short applies regardless of which side of this particular debate one leans toward — the debate is over where the greater risk usually lies, not over whether balance itself matters.

知道這場辯論存在的價值，不在於選出一個贏家；而在於認清中醫，跟大多數扎實的思想傳統一樣，內部含有爭論，而不是單一聲音重複著同一個結論。一個整體圍繞著避免過度、並保護容易不足的那一方所建立起來的框架，不論一個人在這場特定辯論裡比較傾向哪一邊，都同樣適用——爭論的是比較大的風險通常在哪一邊，而不是平衡本身重不重要。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what did the physician Zhu Danxi argue?

That "yang is often in excess, yin is often insufficient," because yin is harder to build and easier to deplete through overactivity and emotional strain, so it deserved more consistent protection.

According to the passage, what opposing view did other physicians in the same tradition hold?

That yang was the more fragile side worth protecting, given its role as an active, warming force that must be continuously generated rather than simply stored.

According to the last paragraph, what is the value of knowing this historical debate exists?

Recognizing that Chinese medicine contains internal argument rather than a single voice, and that a framework built around avoiding excess and protecting what runs short applies regardless of which side of the debate one favors.
