Unit 70: Why Acupuncture Sometimes Uses a Point Far From the Pain

An earlier lesson in this unit explained why meridians make it possible for a point far from a symptom to affect that symptom, using the example of the Heart meridian and referred arm pain. This lesson looks at the specific principle that guides which distant point gets chosen, summarized in a classical phrase: treat the lower body for an upper-body problem, and the upper body for a lower-body problem.

這個單元稍早的一課，用心經跟牽涉性手臂痛的例子，解釋了為什麼經絡讓一個遠離症狀的點能影響到那個症狀。這一課要看的是引導該選哪一個遠端的點的具體原則，用一句經典的話濃縮起來：上病下取，下病上取。

The logic follows from this unit's lesson on the twelve meridians running the full length of a limb. A problem high on a meridian's path — near the organ it's connected to — can sometimes be more effectively addressed at a point low on the same meridian, near the hand or foot, where the channel is more accessible and, according to this framework, more responsive to adjustment. A headache linked to the Stomach meridian, for instance, might be addressed with a point near the foot rather than a point on the head itself, even though the pain is entirely in the head.

這個邏輯延續自這個單元關於十二經絡走完整條肢體的那一課。一條經絡路徑上位置較高的問題——靠近它連結的臟腑——有時候能更有效地透過同一條經絡上位置較低、靠近手或腳的一個點來處理，那裡的通道比較容易觸及，用這個框架來說，對調整也比較有反應。舉例來說，一個跟胃經有關的頭痛，可能會用一個靠近腳的點來處理，而不是頭上的一個點，即使疼痛完全在頭部。

This principle is one more piece of evidence, alongside referred pain and the fifteen connecting channels covered elsewhere in this unit, that the meridian map behaves less like a straight line from cause to effect and more like a genuine network, where the most effective entry point is a separate question from where a problem is felt. Choosing the right distant point, rather than simply the nearest one, is exactly the kind of decision this unit has repeatedly said takes years of trained practice, not something to attempt from a reading passage.

這個原則是另一個證據，跟牽涉痛還有這個單元別處提過的十五絡脈放在一起，說明經絡地圖的表現，不太像一條從因到果的直線，更像一個真正的網絡，最有效的進入點，是跟問題被感覺到的地方分開的另一個問題。挑選正確的遠端點，而不只是最近的那一個，正是這個單元一再說過需要好幾年受訓練的實務經驗，而不是從一篇閱讀文章裡就能嘗試的那種決定。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what classical principle guides which distant point gets chosen for treatment?

"Treat the lower body for an upper-body problem, and the upper body for a lower-body problem."

According to the passage, why might a headache linked to the Stomach meridian be treated with a point near the foot rather than on the head?

Because a point low on the same meridian, near the foot, is more accessible and, according to this framework, more responsive to adjustment than a point higher up near the organ or symptom itself.

According to the last paragraph, what does this principle add to the evidence that the meridian map behaves like "a genuine network"?

It shows the most effective entry point for treatment is a separate question from where a problem is felt, alongside referred pain and the fifteen connecting channels covered elsewhere in the unit.
