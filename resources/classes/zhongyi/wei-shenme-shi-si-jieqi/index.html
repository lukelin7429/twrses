Unit 31: Why Chinese Medicine Has 24 Seasons, Not 4

English divides the year into four seasons, and most health advice that mentions seasons at all stays just as broad — "eat warm foods in winter," "stay hydrated in summer." Chinese medicine divides the year into twenty-four solar terms instead, each lasting roughly fifteen days and marking the sun's precise position along its yearly path. The difference isn't decorative. A piece of advice meant to cover a three-month season is almost always too vague to be useful; a piece of advice tied to a fifteen-day window can be specific enough to actually follow.

英文把一年分成四季，而大部分連季節都會提到的健康建議，也就停留在同樣籠統的層次——「冬天吃溫熱的食物」、「夏天要補充水分」。中醫把一年分成二十四個節氣，每個大約十五天，標記太陽在一年運行路徑上的精確位置。這個差別不是裝飾性的。一則要涵蓋整整三個月一個季節的建議，幾乎必然太模糊、沒辦法真的照著做；一則綁定在一個十五天窗口的建議，可以具體到真正能被執行。

Each solar term carries its own name and its own expected pattern of change — some mark shifts in temperature, like "slight cold" and "great cold" in the depths of winter; some mark shifts in the visible world, like "insects awaken" in early spring, when hibernating creatures are said to stir; some mark agricultural turning points, like "grain in ear," once used to time the harvest. The names function like fifteen-day chapter headings for the year, each one flagging a specific transition worth adjusting for.

每個節氣都有自己的名字，以及自己被預期的變化模式——有些標記溫度的轉變，像深冬的「小寒」跟「大寒」；有些標記可見世界的轉變，像早春的「驚蟄」，這時候冬眠的生物被認為開始甦醒；有些標記農業上的轉折點，像曾經用來抓收成時機的「芒種」。這些名字的功能，就像一年份、每十五天一個章節的標題，每一個都標出一個值得為它調整生活的具體轉變。

For someone learning to apply Chinese medicine's ideas about qi, yin-yang, and the organs to daily life, the twenty-four solar terms are the actual calendar the whole system runs on. Advice like "protect the liver's smooth qi in spring" only becomes useful once it's anchored to a specific window of weeks, rather than left floating across an entire season three months long.

對一個正在學習把氣、陰陽、臟腑這些中醫概念應用到日常生活的人來說，二十四節氣是這整套系統實際運行的曆法。像「春天要保護肝的順暢之氣」這種建議，只有把它綁定在一個具體的、以週為單位的窗口，而不是任它漂浮在一整個長達三個月的季節裡，才會真正變得有用。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what is the main difference between the Western four-season system and the Chinese twenty-four solar term system?

The Western system divides the year into four broad seasons, while Chinese medicine divides it into twenty-four solar terms of roughly fifteen days each, allowing much more specific, actionable advice.

According to the passage, what are three different types of change that solar term names can mark?

Shifts in temperature (like "slight cold" and "great cold"), shifts in the visible natural world (like "insects awaken"), and agricultural turning points (like "grain in ear").

According to the last paragraph, why does advice like "protect the liver's smooth qi in spring" only become useful when anchored to a specific solar term?

Because advice tied to an entire three-month season is too vague to actually follow, while advice anchored to a specific fifteen-day window is specific enough to be practical.
