Unit 21: Meridians Are Maps, Not Pipes

When Western readers first hear about meridians — the channels along which qi is said to travel — the natural assumption is that they must be physical tubes, something like blood vessels or nerves, just not yet found by modern dissection. This assumption is understandable, but it leads to a dead end. No meridian has ever been isolated under a microscope as a distinct anatomical structure, and Chinese medicine doesn't actually claim they will be. A meridian is better understood as a map of connection and influence, not a literal pipe carrying a literal substance.

西方讀者第一次聽到經絡——氣被認為在其中運行的通道——時，自然的假設是它們一定是physical的管子，類似血管或神經，只是現代解剖學還沒找到而已。這個假設可以理解，但會走進死胡同。從來沒有任何一條經絡在顯微鏡下被獨立辨識成一個明確的解剖結構，而中醫其實也不主張它們會被找到。經絡更適合被理解成一張連結與影響的地圖，不是一根搬運著一種實質物質的實體管線。

The map itself was not invented from pure imagination. It was built up over centuries from clinical observation: pressing or stimulating a specific point on the body reliably produced an effect somewhere else — sometimes right next to it, sometimes far away, like along the arm or down the leg. Practitioners recorded these connections point by point, and the lines drawn between them, showing which points tended to affect which distant regions together, became the meridians. The map describes a real, observed pattern of influence, even though the "roads" on the map are not literal physical roads.

這張地圖本身不是憑空想像出來的。它是好幾個世紀累積下來的臨床觀察建構起來的：按壓或刺激身體上某個特定的點，能穩定地在別的地方——有時候就在旁邊，有時候很遠，像是沿著手臂或腿——產生效果。醫師一個點一個點地記錄這些連結，而點與點之間畫出的連線——顯示哪些點傾向於一起影響哪些遠端區域——就變成了經絡。這張地圖描述的是一個真實、被觀察到的影響模式，即使地圖上的「道路」不是字面意義上的實體道路。

This distinction matters because it changes what counts as evidence. Looking for a meridian with a scalpel is like looking for a "trade route" with a shovel — you won't find a paved road, because a trade route was never a paved road to begin with. It was a pattern of consistent, repeated connection between two places. Meridians ask to be evaluated the same way: not by dissection, but by whether the pattern of connection they describe holds up, case after case.

這個分野很重要，因為它改變了什麼才算得上證據。拿手術刀去找一條經絡，就像拿鏟子去找一條「貿易路線」——你不會找到一條鋪好的路，因為貿易路線本來就從來不是一條鋪好的路。它是兩個地方之間一種持續、重複出現的連結模式。經絡也該用同樣的方式來評估：不是靠解剖，而是看它描述的連結模式，是不是一個案例接著一個案例都站得住腳。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what natural assumption do Western readers often make about meridians, and why does the passage call it "a dead end"?

They assume meridians must be physical tubes like blood vessels or nerves, not yet found by dissection. The passage calls this a dead end because no meridian has ever been isolated as a distinct anatomical structure, and Chinese medicine never claimed one would be found that way.

According to the passage, how was the meridian map actually built up, historically?

Through centuries of clinical observation — pressing or stimulating a specific point reliably produced an effect elsewhere, and practitioners recorded these connections, which became the meridian map.

According to the last paragraph, why does the passage compare looking for a meridian with a scalpel to looking for a "trade route" with a shovel?

Because a trade route was never a paved road to begin with — it was a pattern of consistent connection between places — so looking for a meridian with a physical tool misunderstands what kind of thing it is.
