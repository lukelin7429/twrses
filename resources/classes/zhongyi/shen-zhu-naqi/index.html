Unit 110: Why Deep Breathing Depends on the Kidney, Not Just the Lung

An earlier lesson in this unit's qi section treated slow, deep breathing as a practice that supports qi. That lesson credited the practice's effect to the lung alone, which handles breathing in an obvious, mechanical sense. Chinese medicine adds a second organ to the process: the kidney is traditionally described as "receiving qi," anchoring the qi the lung inhales and drawing it down deep into the body rather than letting it stay shallow at the chest.

這個單元講氣的那個部分，稍早有一課把緩慢、深沉的呼吸，當成一種支持氣的練習。那一課把這個練習的效果，單獨歸功於肺，因為肺在明顯、機械性的意義上負責呼吸。中醫在這個過程裡，加了第二個臟腑：腎傳統上被描述成「主納氣」，把肺吸進來的氣固攝住，往身體深處拉下去，而不是讓它停留在胸口的淺層。

This is offered as the explanation for a very concrete, physically noticeable difference between a shallow breath and a deep one: a shallow breath, confined to the upper chest, is read as qi the lung has taken in but the kidney hasn't yet anchored; a deep breath, one that a person can feel reaching down toward the lower abdomen, is read as the kidney successfully receiving and anchoring that same qi. A kidney weak in this receiving function is traditionally associated with breathlessness that gets worse specifically on inhaling rather than exhaling, and with a felt need to strain to complete each breath.

這被拿來當成一個非常具體、身體上感覺得到的差異的解釋：一個淺的呼吸，侷限在上胸腔，被讀成是肺已經吸進來、但腎還沒固攝住的氣；一個深的呼吸，一種一個人能感覺到深達下腹部的呼吸，被讀成是腎成功地把同一份氣接住並固攝住了。一個在這個接納功能上偏弱的腎，傳統上被連結到一種特別在吸氣時、而不是呼氣時變得更嚴重的呼吸急促，以及一種每一口氣都要費力才能完成的感覺。

This also explains why this course's earlier lesson on fear making qi descend named the kidney specifically as the organ overwhelmed by a sudden downward surge: an organ already responsible for anchoring qi at the body's depths is the same organ traditionally described as being overwhelmed when qi rushes downward too fast and too forcefully to be anchored properly. The kidney's receiving-qi function and its qi-anchoring role in that earlier lesson are two descriptions of the same underlying job, seen from two different angles.

這也解釋了為什麼這門課稍早講恐懼讓氣下降的那一課，特別點名腎是被一股突然的向下衝擊壓垮的那個臟腑：一個本來就負責在身體深處固攝氣的臟腑，正是傳統上被描述成，當氣衝得太快、太猛、來不及被好好固攝時，會被壓垮的同一個臟腑。腎主納氣的功能，跟它在那一課裡固攝氣的角色，是同一個底層工作，從兩個不同角度看到的兩種描述。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does "the kidney receives qi" mean, and how does it add to the lung's role in breathing?

The kidney anchors the qi the lung inhales, drawing it down deep into the body rather than letting it stay shallow at the chest — the lung inhales, the kidney anchors.

According to the passage, what is the traditional difference between a shallow breath and a deep breath?

A shallow breath, confined to the upper chest, is qi the lung took in but the kidney hasn't yet anchored; a deep breath is the kidney successfully receiving and anchoring that same qi.

According to the last paragraph, how does the kidney's receiving-qi function connect to the earlier lesson on fear and qi?

They describe the same underlying job from two angles: the kidney anchoring qi at the body's depths is the same organ overwhelmed when qi rushes downward too fast and forcefully to be anchored properly.
