Unit 78: Qi Commands Blood, Blood Mothers Qi

An earlier unit lesson described qi and blood as interconnected substances the organs produce together, but Chinese medicine also describes a specific division of labor between them, summarized in a classical phrase: "qi commands blood, blood mothers qi." The first half describes qi's job: qi moves blood, holds it within its vessels, and can even convert into blood when needed — an active, directing role, the way a commander directs troops rather than marching in formation.

這個單元稍早的一課，把氣與血描述成臟腑一起產生、彼此相連的物質，但中醫也描述了兩者之間一種具體的分工，用一句經典的話濃縮：「氣為血之帥，血為氣之母」。前半句描述氣的工作：氣推動血、把血固攝在血管裡，需要的時候甚至能轉化成血——一種主動、指揮的角色，就像一位指揮官指揮部隊，而不是跟著隊伍一起行軍。

The second half describes the reverse dependency: blood provides the material substance qi needs in order to have anything to direct in the first place, the way a mother provides the body a child is born into. Without adequate blood, qi has nothing to move and nowhere to be held — which is why severe blood loss is traditionally expected to produce qi deficiency symptoms almost immediately, even though the blood loss itself is the more visible problem.

後半句描述反過來的依存關係：血提供了氣一開始需要用來指揮的物質基礎，就像母親提供一個孩子出生進入的身體。沒有足夠的血，氣就沒有東西可以推動，也沒有地方可以被固攝——這也是為什麼嚴重的失血，傳統上被預期幾乎立刻就會產生氣虛的症狀，即使失血本身是比較明顯的問題。

This asymmetry — command versus material support, rather than two equal partners — explains a clinical pattern that would otherwise look strange: treating a blood-deficiency complaint sometimes involves strengthening qi rather than directly replenishing blood, because qi is what will actually generate the new blood the body needs. Knowing which side of the pairing is truly "in charge" changes where a treatment starts, even when the visible symptom points to the other side.

這種不對稱——指揮權對上物質支持，而不是兩個對等的夥伴——解釋了一個原本看起來很奇怪的臨床模式：處理一個血虛的抱怨時，有時候會去強化氣，而不是直接補血，因為氣才是真正會生出身體需要的新血的那一方。知道這個配對裡哪一邊真正「當家」，會改變治療從哪裡開始，即使可見的症狀指向的是另一邊。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does "qi commands blood" describe, and what comparison is used to explain it?

Qi moves blood, holds it within its vessels, and can convert into blood when needed — an active, directing role compared to a commander directing troops.

According to the passage, what does "blood mothers qi" describe, and why does severe blood loss produce qi deficiency symptoms quickly?

Blood provides the material substance qi needs to have anything to direct; without adequate blood, qi has nothing to move and nowhere to be held, so severe blood loss quickly produces qi deficiency symptoms.

According to the last paragraph, why might treating a blood-deficiency complaint involve strengthening qi rather than directly replenishing blood?

Because qi is what will actually generate the new blood the body needs — knowing qi is "in charge" changes where treatment starts, even when the visible symptom points to blood.
