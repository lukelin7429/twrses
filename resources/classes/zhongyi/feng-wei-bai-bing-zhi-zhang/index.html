Unit 27: Wind Is the Spearhead of All Diseases

Among the six excesses introduced in the last lesson, Chinese medicine singles out wind for special attention, calling it "the spearhead of the hundred diseases" — the factor most likely to lead an attack and open the way for others to follow. The reasoning is based on a simple physical observation: wind moves things, changes direction suddenly, and rarely travels alone. In Chinese medicine's logic, an illness that behaves the same way — appearing suddenly, shifting location, or changing character quickly — is read as carrying a "wind" pattern, whether or not actual weather was involved.

在上一課介紹的六淫裡，中醫特別挑出風來重點討論，稱它為「百病之長」——最可能帶頭發動攻擊、為其他病邪打開道路的因子。這個推理立基於一個簡單的物理觀察：風會讓東西移動、突然改變方向，而且很少單獨行動。在中醫的邏輯裡，一種表現得同樣——突然出現、位置轉移，或性質快速改變——的疾病，會被讀成帶有「風」的模式，不管實際的天氣有沒有牽涉在內。

This is why wind is described as "the spearhead": cold, dampness, or heat rarely invade the body cleanly on their own. They are said to ride in on wind, the way dust and pollen travel on an actual breeze. A "wind-cold" pattern, for example, describes an illness with wind's suddenness and cold's contraction and chill combined — closer to what English speakers mean by the early, sudden stage of catching a common cold than to a philosophical metaphor.

這就是為什麼風被稱為「百病之長」：寒、濕或熱，很少乾乾淨淨地單獨入侵身體。它們被認為是搭著風的便車進來的，就像塵埃和花粉搭著實際的風移動一樣。舉例來說，「風寒」的模式描述的是一種結合了風的突然性與寒的收縮、發冷的疾病——這比較接近英語使用者所說的普通感冒早期、突然發作的階段，而不是一個哲學上的比喻。

The practical value of "wind" as a category is that it gives Chinese medicine a name for a recognizable clinical picture: rapid onset, symptoms that move (a headache that shifts location, joint pain that travels from one joint to another), and an illness that seems to arrive from nowhere. Whether or not modern science would classify the cause the same way, the pattern being described — sudden, moving, and rarely isolated — is a genuinely useful thing to notice and name.

「風」作為一個類別的實際價值，是它給了中醫一個名字，來稱呼一種可辨識的臨床表現：發作快速、症狀會移動（頭痛的位置會轉移、關節痛從一個關節跑到另一個關節），還有一種疾病感覺像是憑空冒出來的。不論現代科學會不會用同樣的方式來分類這個成因，這裡被描述的模式——突然、會移動、很少單獨出現——本身就是一件真正值得注意並命名的事。

According to the passage, why does Chinese medicine call wind "the spearhead of the hundred diseases"?

Because wind is the factor most likely to lead an attack and open the way for other excesses to follow, based on the observation that wind moves things, changes direction suddenly, and rarely travels alone.

It combines wind's suddenness with cold's contraction and chill — closer to the early, sudden stage of catching a common cold than to a philosophical metaphor.
