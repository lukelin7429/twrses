Unit 93: Why Fear Makes You Lose Control

This unit's lesson on the seven emotions listed fear as one of the seven, alongside a general principle that each emotion moves qi in its own characteristic direction. Chinese medicine specifies fear's direction precisely: "fear makes qi descend," meaning intense fear is traditionally described as driving qi sharply downward, away from its normal, more evenly distributed circulation.

這個單元講七情的那一課，把恐列為七種情緒之一，同時提到一個一般原則：每種情緒都以自己特徵性的方向推動氣。中醫精確地指定了恐的方向：「恐則氣下」，意思是強烈的恐懼，傳統上被描述成會把氣猛烈地往下推，離開它正常、比較平均分布的循環。

This downward movement is offered as the traditional explanation for a very specific, almost universally recognized physical response: the sudden loss of bladder or bowel control that can accompany extreme fright. Rather than treating this as an unrelated, purely mechanical reflex, the framework reads it as a direct, visible consequence of qi being driven down and out of its normal holding pattern — the same descending qi that, in smaller doses, shows up as the more familiar sensation of a stomach "dropping" at a sudden scare.

這種向下的移動，被拿來當成一個非常具體、幾乎放諸四海皆準的生理反應的傳統解釋：極度驚嚇時，可能伴隨而來的突然失去膀胱或腸道控制。這個框架不是把這個當成一個不相關的、純粹機械式的反射，而是把它讀成氣被驅趕到往下、脫離正常固攝狀態的一個直接、可見的結果——同樣是這種下降的氣，在比較輕微的劑量下，就表現成一種更熟悉的感覺：突然被嚇到時，胃「往下沉」的感覺。

The kidney is traditionally linked to this pattern because the kidney sits at the body's lower region and is traditionally responsible for a function this course's organs unit described as governing water and reception of qi — holding qi and fluids in their proper place. A sharp downward surge driven by fear is traditionally read as overwhelming that holding function, at least momentarily, which is also why prolonged or chronic fear, as distinct from a single startling moment, is traditionally associated with longer-term kidney-related weakness rather than a one-time event.

腎傳統上被連結到這個模式，是因為腎位在身體的下方區域，而且傳統上負責這門課臟腑單元描述過的一個功能：主水與納氣——把氣與津液固攝在它們該在的位置。由恐懼驅動的一股猛烈的向下衝擊，傳統上被讀成至少暫時性地壓垮了這個固攝的功能，這也是為什麼長期或慢性的恐懼，跟單一次的驚嚇時刻不同，傳統上被連結到比較長期的腎相關虛弱，而不是一次性的事件。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what direction does fear traditionally drive qi, and what physical response does this explain?

Fear drives qi sharply downward, traditionally explaining the sudden loss of bladder or bowel control that can accompany extreme fright.

According to the passage, what does a milder version of this same downward movement feel like?

The familiar sensation of a stomach "dropping" at a sudden scare.

According to the last paragraph, why is the kidney traditionally linked to fear's downward effect on qi?

Because the kidney sits at the body's lower region and is traditionally responsible for holding qi and fluids in place; a sharp downward surge from fear is read as overwhelming that holding function.
