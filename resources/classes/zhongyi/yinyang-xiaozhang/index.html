Unit 83: Yin and Yang Consume Each Other: A Slow Trade-off, Not a Fixed Ratio

A lesson elsewhere in this unit described a single day's yin and yang as if they simply took turns, morning giving way to evening. But Chinese medicine describes the shift between them as more than a handoff at a fixed border — it's a continuous, gradual trade-off, named "waxing and waning," in which yin and yang are always quietly consuming each other, one slowly growing as the other slowly shrinks, rather than sitting at a static ratio until a switch flips.

這個單元其他地方的一課，把一天之中的陰陽描述得像是輪流交班，早晨讓位給晚上。但中醫把兩者之間的轉換，描述成不只是在一個固定邊界上的交接——而是一種持續、漸進的取捨，叫做「消長」，陰陽總是在悄悄互相消耗，一方慢慢增長，另一方就慢慢縮減，而不是停在一個固定比例上，直到開關被扳動。

This is different from the doubling-point principle covered in an earlier lesson, where an extreme of one produces a sudden flip into the other. Waxing and waning describes the ordinary, un-dramatic middle stretch: the slow build of yang through a spring morning, the slow build of yin through a autumn evening, each expanding at the direct expense of the other rather than growing independently. Neither side simply appears; it grows by consuming the space the other side gives up.

這跟稍早一課講過的「重陰必陽」的doubling-point原則不同，那是其中一個極端突然翻轉成另一個。消長描述的是普通、不戲劇化的中間過程：陽氣在一個春天早晨緩慢的累積，陰氣在一個秋天傍晚緩慢的累積，各自都是以直接消耗對方為代價來擴張，而不是各自獨立成長。沒有哪一邊是憑空出現的；它是靠消耗對方讓出的空間才成長的。

The distinction matters because it changes what "balance" means in this framework: not a fixed 50/50 split held constant, but a moving target where the ratio itself is expected to keep shifting, hour by hour and season by season, as long as neither side consumes the other completely or so quickly that it can't be replenished. Reading a snapshot of one moment as if it were the system's permanent state is the mistake this principle is designed to prevent.

這個區分很重要，因為它改變了這個框架裡「平衡」的意思：不是一個固定不變的 50/50 切分，而是一個移動的目標，比例本身被預期會持續變動，一個小時一個小時、一個季節一個季節地變，只要沒有哪一邊把另一邊完全消耗掉，或者消耗得太快、來不及補充。把某一刻的快照當成這個系統永久的狀態來讀，正是這個原則要防止的錯誤。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does "waxing and waning" describe?

A continuous, gradual trade-off in which yin and yang are always quietly consuming each other, one growing as the other shrinks, rather than sitting at a static ratio.

According to the passage, how is waxing and waning different from the doubling-point principle?

The doubling-point principle describes a sudden flip when one extreme is reached, while waxing and waning describes the ordinary, gradual middle stretch where growth happens at the other side's direct expense.

According to the last paragraph, what does "balance" mean under this framework?

Not a fixed 50/50 split held constant, but a moving target where the ratio keeps shifting, as long as neither side consumes the other completely or too quickly to be replenished.
