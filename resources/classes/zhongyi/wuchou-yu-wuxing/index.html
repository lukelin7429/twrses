The Five Odors

Unit 85: The Five Odors

This unit has now matched the five elements to facial features, to characteristic tones of voice, and it adds a third sensory channel here: smell. Each organ is associated with a characteristic odor — the liver with a rank, oily smell, the heart with a scorched smell, the spleen with a fragrant smell, the lung with a raw, fishy smell, and the kidney with a putrid smell — collectively called the "five odors."

這個單元目前已經把五行對應到臉部特徵、對應到特徵性的說話音調，這裡再加上第三個感官管道：氣味。每個臟腑都對應到一種特徵性的氣味——肝對應臊（一種油膩的腥味）、心對應焦（燒焦的味道）、脾對應香（芳香的味道）、肺對應腥（生的、魚腥味）、腎對應腐（腐敗的味道）——合稱「五臭」。

As with the five sounds, this isn't a claim that a healthy person's body odor is sorted into five distinct categories that anyone can casually detect; it describes subtle associations that a traditional practitioner was trained to notice, alongside — never instead of — every other channel available. A change in body odor was traditionally read as one more data point to weigh against face, voice, and other signs, not a stand-alone verdict pulled from smell alone.

跟五聲一樣，這不是在說一個健康的人的體味，會被分成任何人隨便都能察覺到的五個明顯類別；這是在描述一些細微的關聯，一位傳統的執業者被訓練來留意——而且永遠是搭配著、而不是取代其他所有可用的管道。體味的變化，傳統上被讀成又一個要拿去跟臉、聲音，還有其他徵象一起權衡的資料點，而不是單獨靠氣味就下的一個結論。

Taken together with the five sounds and the earlier lesson on faces, the five odors completes a pattern worth naming explicitly: the five-element system was designed as a multi-sensory reading of the body, treating sight, sound, and smell as three independent lines of evidence about the same underlying five-element balance, rather than reducing diagnosis to whichever single channel happened to be most convenient to check.

把五臭跟五聲、跟稍早講臉的那一課放在一起看，完成了一個值得明白說出來的模式：五行系統被設計成一種多感官的身體解讀方式，把視覺、聽覺、嗅覺當成三條各自獨立、但指向同一個底層五行平衡的證據線，而不是把診斷簡化成剛好最方便檢查的那一個單一管道。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what are the five odors, and which organ is each one associated with?

A rank, oily smell (liver), scorched smell (heart), fragrant smell (spleen), raw fishy smell (lung), and putrid smell (kidney) — a characteristic odor associated with each organ.

According to the passage, what is the correct way to understand the five odors, as opposed to a literal claim?

Not that healthy body odor sorts neatly into five obvious categories, but subtle associations a trained practitioner notices alongside every other available channel, not instead of them.

According to the last paragraph, what pattern do the five odors, five sounds, and faces together complete?

That the five-element system was designed as a multi-sensory reading of the body, treating sight, sound, and smell as independent lines of evidence about the same underlying balance.
