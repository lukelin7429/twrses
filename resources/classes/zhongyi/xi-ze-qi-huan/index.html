Unit 116: Why Too Much Excitement Can Backfire

This unit's lessons on anger and fear have both described an emotion with a clearly disruptive directional effect on qi. Joy is traditionally treated differently at first: "joy relaxes qi," meaning moderate joy is described as slowing and relaxing qi's movement, associated with an easy, unforced kind of relaxation rather than the sharp surges anger and fear are said to produce — which is part of why joy is generally treated as the most benign of the seven emotions in this framework.

這個單元講怒跟恐的課，各自描述了一種對氣有明顯擾動性方向效果的情緒。喜，一開始傳統上被不同對待：「喜則氣緩」，意思是溫和的喜，被描述成讓氣的運動變慢、放鬆，跟一種輕鬆、不勉強的放鬆連結在一起，而不是怒跟恐被說成會產生的那種猛烈衝擊——這也是為什麼，在這個框架裡，喜通常被當成七情裡最溫和無害的一種。

The exception is what happens at the extreme: joy taken past a moderate, easy relaxation into sudden, overwhelming excitement is traditionally described as "joy injures the heart," scattering heart qi so completely that it can no longer hold itself together in an organized way. The traditional image most often used to illustrate this is a moment of overwhelming, sudden good news producing a scattered, unfocused, almost dazed state rather than simple happiness — qi relaxed so far past its normal tension that it loses its shape entirely.

例外發生在極端的情況：喜一旦超過溫和、輕鬆的放鬆，變成突然、壓倒性的興奮，傳統上被描述成「喜傷心」，把心氣打散得太徹底，讓它沒辦法再以一種有組織的方式把自己聚合起來。傳統上最常用來說明這一點的畫面，是一個壓倒性、突如其來的好消息，產生的是一種渙散、失焦、近乎恍惚的狀態，而不是單純的快樂——氣放鬆得遠遠超過它正常的張力，以至於完全失去了它的形狀。

This exception matters because it completes a pattern this unit's lessons on the seven emotions have built up piece by piece: every one of the emotions, including the one usually treated as harmless, is capable of disrupting qi if it's extreme enough, sudden enough, or sustained long enough. Chinese medicine doesn't sort emotions into a permanently safe half and a permanently risky half; it sorts extremity itself as the actual variable that matters, regardless of which of the seven emotions happens to be involved.

這個例外很重要，因為它完成了這個單元講七情的那些課，一點一點建立起來的一個模式：七情裡的每一種情緒，包括通常被當成無害的那一種，只要夠極端、夠突然、或持續得夠久，都有能力擾亂氣。中醫不會把情緒分成一半永遠安全、一半永遠有風險；它把極端本身，當成真正重要的那個變數，不論七情裡涉入的是哪一種。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does "joy relaxes qi" describe, and why is joy generally treated as the most benign of the seven emotions?

That moderate joy slows and relaxes qi's movement, associated with easy, unforced relaxation rather than sharp surges — which is why it's generally treated as the least disruptive of the seven emotions.

According to the passage, what happens when joy is taken past moderate relaxation into sudden, overwhelming excitement?

"Joy injures the heart" — heart qi is scattered so completely it can no longer hold itself together in an organized way, producing a scattered, dazed state.

According to the last paragraph, what pattern does this exception complete?

That every one of the seven emotions, including joy, is capable of disrupting qi if extreme, sudden, or sustained enough — extremity itself, not the specific emotion, is what matters.
