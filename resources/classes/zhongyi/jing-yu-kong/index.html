Unit 94: Shock vs. Fear: One English Word, Two Emotions

A previous lesson in this unit covered fear's effect of driving qi downward. Chinese medicine actually distinguishes fear from a closely related but separate emotion, shock, which English speakers often flatten into the same word when translating loosely. Fear is traditionally described as a sustained, often anticipated dread — the kind that builds as a threat approaches or lingers after it has passed. Shock is traditionally described as a sudden, unanticipated jolt, with no buildup at all, arising the instant something startling happens without warning.

這個單元前一課講過恐把氣往下推的效果。中醫其實把恐跟一個密切相關、卻是分開的情緒——驚——區分開來，而英語使用者在鬆散翻譯的時候，常常把這兩者壓扁成同一個字。恐傳統上被描述成一種持續、常常是預期中的畏懼——會隨著威脅逼近而累積、或在威脅過去之後仍然徘徊不去的那種。驚傳統上被描述成一種突然、毫無預期的震動，完全沒有累積過程，是在一件令人吃驚的事毫無預警地發生的那個瞬間產生的。

The distinction matters because the two are traditionally said to scatter qi in different ways. Fear, as covered in the previous lesson, drives qi downward in a directional surge. Shock, by contrast, is traditionally described as scattering qi outward in every direction at once, disorganizing it rather than pushing it toward a single pole — closer to a sudden burst than a steady downward pull, which is why a person who has just been shocked is traditionally described as visibly disoriented and unfocused, in a way distinct from someone gripped by a slower-building fear.

這個區分很重要，因為這兩者傳統上被說成用不同的方式打散氣。恐，就像前一課講的，以一種有方向性的衝擊，把氣往下推。驚則傳統上被描述成把氣同時往各個方向打散，讓它失序，而不是把它推向單一的一極——比較接近一次突然的爆發，而不是一股穩定的向下拉力，這也是為什麼一個剛被嚇到的人，傳統上被描述成明顯地失去方向感、注意力渙散，跟一個被比較緩慢累積的恐懼抓住的人不一樣。

Because English commonly uses "fear" to cover both feelings, a word-for-word translation risks losing a distinction Chinese medicine treats as clinically meaningful: two different qi patterns, arising from two different kinds of emotional trigger, calling for two different kinds of understanding rather than a single blanket explanation. Recognizing which of the two is actually present is treated as the necessary first step, not an optional refinement.

因為英文常常用「fear」同時涵蓋這兩種感覺，逐字翻譯有可能丟失一個中醫認為在臨床上有意義的區分：兩種不同的氣的模式，來自兩種不同的情緒觸發，需要兩種不同的理解，而不是一個籠統的單一解釋。認出眼前實際上是哪一種，被當成必要的第一步，而不是一個可有可無的細節。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, how are fear and shock traditionally distinguished from each other?

Fear is a sustained, often anticipated dread that builds or lingers, while shock is a sudden, unanticipated jolt with no buildup, arising instantly.

According to the passage, how does shock's effect on qi differ from fear's effect on qi?

Fear drives qi downward in a directional surge, while shock scatters qi outward in every direction at once, disorganizing it rather than pushing it toward one pole.

According to the last paragraph, what risk does translating both fear and shock as the single English word "fear" create?

It risks losing a clinically meaningful distinction between two different qi patterns arising from two different emotional triggers, collapsing them into a single blanket explanation.
