Unit 19: How Zang and Fu Organs Pair Up

Each of the five zang organs is paired with one of the six fu organs — the liver with the gallbladder, the heart with the small intestine, the spleen with the stomach, the lung with the large intestine, and the kidney with the bladder (the triple burner is paired separately, with the heart's supporting structure). Chinese medicine calls this an "interior-exterior" relationship: the zang organ is the interior partner, doing the deeper, storing work, while the fu organ is the exterior partner, doing the more exposed, processing work.

五臟裡的每一個，都跟六腑裡的一個配對——肝配膽、心配小腸、脾配胃、肺配大腸、腎配膀胱（三焦則另外配對，跟心的輔助結構有關）。中醫把這叫做「表裡」關係：臟是裡（內部）的一方，做較深層、儲存性質的工作；腑是表（外部）的一方，做較外顯、處理性質的工作。

The pairing means the two organs' problems often travel together. A common example: heat building up in the heart can travel to its paired organ, the small intestine, and show up as a burning sensation during urination — a symptom that, read on its own, seems to have nothing to do with the heart at all. Another example: a struggling spleen (digestion and transformation) commonly shows up alongside a struggling stomach (initial breakdown of food) — nausea and poor appetite appearing together, rather than as two coincidental complaints.

這個配對意味著兩個臟腑的問題常常會一起出現。一個常見的例子：心裡積聚的熱，可能傳到它配對的臟腑——小腸，表現成小便灼熱——這個症狀單獨看，似乎跟心完全沒關係。另一個例子：脾（消化與轉化）出問題，常常會伴隨著胃（食物初步分解）也出問題——噁心和食慾不振同時出現，而不是兩個各自獨立、剛好同時發生的抱怨。

This pairing logic is one reason a Chinese medicine consultation can feel like it's asking about seemingly unrelated body systems at once. It isn't scattered questioning — it's checking both halves of a known partnership, because a problem rarely stays confined to just one side of it.

這種配對邏輯，是為什麼中醫問診有時候感覺像是同時在問好幾個看似不相關的身體系統。這不是漫無目的地亂問——是在檢查一組已知搭檔的兩個半邊，因為問題很少只侷限在其中一邊。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does the passage call the relationship between a zang organ and its paired fu organ?

An "interior-exterior" relationship, with the zang organ as the interior (storing) partner and the fu organ as the exterior (processing) partner.

According to the passage, how can heat in the heart show up in a seemingly unrelated symptom?

Heat in the heart can travel to its paired organ, the small intestine, and show up as a burning sensation during urination.

According to the last paragraph, why might a Chinese medicine consultation ask about seemingly unrelated body systems at once?

Because it's checking both halves of a known zang-fu partnership, since a problem rarely stays confined to just one side of the pairing.
