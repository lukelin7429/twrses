Unit 6: Yin and Yang Are Not "Good" and "Bad"

After qi, the next idea a student of Chinese medicine meets is yin and yang — and the most common misunderstanding arrives almost immediately. In popular culture, yin often gets treated as "bad" or "dark" and yang as "good" or "light," the way a villain and a hero get cast in a story. Chinese medicine uses the words very differently. Yin and yang describe a pair of complementary functions, not a moral judgment. Yin is associated with substance, rest, cooling, and the body's interior; yang is associated with function, activity, warming, and the body's exterior. Neither one is better than the other, in the same way that inhaling is not "better" than exhaling.

學完氣之後，中醫學生接下來會遇到的下一個概念是陰陽——而最常見的誤解幾乎馬上就會出現。在流行文化裡，陰常被當成「壞的」或「黑暗的」，陽被當成「好的」或「光明的」，就像故事裡的反派跟英雄。中醫用這兩個字的方式完全不同。陰陽描述的是一對互補的功能，不是道德判斷。陰跟物質、休息、寒涼、身體的內部有關；陽跟功能、活動、溫熱、身體的外部有關。兩者沒有誰比較好，就像吸氣不會比呼氣「更好」一樣。

The two also cannot exist without each other. A body with no yin — no substance, no fluids, no rest — would have nothing for yang to warm or move. A body with no yang — no function, no activity, no warmth — would have substance sitting perfectly still, doing nothing. Health, in this framework, is not about maximizing yang and minimizing yin, or the reverse. It is about the two staying in a working relationship with each other, each doing its job without overtaking the other.

這兩者也無法脫離對方單獨存在。一個完全沒有陰——沒有物質、沒有津液、沒有休息——的身體，會讓陽無物可溫、無物可推動。一個完全沒有陽——沒有功能、沒有活動、沒有溫熱——的身體，物質會完全靜止，什麼都做不了。在這個框架裡，健康不是把陽最大化、把陰最小化，或反過來。健康是讓兩者維持一種持續運作的關係，各自做好自己的工作，不壓過對方。

This is why a Chinese medicine practitioner almost never says a patient has "too much yin" or "too little yang" as a final verdict. Those words only mean something in relation to each other, at a particular moment, for a particular person. Learning yin-yang is really learning to stop asking "which one is winning" and start asking "are they still working together."

這就是為什麼中醫師幾乎不會把「陰太多」或「陽太少」當成一個最終定論來講。這些字只有在彼此相對、在特定時刻、對特定的人身上才有意義。學陰陽，其實是在學怎麼停止問「哪一個贏了」，開始問「他們還有沒有一起運作」。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, why is it a mistake to treat yin as simply "bad" and yang as simply "good"?

Because in Chinese medicine, yin and yang describe complementary functions — substance/rest/cooling versus function/activity/warming — not a moral judgment of good versus bad.

What does the passage mean by saying yin and yang "cannot exist without each other"?

It means that yin (substance, rest) gives yang (function, activity) something to act on, and yang gives yin the activity that keeps it from being static and useless — each needs the other to make sense.

According to the passage, what should the real question be, instead of asking "which one is winning"?

Whether yin and yang are still working together in a balanced relationship, rather than which one currently has the upper hand.
