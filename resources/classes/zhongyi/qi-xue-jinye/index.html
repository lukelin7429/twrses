Unit 44: Qi, Blood, and Body Fluids: The Organs' Raw Materials

This unit has described what each organ does, but not yet what, specifically, the organs are working with. Chinese medicine names three core substances that the organs produce, circulate, and depend on: qi, already covered in detail in the first unit of this course; blood, which nourishes and moistens the body as it circulates, produced through the combined work of the spleen's transformation and the heart's governing of circulation; and body fluids, a broader category covering everything from saliva and sweat to the fluid that keeps joints moving.

這個單元描述了每個臟腑做什麼，但還沒描述臟腑實際上在處理的是什麼。中醫指出三種臟腑生產、運行、並依賴的核心物質：氣，這門課第一個單元已經詳細講過；血，在循環的過程中滋養並滋潤身體，由脾的轉化與心對循環的主導共同運作而產生；還有津液，一個較廣的類別，涵蓋從唾液、汗水，到讓關節保持活動的液體等一切。

These three aren't independent of each other — they're produced from the same raw materials and constantly convert into and support one another. Qi is said to move blood, the way a pump moves water; blood is said to be the material foundation that qi needs to move through. Body fluids and blood share such a close relationship that heavy blood loss is traditionally expected to affect fluid levels, and severe fluid loss, in turn, is expected to affect blood.

這三者不是互相獨立的——它們由同樣的原料生產出來，並持續互相轉化、互相支持。氣被認為推動血，就像幫浦推動水一樣；血被認為是氣需要藉以運行的物質基礎。津液跟血的關係緊密到，大量失血傳統上被預期會影響津液的量，而嚴重的津液流失，反過來也被預期會影響血。

Thinking of qi, blood, and fluids as one interconnected system, rather than three separate topics, explains why a single cause — say, a struggling spleen, discussed earlier in this unit — can ripple outward into fatigue (a qi problem), a pale complexion (a blood problem), and dry skin (a fluid problem) all at once. The organs aren't just doing separate jobs in this unit; they're collaborating on producing and managing this same shared set of three raw materials.

把氣、血、津液想成一個彼此相連的系統，而不是三個各自獨立的主題，解釋了為什麼一個原因——比方說，這個單元稍早討論過的脾出問題——能同時擴散成疲倦（氣的問題）、臉色蒼白（血的問題），和皮膚乾燥（津液的問題）。這個單元裡的臟腑不只是各自做著獨立的工作；它們是在共同生產、管理著同一套共享的三種原料。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what three core substances does Chinese medicine name that the organs produce, circulate, and depend on?

Qi, blood, and body fluids.

According to the passage, how does qi relate to blood, according to Chinese medicine?

Qi is said to move blood, the way a pump moves water, while blood is said to be the material foundation that qi needs to move through.

According to the last paragraph, why can a single cause like a struggling spleen produce symptoms that look unrelated — fatigue, pale complexion, and dry skin?

Because qi, blood, and fluids form one interconnected system rather than three separate topics — a single cause can affect all three at once, showing up as a qi problem (fatigue), a blood problem (pale complexion), and a fluid problem (dry skin) simultaneously.
