Unit 43: The Brain, According to Chinese Medicine

A natural question, after two full lessons on the five zang organs and six fu organs, is: where is the brain in all of this? The honest answer is that it doesn't fit cleanly into either category, and Chinese medicine solves the problem by giving it, along with a small group of other structures, a third category entirely: the "extraordinary organs," which includes the brain, bone marrow, bones, blood vessels, the uterus, and — despite being listed among the fu organs earlier — the gallbladder, which is considered to straddle both categories.

讀完兩整課的五臟與六腑之後，一個自然的問題是：腦在這一切裡放在哪裡？誠實的答案是，它沒辦法乾淨地放進任何一個類別，而中醫解決這個問題的方式，是給它、連同一小群其他結構，一個完全獨立的第三類別：「奇恆之腑」，包括腦、骨髓、骨、脈、女子胞（子宮），還有——儘管前面已經被列在六腑裡——被認為橫跨兩個類別的膽。

The extraordinary organs share a defining feature: like the zang organs, they are solid and store something important, but unlike the zang organs, they aren't matched to one of the five elements or paired with a fu organ. The brain specifically is described as "the sea of marrow," filled and nourished by the kidney's stored constitutional strength — which is why this course's earlier lesson on the kidney's role in growth and reproduction is also, indirectly, a lesson about what's believed to keep the brain properly nourished over a lifetime.

奇恆之腑共享一個決定性的特徵：像五臟一樣，它們是實心的，儲存著重要的東西，但跟五臟不同，它們沒有被對應到五行的其中一個，也沒有跟一個腑配對。腦特別被描述成「髓海」，由腎儲藏的體質根本填充、滋養——這也是為什麼這門課稍早關於腎在生長與生殖上角色的那一課，同時也間接是一堂關於什麼被認為能讓腦一輩子維持適當滋養的課。

This category exists because Chinese medicine's organ system was never meant to force every structure into one of two boxes. The extraordinary organs are the system's own acknowledgment that some things — the brain very much included — don't behave like either a storing zang organ or a processing fu organ, and needed their own place in the framework rather than being awkwardly squeezed into one that didn't fit.

這個類別之所以存在，是因為中醫的臟腑系統從來不打算把每一個結構都硬塞進兩個盒子的其中一個。奇恆之腑是這套系統自己的承認：有些東西——腦絕對包括在內——的表現既不像儲存性質的臟，也不像處理性質的腑，需要在這套框架裡有自己的位置，而不是被彆扭地塞進一個不合身的類別裡。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what is the "extraordinary organs" category, and what structures does it include?

A third category alongside the zang and fu organs, including the brain, bone marrow, bones, blood vessels, the uterus, and the gallbladder (considered to straddle both categories).

According to the passage, what is the brain traditionally described as, and what nourishes it?

The brain is described as "the sea of marrow," filled and nourished by the kidney's stored constitutional strength.

According to the last paragraph, why does this third category exist at all?

Because Chinese medicine's organ system was never meant to force every structure into one of two boxes — the extraordinary organs are the system's acknowledgment that some structures don't behave like either a storing zang organ or a processing fu organ.
