Unit 58: Rebellious Qi: When Direction Is the Problem

Several organs covered earlier in this course have qi that is expected to move in one specific direction as part of normal function: stomach qi is supposed to descend, pushing digested food onward; lung qi is supposed to descend as well, spreading fluids downward through the body. "Rebellious qi" describes what happens when qi that should be moving in that expected direction reverses and moves the opposite way instead — and the resulting symptoms read almost like a direct description of something running backward.

這門課稍早提過的好幾個臟腑，它們的氣被預期作為正常功能的一部分，只往一個特定的方向運行：胃氣本該往下降，把消化過的食物往前推；肺氣也該往下降，把津液往下散布到全身。「氣逆」描述的是當本該往那個預期方向運行的氣反過來、往相反的方向走時會發生什麼事——而隨之而來的症狀，讀起來幾乎像是對一個逆向運作的東西的直接描述。

Rebellious stomach qi, moving upward instead of down, is traditionally linked to nausea, belching, hiccups, and in more severe cases, vomiting — all of them, notably, involving something moving up and out of the body rather than continuing down and through it. Rebellious lung qi, similarly failing to descend, is linked to coughing and wheezing, both of which involve air being forced back up rather than settling into a calm, downward breath.

胃氣上逆，往上而不是往下走，傳統上被連結到噁心、噯氣、打嗝，嚴重的情況下還有嘔吐——值得注意的是，這些全都涉及某個東西往上、往身體外面移動，而不是繼續往下、通過身體。肺氣上逆，同樣沒能往下降，被連結到咳嗽跟喘鳴，兩者都涉及空氣被迫往上衝，而不是安定成一個平靜、向下的呼吸。

What makes this pattern useful, rather than just a colorful label, is that it gives a doctor a specific direction to correct rather than a vague target to chase. Qi deficiency calls for replenishing, qi stagnation calls for freeing movement, qi sinking calls for lifting back up — and rebellious qi calls specifically for redirecting qi back the way it was supposed to go, which is a different task from any of the other three, even though all four ultimately trace back to the same single concept introduced in this course's very first lesson.

讓這個模式有用、而不只是一個花俏標籤的地方，在於它給了醫師一個具體要修正的方向，而不是一個模糊要追逐的目標。氣虛需要回補，氣滯需要疏通，氣陷需要往上提——而氣逆特別需要把氣重新導引回它本該去的方向，這跟另外三種都是不同的任務，即使這四者最終都能追溯回這門課第一課介紹的同一個單一概念。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what direction is stomach qi and lung qi supposed to move, under normal function?

Both are supposed to descend — stomach qi pushes digested food onward, and lung qi spreads fluids downward through the body.

According to the passage, what symptoms are traditionally linked to rebellious stomach qi?

Nausea, belching, hiccups, and in more severe cases, vomiting.

According to the last paragraph, how does the correction for rebellious qi differ from the corrections for deficiency, stagnation, and sinking?

Rebellious qi calls specifically for redirecting qi back the way it was supposed to go, while deficiency calls for replenishing, stagnation calls for freeing movement, and sinking calls for lifting back up — each is a different specific task.
