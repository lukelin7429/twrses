Unit 35: Explaining Seasonal Wellness to a Friend

Suppose a friend, hearing about spring's "rise early" and winter's "rise late," asks, "Isn't that just common sense dressed up in ancient language?" A fair answer gives credit where it's due: "A lot of it does match common sense — no one's surprised that people feel more energetic in spring and want to hibernate in winter. What Chinese medicine adds isn't the observation itself, but a framework that ties the observation to everything else in this course: spring's energy connects to wood and the liver, winter's conservation connects to water and the kidney, and the same organs, emotions, and qi patterns you've already learned explain why the advice runs in opposite directions."

假設一位朋友聽到春天要「早起」、冬天要「晚起」，問：「這不就是包裝成古代語言的常識嗎？」一個公允的回答，該給予它應得的肯定：「有很大一部分確實符合常識——沒有人會驚訝，人在春天會覺得更有活力，在冬天想要冬眠。中醫加進來的，不是這個觀察本身，而是一個把這個觀察跟這門課學過的一切連結起來的框架：春天的活力連結到木與肝，冬天的儲藏連結到水與腎，同一套你已經學過的臟腑、情緒、氣的模式，解釋了為什麼這些建議會朝著相反的方向走。」

It also helps to point out what makes the twenty-four solar terms more useful than "common sense": "General seasonal advice is too vague to actually follow — 'eat healthy in summer' doesn't tell you much. A fifteen-day solar term is specific enough to act on, which is closer to a farmer's almanac than to a horoscope. It's less about mysticism and more about a very old, very detailed habit of paying close attention to timing."

指出是什麼讓二十四節氣比「常識」更有用，也會有幫助：「一般的季節性建議太籠統，沒辦法真的照著做——『夏天要吃得健康』告訴不了你什麼具體的事。一個十五天的節氣，具體到可以真正拿來執行，這比較接近農民曆，而不是星座運勢。與其說是神祕主義，不如說是一個非常古老、非常細緻，密切關注時序的習慣。」

The conversation can close on a note that applies to the whole course, not just this unit: "None of this is about performing a ritual on a fixed date. It's about noticing which direction a season is already leaning — toward growth or toward storage — and choosing not to fight it. And as always, real health concerns still belong with a licensed doctor, not a calendar."

這段對話可以用一個適用於整門課、而不只是這個單元的想法來收尾：「這一切都不是在講在固定的日期表演一個儀式。是在注意一個季節已經傾向哪個方向——朝生長，還是朝儲藏——然後選擇不跟它對抗。而一如既往，真正的健康疑慮，還是該交給合格醫師，不是交給一份曆法。」

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does Chinese medicine add to the "common sense" observation that people feel more energetic in spring?

A framework that ties the observation to organs, emotions, and qi patterns already covered in the course — explaining why spring's and winter's advice run in opposite directions, connected to wood/liver and water/kidney.

According to the passage, why is a fifteen-day solar term described as "closer to a farmer's almanac than to a horoscope"?

Because it's specific enough to actually act on, unlike vague seasonal advice, making it more like a practical, detailed calendar than a mystical prediction.

According to the last paragraph, what closing idea does the passage say applies to the whole course, not just this unit?

That it's about noticing which direction a season is already leaning and choosing not to fight it — and that real health concerns still belong with a licensed doctor.

Words & Phrases · 生字及片語

dressed up(ph.)包裝過的；打扮過的The old idea was just dressed up in new language.這個舊想法，只是用新的語言包裝過而已。almanac(n.)曆書；農民曆Farmers once relied on an almanac to plan planting and harvest.農夫曾經依靠農民曆來規劃播種與收成。mysticism(n.)神祕主義The book explored the mysticism behind ancient rituals.這本書探討了古代儀式背後的神祕主義。ritual(n.)儀式The morning ritual of coffee and quiet helped her start the day calmly.早晨喝咖啡、安靜片刻的儀式，讓她能平靜地展開一天。fixed(adj.)固定的The meeting has a fixed time every week.這場會議每週都有一個固定的時間。credit(n.)肯定；讚許Give credit where it's due, even to an old idea.該肯定的地方就要肯定，即使是一個舊的想法。
