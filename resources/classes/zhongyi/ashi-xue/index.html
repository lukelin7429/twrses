Unit 46: Why Pressing a Sore Spot Sometimes Helps

An earlier lesson in this unit described acupuncture points as fixed, mapped locations along the twelve meridians, each with its own name and traditional set of effects. Chinese medicine also recognizes a second, much less formal category: points with no fixed location at all, found simply by pressing around an area until a person reacts to a tender spot. These are called "ashi points," a name that comes from the sound a person makes when the press finds the right place — roughly, "ah, yes, that's it."

這個單元稍早的一課，把穴位描述成沿著十二正經、固定、已被標定出來的位置，每一個都有自己的名字與傳統記錄的一套效果。中醫也承認第二個、正式程度低得多的類別：完全沒有固定位置的點，單純靠在一個區域周圍按壓，直到一個人對某個痠痛的點有反應而找到。這些被稱為「阿是穴」，這個名字來自按到對的地方時，一個人發出的聲音——大概就是「啊，對，就是這裡」。

An ashi point isn't marked on any traditional chart, doesn't have a name of its own, and can appear in a completely different spot from one person to the next, or even from one week to the next in the same person. What makes it worth treating isn't its location on a map — it's the local tenderness itself, read as a sign that qi and blood have become stuck in that specific spot, regardless of which meridian happens to run nearby.

阿是穴不會被標在任何傳統的圖表上，沒有自己的名字，而且可能在不同的人身上出現在完全不同的地方，甚至在同一個人身上，這週跟下週也可能不一樣。讓它值得被處理的，不是它在地圖上的位置——而是那個局部的痠痛本身，被讀成氣血在那個特定地點卡住的一個徵象，不管附近剛好經過的是哪一條經絡。

This category matters for understanding acupuncture as a living practice rather than a fixed lookup table. Alongside points chosen because a chart says they affect a particular organ, a practitioner is also always working with what the body is showing right now, in real time, at that specific visit — the fixed map and the moment-to-moment finding work together rather than one replacing the other.

這個類別，對理解針灸是一種活的實踐，而不是一份固定的查詢表，很重要。除了因為圖表寫著會影響某個特定臟腑而選定的穴位之外，醫師也一直在處理身體此時此刻、在那次特定的看診當下所顯現出來的狀況——固定的地圖跟當下發現的東西是一起合作的，不是其中一個取代另一個。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what is an "ashi point," and where does its name come from?

An ashi point is a point with no fixed location, found by pressing around an area until a person reacts to a tender spot; its name comes from the sound a person makes when the press finds the right place — roughly "ah, yes, that's it."

According to the passage, what makes an ashi point worth treating, if it isn't its location on a map?

The local tenderness itself, read as a sign that qi and blood have become stuck in that specific spot, regardless of which meridian runs nearby.

According to the last paragraph, what does the existence of ashi points show about acupuncture as a practice?

That acupuncture works as a living practice rather than a fixed lookup table — the fixed map of named points and moment-to-moment findings on the body work together, rather than one replacing the other.
