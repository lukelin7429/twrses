Unit 89: Why the Lung Is Called the "Delicate Organ"

This unit's lessons on the five zang organs and the six fu organs described each organ's job without ranking them by how easily disturbed each one is. Chinese medicine singles out one organ for a specific reputation: the lung is called the "delicate organ," traditionally considered the zang organ most easily and most frequently affected, whether by external factors or by problems originating elsewhere in the body.

這個單元講五臟與六腑的那些課，描述了每個臟腑的工作，但沒有依照各自多容易受到擾動來排名。中醫特別挑出一個臟腑，給了它一個特定的名聲：肺被稱為「嬌臟」，傳統上被認為是五臟裡最容易、也最頻繁受到影響的一個，不論是被外在因素，還是被身體其他地方起源的問題影響。

Two features of the lung's traditional job explain the reputation. First, it's described as directly connecting to the outside air with every breath, making it the organ most exposed to whatever the external environment happens to be carrying, in a way none of the other four zang organs are. Second, it's traditionally described as sitting at the top of the body, in a position where disturbances rising up from other organs are said to reach it before settling anywhere else, making it something of a final, exposed stop for problems that started elsewhere.

肺傳統工作的兩個特點，解釋了這個名聲。第一，它被描述成透過每一次呼吸，直接跟外面的空氣連通，這讓它成為五臟裡最暴露在外在環境當下所帶著的任何東西之下的一個，其他四臟都沒有這種暴露程度。第二，它傳統上被描述成位在身體的最上方，在這個位置上，從其他臟腑往上升起的擾動，被說成會先到達肺，才會在其他地方安頓下來，讓它某種程度上，成了從別處開始的問題，最後一個、也是最暴露的一站。

The "delicate" reputation isn't a claim that the lung is inherently weak or poorly designed; it's a description of exposure, not fragility in the sense of inferiority. A traditionally recognized implication follows from it: because the lung is affected easily, it's also treated cautiously — traditionally approached with milder interventions relative to other organs, on the reasoning that an already-exposed organ is better supported gently and consistently than disturbed further by anything too forceful.

「嬌」這個名聲，不是在說肺本質上就是虛弱或設計不良；它描述的是暴露程度，而不是劣等意義上的脆弱。從這裡延伸出一個傳統上被認可的含意：因為肺容易受到影響，它也因此被謹慎地對待——傳統上相對於其他臟腑，用比較溫和的方式來介入，理由是一個已經很暴露的臟腑，用溫和且持續的方式支持，會比用任何太強力的方式進一步擾動它來得好。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what is the lung's reputation among the five zang organs, and what does "delicate" mean in this context?

The lung is called the "delicate organ," traditionally considered the most easily and frequently affected zang organ; "delicate" describes exposure, not inherent weakness or inferiority.

According to the passage, what two features of the lung's traditional job explain this reputation?

It directly connects to outside air with every breath, exposing it to whatever the environment carries; and it sits at the top of the body, where disturbances rising from other organs are said to reach it first.

According to the last paragraph, what traditional implication follows from the lung's "delicate" reputation?

That the lung is traditionally treated cautiously, approached with milder interventions relative to other organs, on the reasoning that gentle, consistent support suits an already-exposed organ better than forceful disturbance.
