Unit 20: Explaining Zang-Fu to a Friend

Suppose a friend, hearing that Chinese medicine talks about the spleen governing digestion, asks, "Wait, isn't the spleen just part of your immune system?" This is the single most useful moment to clarify the whole unit: "In Western anatomy, yes — the spleen mainly filters blood and supports immunity. But in Chinese medicine, 'spleen' is the English translation for a much broader functional category that governs digestion and the transformation of food into qi and blood. The two systems are using the same English word for two different things — it's less like a factual disagreement and more like a translation collision."

假設一位朋友聽到中醫說脾主導消化，問：「等等，脾不就是免疫系統的一部分嗎？」這正是釐清整個單元最有用的時刻：「在西方解剖學裡，沒錯——脾主要是過濾血液、支持免疫力。但在中醫裡，『脾』這個英文翻譯，指的是一個廣得多的功能類別，主導消化和把食物轉化成氣與血。兩套系統用同一個英文字指稱兩件不同的事——與其說是事實上的分歧，不如說是一場翻譯上的碰撞。」

From there, it helps to explain the logic of the whole organ system in one sentence: "Chinese medicine sorts the body into five deep, storing organs — the zang — and six surface-level, processing organs — the fu — and pairs them up, so a problem in one often shows up in its partner. That's why a Chinese medicine consultation can ask about your sleep, your digestion, and your mood in the same conversation — it's not random, it's checking connected pairs."

接下來，用一句話解釋整個臟腑系統的邏輯會有幫助：「中醫把身體分成五個深層、儲存性質的臟——五臟——和六個表層、處理性質的腑——六腑——再把它們配對起來，所以一個地方的問題常常會表現在它的搭檔身上。這就是為什麼中醫問診會在同一次對話裡問你的睡眠、消化、情緒——這不是隨機的，是在檢查有連結的配對。」

Finally, it's worth closing with the same reminder that ends every lesson in this course: "None of this replaces an actual diagnosis. If you want to know what your spleen, kidney, or any organ is really doing, that's a question for a licensed doctor — Chinese medicine or Western medicine, whichever fits the concern." Naming this boundary clearly is what keeps the whole explanation useful rather than mystifying.

最後，值得用這門課每一課都用的同一句提醒來收尾：「這一切都不能取代真正的診斷。如果你想知道你的脾、腎，或任何一個臟腑實際上在做什麼，那是合格醫師才能回答的問題——中醫或西醫都可以，看你關心的是哪一種。」把這條界線講清楚，才能讓整個解釋保持有用，而不是變得神祕難懂。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, why does the passage describe the spleen confusion as "a translation collision" rather than "a factual disagreement"?

Because both systems are using the same English word ("spleen") to describe two genuinely different things, rather than actually disagreeing about a fact.

According to the passage, what one-sentence explanation is given for the whole zang-fu organ system?

Chinese medicine sorts the body into five deep, storing organs (zang) and six surface-level, processing organs (fu), and pairs them up, so a problem in one often shows up in its partner.

According to the last paragraph, why does the passage say naming the boundary clearly matters?

Because it keeps the whole explanation useful rather than mystifying — the reader understands the system is descriptive, not a substitute for an actual diagnosis.
