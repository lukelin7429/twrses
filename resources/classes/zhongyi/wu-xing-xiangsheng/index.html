Unit 12: The Generating Cycle

Once the five elements are understood as five patterns rather than five materials, the next question is how they relate to each other — and Chinese medicine describes two main cycles. The first is the generating cycle, often described as a parent-child or mother-child relationship: each element feeds and supports the next one in a fixed sequence. Wood generates fire, the way dry wood feeds a flame. Fire generates earth, the way a fire's ash becomes soil. Earth generates metal, the way ore is formed and mined from the ground. Metal generates water, the way condensation forms on a cold metal surface. And water generates wood, the way water nourishes a growing tree, completing the circle back to where it started.

一旦五行被理解成五種模式而不是五種物質，下一個問題就是它們彼此之間的關係——中醫描述了兩個主要的循環。第一個是相生的循環，常被形容成一種母子關係：每個元素依照固定的順序滋養、支持下一個元素。木生火，就像乾木餵養火焰一樣。火生土，就像火燒過的灰燼變成土壤一樣。土生金，就像礦石從地底被形成、開採出來一樣。金生水，就像冰冷的金屬表面會凝結出水珠一樣。水生木，就像水滋養一棵成長中的樹一樣，讓循環回到起點，完整地繞了一圈。

This cycle matters in Chinese medicine because organs are mapped onto the five elements, and the generating relationship describes how organs support one another under normal, healthy conditions. The kidney (water) is said to nourish the liver (wood); the liver nourishes the heart (fire); the heart nourishes the spleen (earth); the spleen nourishes the lung (metal); and the lung nourishes the kidney again, closing the loop. When one organ in this chain is running low, the "child" organ downstream may eventually run low too, simply from not being fed enough.

這個循環在中醫裡很重要，因為臟腑被對應到五行，而相生的關係描述的是臟腑在正常、健康的狀態下如何互相支持。腎（水）滋養肝（木）；肝滋養心（火）；心滋養脾（土）；脾滋養肺（金）；肺再滋養腎，把循環閉合起來。當這條鏈裡的某個臟腑不足時，下游的「子」臟腑最終也可能因為得不到足夠的滋養而跟著不足。

The generating cycle is not a claim that one organ literally manufactures another. It is a way of describing dependency: weakness in one place can show up, with a delay, somewhere else entirely — and a doctor familiar with this cycle knows to look upstream, at the "parent," rather than only treating the symptom where it finally appeared.

相生的循環並不是在主張某個臟腑真的在製造另一個臟腑。它是一種描述依存關係的方式：某個地方的虛弱，可能延遲一段時間後，在完全不同的地方顯現出來——熟悉這個循環的醫師，知道要往上游、往「母」臟腑去看，而不是只治療症狀最後出現的那個地方。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what is the order of the generating cycle, and what everyday comparison is given for each step?

Wood generates fire (like dry wood feeding a flame), fire generates earth (like ash becoming soil), earth generates metal (like ore forming in the ground), metal generates water (like condensation on cold metal), and water generates wood (like water nourishing a tree).

How are organs mapped onto the generating cycle, according to the passage?

The kidney (water) nourishes the liver (wood), the liver nourishes the heart (fire), the heart nourishes the spleen (earth), the spleen nourishes the lung (metal), and the lung nourishes the kidney again.

According to the last paragraph, what does the generating cycle actually describe, and how does that change what a doctor looks for?

It describes a dependency relationship, not literal manufacturing — weakness in one organ can show up later in a "downstream" organ, so a doctor familiar with the cycle looks upstream at the "parent" organ rather than only treating where the symptom appears.
