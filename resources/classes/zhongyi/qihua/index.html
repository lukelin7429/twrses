Unit 100: Qi Transformation: Turning Food and Air Into the Body's Own Substances

An earlier lesson in this unit distinguished source qi, fixed at birth, from acquired qi, generated daily from food and air. That lesson named the raw materials and the organs that gather them, but left out the actual process that turns raw material into something the body can use. Chinese medicine names that process "qi transformation": the conversion of food, water, and air into qi, blood, and body fluids, and the reverse conversion of used-up qi, blood, and fluids back into waste to be expelled.

這個單元稍早的一課，把出生時就固定的先天之氣，跟每天從食物與空氣裡生成的後天之氣區分開來。那一課點出了原料，也點出了收集原料的臟腑，但沒有講到真正把原料轉換成身體能用的東西的那個過程。中醫把這個過程稱為「氣化」：把食物、水、空氣轉換成氣、血、津液，以及反過來，把用完的氣、血、津液轉換回要排出的廢物。

Qi transformation isn't credited to any single organ, but treated as a task shared across the digestive and respiratory organs working together in sequence — the stomach breaking food down, the spleen extracting usable essence from it, the lung combining that essence with air, and the kidney supplying the underlying heat that powers each of these conversions. A failure at any single step in this sequence is traditionally read as a failure of qi transformation specifically, distinct from a simple shortage of raw material going in.

氣化不被歸功於單一一個臟腑，而是被當成消化與呼吸系統裡的臟腑依序合作完成的一項任務——胃把食物分解、脾從中萃取可用的精微、肺把這份精微跟空氣結合、腎則供應驅動每一步轉換所需的底層熱能。這個序列裡任何一個環節出了問題，傳統上都被讀成氣化本身的失職，跟單純進去的原料不夠是不同的問題。

This distinction matters clinically because it separates two problems that can look identical from the outside: a person eating enough food but still showing signs of qi deficiency may not have a supply problem at all, but a conversion problem — food going in without being properly transformed into usable qi. Recognizing which of the two is happening changes where support needs to go: toward providing more raw material, or toward strengthening the conversion process itself.

這個區分在臨床上很重要，因為它把兩個從外表看起來一模一樣的問題分開了：一個吃得夠多、卻仍然出現氣虛徵象的人，可能根本不是供應的問題，而是轉換的問題——食物進去了，卻沒有被好好轉換成能用的氣。認出眼前是哪一種，會改變支援該往哪裡去：是往提供更多原料的方向，還是往強化轉換過程本身的方向。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does "qi transformation" describe?

The conversion of food, water, and air into qi, blood, and body fluids, and the reverse conversion of used-up substances back into waste.

According to the passage, which organs share the task of qi transformation, and what does each contribute?

The stomach breaks food down, the spleen extracts usable essence, the lung combines it with air, and the kidney supplies the heat that powers the conversions.

According to the last paragraph, why does the distinction between a supply problem and a conversion problem matter?

Because a person eating enough food but still qi-deficient may have a conversion problem, not a supply problem — knowing which one changes where support should go.
