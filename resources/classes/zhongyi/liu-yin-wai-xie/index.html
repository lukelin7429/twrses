Unit 26: Six Excesses: When the Weather Attacks

English already has a phrase that captures part of what Chinese medicine calls the "six excesses": "catching a chill." Most English speakers use this loosely, without really believing that cold weather itself causes illness rather than the germs that happen to spread more easily in it. Chinese medicine takes the idea more literally and more systematically, describing six environmental patterns — wind, cold, heat, dampness, dryness, and fire — as external factors capable of invading the body directly and causing a recognizable pattern of illness.

英文本身已經有一個片語，捕捉到了中醫「六淫」概念的一部分：「著涼」（catching a chill）。多數英語使用者這樣講的時候，並不是很認真地相信是寒冷的天氣本身造成生病，而不是恰好在那種天氣裡更容易散播的病菌。中醫把這個概念講得更直白、也更有系統：把風、寒、暑、濕、燥、火這六種環境模式，描述成能直接入侵身體、造成一種可辨識的疾病模式的外在因子。

Each of the six has its own signature. Cold tends to cause contraction, stiffness, and pain that eases with warmth. Dampness tends to cause heaviness, sluggishness, and symptoms that are slow to resolve, the way damp air itself feels heavy and lingers. Heat and fire tend to cause redness, rapid onset, and a feeling of being overheated from within. Dryness tends to cause thirst and irritation of anything moist by nature, such as the throat or skin. Wind — discussed more in the next lesson — tends to cause sudden changes and symptoms that move from place to place.

這六種各自有自己的特徵。寒往往造成收縮、僵硬，還有遇溫暖就緩解的疼痛。濕往往造成沉重感、遲滯，還有慢慢才會好轉的症狀，就像潮濕的空氣本身讓人覺得沉重、久久不散。暑跟火往往造成發紅、發作快速，還有一種從體內發熱的感覺。燥往往造成口渴，還有任何本質濕潤的部位（像喉嚨或皮膚）感到刺激不適。風——下一課會更詳細討論——往往造成突然的變化，還有到處遊走的症狀。

The word "excess" in "six excesses" doesn't mean these six things are bad in normal amounts — a cold breeze or a humid day is simply weather. They become "excesses" specifically when a person's body is not equipped to handle the exposure, which is why the same weather can leave one person unaffected and another person clearly unwell. It's less a claim about the weather itself and more a claim about the meeting point between weather and a particular body's current resilience.

「六淫」裡的「淫」（過度）這個字，不代表這六樣東西在正常份量下是壞的——一陣涼風或一個潮濕的日子，本來就只是天氣而已。它們變成「淫」（過度），特別是在一個人的身體沒有能力應付那次暴露的時候，這也是為什麼同樣的天氣，可以讓一個人完全沒事，卻讓另一個人明顯不舒服。這與其說是對天氣本身的主張，不如說是天氣跟一個人目前的抵抗力之間，那個相遇點的主張。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what English phrase captures part of what Chinese medicine calls the "six excesses," and how does the passage say most English speakers use it?

"Catching a chill" — most English speakers use it loosely, without really believing cold weather itself causes illness rather than germs that spread more easily in it.

According to the passage, what is the signature symptom pattern of "dampness"?

Heaviness, sluggishness, and symptoms that are slow to resolve, the way damp air itself feels heavy and lingers.

According to the last paragraph, what does the word "excess" actually depend on, according to the passage?

It depends on the meeting point between the weather and a particular body's current resilience, not on the weather alone.

Words & Phrases · 生字及片語
