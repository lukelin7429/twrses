Unit 37: The Temperature of Food Has Nothing to Do With a Thermometer

Chinese medicine assigns every food a temperature nature — cold, cool, neutral, warm, or hot — and the label has nothing to do with the food's actual physical temperature. A bowl of watermelon straight out of a hot summer garden is still classified as "cold" in nature, and a cup of ginger tea served lukewarm is still "warm." The nature describes the effect the food tends to produce in the body after it's eaten, not the temperature on a thermometer.

中醫給每一種食物都分配了一種寒熱性質——寒、涼、平、溫、熱——而這個標籤跟食物實際的物理溫度毫無關係。一碗剛從炎熱夏日花園裡端出來的西瓜，性質上仍然被分類成「寒」；一杯溫溫的薑茶，仍然是「溫」的。這個性質描述的是這個食物吃下去之後，傾向在身體裡產生什麼效果，不是溫度計量出來的溫度。

Cooling foods, like watermelon, cucumber, and mung beans, are traditionally used to clear heat and generate fluids, which is why they're especially popular in the summer heat this course discussed in the seasonal unit. Warming foods, like ginger, cinnamon, and lamb, are traditionally used to dispel cold and support yang, which is why they show up so often in winter cooking. Neutral foods, like rice and most staple grains, sit in the middle and rarely push a person's balance in either direction.

涼性的食物，像西瓜、小黃瓜、綠豆，傳統上被用來清熱生津，這也是為什麼它們在這門課節氣單元討論過的夏天特別受歡迎。溫性的食物，像薑、肉桂、羊肉，傳統上被用來驅寒助陽，這也是為什麼它們在冬天的料理裡這麼常出現。平性的食物，像米飯和大部分主食穀類，位在中間，很少把一個人的平衡明顯推向任何一個方向。

The practical use of this system isn't to sort foods into "good" and "bad" categories — that would just recreate the mistake this course corrected in an earlier unit about yin and yang. The use is matching a food's tendency to a person's current state: a person already running warm gains less from more warming foods, and a person already running cold gains little from a diet of only cooling ones. Food temperature, read this way, is simply another application of the same yin-yang logic covered throughout this course.

這套系統實際的用途，不是把食物分成「好」跟「壞」的類別——那只會重演這門課稍早在陰陽單元裡糾正過的那個錯誤。它的用途，是把食物的傾向對應到一個人目前的狀態：一個本來就偏燥熱的人，吃更多溫性食物得到的好處有限；一個本來就偏怕冷的人，只吃涼性食物也得不到什麼好處。用這個角度來看，食物的寒熱性質，只是這門課從頭到尾都在講的同一套陰陽邏輯的另一個應用。

Questions · 閱讀理解

According to the passage, what does a food's "temperature nature" actually describe?

The effect the food tends to produce in the body after it's eaten, not its actual physical temperature.

According to the passage, why are cooling foods like watermelon especially popular in summer?

Because cooling foods are traditionally used to clear heat and generate fluids, matching the season's need discussed in the seasonal wellness unit.

According to the last paragraph, what is the "practical use" of the food temperature system, and what mistake does it avoid repeating?

Matching a food's tendency to a person's current state, rather than sorting foods into "good" and "bad" categories — avoiding the same mistake corrected earlier about treating yin and yang as good versus bad.
