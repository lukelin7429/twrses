Big idea · 一句話看懂

We know the past through evidence: things people left behind, records they wrote or drew, and words passed down from one generation to the next. For the long time before writing, we depend on archaeology, and in ground that has not been disturbed, the deeper layer is the older one.
我們靠證據知道過去：留下來的東西、寫下來或畫下來的記錄、一代一代傳下來的話。沒有文字的漫長時代要靠考古；沒有被擾動過的地層，越下面越古老。

Unit 1: How Do We Know What Happened Long Ago?

What did you eat for breakfast last Tuesday? You may not remember. Now try a harder question: what did people in Taiwan eat three thousand years ago? Nobody alive today saw it, and nobody at the time wrote it down. Yet historians and archaeologists can still tell us a good deal about the distant past. They do not guess. They work like detectives. They look for evidence, and they say only what the evidence lets them say. This lesson is about that evidence: where it comes from, how it is read, and why careful people sometimes read it differently.

上星期二的早餐，你吃了什麼？你可能不記得了。現在換一個更難的問題：三千年前住在台灣的人吃什麼？今天活著的人沒有一個親眼看過，當時也沒有人把它寫下來。可是歷史學家和考古學家，還是能告訴我們不少很久以前的事。他們不是用猜的。他們像偵探一樣工作：先找證據，而且證據能說到哪裡，他們就只說到哪裡。這一課要談的就是這些證據：它們從哪裡來、怎麼解讀，以及為什麼認真的人有時候會讀出不一樣的結果。

Evidence of the past comes in three main kinds. The first is things: a pot, a stone tool, a coin, a house. An object that people made or changed is called an artifact. The second is records that people wrote or drew: letters, diaries, maps, photographs. The third is words passed down by mouth, such as the stories, songs, and memories that older people give to younger people. When someone records those memories in an interview, we call it oral history. A source made at the time of the events is a primary source. A source made later by someone without firsthand experience, such as a textbook, is a secondary source.

關於過去的證據，主要有三種。第一種是東西：一個陶罐、一件石器、一枚硬幣、一棟房子。人做出來或加工過的物品，叫做文物。第二種是人寫下來或畫下來的記錄：信、日記、地圖、照片。第三種是用嘴巴傳下來的話，例如長輩說給晚輩聽的故事、歌謠和回憶。有人用訪談把這些回憶記錄下來，就叫做口述歷史。事情發生的那個時候留下來的資料，是一手史料。後來才由沒有親身經歷的人寫成的資料（例如課本），是二手史料。

Writing is young compared with people. The National Museum of Prehistory in Taitung points to a text written in 1603 as the earliest record of customs in Taiwan. But people were on the island long before that. The oldest culture found so far, called the Changbin Culture, goes back about 30,000 years. The time before written records is called prehistory, and in Taiwan it covers almost all of the human past. Taiwan’s school curriculum says that the island’s earliest inhabitants left no written materials, and it names the two most important paths for tracing them: archaeological finds and oral traditions.

和人類比起來，文字很年輕。位在臺東的國立臺灣史前文化博物館指出，1603 年寫成的一篇文字是最早關於台灣風俗的紀錄。但早在那之前，島上就已經有人了。目前發現最古老的文化叫做長濱文化，可以往回推到大約 3 萬年前。還沒有文字記錄的時代叫做史前時代；在台灣，它幾乎占了人類在這裡的全部時間。台灣的課程綱要說，臺灣最早的主人們沒有留下文字資料；要追索他們的足跡，最重要的兩條途徑是考古資料和口頭傳說。

Archaeology is the study of the human past through the things people left behind. Much of that evidence is under the ground. Year after year, soil, sand, and trash pile up, one layer on top of another. So in ground that has not been disturbed, a deeper layer is older than the layer above it. This rule is called the law of superposition. It lets an archaeologist put finds in order (this pot is older than that knife) even without knowing how many years old either one is. That is why archaeologists dig slowly and write down exactly where each thing lay.

考古學是透過人留下來的東西，來研究人類的過去。這些證據有很多埋在地下。一年又一年，泥土、沙子和垃圾越堆越高，一層疊著一層。所以，只要地層沒有被擾動過，下面那一層就比上面那一層古老。這條規則叫做疊置定律。有了它，考古學家就算不知道兩樣東西各是幾年前的，也能排出先後（這個陶罐比那把刀早）。這就是為什麼考古學家挖得很慢，而且要把每一樣東西躺在哪裡，一五一十地記下來。

To put a number on the age, scientists need other tools. One of the best known is radiocarbon dating. It works on things that were once alive, such as wood, bone, or shell, and it reaches back about 50,000 years. The answer is never one exact year. It comes as a range, and it can change when new samples are tested. At the Baxian Caves in eastern Taiwan, earlier tests placed the site between 5,000 and 15,000 years ago. Tests reported in 2009 gave an age of more than 20,000 years. When the evidence changes, good history changes with it.

要說出「幾年前」這個數字，科學家還需要別的工具。最有名的方法之一是碳十四定年。它可以用在曾經活過的東西上，例如木頭、骨頭或貝殼，最遠能測到大約 5 萬年前。答案從來不是準確的某一年，而是一個範圍；有新的樣本拿去測，答案也可能改變。在台灣東部的八仙洞，早期的測定把這個遺址放在距今 5,000 到 1 萬 5,000 年之間；2009 年公布的測定結果則是超過 2 萬年。證據變了，好的歷史也跟著改。

Evidence is never complete, and people do not always read it the same way. Two witnesses can remember one day differently. Two institutions can give different dates for one culture. People who stood on different sides of an event often tell it in different words. So every lesson in this series has two special cards. “How Do We Know?” shows the evidence behind the lesson and where it is kept. “Different Views” puts accounts that disagree side by side, with their sources, and does not pick a winner. Your part is to ask: Who made this? When? Why? Does anything else agree with it?

證據永遠不會是完整的，人們解讀證據的方式也不一定相同。兩個目擊者，對同一天的記憶可能不一樣。兩個機構，對同一個文化可能給出不同的年代。站在事件不同位置的人，常常用不同的話來說它。所以這個系列的每一課都有兩張特別的卡。「我們怎麼知道？」告訴你這一課依據的證據是什麼、收藏在哪裡。「不同的說法」把互相不一致的說法並列，各自附上出處，不替誰下結論。你要做的是問：這是誰做的？什麼時候？為什麼？還有別的證據和它一致嗎？

Questions · 閱讀理解

What are the three main kinds of evidence about the past? Give one example of each.

Things (for example a pot or a stone tool), records that people wrote or drew (a letter, a map, a photograph), and words passed down by mouth (a grandmother’s story or a song).

An archaeologist finds a pot in a layer below an iron knife. What can she say about their ages, and what must be true about the ground?

She can say the pot is older than the knife, even without knowing either age in years. This holds only if the ground has not been disturbed, for example by a pit dug later.

Why does every lesson in this series have a card called “Different Views”?

Because evidence is never complete and people read it differently. The card puts the accounts side by side with their sources and does not choose one, so readers can think for themselves.

On the Timeline · 在時間軸上

Where this lesson sits 這一課講的是哪裡

Most lessons in this series cover one stretch of time. This one covers the whole line, because it is about how we know any of it. Try the second scale: every year the same width.
這個系列大部分的課只講一段時間。這一課講的是整條線，因為它談的是「我們怎麼知道」。試試第二種尺度：每一年一樣寬。

On the “every year the same width” scale, the time with written records, from 1603 to today, is less than 1 percent of this line. For the long stretch to the left of it, the main evidence is archaeology and oral tradition. Think Like a Historian · 像歷史學家一樣想

Two People, Two Stories 兩個人說的不一樣

A made-up case from an ordinary school. Two records of the same school field day do not agree. There is no right answer to find here. The point is to learn what to ask.
一個編出來的例子，發生在一所普通的學校。同一場運動會的兩份記錄，說的不一樣。這裡沒有標準答案要找；重點是學會問問題。

The school, the classes, and both records are invented for this exercise. The four questions follow the reading habits that historians use, called sourcing and corroboration (see Sources).這所學校、這些班級和兩份記錄，都是為了這個練習編出來的。四個問題依據的是歷史學家閱讀史料的習慣：先看來源（sourcing），再找別的資料互相印證（corroboration），見資料出處。

🧪A method方法

Radiocarbon dating碳十四定年

The ages of the Baxian Caves in this lesson come from this method. It measures how much carbon-14 is left in something that was once alive. Results are given as a range of years.這一課裡八仙洞的年代，靠的就是這個方法。它測量「曾經活過的東西」裡還剩下多少碳十四；結果是一個年代範圍。

History English · 歷史英語

Eight sentences for talking about the past 談過去的八句英文

Historians choose their words to show how sure they are. Tap a speaker to hear each sentence, then try changing the part in bold.
歷史學家用字很小心，為的是讓人知道他有多大的把握。點 🔊 聽每一句，再試著把粗體的部分換掉。

  1. About 30,000 years ago, people were already living in Taiwan.大約 3 萬年前，台灣就已經有人居住了。about … years ago: for a date that is an estimate.about … years ago：年代是估計的時候用。
  2. In 1603, Chen Di wrote a text called Dongfan ji.1603 年，陳第寫了一篇叫《東番記》的文字。in + year: for a date we know exactly. Say “sixteen oh three.”in＋年份：確切知道年份時用。1603 唸作 sixteen oh three。
  3. The seventeenth century means the years from 1601 to 1700.十七世紀指的是 1601 年到 1700 年。1603 is in the seventeenth century, not the sixteenth: the first hundred years were the first century.1603 年屬於十七世紀，不是十六世紀：因為最開始的一百年是「第一世紀」。
  4. According to the museum, the culture began about 30,000 years ago.根據博物館的說法，這個文化大約從 3 萬年前開始。according to: says whose statement it is.according to：說明這是誰的說法。
  5. This layer is older than the one above it.這一層比它上面那一層古老。older than / younger than: to put two things in order.older than／younger than：替兩樣東西排先後。
  6. We know this because archaeologists found charcoal in the cave.我們知道這件事，是因為考古學家在洞裡找到了木炭。We know this because …: gives the evidence.We know this because …：把證據說出來。
  7. The evidence suggests that people made fires here.證據顯示，曾經有人在這裡生火。suggests: likely, but not certain. Stronger: shows. Weaker: may mean.suggests：很可能，但不是百分之百。更肯定用 shows，更保留用 may mean。
  8. Historians disagree about this, so we show both views.歷史學家對這件事看法不一，所以我們把兩種說法都列出來。disagree about: there is more than one view.disagree about：對某件事有不只一種看法。

In Taiwan · 在台灣

A long time without writing, and where to see the evidence 一段很長、沒有文字的時間，以及去哪裡親眼看證據

Four short notes that connect this lesson to Taiwan.
四則短短的說明，把這一課和台灣連起來。

Prehistory · 史前

What “prehistory” means「史前」是什麼意思

The National Museum of Prehistory explains it this way: prehistory is the age without written records, when people passed on what had happened by word of mouth, from one generation to the next; once written records appear, the historical period begins. “Prehistory” does not mean that nothing happened, or that people had no past. It only says what kind of evidence we have.
國立臺灣史前文化博物館這樣說明：史前時期是指沒有文字紀錄的年代，史前人們用口語流傳的方式，把過去發生的事一代傳一代地說給後代子孫聽；當文字記錄開始出現，就是所謂的歷史時期。「史前」不是說那時候沒發生什麼事，也不是說那時候的人沒有過去——它說的只是「我們手上有哪一種證據」。

Oral tradition · 口傳

Knowledge carried by voice用聲音傳下來的知識

A study published by the Council of Indigenous Peoples notes that Taiwan’s Indigenous peoples have long been described as passing on knowledge by word of mouth. The Council’s website introduces the officially recognized peoples one by one. The school curriculum uses “Indigenous peoples” in a wider, historical sense that also includes the Pingpu peoples and other Indigenous groups.
原住民族委員會刊登的一篇研究指出，臺灣原住民族一向被認為是以口傳方式溝通交流、傳承知識。原民會的網站逐一介紹法定的原住民族；課程綱要裡的「原住民族」則是從歷史文化的角度來說，範圍比較廣，包含法定原住民族、平埔族群及其他原住民族群。

A date that moved · 會改的年代

The Baxian Caves八仙洞

For decades the Baxian Caves in Taitung were placed between 5,000 and 15,000 years ago. In 2009 a new survey found fire pits, and radiocarbon dating put the site at more than 20,000 years. Today’s site record says about 30,000. The lesson is not that the old books were bad. It is that a date is an answer to a question, and the answer improves.
幾十年來，臺東的八仙洞一直被放在距今 5,000 到 1 萬 5,000 年之間。2009 年，新的調查找到火塘，碳十四定年把這個遺址推到 2 萬年以上；現在的遺址資料寫的是大約 3 萬年。這不是說舊書不好，而是：年代是「一個問題的答案」，而答案會越來越好。

Go and see · 親眼看

Three national museums三座國立博物館

You can see this kind of evidence yourself. The National Museum of Prehistory is in Taitung City, and its Museum of Archaeology, Tainan Branch, is in Xinshi District, Tainan. The National Museum of Taiwan History is in Annan District, Tainan. The National Taiwan Museum is in Zhongzheng District, Taipei. When you visit, read the labels the way a historian would: what is it, where was it found, and how do they know its age?
這類證據，你可以自己去看。國立臺灣史前文化博物館在臺東市，它的南科考古館在臺南市新市區；國立臺灣歷史博物館在臺南市安南區；國立臺灣博物館在臺北市中正區。去的時候，試著像歷史學家一樣讀說明牌：這是什麼？在哪裡出土的？他們怎麼知道它的年代？

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思History is a fixed list of facts. Once it is in a book, it never changes.歷史就是一串固定的事實，寫進書裡就不會再變。

✓ Fact · 事實What happened does not change, but what we know about it does. New evidence moved the age of the Baxian Caves from under 15,000 years to more than 20,000.發生過的事不會變，但我們對它的了解會變。新的證據，把八仙洞的年代從 1 萬 5,000 年以內改成 2 萬年以上。

✗ Myth · 迷思If nobody wrote it down, we cannot know anything about it.沒有人寫下來的事，我們就什麼都不可能知道。

✓ Fact · 事實Things and spoken words are evidence too. Most of the human past in Taiwan lies in the time before written records.東西和口耳相傳的話也是證據。人類在台灣的過去，大部分落在沒有文字記錄的時代。

✗ Myth · 迷思Whatever lies deeper is always older.埋得比較深的東西，一定比較古老。

✓ Fact · 事實Only in ground that has not been disturbed. A pit dug later, or a plow turning the soil, can mix the layers and leave younger things below older ones.只有在沒被擾動過的地層才成立。後來挖的坑，或犁田翻土，都可能把地層弄亂，讓年輕的東西跑到古老的東西下面。

✗ Myth · 迷思A primary source always tells the truth.一手史料說的一定是真的。

✓ Fact · 事實A primary source is close to the event, which is valuable. But its maker stood in one place and had a point of view. Historians compare it with other evidence.一手史料離事情很近，這很寶貴。但留下它的人只站在一個位置，也有自己的觀點。歷史學家會拿別的證據來對照。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

Interview an Elder 訪問一位長輩

You need · 準備材料

  • A notebook and pencil, or a phone that can record sound (ask permission first)筆記本和鉛筆，或可以錄音的手機（先徵求同意）
  • Three questions prepared in advance事先準備好的三個問題
  • An older family member or neighbor who is willing to talk一位願意聊聊的家中長輩或鄰居

Steps · 步驟

  1. Choose one small thing to ask about: “What was your school like?” or “What did you eat on New Year’s Eve when you were ten?”選一件小事來問：「你的學校長什麼樣子？」或「你十歲那年的除夕吃什麼？」
  2. Ask, and then listen. Write down the person’s own words where you can. Note the date, the place, and who was speaking.問完就專心聽。盡量照對方的原話記下來。寫上日期、地點，以及說話的人是誰。
  3. Ask one follow-up question: “How do you know?” or “Who else was there?”再追問一個問題：「你怎麼知道的？」或「當時還有誰在場？」
  4. In class, tell the story in three English sentences, starting with “According to my …”回到課堂上，用三句英文把故事說出來，第一句用「According to my …」開頭。

You have just made a primary source: an oral history, with a date and a name on it. If two students interview people about the same event, compare what they heard. Do not correct the elders. Ask instead why the memories might differ. Always ask before recording, and let the person skip any question.
你剛剛做出了一份一手史料：一份口述歷史，上面有日期，也有名字。如果有兩位同學訪問到同一件事，把聽到的拿來比一比。不要去糾正長輩，而是問：為什麼兩個人的記憶會不一樣？錄音之前一定要先問；對方不想回答的問題，就跳過。

Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

The Story of One Old Thing 一樣舊東西的故事

You need · 準備材料

  • One old object from home that is safe to carry (or a photo of it)從家裡帶一樣可以安全攜帶的舊東西（或它的照片）
  • An index card for a museum label一張小卡片，用來寫說明牌
  • A ruler一把尺

Steps · 步驟

  1. Look before you ask. What is it made of? How big is it? Is it worn, mended, or marked?先看，再問。它是什麼做的？多大？有沒有磨損、修補過的痕跡，或上面有沒有字？
  2. Guess how old it is and write down your reason: “I think it is about … years old because …”猜猜它有多舊，並寫下理由：「I think it is about … years old because …」
  3. Now ask at home. Who used it? When? Is there a date on it, or a photo that shows it?現在回家問。誰用過它？什麼時候？它上面有沒有日期，或有沒有哪張照片拍到它？
  4. Write a label: what it is, about how old, and how you know. Set out the class’s objects from oldest to newest.寫一張說明牌：這是什麼、大約多舊、你怎麼知道的。把全班的東西從最舊排到最新。

Step 2 is relative dating from the object alone; step 3 adds a record or an oral account. Ask which objects were hardest to place in the line, and what evidence would settle it. Remind students to ask permission before taking anything from home, and not to bring anything fragile or valuable.
第二步是只憑東西本身做相對定年；第三步加上了文字記錄或口述。問問學生：哪幾樣東西最難排進隊伍裡？要有什麼證據才排得定？提醒學生：從家裡拿東西之前要先問過大人，易碎或貴重的東西不要帶來。

  1. “Primary sources” are “the raw materials of history — original documents and objects which were created at the time under study”; secondary sources are “accounts or interpretations of events created by someone without firsthand experience.”一手史料是歷史的原料：在所研究的那個時代留下來的文件與物品；二手史料是沒有親身經歷的人所做的敘述或詮釋Library of Congress, Teaching with the Library blog (2011) ↗
  2. Artifact: “Objects humans made or modified.” Disturbance: an event “that dislodges and disrupts archeological deposits.” Relative dating; absolute dating; radiocarbon dating works on “a once-living organism.”遺物（artifact）：人做的或改造過的物品；擾動：使考古堆積移位、打亂的事件；相對定年、絕對定年；碳十四定年用在曾經活過的生物上U.S. National Park Service, Archeology Program glossary ↗
  3. “Oral history is a field of study and a method of gathering, preserving, and interpreting the voices and memories of people, communities, and participants in past events.”口述歷史是一門學問，也是一種方法：蒐集、保存並詮釋人們、社群與親歷者的聲音與記憶Oral History Association, “Oral History: Defined” ↗
  4. “Archaeology is the study of the ancient and recent human past through material remains.” “Prehistoric archaeological sites are those without a written record.”考古學是透過物質遺留來研究古代與近代人類的過去；史前的考古遺址是沒有文字記錄的遺址Society for American Archaeology, “What Is Archaeology?” ↗
  5. The law of superposition: “in any undisturbed deposit the oldest layers are normally located at the lowest level.”疊置定律：沒有被擾動的堆積裡，最古老的地層通常在最下面Encyclopaedia Britannica, “stratigraphy” ↗
  6. “Typically, the strata get older as archeologists dig into the earth, because layers accumulate one on top of another.”考古學家越往下挖，地層通常越古老，因為地層是一層疊一層堆起來的U.S. National Park Service, “Stratigraphy and Superposition in Archeology” ↗
  7. Radiocarbon: a half-life of 5730 years; “the age range within which this method is useful is between 50 and 50,000 years old.”碳十四的半衰期是 5730 年；這個方法適用的年代範圍大約在 50 年到 5 萬年之間Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, “Dating” ↗
  8. A radiocarbon result is calibrated and reported as an age range, for example a range “we could be 95% sure” the sample comes from.碳十四的測定結果要經過校正，並以年代範圍表示，例如「有 95% 的把握樣本來自這個範圍」Oxford Radiocarbon Accelerator Unit, “Radiocarbon Calibration” ↗
  9. Prehistory is the age without written records; Chen Di’s Dongfan ji (1603) left the earliest record of customs in Taiwan「史前時期是指沒有文字紀錄的年代，史前人們用口語流傳的方式，把過去發生的事一代傳一代地說給後代子孫聽。當文字記錄開始出現，就是所謂的歷史時期。」「1603年，明朝的陳第……在《東番記》……裡面留下最早關於臺灣風俗的紀錄。讓臺灣走進了漢人的歷史時期。」國立臺灣史前文化博物館〈從史前走進歷史〉語音導覽 ↗
  10. The Changbin Culture, about 30,000 to 5,000 years ago; stone tools made only by chipping, no pottery; fire pits found「長濱文化是臺灣舊石器時代的代表，時間大約在30,000年到5,000年前之間，是臺灣最古老的史前文化。」石器完全以打製的方式製作，還沒有燒製陶器的技術；有發現人類使用的「火塘」遺跡國立臺灣史前文化博物館〈臺灣的史前時代：舊石器時代〉 ↗
  11. The Dapenkeng Culture, Taiwan’s earliest Neolithic culture, about 5,500 to 4,200 years ago; cord-marked pottery and polished stone tools (the same page also says the first Neolithic people arrived about 6,000 to 5,000 years ago)「『大坌坑文化』是臺灣最早的新石器時代文化，時間大約在距今5,500到4,200年前之間，又稱為『粗繩紋陶文化』。」帶著磨製石器及製作陶器的技術（同一頁另寫「約距今6,000至5,000年前」新石器時代文化傳入）國立臺灣史前文化博物館〈臺灣的史前時代：新石器時代〉 ↗
  12. Ironworking reaches Taiwan about 2,500 years ago; glass beads among the ornaments of the period「約距今2,500年前，製作鐵器的技術傳入臺灣」；出現銅飾、金飾及玻璃珠或瑪瑙珠等裝飾品國立臺灣史前文化博物館〈臺灣的史前時代：鐵器時代〉 ↗
  13. Baxian Caves (Changbin Township, Taitung County), a National Archaeological Site: cultural layers of about 30,000–15,000 years (Paleolithic) and 6,000–4,800 years (pre-ceramic); a small exhibition room at the visitor center八仙洞考古遺址（臺東縣長濱鄉，國定考古遺址）：「代表文化層 距今約30000-15000年（舊石器時代）、6000-4800年（先陶時代）……」；「長濱文化是至今在台灣所發現最古老的史前文化，以打製石器為主要工具，尚無製陶技術出現。」遊客服務中心內有小展覽室文化部文化資產局 國家考古遺址出土遺物典藏系統 ↗
  14. Baxian Caves: 1968–69 samples gave about 15,000 and about 5,000 years; in 2009 a survey found fire pits and radiocarbon dating showed more than 20,000 years民國 57 至 58 年的樣本測得 1 萬 5000 年與 5000 年前後，「八仙洞遺址一直被界定在5000年至1萬5000年間」；2009 年在洞內找到舊石器時代火塘，「經美國哈佛大學碳14年代測定，證明八仙洞遺址距今超過2萬年」中央社 2009 年報導，國立臺灣史前文化博物館考古網轉載 ↗
  15. The earliest inhabitants left no written materials; archaeology and oral tradition are the two most important paths. Curriculum items 歷 A-Ⅳ-1, 歷 Ba-Ⅳ-1, 歷 Ba-Ⅳ-2, 歷 1c-Ⅳ-1, Cb-Ⅲ-2, 3b-Ⅲ-1; the curriculum’s wider use of “Indigenous peoples”「臺灣最早的主人們沒有留下文字資料，要追索他們在臺灣的足跡，考古資料和口頭傳說是最重要的兩個途徑。」「原住民族」「包含法定原住民族、平埔族群及其他原住民族群」；歷 A-Ⅳ-1、歷 Ba-Ⅳ-1、歷 Ba-Ⅳ-2、歷 1c-Ⅳ-1、Cb-Ⅲ-2、3b-Ⅲ-1十二年國民基本教育課程綱要 社會領域（教育部，2018） ↗
  16. Taiwan’s Indigenous peoples have long been described as passing on knowledge orally (the article goes on to discuss later forms of writing)「臺灣原住民族一向被認為是以口傳方式溝通交流、傳承知識，是無文字傳統的族群。」（文章接著討論後來出現的書寫形式）李慧慧，《原住民族文獻》（原住民族委員會，2020） ↗
  17. Introductions to the officially recognized Indigenous peoples原住民族各族簡介原住民族委員會 ↗
  18. “There is a point of view present in every textbook, news program, or advertisement.”每一本課本、每一則新聞、每一個廣告裡，都有一個觀點Library of Congress, Teaching with the Library blog, “Thinking About Perspective” (2011) ↗
  19. “historians source, contextualize, and corroborate whenever they read historical documents.”歷史學家讀史料時，都會看來源、放回時空背景，並找別的資料互相印證Digital Inquiry Group, “Getting Started with Reading Like a Historian Lessons” ↗
  20. Names and locations of the museums: National Museum of Prehistory (Taitung City; Museum of Archaeology, Tainan Branch, Xinshi District, Tainan)國立臺灣史前文化博物館（康樂本館：臺東縣臺東市；南科考古館：臺南市新市區）National Museum of Prehistory · 國立臺灣史前文化博物館 ↗
  21. National Museum of Taiwan History (Annan District, Tainan City)國立臺灣歷史博物館（臺南市安南區）National Museum of Taiwan History · 國立臺灣歷史博物館 ↗
  22. The Penghu 1 jaw: a male Denisovan, “estimated to be between 10,000-70,000 or 130,000-190,000 years old” (study published in Science, 2025)澎湖 1 號下顎骨：男性丹尼索瓦人，年代估計在 1 萬到 7 萬年，或 13 萬到 19 萬年之間（2025 年發表於《Science》）University of Copenhagen, Section for Archaeology (2025) ↗
