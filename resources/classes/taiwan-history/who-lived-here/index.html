Taiwan's Story · Unit 1 · Lesson 2 · 台灣歷史 單元一 第二課

Who Lived in Taiwan Thousands of Years Ago?幾千年前，誰住在台灣？

Slide through 30,000 years on a map of eight real archaeological sites and watch them light up. Look closely at a jade earring more than 2,000 years old, and sort eight finds into the three ages of Taiwan’s prehistory.
在一張有八個真實考古遺址的地圖上，拖著滑桿走過三萬年，看它們一個一個亮起來。仔細看一件兩千多年前的玉耳飾，再把八樣出土物分到台灣史前的三個時代。

Big idea · 一句話看懂

People lived in Taiwan for tens of thousands of years before anyone wrote about them. Archaeological sites show their lives changing: first hunters who chipped stone, then farmers who made pottery and jade, then people who smelted iron and traded across the sea. How these cultures are related to today’s Indigenous peoples is a question the evidence has not fully answered.
還沒有人把他們寫下來之前，已經有人在台灣住了好幾萬年。考古遺址讓我們看見生活的改變：先是打製石器的獵人，然後是會做陶器和玉器的農人，再來是會煉鐵、渡海做交易的人。這些文化和今天的原住民族是什麼關係，證據還沒有完全回答。

Unit 2: Who Lived in Taiwan Thousands of Years Ago?

In what is now a park in Taipei, archaeologists found mounds made mostly of shells. About three thousand years ago, people who lived here ate shellfish and threw the shells away in the same place. Animal bones, fish bones, broken pots, and worn-out stone tools went onto the pile too. It was their trash heap, and more than that: they also buried their dead in it. It is called the Yuanshan shell mound. Building work has destroyed most of it, but it is still one of the most important sites in northern Taiwan. Nobody wrote a word about these people, yet what they left tells us what they ate and what they made.

在今天臺北的一座公園裡，考古學家找到主要由貝殼堆成的小丘。大約三千年前，住在這裡的人吃貝類，吃完就把殼丟在同一個地方。獸骨、魚骨、破掉的陶罐、用壞的石器，也都丟到這一堆上面。這是他們的垃圾堆，但不只是垃圾堆：他們也把過世的人葬在裡面。它叫做圓山貝塚。後來的工程破壞了它的大部分，但它仍然是台灣北部最重要的遺址之一。沒有人為這些人寫下一個字，但他們留下的東西告訴我們，他們吃什麼、做了什麼東西。

Archaeologists divide Taiwan’s long prehistory into three ages, named for what people made their tools from. In the Paleolithic, or Old Stone Age, people made stone tools only by chipping, and they did not make pottery. In the Neolithic, or New Stone Age, people ground and polished their stone tools, made pottery, and began to farm. In the Iron Age, people learned to make iron. These ages did not begin on one day. They are names that archaeologists use to put thousands of finds in order.

考古學家把台灣漫長的史前時代分成三個時代，名字來自人們用什麼材料做工具。舊石器時代，人只會用打製的方式做石器，還不會做陶器。新石器時代，人把石器磨光、會做陶器，也開始耕種。鐵器時代，人學會了製作鐵器。這些時代不是在某一天開始的；它們是考古學家用來替成千上萬件出土物排順序的名稱。

The oldest culture found so far is the Changbin Culture. At the Baxian Caves on the east coast, people lived in caves beside the sea. They hunted and gathered their food, and they left stone tools and the remains of fires. Then, about 6,000 to 5,000 years ago, according to the National Museum of Prehistory, another group of people arrived by sea from the southeast coast of the Asian mainland. They brought pottery and polished stone tools, and they had some knowledge of farming. Many scholars think so, the museum adds, although no boats from that time have been found. Archaeologists call their culture Dapenkeng, after a site in what is now New Taipei City.

目前發現最古老的文化是長濱文化。在東海岸的八仙洞，人們住在海邊的山洞裡，靠打獵和採集過活，留下了石器和用火的痕跡。後來，依國立臺灣史前文化博物館的說明，大約 6,000 到 5,000 年前，另一群人從亞洲大陸東南沿海渡海而來。他們帶來陶器和磨製石器，對農作也有一定的知識。博物館補充：許多學者這樣認為，不過目前還沒有發現那個時代的船。考古學家把他們的文化叫做大坌坑文化——名字來自今天新北市的一個遺址。

By about 3,500 years ago, some villages had grown large. At the Peinan Site in Taitung, houses stood in planned rows. Archaeologists have found a great many coffins there, made of flat slabs of slate: more than 2,000 by the museum’s count. Some of the dead were buried with pots and with jade ornaments. The museum says the people of Peinan got their jade from more than 100 kilometers away. One small jade earring from a Peinan coffin, carved with two people and an animal, is now a National Treasure.

到了大約 3,500 年前，有些聚落已經變得很大。在臺東的卑南遺址，家屋排列得有規劃。考古學家在那裡找到非常多用石板做成的棺——照博物館的說法，超過兩千具。有些人下葬的時候，身邊放著陶罐和玉做的裝飾品。博物館說，卑南的人是從 100 多公里外取得玉材的。卑南的一具石板棺裡，有一件小小的玉耳飾，雕著兩個人和一隻獸，現在是國寶。

About 2,500 years ago, the skill of making iron reached Taiwan. At the Shihsanhang Site, near the north coast, which dates from about 1,800 years ago, archaeologists found a furnace and a great deal of iron slag, the waste left over from smelting. They also found glass, gold, and agate ornaments that came from outside the island, a sign of frequent trade. People did not live only near the sea. At Qubing, high in the mountains of Nantou, radiocarbon dates show that people were living there between about 1,000 and more than 2,700 years ago.

大約 2,500 年前，製作鐵器的技術傳入台灣。在靠近北海岸的十三行遺址（年代從大約 1,800 年前開始），考古學家找到煉鐵爐和大量的鐵渣（煉鐵剩下的廢料），也找到來自島外的玻璃、黃金和瑪瑙裝飾品——這是頻繁交易的痕跡。人不是只住在海邊。在南投高山裡的曲冰，碳十四年代顯示，一千年到兩千七百多年前，就有人住在那裡。

So who were all these people? That is harder to answer than it sounds. Pots and tools do not tell us what language people spoke or what they called themselves. The National Museum of Prehistory says there is no direct evidence that the Peinan Culture was handed down to the Pinuyumayan people of today. For some Iron Age cultures, it says only that they may be related to Indigenous cultures. A careful historian says “may be” here, and keeps looking. Lesson 3 follows this question further.

那麼，這些人是誰？這個問題比聽起來難回答。陶罐和工具不會告訴我們，這些人說什麼語言、怎麼稱呼自己。國立臺灣史前文化博物館說，沒有直接證據顯示考古遺址所見的卑南文化和今天的卑南族文化之間有傳承關係；對於鐵器時代的一些文化，博物館也只說「可能」與原住民的文化有所關聯。謹慎的歷史學家在這裡會說「可能」，然後繼續找證據。第三課會接著談這個問題。

Questions · 閱讀理解

What are the three ages of Taiwan’s prehistory, and what is the main difference between them?

The Paleolithic (chipped stone tools, no pottery), the Neolithic (polished stone tools, pottery, and farming), and the Iron Age (iron tools). The difference is in what people made and how they made it.

What did archaeologists find at the Shihsanhang Site that shows people there traded with others far away?

Glass, gold, and agate ornaments that came from outside the island, along with a furnace and iron slag that show they smelted iron themselves.

Why is it hard to say which of today’s peoples are descended from the people of an ancient site?

Because pots and tools do not tell us what language people spoke or what they called themselves. Museums say “no direct evidence” or “may be related,” so the question is still open.

Game · 小遊戲

Which Age Is It From? 這是哪一個時代的東西？

Eight finds, one at a time. Use the rule from the reading: no pottery, pottery and polished stone, or iron and glass. There is no timer; the game only counts how many you place correctly on the first try.
八樣出土物，一次出現一樣。用課文裡的規則來判斷：沒有陶器、有陶器和磨製石器，還是有鐵器和玻璃。這裡不計時；遊戲只記下你第一次就分對幾樣。

The three ages follow the National Museum of Prehistory, which calls the last one the Iron Age. The Bureau of Cultural Heritage calls it the Metal Age. Both names are in use. The museum also describes a “pre-ceramic” stage, when some groups still lived by hunting and gathering, with chipped stone tools, as late as 6,000 years ago; this game leaves that stage out.三個時代的分法依國立臺灣史前文化博物館，最後一個時代稱為「鐵器時代」；文化資產局稱為「金屬器時代」。兩種說法都有人用。史前館另外提到「先陶文化」：到距今 6,000 年前，島內局部地區仍有人以狩獵、採集維生，並以打製石器為工具；這個遊戲沒有把它分出來。

How are these ancient cultures related to today’s Indigenous peoples?這些古老的文化，和今天的原住民族是什麼關係？

On the Peinan Culture關於卑南文化

No direct evidence shows that the Peinan Culture seen at archaeological sites was handed down to the present-day Pinuyumayan people.「考古遺址所見的卑南文化與現生原住民族群卑南族文化並沒有直接證據顯示兩者之間的傳承關係。」

National Museum of Prehistory, “Neolithic” · 史前館〈新石器時代〉 ↗

On Iron Age cultures關於鐵器時代的文化

The cultures of this period may be related to Indigenous cultures. Snake patterns on pottery and stone show a link between prehistoric and Indigenous cultures, and people often discuss whether they are connected with the origin of Paiwan culture. The same page infers that the Jingpu Culture was left by ancestors of the Amis, and says the Siraya were very likely closely related to the Niaosong Culture.「本時期的文化可能與原住民的文化有所關聯。」蛇紋的出現「展露史前文化與原住民文化的連結；常被討論是否為與排灣族文化的起源有關」；「推測靜浦文化是阿美族祖先所留下的文化」；「許多證據都顯示西拉雅族很可能就與蔦松文化有著密切關係」。

National Museum of Prehistory, “Iron Age” · 史前館〈鐵器時代〉 ↗

On the Dapenkeng Culture關於大坌坑文化

It may be the ancestral culture of the earliest speakers of Austronesian languages. The museum puts it this way: many scholars think it may be one of their earliest ancestors.大坌坑文化「可能是最早的南島語族的祖型文化」。史前館的說法是：「許多學者認為，大坌坑文化是從亞洲東南沿海移入的新文化，並可能是南島語族最早的祖先之一。」

Bureau of Cultural Heritage, Dapenkeng site record · 文資局 大坌坑考古遺址 ↗

Notice the careful words: “no direct evidence,” “may be,” “people often discuss.” The later the culture, the more links the museum sees; for the older ones it is more cautious. This lesson does not go further than its sources. Each Indigenous people also has its own account of where it came from, and Lesson 3 will listen to those accounts.注意這些小心的字眼：「沒有直接證據」「可能」「常被討論」。越晚的文化，博物館看到的連結越多；對比較早的文化，就比較保留。這一課不會說得比出處更多。每一個原住民族對自己的來歷也有自己的說法，第三課會聽這些說法。

National Museum of Prehistory國立臺灣史前文化博物館

About 5,500 to 4,200 years ago. (The same page also says these people arrived about 6,000 to 5,000 years ago.)「時間大約在距今5,500到4,200年前之間」（同一頁另寫「約距今6,000至5,000年前」這群人來到台灣）。

The Bureau’s records for other sites文資局：其他遺址

At Yuanshan, about 6,000 to 5,000 years ago; at Peinan, 5,300 to 4,200; at Fengbitou, about 5,200 to 4,350.圓山：「距今約6,000∼5,000年前」；卑南：「距今5,300 ∼4,200年前」；鳳鼻頭：「距今約5,200∼4,350年前」。

Bureau of Cultural Heritage, Yuanshan site record · 文資局 圓山考古遺址 ↗

Five sets of numbers for one culture, all from official pages. Part of the reason is simple: a culture does not start and stop at the same moment everywhere, and each site is dated from its own samples. That is why the map uses each site’s own numbers instead of one number for the whole island.同一個文化，五組數字，全都來自官方的頁面。一部分的原因很單純：一個文化不會在每個地方同時開始、同時結束，而每個遺址的年代是用自己的樣本測出來的。所以地圖上用的是每個遺址自己的數字，而不是全島共用一個數字。

How old are the Wanshan rock carvings?萬山岩雕有多古老？

The researcher who recorded them記錄它們的研究者

Comparing the carvings with later wood carving and with changes in pattern and style, he estimated that they are about 500 to 1,600 years old.發現者「依照露天石刻早於原住民石板屋內木雕，參酌刻紋、圖騰風格變遷，推估岩雕約有500年到1600年歷史」。

Bureau of Cultural Heritage, Wanshan site record · 文資局 萬山岩雕群考古遺址 ↗

The same record, under “layers”同一份資料的「代表文化層」

One view is about 500 years, but the exact age is hard to confirm. The record guesses that the carvings are from the same time as one of two cultures, dated 2,000 to 400 and 2,500 to 1,000 years ago.「有一說約距今500年，但詳細年代難以確認，推測與東埔一鄰（2,000∼400年前）或北葉文化層（2,500∼1,000年前）同時期。」

Bureau of Cultural Heritage, Wanshan site record · 文資局 萬山岩雕群考古遺址 ↗

Lesson 1 said that radiocarbon dating needs something that was once alive, and a line cut into rock is not. The estimate in the record is argued from style. On the map this site is drawn with a dashed ring and never lights up. Saying “we do not know yet” is also an answer.第一課說過，碳十四定年需要「曾經活過的東西」。刻在石頭上的線條不是這樣的東西；資料裡的推估，是從風格去推論的。地圖上這個遺址畫成虛線圈，永遠不會亮。說「我們還不知道」，也是一種答案。

The rule stays the same as in Lesson 1: views are set side by side, each with its source, and the lesson does not choose.規則和第一課一樣：說法並列、各附出處，課文不替誰選邊。

History English · 歷史英語

Eight sentences for talking about a site 談一個遺址的八句英文

These are the sentences a museum label or a guide uses. Tap a speaker to hear each one, then try changing the part in bold.
這些是博物館說明牌和導覽員會用的句子。點 🔊 聽每一句，再試著把粗體的部分換掉。

  1. Archaeologists have found more than 1,500 slate coffins at the Peinan Site.考古學家在卑南遺址找到了一千五百多具石板棺。have found: the finding is done, and the result still matters now.have found：已經找到了，而且結果到現在還有意義。
  2. This layer dates from about 3,500 to 2,300 years ago.這一層的年代大約是 3,500 到 2,300 年前。dates from: says how old something is.dates from：說一樣東西的年代。
  3. The culture is named after the site where it was first recognized.這個文化是用最早認出它的那個遺址來命名的。is named after: where a name comes from.is named after：說一個名字的由來。
  4. The jade was brought from more than 100 kilometers away.這些玉是從 100 多公里外帶來的。was brought from: we know it moved, even if we do not know who carried it.was brought from：知道它被帶過來，即使不知道是誰帶的。
  5. By about 1,800 years ago, people here were smelting iron.到了大約 1,800 年前，這裡的人已經在煉鐵了。by + time: at that time or earlier.by＋時間：最晚到那個時候，事情已經發生了。
  6. Some scholars think the two figures are hunters.有些學者認為，那兩個人是獵人。Some scholars think …: one view, not everyone’s.Some scholars think …：這是一種看法，不是所有人的看法。
  7. There is no direct evidence that the two are linked.沒有直接證據顯示兩者有關聯。no direct evidence: it has not been shown. It has not been ruled out either.no direct evidence：還沒有被證明；但也沒有被排除。
  8. We still do not know how old the carvings are.我們還不知道這些岩雕有多古老。We still do not know …: an honest sentence, and a useful one.We still do not know …：一句老實、也很有用的話。

In Taiwan · 在台灣

A trash heap, a journey of jade, a mountain village, and where to visit 一座垃圾山、玉走過的路、山裡的聚落，以及去哪裡看

Four short notes on what these sites show.
四則短短的說明：這些遺址讓我們看見什麼。

Yuanshan · 圓山

The biggest prehistoric trash heap最大的史前垃圾山

The Bureau of Cultural Heritage calls Yuanshan the place where the study of Taiwan’s archaeology began, and Taiwan’s largest prehistoric garbage hill. Researchers first recorded the site in 1897. The museum explains that the shell mound is a small hill built up mainly from food waste, with shells, animal bones, fish bones, pottery, and stone tools buried in it. Burials were found in the mound as well, and the Bureau’s record says that for the people of Yuanshan it was more than a place to pile trash: it must have had cultural and social meaning too.
文化資產局說圓山是「台灣考古學研究的濫觴、台灣最大的史前垃圾山」。這個遺址發現於 1897 年。史前館說明，圓山貝塚是以食餘為主的廢棄物所堆積起來的小丘，裡面埋藏大量的貝殼、獸骨、魚骨以及陶片、石器；貝塚中也發現了墓葬；文資局的資料說，貝塚對圓山文化人「不只是堆置垃圾之處，應當也是蘊涵了重要的文化與社會意義」。

Jade · 玉

Jade that traveled會旅行的玉

The people of Peinan could get the material for their jade from more than 100 kilometers away, the museum says, and they made many kinds of fine ornaments and tools from it. The same style of earring, with people and an animal, has turned up at other sites in the north and the east.
史前館說，卑南的人能從 100 多公里外的地區取得玉器材料，製作各式各樣精美的裝飾品與工具。同一種人獸形的玉耳飾，在北部和東部的其他遺址也出土過。

Qubing · 曲冰

A village in the high mountains高山上的聚落

At Qubing, on a river terrace in Nantou, an Academia Sinica project uncovered a whole settlement between 1981 and 1987: the remains of houses, 171 slate coffins, and tens of thousands of stone tools and pieces of pottery. The Bureau of Cultural Heritage’s record says the radiocarbon dates show people were living there from about 1,000 to more than 2,700 years ago. The finds do not tell us what these people called themselves.
在南投一處河階上的曲冰，中央研究院的研究計畫在民國 70 到 76 年間挖出一整個聚落：房屋的殘跡、171 座石板棺，以及數以萬計的石器與陶片。文化資產局的資料說，碳十四測定的年代顯示，一千年至二千七百多年前就有人住在這裡。出土的東西沒有告訴我們，這些人怎麼稱呼自己。

Go and see · 親眼看

Museums built beside the sites蓋在遺址旁邊的博物館

The National Museum of Prehistory has three places to visit: its main museum in Taitung City, the Peinan Site Park, also in Taitung City, and the Museum of Archaeology in Tainan. In the north, the Shihsanhang Museum of Archaeology in Bali District, New Taipei City, stands beside the Shihsanhang Site. A site is not a place to dig for souvenirs. Whatever is taken out without being recorded loses its place in the layers, and with it most of what it could have told us.
國立臺灣史前文化博物館有三個地方可以參觀：臺東市的康樂本館、同樣在臺東市的卑南遺址公園，以及臺南的南科考古館。北部的新北市立十三行博物館在八里區，就在十三行遺址旁邊。遺址不是挖紀念品的地方：沒有經過記錄就拿走的東西，會失去它在地層裡的位置——它能告訴我們的事，也跟著失去大半。

Myth vs. Fact · 常見迷思

Four things people get wrong 四個常見的誤會

✗ Myth · 迷思Stone Age people lived simple, rough lives.石器時代的人，過的是簡單粗糙的生活。

✓ Fact · 事實Between 3,500 and 2,300 years ago the people of Peinan planned their village in rows, carved jade a few millimeters thick, and mended a broken earring so they could go on wearing it.距今 3,500 到 2,300 年前，卑南的人把聚落規劃成一排一排的，雕出只有幾公釐厚的玉器，耳飾斷了還修補起來繼續戴。

✗ Myth · 迷思One group of people lived in Taiwan, unchanged, until writing began.台灣一直住著同一群人，直到有文字為止都沒有變。

✓ Fact · 事實The sites show many cultures in different regions. The museum describes people arriving by sea, possibly more than once, and many changes over thousands of years.遺址顯示不同的地區有許多不同的文化。博物館的說明裡，有人渡海而來（可能不只一批），幾千年間也有許多變化。

✗ Myth · 迷思Scientists know exactly which of today’s peoples came from each ancient site.科學家清楚知道，今天的哪一個族群是從哪一個古老的遺址來的。

✓ Fact · 事實Not exactly. For the Peinan Culture and the Pinuyumayan people, the museum says there is no direct evidence of a handed-down link. For some later cultures it sees a likely link, and it words each one with care.沒有這麼確定。對卑南文化和卑南族，博物館說沒有直接證據顯示傳承關係；對一些比較晚的文化，博物館認為很可能有關聯，但每一句都說得很小心。

✗ Myth · 迷思A dark dot on the map means nobody lived there at that time.地圖上的點暗著，就表示那個時候那裡沒有人。

✓ Fact · 事實It means only that no dated layer from that time has been recorded at that site. People may have lived nearby, or their traces may not have been found yet.它只表示那個遺址沒有登錄那個時候的、有年代的文化層。人可能住在附近，或者他們的痕跡還沒被找到。

For Teachers · 給老師的提醒

Curriculum links, names, and care with sensitive points 課綱對應、名稱，以及幾個需要小心的地方

  • Curriculum links (12-Year Basic Education, social studies). Junior high history content: 歷 Ba-Ⅳ-1 (archaeological excavation and prehistoric cultures); the curriculum’s note suggests discussing a prehistoric site or Indigenous community near the school.   • Names. English spellings follow the institutions: Peinan and Pinuyumayan (National Museum of Prehistory; Council of Indigenous Peoples), Shihsanhang (the museum’s own spelling). Other site names are in Hanyu Pinyin; older books in English may use other spellings, such as Tapenkeng. The last age is the Iron Age in the museum’s pages and the Metal Age in the Bureau’s.名稱。英文拼法依各機構：Peinan 與 Pinuyumayan（史前館、原住民族委員會）、Shihsanhang（十三行博物館自己的拼法）。其他遺址名用漢語拼音；較早的英文書可能用別的拼法，例如 Tapenkeng。最後一個時代，史前館稱「鐵器時代」，文資局稱「金屬器時代」。
  • On links to present-day peoples, please do not go beyond the sources. The museum’s wording is “no direct evidence” for Peinan and “may be related” for some Iron Age cultures.   • Burials. Much of what we know about Peinan comes from graves. The lesson says so plainly and asks for respect; it shows no human remains. The 3D earring is a simplified sketch, and the map dots mark districts, not exact locations.墓葬。我們對卑南的了解，很多來自墓葬。課文照實說明，並請學生帶著敬意；頁面上沒有任何人骨的影像。3D 的玉玦是簡化的示意圖；地圖上的圓點標的是鄉鎮市區，不是確切的位置。

Classroom Activity 1 · 課堂活動一

Write a Site Card 寫一張遺址卡

You need · 準備材料

  • The map in this lesson, on a screen or projector這一課的地圖（用螢幕或投影機）
  • One index card for each pair of students每兩人一張小卡片
  • A wall or board for the finished cards一面可以貼卡片的牆或白板

Steps · 步驟

  1. Each pair chooses one of the eight sites and opens its card on the map.每兩人選八個遺址裡的一個，在地圖上打開它的卡片。
  2. On the index card, write four lines in English: its name, where it is, how old its main layer is, and one thing that was found there.在小卡片上用英文寫四行：它的名字、在哪裡、主要那一層有多古老、出土了什麼（寫一樣）。
  3. Add one sentence that begins “We still do not know …”再加一句用「We still do not know …」開頭的話。
  4. Put the cards on the wall from the oldest site to the youngest, and read them aloud in order.把卡片從最古老排到最年輕貼在牆上，照順序唸出來。

Step 3 is the important one. Students will find that every site has an open question: who the people were, where the jade came from exactly, how old a carving is. Use the History English sentences as models. If a pair chooses Wanshan, ask the class where its card should go in the line, and why that is hard to decide.
第三步最重要。學生會發現每個遺址都有還沒解開的問題：這些人是誰、玉確切從哪裡來、岩雕有多古老。可以拿「歷史英語」的句子當範本。如果有一組選了萬山岩雕，問問全班：它的卡片該排在隊伍的哪裡？為什麼很難決定？

Classroom Activity 2 · 課堂活動二

Our Own Shell Mound 我們自己的貝塚

You need · 準備材料

  • One day of clean, dry classroom waste: paper, wrappers, bottle caps, pencil stubs (nothing wet or sharp)教室一天份乾淨、乾燥的回收物與垃圾：紙、包裝、瓶蓋、鉛筆頭（不要濕的或尖銳的東西）
  • Gloves and a large sheet of paper to sort on手套，和一大張可以攤開分類的紙
  • A worksheet with three columns: What it is, What it tells us, What it cannot tell us一張三欄的學習單：這是什麼／它告訴我們什麼／它不能告訴我們什麼

Steps · 步驟

  1. Spread the waste on the paper and sort it into groups, as an archaeologist sorts finds.把東西攤在紙上分成幾堆，就像考古學家替出土物分類。
  2. Imagine you are an archaeologist 3,000 years from now. What could you say about the people who made this pile?想像你是三千年後的考古學家。關於留下這一堆東西的人，你能說出什麼？
  3. Now list what you could not know: their names, their language, what they were laughing about.再列出你不可能知道的事：他們的名字、說的語言、在笑什麼。
  4. Share one sentence with “The evidence suggests that …” and one with “We still do not know …”各說一句：一句用「The evidence suggests that …」，一句用「We still do not know …」。

This is the Yuanshan shell mound in one class period, with one big difference to point out: the real mound also held burials, so it was more than trash to the people who made it. The third step is the bridge to the reading’s last paragraph: things tell us what people ate and made, and very little about who they said they were. Wash hands afterward, and leave out food waste.
這是用一節課做一座「圓山貝塚」。有一點很不一樣，要告訴學生：真正的貝塚裡也有墓葬，對當時的人來說，它不只是垃圾堆。第三步接到課文最後一段：東西能告訴我們人們吃什麼、做什麼，卻很少告訴我們「他們說自己是誰」。活動後要洗手；廚餘不要放進來。
